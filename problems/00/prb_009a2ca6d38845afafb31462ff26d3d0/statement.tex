\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For an integer \(n\geq 2\) and \(p\in[0,1]\), let \(G(n,p)\) be the random simple graph on \(\{1,\ldots,n\}\) in which each unordered pair of distinct vertices is an edge independently with probability \(p\). A tree is a finite connected simple graph with no cycles. Write \(\Delta(T)\) for its maximum vertex degree. An \(n\)-vertex graph contains a spanning copy of an \(n\)-vertex tree \(T\) if there is a bijection from \(V(T)\) to its vertex set that sends every edge of \(T\) to an edge of the graph; the copy need not be induced.

The supplied conjecture asks whether the threshold for containing a spanning comb \(\operatorname{Comb}_n\), and more generally for containing any prescribed bounded-degree spanning tree, has order
\[
 t_n=\frac{\log n}{n}.
\]
Here \(\log\) denotes the natural logarithm; changing its base does not change the proposed order.

One precise fixed-sequence interpretation of the general conjecture is the following. Fix an integer \(D\geq 2\) and a deterministic sequence \((T_n)_{n\geq 2}\) of trees satisfying \(|V(T_n)|=n\) and \(\Delta(T_n)\leq D\). Do there exist constants \(0<c<C<\infty\), independent of \(n\), such that
\[
\begin{aligned}
\lim_{n\to\infty}\Pr\!\left(G\!\left(n,c\frac{\log n}{n}\right)\text{ contains a spanning copy of }T_n\right)&=0,\\
\lim_{n\to\infty}\Pr\!\left(G\!\left(n,C\frac{\log n}{n}\right)\text{ contains a spanning copy of }T_n\right)&=1?
\end{aligned}
\]
The displayed probabilities are understood for sufficiently large \(n\), when their edge probabilities belong to \([0,1]\).
