\section{Research attempt: A tree boundary case for factors of IID and a finite-radius defect bound}

\paragraph{A product-shift fact with a direct proof.}
On $\Omega=\{0,1\}^{\mathbb Z}$ with independent fair-coordinate product
measure $\mu$, let $T$ be the coordinate shift. The map $T^2$ is ergodic.
Here is the needed proof. Events determined by finitely many coordinates become
independent of their $T^{2j}$ translates once the coordinate sets are disjoint.
Every measurable event can be approximated in measure by such events: the
class of approximable events contains finite-coordinate events, is closed under
complements, and is closed under countable unions by first taking a finite union
whose difference in measure from the full union is small. This proves the
claim for the sigma algebra they generate, and null-set completion does not
change it. Approximating two events and using
$\mu(E\mathbin\triangle F)$ to bound the error in an intersection proves
$\mu(E\cap T^{-2j}E)\to\mu(E)^2$.
If $E$ is $T^2$-invariant up to null sets, the left side equals $\mu(E)$;
hence $\mu(E)\in\{0,1\}$.

\paragraph{No factor-of-IID perfect matching on the bi-infinite path.}
Index path vertices by $\mathbb Z$. A shift-equivariant measurable rule from
IID vertex labels to a perfect matching would produce indicators $X_i$ of
whether edge $\{i,i+1\}$ is selected. Perfect matching requires
\[
 X_{i-1}+X_i=1\quad\hbox{for every }i.
\]
It follows that $X_{i+2}=X_i$ and $X_{i+1}=1-X_i$.
As functions of the IID labels, $X_0$ is thus invariant under the squared
shift; the proved ergodicity forces $X_0$ to be almost surely a constant zero
or one. Shift equivariance gives the same constant for $X_1$, contradicting
$X_0+X_1=1$. The argument works for any nontrivial IID product labels by the
same finite-coordinate independence proof, not just binary labels.
It rules out even measurable factors here, so in particular it rules out
finitary factors. There are nevertheless two ordinary perfect matchings,
obtained by choosing one of the two alternating edge sets.

\paragraph{Quantifying the obstruction for a bounded-radius local rule.}
Suppose an edge indicator $X_i$ depends only on IID labels at vertices
$i-r,\ldots,i+1+r$, with a fixed integer $r\geq0$, by a shift-equivariant
rule. Let
$d=\Pr\{X_{i-1}+X_i\ne1\}$, independent of $i$.
Set $L=2r+3$, which is odd. The label sets determining $X_0$ and $X_L$
are disjoint, so they are independent with the same Bernoulli parameter $p$.
Consequently
\[
 \Pr\{X_0=X_L\}=p^2+(1-p)^2\geq\frac12.
\]
If none of the $L$ constraints $X_{j-1}+X_j=1$, $1\leq j\leq L$,
fails, alternation along the odd number $L$ of steps gives $X_L=1-X_0$.
Thus the equality event implies at least one failed constraint. A union bound
proves
\[
 d\geq\frac1{2L}=\frac1{4r+6}.
\]
If the rule always outputs a partial matching, failures are precisely unmatched
vertices; otherwise the bound counts both unmatched and double-matched
vertices. The distinction prevents treating an arbitrary edge-label rule as a
valid partial matching.

\paragraph{What this rules out and what it leaves.}
Adding ever more IID information at a fixed radius cannot drive the defect
probability below the displayed bound. Allowing an unbounded random coding
radius is not controlled by that numeric estimate, but an exact measurable
perfect matching remains impossible on this particular tree by the preceding
invariance argument. This provides a concrete model boundary, not a solution
of the higher-degree tree, unfriendly-coloring, local-lemma or derandomization
questions grouped in the source.

\section{Verification and adversarial audit}

The separation distance $L=2r+3$ is both odd and large enough to make the two
label windows disjoint. The union bound has exactly $L$ intermediate vertex
constraints. The no-factor proof uses equivariance and measurability, while the
finite-radius bound needs independence of disjoint IID windows. Ordinary
choice-based alternating matchings are not claimed to be factors.
