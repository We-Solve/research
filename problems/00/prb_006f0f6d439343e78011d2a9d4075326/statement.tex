\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A group is finitely presented if it has a presentation with finitely many generators and relators. A finitely generated infinite group is one-ended if, in a Cayley graph for a finite generating set, removing any finite vertex set leaves exactly one infinite connected component.

For infinite-order elements $a,b\in G$, let
\[
H(G;a,b)=\langle G,t\mid t^{-1}at=b\rangle
\]
be the HNN extension of $G$ identifying the infinite cyclic subgroups $\langle a\rangle$ and $\langle b\rangle$ by $a^k\mapsto b^k$ for $k\in\Z$. Its vertex group $G$ embeds by the natural inclusion. The notation $\langle G,t\mid\cdots\rangle$ means that all relations of $G$ are retained.

Sageev's question, with the supplied research summary's explicit interpretation of the source notation, is:
\[
\exists\,G,a,b:\quad G\text{ is finitely presented and one-ended},\quad
\operatorname{ord}(a)=\operatorname{ord}(b)=\infty,\quad
G\cong H(G;a,b)?
\]
The isomorphism is an abstract group isomorphism; it is not required to equal the natural inclusion of the base group. The terse transcribed expression $G\cong G\ast\Z$ omits the associated subgroups and can look like a free product. The supplied summary expressly says that the verified intended operation is an HNN extension over $\Z$, not a free product; the statement above uses that clarification.

The supplied summary labels the question open and reports no solution or direct literature attack. It also claims, as its author's own analysis, necessary conditions on any example: $G$ is non-co-Hopfian (admits an injective nonsurjective endomorphism), is not torsion-free hyperbolic, and contains an infinite descending chain of proper subgroups isomorphic to itself. Transporting the stable-letter map $H(G;a,b)\to\Z$, which kills the base and sends $t$ to $1$, gives a map $\chi:G\to\Z$. The summary claims its kernel is an infinitely generated graph-of-groups group with vertex groups isomorphic to $G$, and that $[\chi],[-\chi]\notin\Sigma^1(G)$, the Bieri--Neumann--Strebel invariant. It further claims that $\bar a-\bar b$ has infinite order and is primitive in $G^{\mathrm{ab}}=G/[G,G]$, and in particular $b\ne a$ and $G\not\cong G\times\Z$. The record supplies no proofs or precise conventions for these auxiliary claims. Its statement that Euler characteristic and $L^2$-Betti numbers give no obstruction is likewise reported analysis, with the assumptions needed to use these invariants left unspecified; it does not establish existence of an example.
\subsection{Short English statement}
The intended question asks whether a finitely presented one-ended group can be abstractly isomorphic to an HNN extension of itself along two infinite cyclic subgroups. The supplied summary explicitly resolves the ambiguous star notation in favor of HNN extension and reports the problem open; its additional necessary-condition analysis is not supplied with proofs.
