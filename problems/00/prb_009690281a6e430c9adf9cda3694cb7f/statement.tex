\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
The supplied record asks whether helicity is preserved by topological conjugacy of exact volume-preserving vector fields. It does not specify the ambient manifold, its boundary conditions, the volume form, or whether the conjugating homeomorphism must preserve volume. Consequently, the intended quantified statement cannot be recovered uniquely.

For a conditional formulation in the standard closed three-dimensional setting, let \(M\) be a smooth closed oriented three-manifold with a smooth positive volume form \(\mu\). A smooth vector field \(X\) preserves volume if \(\mathcal L_X\mu=0\), and is exact volume-preserving if there exists a smooth one-form \(\alpha\) such that
\[
\iota_X\mu=\dd\alpha.
\]
Its helicity relative to \(\mu\) is
\[
\mathcal H_\mu(X)=\int_M\alpha\wedge\dd\alpha.
\]
In this closed setting the integral is independent of the choice of primitive \(\alpha\). Write \(\varphi_X^t\) for the flow of \(X\). A time-preserving topological conjugacy between \(X_1\) and \(X_2\) is a homeomorphism \(h:M\to M\) satisfying
\[
 h\circ\varphi_{X_1}^t=\varphi_{X_2}^t\circ h
 \qquad\text{for every }t\in\R.
\]
Under this conditional interpretation, the question is whether, for every such \((M,\mu)\) and every pair of smooth exact volume-preserving vector fields \(X_1,X_2\) on \(M\), the existence of a topological conjugacy implies

\mathcal H_\mu(X_1)=\mathcal H_\mu(X_2).
