The mathematical objects involved can be described conditionally. For a one-dimensional nonconservative system $u_t+A(u)u_x=0$ on a state domain $\mathcal U\subset\R^m$, an admissible path family joins $u_L$ to $u_R$ by a piecewise Lipschitz path $\Phi:[0,1]\to\mathcal U$. Given approximate intermediate Riemann states $u_0=u_L,u_1,\ldots,u_s=u_R$, the chosen subpaths concatenate paths from $u_{r-1}$ to $u_r$. They determine the nonconservative jump integral
\[
 \mathcal I(u_L,u_R)=\int_0^1 A(\Phi(t))\Phi'(t)\,dt.
\]
Thus specifying endpoints alone need not specify the weak jump relation. An entropy pair consists of a convex $\eta$ and flux $q$ satisfying $\nabla q=(\nabla\eta)^{\mathsf T}A$ wherever smooth.

For example, in a semidiscrete finite-volume fluctuation formulation, let $D^-+D^+=\mathcal I$, set $v=\nabla\eta$, and use the convention
\[
 \dot u_j=-\frac{D^-(u_j,u_{j+1})+D^+(u_{j-1},u_j)}{\Delta x}.
\]
An interface entropy-conservation criterion is
\[
 v_L^{\mathsf T}D^-+v_R^{\mathsf T}D^+=q_R-q_L;
\]
the corresponding entropy-stability inequality has $\geq$ in place of equality and yields nonincreasing total discrete entropy on a periodic grid. This convention illustrates what a subpath-and-viscosity construction must verify; it does not specify the missing approximate solver, admissibility conditions, or a time-discrete CFL theorem.
