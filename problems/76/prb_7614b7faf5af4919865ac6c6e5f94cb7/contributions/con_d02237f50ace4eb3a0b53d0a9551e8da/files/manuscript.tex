argument for that proposition, not a claim that a biological system satisfies

identified parameter, or measured response function is an input when the text
says it is known. Computing with such an input does not solve its identification.

mathematical baselines or direct counterexamples.

\section*{Genome-wide background selection}
\label{prob:165}
\noindent\textbf{Status: restricted result or obstruction; full target not established.}
\subsection{Target and scope}
\paragraph{Catalogue definitions and assumptions.}
At a neutral genomic coordinate $x$, let $\pi(x)$ be expected pairwise differences per base. In diploid panmixia $4N_e\mu(x)$ is its neutral benchmark. Deleterious mutations arise at annotated sites with rate $U_j$ and a finite-parameter distribution of effects $(s_j,h_j)$; a measured recombination map $r(x)$ and population history are inputs. Define $B_\theta(x)=\pi_{\mathrm{BGS},\theta}(x)/\pi_{0,\theta}(x)$ under purifying selection only. Functional annotation uncertainty is explicitly modelled instead of declaring every unannotated site neutral. Neutral baselines must use the same demographic and mutation parameters as the purifying-selection model. Comparing with a different population-size history would attribute demographic diversity change to background selection. Recombination-map error is propagated into prediction uncertainty.
\paragraph{Original target.}
Develop a tractable approximation $\widehat B$ with a declared accuracy target over a genomic model envelope:
\[
 \sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\frac1{|X|}
 \int_X |\widehat B_\theta(x)-B_\theta(x)|\,\,\mathrm{d} x\le\varepsilon,
\]
and determine whether parameters calibrated on one chromosome predict $\pi$ on held-out chromosomes without absorbing mutation-rate error into selection strength.
\paragraph{Catalogue alternative formulations.}
(i) Under strong deleterious selection and high recombination, recover classical independent-site approximations. (ii) In selfing or low-recombination genomes include interference and recessivity, allowing departures from a simple effective-size multiplier. These regimes require different genealogical approximations.
\subsection{Attempted answer}
\begin{proposition}
In the explicit approximation $\pi_\ell=4N_e\mu_\ell B_\ell$, diversity alone identifies products rather than background-selection factors. If mutation rates are calibrated and a matched reference has known $B_0=1$, then $B_\ell=(\pi_\ell/\mu_\ell)/(\pi_0/\mu_0)$ under the common-demography assumption.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Rearrange the diversity equation and divide by the reference expression. Without the anchor, multiplying all $B$ values by an admissible common factor and dividing $N_e$ by it preserves predictions. Unknown locus-specific mutation introduces additional ambiguity.
\end{proof}
\subsection{What remains unresolved}
The approximation omits more general linked selection and structure. A valid neutral anchor, fitness-effect distribution and held-out recombination dependence require empirical support.
\subsection{Completion Estimate}
\textbf{20\%.} The displayed calculation is established under its additional assumptions. The broader theoretical and biological requirements listed above are not established.

\section{Shared mathematical bounds}\label{app:bounds}
\begin{lemma}[Independent bounded sums]\label{lem:bounded}
Let independent real variables $X_i$ satisfy $a_i\leq X_i\leq b_i$ almost surely,
and let $S=\sum_{i=1}^n(X_i-\mathbb{E} X_i)$. If
$V=\sum_i(b_i-a_i)^2>0$, then for every $t>0$,
\[
 \mathbb{P}(|S|\geq t)\leq 2\exp(-2t^2/V).
\]
In particular, independent losses in $[0,1]$ have mean-error bound
$\mathbb{P}(|\overline X-\mathbb{E}\overline X|\geq\epsilon)
\leq2e^{-2n\epsilon^2}$. The same expression for variables in $[-1,1]$
is $2e^{-n\epsilon^2/2}$. If $V=0$ then $S=0$ almost surely.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $X\in[a,b]$ with mean $\mu$, the nonnegative product
$(X-a)(b-X)$ gives
$\operatorname{Var} X\leq(\mu-a)(b-\mu)\leq(b-a)^2/4$.
Let $K(s)=\log\mathbb{E}\exp[s(X-\mathbb{E} X)]$.
Boundedness permits two differentiations. Its second derivative is the variance
under the exponentially tilted law, which has the same support, so
$K''(s)\leq(b-a)^2/4$ for every real $s$.
Because $K(0)=K'(0)=0$, integration yields
$K(s)\leq s^2(b-a)^2/8$.
Independence adds these log moment-generating bounds for $S$.
For $s>0$, Markov's inequality gives
$\mathbb{P}(S\geq t)\leq\exp(-st+s^2V/8)$.
Choosing $s=4t/V$ yields the upper-tail bound $e^{-2t^2/V}$.
Apply it also to $-S$ and add. The stated mean versions follow by substituting
$t=n\epsilon$ and the respective support widths.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Finite-family extension.}
For $M$ prespecified bounded-loss comparisons, apply the relevant inequality to
each and use a union bound. Independence between comparisons is not required;
independence of the biological units within each comparison is. Repeated cells,
reads or time points from one dependent biological unit cannot automatically be
counted as independent units.

\paragraph{No data-dependent shortcut.}
The bound does not justify choosing a model or validation loss using the same
outcomes and then reporting single-model coverage. A prespecified finite family,
a separate validation sample, or an appropriate complexity or sequential argument
is needed. Marginal predictive coverage likewise should not be read as coverage
for every subgroup or under arbitrary distribution shift.
