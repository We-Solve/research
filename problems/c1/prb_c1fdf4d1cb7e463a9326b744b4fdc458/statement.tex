\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Takagi's recovered question is: let \((R,\mathfrak m)\) be a regular local ring of characteristic \(p\), with ideals \(I,\mathfrak a,\mathfrak b\) satisfying
\[
\mathfrak a\subset I,\qquad
\mathcal J(I^{-\epsilon}\mathfrak a^s)\subset\mathfrak m,\qquad
\mathcal J(\mathfrak b^t)\subset\mathfrak m.
\]
Does one have
\[
\mathcal J(I^{-\epsilon}\mathfrak a^s\mathfrak b^t)
\subseteq\mathcal J(I^{-\epsilon}\mathfrak a^s)\mathcal J(\mathfrak b^t)?
\]
The calligraphic \(\mathcal J\), admissible exponents, and meaning of the negative perturbation \(I^{-\epsilon}\) are not defined in the source. This is a formal decorated-ideal expression, not an ordinary negative power of a possibly noninvertible ideal. In particular a characteristic-zero multiplier ideal cannot silently be substituted for an unspecified characteristic-\(p\) invariant.

The report gives an explicitly restricted common-simple-normal-crossings interpretation. Let \(x_1,\ldots,x_r\) belong to one regular parameter system and take nonzero principal monomial ideals
\[
I=(\prod_i x_i^{\iota_i}),\quad
\mathfrak a=(\prod_i x_i^{\alpha_i}),\quad
\mathfrak b=(\prod_i x_i^{\beta_i}),
\qquad \iota_i,\alpha_i,\beta_i\in\Z_{\geq0},
\]
with \(\alpha_i\geq\iota_i\), expressing \(\mathfrak a\subseteq I\). In the nonnegative-exponent regime put
\[
c_i=s\alpha_i-\epsilon\iota_i\geq0,\qquad t\geq0.
\]
The report notes \(0<\epsilon<s\) is sufficient for these coefficient inequalities. For the characteristic-zero SNC multiplier ideal, or for the characteristic-\(p\) SNC generalized test ideal under the standard \(F\)-finite regular hypotheses, it proposes
\[
\mathcal J(I^{-\epsilon}\mathfrak a^s)
=(\prod_i x_i^{\lfloor c_i\rfloor}),\qquad
\mathcal J(\mathfrak b^t)=(\prod_i x_i^{\lfloor t\beta_i\rfloor}),
\]
\[
\mathcal J(I^{-\epsilon}\mathfrak a^s\mathfrak b^t)
=(\prod_i x_i^{\delta_i})\,
\mathcal J(I^{-\epsilon}\mathfrak a^s)\mathcal J(\mathfrak b^t),
\qquad
\delta_i=\left\lfloor\{c_i\}+\{t\beta_i\}\right\rfloor\in\{0,1\}.
\]
Braces denote fractional parts, so the correction is a squarefree carry monomial. The claimed inclusion follows in this interpretation, with equality exactly when every \(\delta_i=0\), including the boundary rule that a fractional-part sum equal to one gives a carry. This supplies a special case of the original proper-factor question when its two \(\mathfrak m\)-containments hold; it is not a definition of the source's \(\mathcal J\) in general. The report includes all nonzero ideals in a DVR under the same exponent regime, but does not resolve the non-SNC case or negative combined coefficients.
