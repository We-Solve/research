\paragraph{Definitions and conventions.}
A Hilbert manifold is a differentiable manifold modeled on a Hilbert space. A densely defined vector field is defined on a dense domain and a flow solves its evolution equation. Stone\textquotesingle s theorem identifies strongly continuous unitary groups with self-adjoint generators in the linear case. For a composite quantum system, no signaling means that local operations on one subsystem do not alter another subsystem\textquotesingle s observable statistics. Dissipative hydrodynamics permits irreversible evolution, rather than only Hamiltonian transport.

\paragraph{An explicit ensemble-dependence obstruction.} On a qubit pure state $|\psi\rangle$, consider nonlinear evolution $i\dot\psi=\langle\psi,Z\psi\rangle Z\psi$. In Bloch-vector coordinates $r=(x,y,z)$ with $|r|=1$, the effective Hamiltonian is $zZ$, so $\dot r=2z(e_z\times r)$. The maximally mixed density matrix has an ensemble of opposite $z$-axis states, each stationary, so its ensemble-averaged derivative is zero.

Choose instead $r_\pm=\pm(\sin\theta,0,\cos\theta)$ with $0<\theta<\pi/2$. Their equal mixture is the same maximally mixed state, but both Bloch derivatives equal $(0,2\sin\theta\cos\theta,0)$. Thus evolving the pure members and averaging gives a nonzero mixed-state derivative. The evolution is not determined by the density matrix alone.

\paragraph{A composite-system consequence under an explicit rule.} A maximally entangled pair allows the remote subsystem to be decomposed into either of those equal antipodal ensembles by measuring the other subsystem in the corresponding basis. If the nonlinear law is applied separately to those conditional pure states and outcomes are ignored, the local predictions depend on the remote basis. This violates no signaling under that ensemble-evolution rule. Another extension rule would need to be defined and checked; the example is not a theorem against every nonlinear formulation.
