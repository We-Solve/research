\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(P=(P_1,P_2):\R^2\to\R^2\), where \(P_1,P_2\in\R[x,y]\). Its real Jacobian determinant is
\[
J_P(x,y)=\det\begin{pmatrix}
\partial P_1/\partial x & \partial P_1/\partial y\\
\partial P_2/\partial x & \partial P_2/\partial y
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Under the usual total-degree convention, set
\[
\deg P=\max\{\deg P_1,\deg P_2\}.
\]
If ``invertible'' means invertible as a map from \(\R^2\) onto \(\R^2\), define
\[
\mathcal D=\left\{\deg P:\begin{array}{l}
P\in\R[x,y]^2,\\
J_P(a)\ne0\text{ for every }a\in\R^2,\\
P:\R^2\to\R^2\text{ is not bijective}
\end{array}\right\}.
\]
The question, under these conventions, is to determine \(\min\mathcal D\), provided \(\mathcal D\ne\varnothing\): what is the lowest degree of a real polynomial map of the plane whose Jacobian determinant never vanishes but which fails to be globally invertible?
