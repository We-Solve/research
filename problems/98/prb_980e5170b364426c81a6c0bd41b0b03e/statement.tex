\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 700601}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A variety of groups is the class of all groups satisfying a specified collection of group identities. It is proper if it is not the variety of all groups.

Fix a prime \(p\). A finite \(p'\)-group is a finite group whose order is not divisible by \(p\). A finite group \(G\) is \(p\)-soluble if it admits a series
\[
1=G_0\leq G_1\leq\cdots\leq G_m=G,
\qquad G_i\trianglelefteq G,
\]
in which every factor \(G_i/G_{i-1}\) is either a \(p\)-group or a \(p'\)-group. Its \(p\)-length, denoted \(\ell_p(G)\), is the minimum number of nontrivial \(p\)-group factors among all such series. Here \(p\)-solubility does not require the \(p'\)-group factors to be soluble.

\textbf{Question (John S. Wilson, 1984).} For every proper variety of groups \(\mathfrak V\) and every prime \(p\), does there exist an integer \(L=L(\mathfrak V,p)\geq 0\) such that every finite \(p\)-soluble group \(G\) whose Sylow \(p\)-subgroups belong to \(\mathfrak V\) satisfies
\[
\ell_p(G)\leq L?
\]
Membership in \(\mathfrak V\) for one Sylow \(p\)-subgroup is equivalent to membership for all of them, since they are conjugate. The requested bound may depend on \(\mathfrak V\) and \(p\), but must be independent of \(G\).
\subsection{Short English statement}
For each fixed proper variety of groups and each fixed prime \(p\), are the \(p\)-lengths of all finite \(p\)-soluble groups with Sylow \(p\)-subgroups in that variety bounded? The supplied registry labels this problem open; that status has not been independently verified.
