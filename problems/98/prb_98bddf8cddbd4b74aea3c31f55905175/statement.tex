\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 30002368}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(M\) be a closed smooth \(3\)-manifold, where closed means compact and without boundary. Consider a Ricci flow with surgery originating on \(M\), written \((M_t,g(t))\), which exists for all sufficiently large times. On each smooth time interval between surgeries, its metric satisfies
\[
\frac{\partial g}{\partial t}=-2\operatorname{Ric}_{g(t)}.
\]
The notation \(M_t\) allows the underlying manifold to change at surgery times.

\textbf{Problem.} For such flows, determine whether, and in what precise geometric sense, the behavior of \((M_t,g(t))\) as \(t\to\infty\) exhibits the full geometric decomposition of the original manifold \(M\).

The supplied record does not define ``full geometric decomposition'' or the relation by which the evolving metrics should exhibit it. It specifies neither a normalization or convergence notion nor the admissible surgery procedure. Consequently, this is a statement of the supplied research question, rather than a uniquely specified conjecture about convergence. In particular, the intended treatment of components removed by surgery or lost through extinction, and their relation to the decomposition of the original \(M\), requires clarification.

The original formulation states that the finite-surgery part of Perelman's conjecture is known. The supplied literature assessment further attributes finite surgery, curvature control for arbitrary closed \(3\)-manifolds, and a qualitative asymptotic description to Bamler, while labeling the stronger question about the full geometric decomposition unresolved. These are supplied literature qualifications, not independently verified assertions in this draft.
