\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 30000956}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(\Sigma\) be a compact orientable surface with \(\chi(\Sigma)<0\), and let \(\operatorname{MCG}(\Sigma)\) be its intended mapping class group. A mapping class \(\phi\) has a pseudo-Anosov piece if, after taking a positive power that preserves the components of a Nielsen--Thurston reduction, its restriction to at least one complementary component is pseudo-Anosov. Thus the hypothesis includes reducible mapping classes with a pseudo-Anosov component; it is not restricted to classes pseudo-Anosov on the whole surface.

Fix the intended family of Reshetikhin--Turaev TQFT representations at allowed levels \(k\),
\[
 \rho_k:\operatorname{MCG}(\Sigma)\longrightarrow\operatorname{PGL}(V_k)
\]
in the projective convention, or the precisely specified linear/central-extension version. For projective images, infinite order means that for every integer \(m\geq1\), \(\rho_k(\phi)^m\ne1\); equivalently, no positive power of a linear representative is scalar. The family, label data, and allowed level set \(\mathcal I\) must be supplied.

The extracted Andersen--Masbaum--Ueno conjecture is
\[
 \forall\phi\in\operatorname{MCG}(\Sigma),\qquad
 \phi\text{ has a pseudo-Anosov piece}
\]
\[
 \Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \exists k_0(\phi)\ \forall k\in\mathcal I\text{ with }k\geq k_0(\phi),
 \quad\rho_k(\phi)\text{ has infinite order}.
\]
The surface and representation family are fixed before \(\phi\). The threshold may depend on \(\phi\); no uniform threshold for all mapping classes is asserted. The right side requires every sufficiently large allowed level, not merely infinitely many levels or a nontrivial image.

The phrase ``a Reshetikhin--Turaev TQFT representation'' is insufficient to make this assertion hold for an arbitrary choice of family. On a surface with boundary or marked points, labels, their dependence on level, and whether all labels are combined or chosen existentially can affect the conclusion. Likewise, ordinary linear order can differ from projective order because of scalar factors. These conventions are unresolved in the supplied extraction and have not been filled by silently quantifying over every quantum theory or every fixed coloring.

The supplied assessment dated 2026-08-21 reports proofs for important special surfaces and representation families but says the general AMU conjecture remains open. It supplies no full list of those cases or their coloring and level hypotheses.
\subsection{Short English statement}
The conjecture says that a mapping class has a pseudo-Anosov component exactly when its quantum image has infinite order at every sufficiently large level. The quantum theory, boundary labels, allowed levels, and linear versus projective meaning of order must be fixed; the claim is not about every arbitrary TQFT family.
