\paragraph{An expanding-background energy test.} On a flat periodic spatial torus, take the auxiliary scalar equation
\[
 \phi_{tt}+3H\phi_t-e^{-2Ht}\Delta\phi=0,\qquad H>0,
\]
and define \(E(t)=\frac12\int(\phi_t^2+e^{-2Ht}|\nabla\phi|^2)dx\). For smooth real solutions, multiplication by \(\phi_t\) and periodic integration by parts give
\[
 E'(t)=-3H\int\phi_t^2dx-H e^{-2Ht}\int|\nabla\phi|^2dx\leq-2HE(t).
\]
Integrating \((e^{2Ht}E)'\leq0\) yields \(E(t)\leq e^{-2Ht}E(0)\).
\paragraph{The nondecaying mode check.} Spatially constant solutions are \(\phi(t)=A+B e^{-3Ht}\). Their derivative energy decays, yet \(\phi\) approaches an arbitrary constant \(A\). An energy that controls derivatives alone therefore cannot force convergence to one fixed background parameter. In a geometric problem the analogous zero or parameter modes must be identified and included in the asymptotic description, not discarded as an energy-estimate detail.
