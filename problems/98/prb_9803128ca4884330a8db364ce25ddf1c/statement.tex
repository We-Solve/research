\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 30000468}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(M\) be a closed connected aspherical \(n\)-manifold. Closed means compact without boundary, and aspherical means that its universal cover is contractible, in the usual manifold/CW setting. For an oriented \(M\), its real simplicial volume is
\[
 \|M\|=\inf\left\{\sum_{j=1}^s|a_j|:
 c=\sum_{j=1}^s a_j\sigma_j\text{ is a real singular }n\text{-cycle with }[c]=[M]_{\R}\right\}.
\]
This uses real singular chains and the real fundamental class, not integral or stable integral simplicial volume.

Let \(\Gamma=\pi_1(M)\), and let \(b_i^{(2)}(M)\) be the usual group/von-Neumann-dimension \(L^2\)-Betti number associated with the universal cover and its deck action. In the aspherical case it is the group invariant, expressible as
\[
 b_i^{(2)}(M)=\dim_{L\Gamma}\operatorname{Tor}^{\C\Gamma}_i(L\Gamma,\C),
 \qquad i\in\Z_{\geq0},
\]
with trivial coefficient module \(\C\), canonical trace on \(L\Gamma\), and extended von Neumann dimension. It is not the ordinary real Betti number or the Hilbert dimension of harmonic forms on the compact base.

The source, attributing the assertion to Gromov, asks whether
\[
 \forall M\text{ closed and aspherical},\qquad
 \|M\|=0\ \Longrightarrow\ b_i^{(2)}(M)=0
 \quad\text{for every }i\geq0.
\]
No dimension restriction, residual finiteness, amenability or other group hypothesis is added. The assertion is an implication from vanishing simplicial volume to vanishing of all degrees, not just vanishing of the Euler characteristic, and no converse is asserted.

Orientability is not explicit in the supplied statement. A standard extension for a connected nonorientable manifold defines its simplicial volume as one half that of its oriented double cover; this convention should be confirmed if the unrestricted wording is intended. The connected convention is explicit here, with a disconnected version requiring the usual componentwise conventions. These do not authorize replacing the stated invariant by a different simplicial-volume theory.

The literature triage checked 2026-08-21 says the source describes the conjecture as wide open and reports no general resolution found. This dated assessment, the open label and the reported official-source verification are retained without independent confirmation.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Gromov's conjecture asks whether zero real simplicial volume forces every \(L^2\)-Betti number to vanish for a closed aspherical manifold. The universal-cover/group \(L^2\) invariants and real fundamental-cycle norm are specified, with no extra group assumptions. The source leaves orientability conventions implicit.
