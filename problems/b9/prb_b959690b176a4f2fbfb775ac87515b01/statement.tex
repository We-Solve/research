\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $N$ be a positive composite integer with an intended nontrivial factorization $N=ab$, $a,b>1$. Write
\[
 a=\sum_{i\ge0}a_i2^i,\qquad
 b=\sum_{j\ge0}b_j2^j,\qquad a_i,b_j\in\{0,1\}.
\]
Nonconsecutive known bits can be encoded by finite position sets $I,J\subseteq\N_0$ and supplied values $(a_i)_{i\in I}$, $(b_j)_{j\in J}$, with gaps allowed between positions. This notation is a two-factor input model, not a source-imposed restriction to semiprimes or balanced prime factors.

The task is to develop methods using such scattered partial information to factor $N$, or recover the intended factors when that is required. The source does not prescribe how many bits are known, their distribution, whether bits of one or both factors are given, or whether factor lengths are known. It does not restrict the factors to primes, fix a balanced-size promise, or specify treatment of multiple consistent factorizations.

A precise algorithmic target must state those input promises, whether positions are adversarial or random, the desired success guarantee, and a running-time bound in terms of input size. No efficient algorithm for every arbitrary bit pattern is demanded without sufficient leakage assumptions: the record gives no universal threshold or complexity theorem. The central request is to handle nonconsecutive information rather than silently replacing it with a known contiguous block.
