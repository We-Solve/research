\section{Classical 3-manifold theory — Question 9.1}\label{10300034}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 10300034 · Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A codimension-one foliation $\mathcal F$ decomposes a $3$-manifold $M$ into surface leaves, locally as the horizontal slices of a product chart. In the terminology of the source, it is taut if a closed curve transverse to $\mathcal F$ meets every leaf. A minimal foliation means that every leaf is dense; a minimal surface means a surface with vanishing mean curvature for the specified Riemannian metric. These two uses of minimal are distinct. Dehn surgery on a link removes disjoint solid-torus neighborhoods and reglues solid tori with chosen meridian slopes. A transverse link has every component everywhere transverse to the given foliation.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Does there exist a fixed $3$-manifold $M$ such that, for every tautly foliated $3$-manifold $(N,\mathcal F)$, some link $L\subset N$ transverse to $\mathcal F$ has surgery yielding $M$? All surgery slopes and the link may depend on $(N,\mathcal F)$; $M$ must be independent of them. \sref{10300034}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can surgery on a link transverse to any given taut foliation always produce one fixed universal manifold?
