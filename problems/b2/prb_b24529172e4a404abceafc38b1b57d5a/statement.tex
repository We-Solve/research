\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
\emph{Question (Agol).} Does every hyperbolic \(3\)-manifold \(M\) have a finite-sheeted covering space \(\widetilde M\) homeomorphic to a CAT(0) cube complex? In literal quantified form, ask whether there exist a finite-sheeted covering \(p:\widetilde M\to M\), a cubical complex \(X\) with its intended CAT(0) metric, and a homeomorphism \(\widetilde M\cong X\), for each \(M\) in the intended class. The source does not fix compactness, completeness, finite volume, boundary, or orientation conventions. It suggests arithmetic \(3\)-manifolds containing a geodesic surface as a test case.

There is a necessary terminology qualification. In standard metric terminology a globally CAT(0) space is contractible. A locally CAT(0), or nonpositively curved, cube complex can have nontrivial fundamental group; its universal cover is CAT(0) under the usual hypotheses. These are different conditions. For example, a finite cover of a closed hyperbolic \(3\)-manifold has nontrivial fundamental group and so cannot be homeomorphic to a globally CAT(0) space. Thus the literal global-CAT(0) wording conflicts with the intended inclusion of such manifolds. A possibly intended question asks for a locally CAT(0) cubical structure on the finite cover, but this is a distinct formulation and is not silently substituted.

The supplied summary describes virtual-specialness-type results, credits Agol and Haglund--Wise, and mentions finite-index fundamental groups inside right-angled Coxeter groups acting on CAT(0) cube complexes. Such group actions or cubical classifying spaces do not by themselves exhibit a cube complex homeomorphic to the covering manifold. The summary calls the stronger wording open and says the relevant cover is a special cube complex; its scope and use of ``cover'' versus group model require review in light of the global/local distinction.
\subsection{Short English statement}
The source asks for a finite cover of every hyperbolic \(3\)-manifold that is homeomorphic to a CAT(0) cube complex. Its wording needs clarification: global CAT(0) implies contractibility, whereas the plausible cubical-manifold requirement may be local CAT(0). Group cubulations alone do not settle the homeomorphism requirement.
