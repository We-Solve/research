\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
The source asks which conditions on a Dirichlet-form generator \(\delta^*\delta\) guarantee that its associated bimodule embeds into a direct sum of coarse bimodules \(L^2(N)\otimes L^2(N)\). To fix the setting used by the supplied aid, let \((N,\tau)\) be a finite tracial von Neumann algebra with a conservative symmetric completely Dirichlet form, represented on a dense algebraic core \(\mathcal A\subset N\) by a closable derivation \(\delta\), with
\[
 \mathcal E(a)=\|\delta a\|^2,
 \qquad L=\delta^*\overline\delta
\]
in the associated closed-form sense. Write \(H_\delta\) for the essential Cipriani--Sauvageot gradient correspondence, the closure of the span of \(a\delta(b)c\), \(a,b,c\in\mathcal A\). Using the essential correspondence matters: arbitrary unused summands of a derivation target are not determined by its form.

The literal embedding target is a bimodular isometry
\[
 H_\delta\hookrightarrow
 \bigoplus_{i\in I}\bigl(L^2(N,\tau)\otimes L^2(N,\tau)\bigr)
\]
for an appropriate index set \(I\). The two tensor factors carry the left and right actions. This is stronger than weak containment in the coarse correspondence; the source does not explicitly distinguish these interpretations, and the aid addresses literal embedding.

\textbf{Criterion reported in the supplied aid.} Let \(Q=N\,\overline\otimes\,N^{\mathrm{op}}\) with product trace. The following are reported equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The above literal coarse-amplification embedding exists.
\item The joint left-right representation \(\rho(a\otimes b^{\mathrm{op}})\xi=a\xi b\) extends normally to \(Q\).
\item For every algebraic gradient vector \(\xi\in\operatorname{span}\mathcal A\delta(\mathcal A)\mathcal A\), its positive coefficient functional has a positive normal density: there is \(h_\xi\in L^1(Q)_+\) with
\[
 \langle\rho(z)\xi,\xi\rangle
      =(\tau\otimes\tau^{\mathrm{op}})(h_\xi z)
      \qquad(z\in N\odot N^{\mathrm{op}}).
\]
\end{enumerate}
The coefficient formula uses the convention in which the displayed vector functional is linear in \(z\). Polarization and density then recover the corresponding normal coefficient condition on the full essential module. In contrast, weak coarse containment requires only continuity for the minimal \(C^*\)-tensor norm, not normal extension to the von Neumann tensor product.

The aid mentions the established unregularized gradient-\(S_2\) condition as a concrete sufficient criterion under its applicable hypotheses. Its precise definition and theorem assumptions are not supplied. It separately uses immediate, positive-time heat-regularized gradient-\(S_2\), which must not be silently identified with that unregularized sufficient condition.

\textbf{Separations reported in the aid.} For the circle Laplacian on \(N=L^\infty(\mathbb T)\), the essential gradient module is the diagonal module \(L^2(\mathbb T)\), with \(a\xi b=ab\xi\). Its joint coefficients are supported on the diagonal in \(\mathbb T^2\), singular to product Haar measure. Hence it has no literal coarse-amplification embedding. The aid reports that it is weakly coarse and has compact positive-time heat operators and immediate heat-regularized gradient-\(S_2\). Thus these latter properties together do not imply normality of coefficients.

For \(N=L(\mathbb F_2)\), take the conditionally negative function
\[
 \psi(g)=|g|+|\varphi(g)|^2,
\]
where \(|g|\) is standard word length and \(\varphi:\mathbb F_2\to\Z\) is a nonzero homomorphism. The Fourier heat semigroup satisfies \(P_t(u_g)=e^{-t\psi(g)}u_g\). The aid reports that properness gives compact heat operators, while the associated mixed-cocycle gradient is not even weakly coarse. This is stronger than failure of literal embedding.

Conversely, for word length on \(\mathbb F_\infty\), the aid reports literal coarse-amplification embedding of the gradient correspondence but noncompact heat operators: infinitely many length-one group elements have the same positive heat eigenvalue. Therefore neither heat compactness nor its failure decides the original embedding question. These examples and the density criterion are reported auxiliary results, not a complete effective classification of arbitrary Dirichlet forms.
\subsection{Short English statement}
The question asks when a gradient bimodule literally embeds into a sum of coarse bimodules. The aid characterizes this by normality of the joint left-right representation, equivalently positive normal densities for gradient coefficients. It distinguishes that property from weak containment and heat compactness, with circle and free-group examples separating the notions.
