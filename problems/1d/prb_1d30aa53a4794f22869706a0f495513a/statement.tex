Let \(M\) be the torus with a Liouville metric intended in the source. In the usual two-dimensional convention, such a metric is expressed in suitable periodic coordinates as \((a(s)+b(t))(ds^2+dt^2)\), with positive conformal factor. The source gives no coordinate periods, regularity, nondegeneracy or exclusion of flat cases, and does not explicitly state the dimension.

To make a standard spectral normalization visible, suppose \(-\Delta\phi_j=\lambda_j^2\phi_j\), with \((\phi_j)\) an orthonormal eigenbasis and \(\lambda_j\geq0\). In this frequency convention define
\[
 N(\lambda)=\#\{j:\lambda_j\leq\lambda\},\qquad
 R(\lambda)=N(\lambda)-\frac{\operatorname{Area}(M)}{4\pi}\lambda^2,
\]
\[
 R_x(\lambda)=\sum_{\lambda_j\leq\lambda}|\phi_j(x)|^2-\frac{\lambda^2}{4\pi},
 \qquad
 N_{x,y}(\lambda)=\sum_{\lambda_j\leq\lambda}\phi_j(x)\overline{\phi_j(y)}.
\]
These formulas specify the customary surface Laplacian version only. The record names the remainders but does not define the operator, cutoff or whether \(\lambda\) is frequency or eigenvalue. An eigenvalue cutoff would change the main term and numerical exponent; no such change is silently made.

The printed question asks whether, for every \(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
 R(\lambda)=O(\lambda^{1/2+\varepsilon}),\qquad

\]
\[
 N_{x,y}(\lambda)=O(\lambda^{1/2+\varepsilon})
 \quad\text{for every distinct }x,y\in M,
 \qquad\lambda\to\infty.
\]
