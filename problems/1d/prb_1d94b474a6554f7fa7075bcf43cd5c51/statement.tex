\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $\mathbb D=\{z\in\C:|z|<1\}$ and $\mathbb T=\partial\mathbb D$. A finite asymptotic value at $\zeta\in\mathbb T$ is a limit in $\C$ of $f$ along a curve in $\mathbb D$ tending to $\zeta$. MacLane's class $A$ is the source's class of nonconstant analytic functions with asymptotic values at a dense set of boundary points; the finite-versus-infinite-value convention should be confirmed, since the theorem's first alternative specifically requires finite values.

For a nonconstant analytic $f$ and an integer $m\geq1$, put $\psi=f^mf'$. The source attributes to Sons the theorem that, when $f,\psi\in A$, at least one of the following holds:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $f$ has finite asymptotic values at a dense subset of $\mathbb T$;
\item for every $a\in\C$, there are infinitely many distinct $z\in\mathbb D$ such that $\psi(z)=a$.
\end{enumerate}
The first task asks for other meaningful hypotheses/conclusions in place of alternative (i) that yield a correct dichotomy; it specifies no candidate replacement or optimality criterion.

The second task asks whether $\psi$ can instead be a differential monomial
\[
 \psi=\prod_{j=0}^{k}(f^{(j)})^{m_j},\qquad
 k,m_0,\ldots,m_k\in\N\cup\{0\},\quad f^{(0)}=f.
\]
The natural contextual membership assumptions are again $f,\psi\in A$, but their scope should be confirmed. The all-zero exponent choice gives $\psi=1$, and other choices can also give constant output, so these cannot satisfy the nonconstant MacLane-class requirement. No unstated nontriviality restriction is silently inserted into the printed exponent range.

The source also reports the same dichotomy for
\[
 \psi=f^{(l)}+\sum_{\nu=0}^{i-1}a_\nu f^{(\nu)},\qquad l\in\N,
\]
with coefficients analytic in $|z|<1+s$ for some $s>0$. The printed upper index is $i-1$, but $i$ is undefined; a possible intended $l-1$ is not silently substituted. The extension-radius parameter is renamed $s$ to distinguish it from a radial variable. It asks both for replacements of alternative (i) in this linear-differential setting and whether the coefficients may instead be analytic only in $\mathbb D$ with
\[
 T(r,a_\nu)=o(T(r,f))\qquad(r\uparrow1),
\]
where for analytic $g$, $T(r,g)=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_0^{2\pi}\log^+|g(re^{it})|\,\dd t$. The exact coefficient range and inherited class assumptions require recovery of the source context.
