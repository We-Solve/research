\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.


The primal-dual central trajectory satisfies primal and dual feasibility with positive primal variables $x$ and dual slacks $s$, and $x_i s_i=\nu$. A dot denotes differentiation with respect to $\nu$, $\circ$ is componentwise multiplication, and $\lVert z\rVert_1=\sum_i|z_i|$.

A linear program optimizes $c^Tx$ over a polyhedron $\{x:Ax\leq b\}$. In the simplex method a pivot moves between adjacent basic feasible solutions; a pivot rule chooses the next such move.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For the primal-dual central trajectory $(x(\nu),s(\nu),y(\nu))$ of a linear program with constraint matrix $A$, define $\kappa(\nu)=\lVert\nu\dot x(\nu)\circ\dot s(\nu)\rVert_1^{1/2}$ and $$F(\nu_0,\nu_1)=\int_{\nu_1}^{\nu_0}\frac{\kappa(\nu)}{\nu}\,d\nu.$$ Is $F(\infty,0)$ bounded by a quantity depending only on $A$? More specifically, is $F(\infty,0)=O\!\left(n^{3.5}\log(\bar\chi_A^*+n)\right)$? \href{https://doi.org/10.4171/owr/2005/02}{[S2]}

\section{Research attempt: A two-variable integral and a conditional superlevel reduction}

\paragraph{A direct bound on curvature}
Use standard-form feasibility $Ax=b$, $A^Ty+s=c$, positivity, and $x_is_i=\nu$. Assume the central trajectory is differentiable on the interval in question. Differentiating gives $A\dot x=0$, $\dot s=-A^T\dot y$, and $s_i\dot x_i+x_i\dot s_i=1$. Set
\[
 u_i=\sqrt{s_i/x_i}\,\dot x_i,\qquad
 v_i=\sqrt{x_i/s_i}\,\dot s_i.
\]
Then $u+v=\nu^{-1/2}{\bf1}$ and $u\cdot v=\dot x\cdot\dot s=0$. Consequently $\lVert u\rVert_2^2+\lVert v\rVert_2^2=n/\nu$, and Cauchy's inequality followed by $2ab\leq a^2+b^2$ gives
\[
 \kappa(\nu)^2=\nu\sum_i|u_iv_i|
 \leq\nu\lVert u\rVert_2\lVert v\rVert_2\leq n/2.
\]
This is a finite-interval pointwise estimate; integrating a constant against $d\nu/\nu$ over all positive $\nu$ would diverge.

\paragraph{The entire improper integral for one fixed matrix}
Take $A=(1,1)$, right side $b>0$, and any two real costs. Subtracting a constant from both costs changes only the dual variable, and swapping coordinates does not change the integral. For unequal costs it therefore suffices to use costs $(0,c)$ with $c>0$. Write the slacks as $(s,s+c)$ and set $t=s/c>0$. Solving feasibility and complementarity gives
\[
 x_1=\frac{b(t+1)}{2t+1},\quad x_2=\frac{bt}{2t+1},\quad
 \nu=\frac{bc\,t(t+1)}{2t+1}.
\]
The derivative $d\nu/dt=bc(2t^2+2t+1)/(2t+1)^2$ is positive; $t$ parametrizes all $0<\nu<\infty$. Differentiating $x_i$ and $s_i$ and using $d/d\nu=(d\nu/dt)^{-1}d/dt$ yields
\[
 \kappa^2=\frac{2t(t+1)(2t+1)}{(2t^2+2t+1)^2},\qquad
 F(\infty,0)=\sqrt2\int_0^\infty\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t(t+1)(2t+1)}}.
\]
On $(0,1]$ the integrand is at most $\sqrt2/\sqrt t$; on $[1,\infty)$ it is at most $t^{-3/2}$. Hence
\[
 F(\infty,0)\leq2\sqrt2+2,
\]
independently of $b$ and of the nonzero cost difference. With equal costs the two primal coordinates are constantly $b/2$, so $\dot x=0$ and $F=0$. This proves a matrix-only bound for this one-row two-variable family, including the cost-degeneracy case, without claiming continuity of the total integral in that degeneracy.

\paragraph{Exactly what a uniform superlevel bound would imply}
For general $A$, put $H=\log(\bar\chi_A^*+n)$ and suppose, as an additional hypothesis, that one absolute constant $C$ gives
\[
 \mu\{\nu>0:\kappa(\nu)>t\}
 \leq Cn^3\bigl(H+\log^+(1/t)\bigr),\quad 0<t\leq\sqrt n,
 \qquad d\mu=d\nu/\nu.
 \tag{1}
\]
Here $\log^+(z)=\max(0,\log z)$, and the same $C$ must work for every threshold, dimension and instance covered by the theorem. Pointwise $\kappa(\nu)=\int_0^{\sqrt n}{\bf1}_{\{t<\kappa(\nu)\}}dt$. Integrating this nonnegative identity first on finite rectangles, then taking increasing limits, gives the layer-cake identity
\[
 F(\infty,0)=\int_0^{\sqrt n}\mu\{\kappa>t\}\,dt.
\]
Under (1) this is at most
\[
 Cn^3\left(\sqrt n H+\int_0^1\log(1/t)\,dt\right)
 =Cn^3(\sqrt n H+1).
\]
For $n\geq2$ this has the conjectured order. The elementary integral equals one by evaluating $t-t\log t$ at the endpoints; the limit $t\log t\to0$ follows, for example, by writing $t=e^{-u}$.

The report covers each superlevel set by at most $n(n-1)/2$ intervals of logarithmic length of the stated order $nH+n\log(1/t)$. If their hidden constant were uniform as in (1), summing their lengths and applying the displayed argument would finish this route. A theorem only for each fixed threshold, with an uncontrolled threshold-dependent constant, is insufficient: integrability of that dependence must be proved. We have not checked the underlying full proof or obtained a uniform replacement. The report's explicit logarithmic term makes this route worth checking, but is not a substitute for that missing audit. No assertion is made that its constant actually fails to be uniform.
