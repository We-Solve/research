\subsection{Testing direct transfer of an average intervention effect}
Starting departmental conversations outside the US is a local organizational question. As a limited check on importing evidence, suppose an intervention has a binary contextual variable $Z$ and conditional mean effects $\tau_1=1/5$, $\tau_0=-1/5$. Assume, solely for this model, that these conditional effects are identical in a source and target population. Let $P_S(Z=1)=4/5$ and $P_T(Z=1)=1/5$.
\begin{proposition}
The source average effect is $3/25>0$ and the target average effect is $-3/25<0$, despite equality of both conditional effects across populations.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For potential outcomes with integrable difference $D=Y(1)-Y(0)$, the law of total expectation gives $\mathbb E[D]=P(Z=1)\mathbb E[D\mid Z=1]+P(Z=0)\mathbb E[D\mid Z=0]$. Substituting the source weights gives $(4/5)(1/5)+(1/5)(-1/5)=3/25$. Substituting the target weights gives $(1/5)(1/5)+(4/5)(-1/5)=-3/25$. Such laws are realizable, for example by taking $D$ deterministically equal to the declared conditional effect in each stratum.
\end{proof}
The failed inference is that a favorable overall effect elsewhere necessarily has the same sign locally. The example does not assert that any specific conversation practice has either sign, that $Z$ is a country's identity, or that the assumed conditional invariance holds in practice. Even with that strong invariance, composition matters; without it further obstacles arise.
