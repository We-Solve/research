\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Equip the vertex set of a connected graph \(X\) with its combinatorial path metric \(d_X\), and write \(B_X(v,r)=\{w:d_X(v,w)\le r\}\), where \(r\in\Z_{\ge0}\). Under the finite-family convention, \(X\) is a \emph{Helly graph} if every finite pairwise-intersecting family of vertex balls has nonempty total intersection. It is \emph{coarse Helly} if there exists \(\xi\in\Z_{\ge0}\) such that, for every \(n\ge1\), vertices \(v_1,\ldots,v_n\), and radii \(r_1,\ldots,r_n\in\Z_{\ge0}\),
\[
\left(\forall i,j,\ B_X(v_i,r_i)\cap B_X(v_j,r_j)\ne\varnothing\right)
\Longrightarrow
\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}B_X(v_i,r_i+\xi)\ne\varnothing.
\]
The constant \(\xi\) is independent of the family. An action is called \emph{geometric} if it is proper and cocompact by graph automorphisms, regarded as isometries of the graph. A \emph{Helly group} is a group admitting a geometric action on some Helly graph. The supplied record does not specify the finite-family versus arbitrary-family convention for the Helly conditions, or further graph hypotheses; the definitions here are provisional conventions requiring source review.

\textbf{Question 1 (group interpretation).} Does there exist a group \(G\) and a connected coarse-Helly graph \(X\) such that \(G\) acts geometrically on \(X\), but \(G\) is not a Helly group? Equivalently, is
\[
\exists G\ \exists X\ \Bigl[
X\text{ is coarse Helly},\quad G\curvearrowright X\text{ geometrically},\quad
\nexists Y\text{ a Helly graph with }G\curvearrowright Y\text{ geometrically}
\Bigr]
\]
true? The background assessment supports this group interpretation. However, the wording ``a coarse Helly graph that is not Helly'' also admits the different interpretation that \(X\) itself is not Helly. That alternative asks only for a geometric action on a coarse-Helly, non-Helly graph and does not require \(G\) to fail to admit a geometric action on another Helly graph. The supplied record does not conclusively resolve this grammatical ambiguity.

For the second question, let \(S\) be a finite set and let \(M=(m_{st})_{s,t\in S}\) be symmetric, with \(m_{ss}=1\) and \(m_{st}\in\{2,3,\ldots\}\cup\{\infty\}\) for \(s\ne t\). The associated \emph{Artin group} is
\[
A_M=\left\langle S\ \middle|\ 
\underbrace{sts\cdots}_{m_{st}\text{ letters}}
=
\underbrace{tst\cdots}_{m_{st}\text{ letters}}
\text{ for }s\ne t\text{ with }m_{st}<\infty
\right\rangle.
\]
There is no defining relation for a pair with \(m_{st}=\infty\).

Here is the usual finite-approximation meaning of \emph{coarse median}. A finite median graph \(P\) is a finite connected graph such that, for each triple of vertices \(a,b,c\), the intersection
\[
I_P(a,b)\cap I_P(b,c)\cap I_P(c,a)
\]
consists of one vertex, denoted \(m_P(a,b,c)\), where \(I_P(a,b)=\{z:d_P(a,z)+d_P(z,b)=d_P(a,b)\}\). A metric space \((Z,d)\) is coarse median if there exist a map \(\mu:Z^3\to Z\), a constant \(K\ge0\), and a function \(H:\Z_{\ge0}\to[0,\infty)\) satisfying both of the following conditions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item For all \(x_i,y_i\in Z\),
\[
d\bigl(\mu(x_1,x_2,x_3),\mu(y_1,y_2,y_3)\bigr)
\le K\sum_{i=1}^{3}d(x_i,y_i)+H(0).
\]
\item For every integer \(p\ge1\) and every nonempty finite \(F\subseteq Z\) with \(|F|\le p\), there exist a finite median graph \(P\) and maps \(\pi:F\to V(P)\), \(\lambda:V(P)\to Z\) such that
\[
d(a,\lambda\pi(a))\le H(p)\qquad(a\in F)
\]
and
\[
d\bigl(\lambda(m_P(u,v,w)),\mu(\lambda(u),\lambda(v),\lambda(w))\bigr)
\le H(p)\qquad(u,v,w\in V(P)).
\]
\end{enumerate}
A finitely generated group is called coarse median if its word metric for a finite generating set is coarse median; this property is independent of the finite generating set. No uniform bound on the ranks of the finite median approximations is imposed here.
