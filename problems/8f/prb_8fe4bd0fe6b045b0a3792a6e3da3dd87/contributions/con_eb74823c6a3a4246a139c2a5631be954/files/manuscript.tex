\subsection{Deterministic forward queries require the full domain in the worst case}
Let an unknown real $m\times n$ matrix be accessed by exact forward queries $x\mapsto Ax$, where $m,n\ge1$. Algorithms may adapt queries to previous answers but are deterministic.
\begin{proposition}
After $k<n$ queries, no such algorithm can guarantee spectral-norm error strictly below one for every rank-one norm-one matrix. In particular, worst-case exact recovery of all matrices of rank at most one requires $n$ queries; $n$ suffice.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Run the algorithm against answers all equal to zero. Its resulting query vectors span a subspace $Q$ of dimension at most $k<n$. Choose unit vectors $v\in Q^\perp$ and $u\in\mathbb R^m$. The two matrices $A_+=uv^T$ and $A_-=-uv^T$ both have rank one, norm one, and give zero for every query on this history. By induction over queries, the deterministic algorithm follows this same history on both matrices, so its output $B$ is identical. Since $\|A_+-A_-\|_2=2$, the triangle inequality implies $\max(\|A_+-B\|_2,\|A_--B\|_2)\ge1$. Querying the $n$ standard basis vectors returns all columns and proves sufficiency.
\end{proof}
\begin{proposition}
If a known matrix $V\in\mathbb R^{n\times s}$ has orthonormal columns and $\operatorname{range}(A^T)\subseteq\operatorname{range}(V)$, then $s$ forward queries recover $A$ exactly as $(AV)V^T$. Without this containment the same formula has error exactly $\|A(I-VV^T)\|_2$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Under containment, every row of $A$ is in the column space of $V$, so $A=AVV^T$. Each column of $AV$ is one forward answer. In general subtracting $AVV^T$ from $A$ gives the claimed error formula. For $s<n$, the preceding $uv^T$ with $v\perp V$ shows that the observed answers cannot by themselves certify a small omitted tail.
\end{proof}
