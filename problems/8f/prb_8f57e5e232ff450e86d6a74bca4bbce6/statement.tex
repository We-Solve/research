\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 2307039}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a compact set $K\subset\C$, its analytic capacity is
\[
\gamma(K)=\sup\{|f'(\infty)|:
 f\text{ holomorphic on }\widehat\C\setminus K,
\ |f|\leq1,\ f(\infty)=0\},
\qquad f'(\infty)=\lim_{z\to\infty}zf(z).
\]
Equivalently, $f'(\infty)$ is the coefficient of $z^{-1}$ in the expansion at infinity.

The question asks whether there is a universal finite constant $M$ such that
\[
\gamma(K_1\cup K_2)\leq M\bigl(\gamma(K_1)+\gamma(K_2)\bigr)
\]
for every pair of compact subsets $K_1,K_2\subset\C$. The constant must be independent of the sets. This is semiadditivity; the question does not require the sharper constant $M=1$.

The source historically says even the case $\gamma(K_2)=0$ is open. The supplied research summary reports Tolsa's semiadditivity theorem for all compact sets (2003), giving an affirmative resolution of the stated universal-constant problem, including that special case. The historical open remark is retained as source context rather than asserted as current status. No value of the optimal constant or new proof is claimed.
\subsection{Short English statement}
The problem asks for one constant controlling analytic capacity of a union by the sum of the two capacities. The supplied record cites Tolsa's semiadditivity theorem as an affirmative solution; it does not ask for constant one.
