\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(n\) be a positive integer and write \(z=(w_1,\ldots,w_n)\in\C^n\). Let \(g,f\in\C[w_1,\ldots,w_n]\) be homogeneous polynomials of degrees \(2\) and \(3\), respectively, so that
\[
 g(tz)=t^2g(z),\qquad f(tz)=t^3f(z)\qquad(t\in\C).
\]
Define the affine algebraic hypersurface
\[
 X_{f,g}=\bigl\{(w,z)\in\C^{n+1}:w^3+w g(z)+f(z)=1\bigr\}.
\]
This is a smooth complex manifold of dimension \(n\): the polynomial \(H(w,z)=w^3+w g(z)+f(z)\) is homogeneous of degree \(3\), and Euler's identity gives
\[
 w\frac{\partial H}{\partial w}+\sum_{j=1}^n w_j\frac{\partial H}{\partial w_j}=3H=3
 \quad\text{on }X_{f,g},
\]
so its differential does not vanish there.

A holomorphic immersion into \(\C^n\) means a holomorphic map \(\Phi:X_{f,g}\to\C^n\) such that
\[
 \operatorname{rank}_{\C}(\dd\Phi_p)=n\qquad\text{for every }p\in X_{f,g}.
\]
Injectivity is not required.

\textbf{Question (R. Narasimhan; supplied Problem 61).} Does every hypersurface of the displayed form admit a holomorphic immersion into \(\C^n\)? Under the general reading of the question, the quantifiers are: for every positive integer \(n\) and every pair \(f,g\) satisfying the stated homogeneity assumptions, does such a map \(\Phi\) exist?
