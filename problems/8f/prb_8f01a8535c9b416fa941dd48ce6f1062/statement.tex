\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A group \(G\) is \emph{locally nilpotent} if every subgroup generated by a finite subset of \(G\) is nilpotent. Here a group \(K\) is nilpotent if its lower central series, defined by
\[
\gamma_1(K)=K,\qquad \gamma_{n+1}(K)=[\gamma_n(K),K],
\]
satisfies \(\gamma_{c+1}(K)=\{1\}\) for some integer \(c\geq 1\). For subgroups \(A,B\leq K\), the subgroup \([A,B]\) is generated by the commutators \(a^{-1}b^{-1}ab\), where \(a\in A\) and \(b\in B\). A group \(H\) is \emph{torsion-free} if \(h^n=1\), for \(h\in H\) and an integer \(n\geq 1\), implies \(h=1\).

The problem asks whether, for every locally nilpotent group \(G\), there exist a torsion-free locally nilpotent group \(H\) and a surjective group homomorphism
\[
\pi:H\twoheadrightarrow G.
\]
There is no restriction on the cardinality of \(G\) or \(H\).

The supplied editors' comment reports affirmative answers when \(G\) is periodic, meaning every element has finite order, and when \(G\) is countable. These are supplied historical claims, not independently verified here. The supplied registry labels the unrestricted problem open.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every locally nilpotent group occur as a quotient of a torsion-free locally nilpotent group? The source attributes this question to A. I. Maltsev and lists B. I. Plotkin as proposer. Its editors report affirmative answers for periodic groups and for countable groups.
