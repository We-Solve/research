\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A map $f:X\to Y$ is a quasi-isometric embedding if there are constants $L\geq1$, $A\geq0$ such that
\[
L^{-1}d_X(x,x')-A\leq d_Y(f(x),f(x'))\leq Ld_X(x,x')+A
\qquad(x,x'\in X).
\]
No coarse-surjectivity condition is imposed. The source asks whether all such embeddings between higher-rank symmetric spaces are ``either isometries or algebraic in this way''.

Rigidity conclusions also usually distinguish equality from finite distance: being at bounded distance from a model $F$ means $\sup_{x\in X}d_Y(f(x),F(x))<\infty$. Quasi-isometric embeddings are unchanged by bounded perturbations, so literal pointwise identification with an isometry or a rigid algebraic map is much stronger than coarse identification.
