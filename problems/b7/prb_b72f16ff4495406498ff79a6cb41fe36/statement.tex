\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a metric space $(X,d)$ with a Markov kernel $P$, let $P_x=P(x,\cdot)$ have finite first moment. Its coarse Ricci curvature is
\[
 \kappa(x,y)=1-\frac{W_1(P_x,P_y)}{d(x,y)}\qquad(x\ne y),
\]
where $W_1$ is the infimum of $\int d(u,v)\,d\pi(u,v)$ over couplings of the two measures. Fix $o\in X$ and set
\[
 \kappa_R=\inf\{\kappa(x,y):x,y\in B(o,R),\ x\ne y\}.
\]
The source hypotheses are
\[
 \inf_{x\ne y}\kappa(x,y)=0,\qquad \kappa_R>0\text{ for every finite }R,
\]
with an empty pair set treated separately. Thus positive lower bounds on bounded balls deteriorate toward the global zero infimum as radius grows.

\textbf{Question (Yann Ollivier).} Is there a systematic relation between the rate of this deterioration at infinity and the isoperimetric profile? Analyze the $M/M/k$ queue as an example. The source supplies no particular comparison formula or asserted decay law.

The isoperimetric profile needs additional measure and boundary conventions. For example, if $P$ has an invariant probability $\nu$, one Markov-boundary profile is
\[
 I(v)=\inf_{\nu(A)=v}\int_A P(x,X\setminus A)\,d\nu(x),
\]
for attainable masses $v$. Metric boundary measure, conductance profiles, and counting-measure profiles are different possible choices. This formula is explanatory, since invariance, reversibility, the reference measure, and the intended profile are absent from the excerpt.

An $M/M/k$ queue customarily has state $q\in\{0,1,2,\ldots\}$, arrival rate $\lambda>0$, $k\geq1$ servers, and service rate $\mu>0$ per busy server. Its generator has birth rate $\lambda$ and death rate $\mu\min(q,k)$, with no death from state $0$. If stationary probability is needed, the usual stability condition is $\lambda<k\mu$. To turn this example into coarse Ricci data one must also specify a metric and a transition kernel or semigroup time scale; these choices and parameters are not given by the source.
