\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 1100507}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For an integer \(n\ge2\), let \(\mathbb H^n\) be real hyperbolic space with ideal boundary \(\partial\mathbb H^n\cong S^{n-1}\). For a finitely generated discrete subgroup \(\Gamma\le\operatorname{SO}(n,1)\), its limit set \(\Lambda(\Gamma)\) is the set of accumulation points in \(\partial\mathbb H^n\) of an orbit \(\Gamma o\), \(o\in\mathbb H^n\). Kapovich asks separately whether there exist such \(n,\Gamma\) for which the boundary action \(\Gamma\curvearrowright\Lambda(\Gamma)\) is (i) not ergodic, or (ii) not recurrent.

Both properties require a measure class, absent from the literal question. For example, if \(\mu\) is the restriction of spherical Lebesgue measure to \(\Lambda(\Gamma)\), ergodicity means that every measurable set \(A\subseteq\Lambda(\Gamma)\) invariant modulo null sets satisfies \(\mu(A)=0\) or \(\mu(\Lambda(\Gamma)\setminus A)=0\). A usual recurrence convention for a nonsingular countable-group action requires that for every measurable \(A\) of positive measure, almost every \(\xi\in A\) has infinitely many distinct \(\gamma\in\Gamma\) with \(\gamma\xi\in A\). These give a precise conditional reading of the two existence questions; they do not resolve which measure and recurrence convention the source intends. When \(\mu(\Lambda(\Gamma))=0\), these restricted-measure conditions are vacuous, so a nontrivial example under this reading must have positive-measure limit set.

The supplied research summary reports negative answers to both existence questions in dimensions \(n\le3\): recurrence attributed to Ahlfors (1980), and ergodicity for positive-measure limit sets attributed to the Ahlfors measure conjecture through Canary (1993) and Agol (2004)/Calegari--Gabai (2004). It reports both questions open for \(n\ge4\) and related to the higher-dimensional Ahlfors measure conjecture. Its analogy with nonergodic recurrent \(\operatorname{PU}(2,1)\) actions having \(\Lambda=S^3\) is not an example in \(\operatorname{SO}(n,1)\). It attributes a conjecture of higher-dimensional nonergodic examples to the question's author. These are supplied triage claims, whose precise hypotheses and measure conventions need review.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can a finitely generated discrete real-hyperbolic group act nonergodically, or nonrecurrently, on its limit set? The measure and meaning of recurrence must be specified. The supplied triage distinguishes low-dimensional negative results from higher-dimensional open questions.
