\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
All groups below are finite. A formation \(\mathfrak F\) is a nonempty class of groups, closed under isomorphisms, homomorphic images, and finite subdirect products. Here a subdirect product of groups \(G_1,\ldots,G_n\) is a subgroup of \(\prod_{i=1}^nG_i\) whose coordinate projections are surjective. The formation is saturated if, for every finite group \(X\),
\[
X/\Phi(X)\in\mathfrak F\ \Longrightarrow\ X\in\mathfrak F,
\]
where \(\Phi(X)\) is the intersection of the maximal subgroups of \(X\), with \(\Phi(1)=1\). A nonempty isomorphism-closed class \(\mathfrak F\) is a Fitting class if it is closed under taking normal subgroups and if, whenever \(H,K\trianglelefteq X\) belong to \(\mathfrak F\), their product \(HK\) also belongs to \(\mathfrak F\).

Let \(f\) be an inner screen of a saturated formation \(\mathfrak F\), as in the source. An action of \(A\) on \(G\) is a homomorphism \(\rho:A\to\operatorname{Aut}(G)\), and it is faithful if \(\ker\rho=1\). The semidirect product \(G\rtimes_\rho A\) has multiplication
\[
(g,a)(h,b)=(g\rho(a)(h),ab).
\]
For purposes of recording the question, write \(\operatorname{Hyp}_f(G,A,\rho)\) for the source condition that this action is \(f\)-hypercentral. The supplied record gives no technical definition of either an inner screen or an \(f\)-hypercentral action; these terms remain unresolved source qualifications, and this predicate is not an independent definition of hypercentrality.

Under the interpretation that the acting group and its action are universally quantified, the proposed statement is

\begin{aligned}
\mathfrak F\text{ is a Fitting class}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
&\text{for every finite }G\in\mathfrak F,\text{ every finite }A,\\
&\text{and every injective }\rho:A\to\operatorname{Aut}(G),\\
&\operatorname{Hyp}_f(G,A,\rho)
