\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(M\) be a lattice, meaning a free abelian group of finite rank, and let \(M_{\R}=M\otimes_{\Z}\R\). A lattice polytope in \(M_{\R}\) is the convex hull of a finite nonempty subset of \(M\). A rational polytope is the convex hull of a finite nonempty subset of \(M\otimes_{\Z}\Q\). For a rational polytope \(P\), its denominator relative to \(M\) is
\[
\operatorname{den}_{M}(P)=\min\{d\in\Z_{>0}:dP\text{ is a lattice polytope relative to }M\}.
\]
A zonotope is a Minkowski sum of finitely many line segments:
\[
Z=[a_1,b_1]+\cdots+[a_r,b_r],\qquad [a,b]=\{(1-t)a+tb:0\leq t\leq1\}.
\]
It is a lattice zonotope when such a representation exists with all endpoints in \(M\).
