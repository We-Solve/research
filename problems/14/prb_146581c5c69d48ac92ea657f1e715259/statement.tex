\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a positive integer \(n\), write \([n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}\). For an integer \(d\geq 3\), a \(d\)-dimensional permutation of order \(n\) is a function
\[
A:[n]^{d+1}\longrightarrow\{0,1\}
\]
such that, for every \(j\in\{1,\ldots,d+1\}\) and every fixed choice of \(i_k\in[n]\) for \(k\ne j\),
\[
\sum_{t=1}^{n}A(i_1,\ldots,i_{j-1},t,i_{j+1},\ldots,i_{d+1})=1.
\]
Thus every axis-parallel line contains exactly one entry equal to \(1\). Let \(P_d(n)\) denote the number of these functions, counted as arrays on the specified indexed domain \([n]^{d+1}\).

The supplied clean statement asks whether, for every fixed integer \(d\geq3\),
\[
P_d(n)=\left((1+o(1))\frac{n}{e^d}\right)^{n^d}
\qquad(n\to\infty),
\]
where \(n\) tends to infinity through positive integers, \(e\) is the base of the natural logarithm, and the \(o(1)\) term may depend on the fixed dimension \(d\). Equivalently, is there, for each such \(d\), a real sequence \(\varepsilon_d(n)\to0\) such that
\[
P_d(n)=\exp\!\left(n^d\bigl(\log n-d+\varepsilon_d(n)\bigr)\right)?
\]

\subsection{Short English statement}
Count the zero--one arrays on \([n]^{d+1}\) having exactly one \(1\) on every axis-parallel line. For each fixed integer \(d\geq3\), does their number equal \((n/e^d)^{n^d}\exp(o(n^d))\) as \(n\to\infty\)? The original broader request about structural properties remains unspecified.
