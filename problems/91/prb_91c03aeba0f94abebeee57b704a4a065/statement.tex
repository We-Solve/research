\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
The source's Conjecture 6.25 asserts that a digraph $D$ on $n$ vertices satisfying
\[
 \sum_{v\in V(D)}\log\left(1+\frac1{d_D^+(v)}\right)
 \geq n\log\left(1+\frac1r\right)
\]
has a directed cycle of length at most $\lceil n/r\rceil$. The supplied verification confirms a ceiling on page 10 of Blair D. Sullivan's 2006 survey. The inequality direction is retained literally. The logarithms are undefined at outdegree zero, so a finite-valued formulation must require positive outdegrees; treating a zero-degree term as infinity would give an immediate defect for acyclic graphs. The aid's counterexamples have all outdegrees positive.

The source itself prints a counterexample: start with a transitive tournament on $n-1$ vertices, remove its arc from the maximum-outdegree vertex to the sink, and replace it by an oppositely directed path of length two. The aid verifies, for every $n\geq4$, the exact product
\[
 P(D):=\prod_v\left(1+\frac1{d_D^+(v)}\right)
       =\frac{4(n-2)^2}{n-3},
\]
and the complete directed cycle-length spectrum $\{4,5,\ldots,n\}$. It reports that this graph refutes the proposed implication exactly for positive integer parameters $r\geq\lceil n/3\rceil$.

More generally, let $T_{m,q}$ be obtained from a transitive tournament on ordered vertices $1,\ldots,m$, $m\geq3$, by removing $1\to m$ and inserting a reversed $q$-arc path from $m$ to $1$, with new internal vertices, $q\geq2$. Then $N=m+q-1$ and the aid's formulas are
\[
 P(T_{m,q})=\frac{2^q(m-1)^2}{m-2},\qquad
 \{\text{directed cycle lengths}\}=\{q+2,\ldots,N\}.
\]
Writing $P=2^q(m-1)^2/(m-2)$, the exact two-threshold counterexample criterion is
\[
 r\geq\max\left\{
 \left\lceil\frac N{q+1}\right\rceil,
 \left\lceil\frac1{P^{1/N}-1}\right\rceil
 \right\},\qquad r\in\N.
\]
The second threshold is precisely the displayed logarithmic hypothesis; the first makes its ceiling conclusion strictly shorter than the actual girth. Failure of one threshold alone is not a counterexample to the implication.
