\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $k$ be a positive derivative order and let $F(X,Y)\in\C[X,Y]$ be irreducible. If its total degree is $d\geq1$, its highest-degree homogeneous part is the sum of its monomials of total degree $d$. The source considers the remaining case where that part has only one distinct linear factor, and gives the normalized form
\[
 F(X,Y)=(X-aY)^d+Q_{d-1}(X,Y),\qquad
 a\in\C,\quad \deg Q_{d-1}\leq d-1,
\]
with irreducibility retained for the whole polynomial. All coefficients are constant; the degree bound is total degree.

An entire solution is a holomorphic function $y:\C\to\C$ satisfying identically
\[
 (y^{(k)}(z)-ay(z))^d+Q_{d-1}(y^{(k)}(z),y(z))=0.
\]
\textbf{Problems.} Classify all entire solutions for the stated remaining equations. Are all of them exponential polynomials? Under the common constant-coefficient differential-equation meaning, this last assertion asks whether every solution has a representation
\[
 y(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}p_j(z)e^{\lambda_jz},\qquad
 p_j\in\C[z],\quad\lambda_j\in\C,\quad m<\infty,
\]
with the zero solution allowed when it satisfies the equation. The excerpt does not define exponential polynomial; alternatives allowing polynomial exponents $e^{P_j(z)}$ give a different class and must not be silently identified with this convention.

The claimed equivalence of the linear-factor description and the displayed form also needs a qualification: a general linear factor is $\alpha X+\beta Y$, and the normalization $X-aY$ presupposes $\alpha\ne0$. The excerpt does not discuss a purely $Y$-factor. Its title says higher-order equations without an explicit lower bound on $k$, so the exact derivative-order range also requires confirmation.

\subsection{Short English statement}
Classify entire solutions of irreducible autonomous equations whose top homogeneous part is a power of $y^{(k)}-ay$, and determine whether every solution is an exponential polynomial. The exact derivative-order range, exponential-polynomial convention, and normalization of the unique linear factor need checking.
