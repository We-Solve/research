\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Does there exist a surface in \(\mathbb R^3\), complete for its induced metric, with strictly negative Gaussian curvature, negative Euler characteristic, and bounded height between two parallel planes?

Under the standard embedded-surface reading, seek a connected boundaryless \(C^2\) surface \(\Sigma\subset\mathbb R^3\) such that
\[
 K_\Sigma(p)<0\quad(p\in\Sigma),\qquad\chi(\Sigma)<0,
\]
and there are a unit vector \(u\in\mathbb R^3\) and finite real numbers \(a<b\) with
\[
 a\le\langle p,u\rangle\le b\qquad(p\in\Sigma).
\]
The slab has positive finite width \(b-a\) but is unbounded in directions parallel to its planes. Completeness is intrinsic and does not add properness of the inclusion. Negativity is pointwise: no bound \(K\le-c<0\), constant-curvature assumption, or finite-total-curvature condition is inserted.
