\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For an ordinary Euclidean billiard in a convex body $\Omega\subset\R^d$ with sufficiently regular boundary, a customary fixed-speed collision phase space consists of pairs $(q,v)$ with $q\in\partial\Omega$, $|v|=1$, and $v$ pointing inward. The billiard map follows the straight segment to the next collision and applies specular reflection,
\[
 v_{\mathrm{after}}=v_{\mathrm{before}}-2\langle v_{\mathrm{before}},n\rangle n,
\]
where $n$ is the unit normal at that collision. Away from singular/grazing collisions this phase space has dimension $2d-2$. An invariant hypersurface, under a smooth embedded interpretation, is a nonempty codimension-one submanifold $\Sigma$ in the regular phase space satisfying $B(\Sigma)=\Sigma$. The source does not specify the required regularity or nontriviality of such a surface.
