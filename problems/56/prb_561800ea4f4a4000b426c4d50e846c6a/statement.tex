\section{Covering systems with big moduli}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For integers $a$ and $q\ge1$, let $a+q\mathbb Z=\{a+qt:t\in\mathbb Z\}$. A covering system is a finite family of these residue classes whose union is $\mathbb Z$. It is distinct if its moduli are pairwise unequal.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every integer $N\ge1$, does there exist a finite covering system
\[
 \mathbb Z=\bigcup_{i=1}^k(a_i+q_i\mathbb Z)
\]
with pairwise distinct moduli $q_i\ge N$? \sref{statement}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can distinct covering systems have arbitrarily large minimum modulus? The historical question has a negative answer: Hough proves a uniform upper bound of $10^{16}$ for the least modulus. \sref{statement}{3}
