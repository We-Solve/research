\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Give explicit moduli descriptions and stable degenerations of surfaces of general type, especially octic double planes, quintic hypersurfaces, bidouble covers and other abelian covers, following the work named in the original. An octic double plane is a double cover of $\PP^2$ branched over a degree-eight curve; a quintic surface is a degree-five hypersurface in $\PP^3$; a bidouble cover has group $(\Z/2)^2$. Stable degenerations are understood in a specified KSBA family, with semi-log-canonical central surface and ample $\Q$-Cartier canonical divisor. The source does not fix invariants, cover data or a classification target beyond these examples.

The attached unreviewed aid studies two complex simple-normal-crossing limits. First, $X_0=V(\ell q_4)\subset\PP^3$ is a plane and a smooth quartic meeting transversely in a smooth plane quartic $C$. With $V_j=H^0(\PP^3,\mathcal O(j))$, it claims the fixed-ambient normal quotient
\[
V_5/(\ell V_4+q_4V_1)\simeq H^0(C,\mathcal O_C(5))\simeq H^0(C,T^1_{X_0}),\quad\dim=18.
\]
Second, the octic double plane $z^2=q_4^2$ is the union of two plane components along a smooth quartic $C$. For $V_j=H^0(\PP^2,\mathcal O(j))$ in this second formula, it claims
\[
V_8/(q_4V_4)\simeq H^0(C,\mathcal O_C(8))\simeq H^0(C,T^1_{X_0}),\quad\dim=30.

These are fixed-ambient deformation calculations; they are not a complete quotient-moduli description or a classification of stable limits of all bidouble and abelian covers.
