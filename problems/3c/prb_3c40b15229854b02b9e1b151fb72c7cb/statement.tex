\section{Symmetries and Action Principles for Biology}\label{82}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 82 · Mathematical Physics}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


\paragraph{Definitions and notation.}A symmetry is a transformation preserving a model’s governing equations or relevant observables. An action principle selects trajectories by stationarity of a functional, commonly an integral of a Lagrangian. Robustness concerns persistence under perturbations; modularity concerns interacting subsystems; evolvability concerns accessible heritable changes; variability concerns differences among states or individuals. These concepts require model-specific definitions before a theorem can be posed.

\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}
Extend understanding of symmetries and action principles in biology to include robustness, modularity, evolvability, and variability.
\paragraph{Scope and source qualification.}The problem is a modelling programme and does not assert that all biological systems already possess an action principle. The historical DARPA mirror was unavailable during this check.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Develop symmetry and variational principles that explain biological robustness, modularity, evolvability and variability.
