\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
N. Katz's source question fixes $k\geq3$ and $C\geq2$ and asks whether every finite integer set with $|A+A|<C|A|$ has at least $c(k,C)|A|^2$ arithmetic progressions of length $k$. Interpret a proper progression as $(x,x+d,\ldots,x+(k-1)d)$ with $d\neq0$; count increasing ones by taking $d>0$. The source does not state a size threshold or whether zero differences are allowed.

The literal all-finite-set assertion is false under this proper-progression convention. For every $k\geq3$, take $A=\{0,\ldots,k-2\}$. Then
\[
 |A|=k-1,\qquad |A+A|=2k-3<2|A|\leq C|A|,
\]
but there is no proper $k$-term progression.

The aid reports the following precise asymptotic repair. Let $L=S_k(1/(8C^4))$ be a Szemer\'edi threshold, chosen so every integer interval of length at least $L$ has a proper $k$-term progression in each subset of relative density at least $1/(8C^4)$. Then every $A\subset\Z$ with
\[
 |A|\geq2L,\qquad |A+A|\leq C|A|
\]
contains at least
\[
 \frac{|A|^2}{128C^4L^2}
\]
increasing $k$-term progressions. Thus the reported valid constants are $N_0=2L$ and $c=1/(128C^4L^2)$; the source's strict-doubling hypothesis is included in this nonstrict version.

The stated method models a subset of at least half of $A$ as a dense subset of a prime cyclic group using Ruzsa's Freiman modeling lemma, then applies Varnavides averaging rather than only an existence conclusion. A Freiman isomorphism of order two preserves two-sum equalities, including repetitions, in both directions. This suffices to pull back a cyclic progression, even if it wraps around: its consecutive identities
\[
 b_j+b_{j+2}=2b_{j+1}\qquad(0\leq j\leq k-3)
\]
become the same identities in the integers, forcing a single common difference. Injectivity retains nontriviality. No Freiman order $k$ assumption is needed for this transfer.
