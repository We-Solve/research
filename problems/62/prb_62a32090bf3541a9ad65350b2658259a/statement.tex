\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $H^\infty=H^\infty(\mathbb D)$ be the algebra of bounded holomorphic functions on the unit disk. For $f,g\in H^\infty$, the desired conclusion is
\[
\exists a,b\in H^\infty\quad
h(z)=a(z)f(z)+g(z)b(z)\ne0\quad\text{for every }z\in\mathbb D.
\]
Equivalently, the ideal generated by $f,g$ should contain a zero-free bounded holomorphic function. Zero-free does not require $1/h$ to be bounded; no uniform positive lower bound for $|h|$ is requested.

The first sentence literally asks this for every pair $f,g$. It omits any hypothesis excluding a common zero: at a common zero every such combination vanishes. Thus a nontrivial universal statement needs a qualification; the source's subsequent proposed harmonic-minorant condition may be intended to supply one.

For a real extended-valued function $\psi$ on $\mathbb D$, a harmonic minorant means a finite real harmonic function $u$ on $\mathbb D$ with $u(z)\le\psi(z)$ everywhere. The source claims as a necessary condition that
\[
\psi(z)=\log\bigl(|f(z)|-|g(z)|\bigr)
\]
have a harmonic minorant, and asks whether this condition is also sufficient. This expression is retained exactly as printed. It is not a real logarithm where $|f|<|g|$, and its definition at equality would require the convention $\log0=-\infty$. No assumption $|f|>|g|$ is stated, and the expression is asymmetric although the desired combination property is symmetric in $f,g$.

A possible intended correction is the symmetric quantity $\log(|f|+|g|)$, but no such correction is adopted without source confirmation. Under that candidate reading the precise question would ask whether existence of a harmonic minorant of $\log(|f|+|g|)$ forces the displayed zero-free ideal element for every $f,g\in H^\infty$. It would still concern a zero-free element rather than the stronger equation $af+gb=1$. The extract alone does not establish which condition is intended or validate its necessity.

\subsection{Short English statement}
When does the ideal generated by two bounded disk functions contain a bounded function with no zeros? The source's universal wording lacks a common-zero qualification, and its proposed logarithmic condition has a subtraction that may not define a real function. A symmetric sum is only a possible correction, and zero-free combinations must not be confused with a bounded inverse or a Bezout identity equal to one.
