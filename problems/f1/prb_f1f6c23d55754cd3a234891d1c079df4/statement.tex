\section{Are there infinitely many prime quadruplets}\label{9400158}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 9400158 · Number Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A prime quadruplet is a tuple $(p,p+2,p+6,p+8)$ whose four entries are prime.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Are there infinitely many primes $p$ for which all four entries of this tuple are prime? \sref{9400158}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Do infinitely many primes start the four-prime pattern with offsets zero, two, six and eight?
