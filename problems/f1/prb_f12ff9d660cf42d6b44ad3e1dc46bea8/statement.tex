\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
The supplied question is: \emph{Does every coarse median space admit a $\delta$-median metric?} Its precise interpretation is unresolved because the excerpt contains inconsistent interval notation and does not specify how the new metric must relate to the original metric.

For a standard working convention, a coarse median structure on a metric space $(X,d)$ is a map $\mu\colon X^3\to X$ with constants $K,C\geq 0$ and a function $H\colon\N_{\geq 1}\to[0,\infty)$ satisfying the following conditions. First,
\[
 d\bigl(\mu(x_1,x_2,x_3),\mu(y_1,y_2,y_3)\bigr)
 \leq K\sum_{i=1}^3 d(x_i,y_i)+C.
\]
Second, for every nonempty finite $A\subseteq X$ with $|A|\leq p$, there are a finite median algebra $(M,m_M)$ and maps $\pi\colon A\to M$ and $\lambda\colon M\to X$ such that
\[
 d(a,\lambda\pi(a))\leq H(p)\qquad(a\in A)
\]
and
\[
 d\bigl(\lambda m_M(u,v,w),\mu(\lambda u,\lambda v,\lambda w)\bigr)
 \leq H(p)\qquad(u,v,w\in M).
\]
Here a finite median algebra may be represented as a nonempty subset of $\{0,1\}^n$, for some finite $n$, closed under coordinatewise majority, with $m_M$ the restricted majority operation. This is an explicit working convention; the supplied excerpt does not itself define coarse median spaces or specify a convention.
