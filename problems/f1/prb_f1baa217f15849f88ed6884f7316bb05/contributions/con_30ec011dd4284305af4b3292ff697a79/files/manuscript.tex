\section{Completeness of Realignment and SIC-POVM Entanglement Tests}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


A density matrix is a positive semidefinite operator of trace one. Multipartite separable states are convex combinations of products of local density matrices. A realignment test reshapes matrix coefficients and bounds the resulting trace norm on separable states. A SIC-POVM is a symmetric informationally complete positive-operator-valued measurement; its test uses corresponding measurement correlations.

\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}
Without preliminary reshuffling of the subsystems, is the family of realignment and SIC-POVM entanglement tests complete? Which multipartite mixed states can these tests detect, and how do the two families compare in detection performance?

\paragraph{Scope and source qualification.}The primary report asks about the entire family of entanglement testers, with realignment and SIC-POVM tests as examples. It excludes preliminary reshuffling of matrix indices, a broader operation than permuting subsystems. It also asks about output-dimension and efficiency trade-offs. Completeness for a bipartite enlarged family does not settle this restricted multipartite question.

\paragraph{Exact realignment on pure states.} In product bases define \(R(\rho)_{ik,jl}=\rho_{ij,kl}\). For a product density operator \(A\otimes B\), this is an outer product of the coefficient vectors of \(A,B\), so \(\|R(A\otimes B)\|_1=\|A\|_2\|B\|_2\le1\). The triangle inequality extends the bound to convex combinations of products. Now take a Schmidt-form pure vector \(\psi=\sum_i\sqrt{\lambda_i}|i,i\rangle\), with \(\lambda_i\ge0\), \(\sum_i\lambda_i=1\). Its realignment is diagonal on the indexed coefficient pairs, with nonzero entries \(\sqrt{\lambda_i\lambda_k}\). Therefore
\[
 \|R(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|)\|_1
 =\left(\sum_i\sqrt{\lambda_i}\right)^2
 =1+2\sum_{i<j}\sqrt{\lambda_i\lambda_j}.
\]
It exceeds one exactly when at least two Schmidt weights are nonzero. Thus this standard tester detects every entangled bipartite pure state.
\paragraph{Gap and source scope.} The calculation does not prove completeness for mixed states or for the entire restricted multipartite tester family. It uses the realignment built into the test, not an additional preliminary reshuffling of matrix indices. A local SIC measurement is informationally complete, but discarding its full probability array into a particular norm statistic may lose entanglement information; no equivalence of the full reconstruction and that statistic is inferred. The first gap is a characterization of all allowed testers and their jointly undetected mixed states without forbidden reshuffling. Output-dimension, efficiency and relative SIC detection performance remain unanswered. The pure-state calculation is known auxiliary material, not a new completeness theorem.
