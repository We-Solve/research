\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A Poisson manifold \((M,\pi)\) has a bivector \(\pi\) defining a bracket \(\{f,g\}=\pi(\dd f,\dd g)\) satisfying the Jacobi identity. Its Hamiltonian vector fields are \(X_f=\pi^\sharp(\dd f)\). A point is singular for the Poisson structure when the rank of \(\pi^\sharp:T^*M\to TM\) is not locally constant there; this is distinct from a singularity of an integrable momentum map.

In the relevant integrable-system setting, a Liouville torus \(T\cong(\R/\Z)^r\) is a compact connected invariant torus supporting independent commuting Hamiltonian flows. \[
\pi=\sum_{i=1}^r\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_i}\wedge
\frac{\partial}{\partial I_i}.
