\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 11000253}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A closed hyperbolic \(4\)-manifold is a compact boundaryless manifold with its real hyperbolic metric. A symplectic structure is a smooth closed nondegenerate \(2\)-form. Call \(X\) virtually symplectic if some finite-sheeted covering manifold admits such a form; no compatibility with the hyperbolic metric is required.

\emph{Question 4.6.} Does there exist a closed hyperbolic \(4\)-manifold \(X\) that is not virtually symplectic? Equivalently, is there an \(X\) such that
\[
 \forall\ p:Y\to X\text{ finite-sheeted connected covering},\qquad
 Y\text{ admits no symplectic structure}?
\]
The identity covering is included. The source does not explicitly require orientability of \(X\) or compatibility with a chosen orientation; the finite-cover question is retained without adding those conditions. Symplectic is not replaced by the stronger K\"ahler requirement.

The source distinguishes this from constructing a nonsymplectic \(X\) itself, which it calls easy. Its example is the Davis manifold \(D\), citing [9]. It uses the parity rule from [14], Corollary 10.1.10: for a closed symplectic \(4\)-manifold \((M,\omega)\),
\[
 1-b_1(M)+b_2^+(M)\text{ is even},
\]
where \(b_i\) are real Betti numbers and \(b_2^+\) is the positive index of the intersection form in the relevant orientation. The source reports [35]
\[
 b_1(D)=24,\quad b_2(D)=72,\quad\sigma(D)=0,
 \quad b_2^+(D)=36,
\]
so the expression is \(13\), violating parity. These are supplied facts about \(D\), not a calculation for every finite cover. The source supplies no reason that the obstruction persists under covering, so this example is not asserted to answer the virtual question. The next announced weakening of Question 4.1 is not included.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is there a closed hyperbolic \(4\)-manifold with no symplectic finite cover? The source's Davis-manifold parity obstruction rules out a symplectic form on that manifold itself, but does not establish the required obstruction on every cover.
