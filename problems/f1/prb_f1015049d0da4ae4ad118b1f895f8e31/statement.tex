\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let
\[
\operatorname{SL}_2(\Q)=\{A\in M_2(\Q):\det A=1\},
\]
with matrix multiplication. A group \(G\) is simple if \(G\ne\{I_2\}\) and every normal subgroup \(N\trianglelefteq G\) satisfies \(N=\{I_2\}\) or \(N=G\). Simplicity is understood in the abstract group sense.

\textbf{Question.} Does there exist a subgroup \(G\leq\operatorname{SL}_2(\Q)\) such that \(G\) is infinite and simple? Equivalently, is the following statement true?
\[
\exists G\leq\operatorname{SL}_2(\Q)\quad
\bigl[|G|=\infty\ \land\ 
\forall N\trianglelefteq G,\ (N=\{I_2\}\ \lor\ N=G)\bigr].
\]

The malformed matrix-group notation in the supplied record is interpreted as \(\operatorname{SL}_2(\Q)\). The source describes this as a special case of a question Serge Cantat asked J.-P. Serre about ``non-algebraic'' simple subgroups of \(\operatorname{GL}_n(\C)\), and also as a special case of Kourovka Notebook Problem 15.57. Neither the meaning of ``non-algebraic'' nor the statement of Problem 15.57 is supplied; these contextual references impose no additional specified condition on the displayed question.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does the group of determinant-one \(2\times2\) rational matrices contain an infinite subgroup with no nontrivial proper normal subgroup? The supplied registry labels this question open; that label has not been independently verified.
