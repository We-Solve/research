 |\sec_{g_i}|\leq1,

Assume every unit-speed geodesic from every starting point has a conjugate point to that starting point before $L_i=\pi+1/i$. The aid gives unconditional comparison consequences. By Rauch comparison the first conjugate time satisfies
\[
 \pi\leq t_{\mathrm{conj}}(\gamma)<\pi+1/i.
\]
Minimizing geodesics cannot continue past that time, so completeness gives compactness and
\[
 \operatorname{diam}(M_i)<\pi+1/i,\qquad
 \operatorname{diam}(X)\leq\pi.
\]
Completeness and absence of boundary are needed for this global use of minimizing geodesics.

Its quantitative index-form statement uses any unit-speed geodesic segment of length $L>\pi$ with a conjugate point before $L$ and sectional curvature at most $1$. There is a normal field $V\in H_0^1([0,L];\gamma^\perp)$ normalized by $\int_0^L|V|^2dt=1$ with nonpositive index form. Set
\[
 E(V)=\int_0^L|D_tV|^2dt-\frac{\pi^2}{L^2},\qquad
 Q(V)=\int_0^L\bigl(|V|^2-
       \langle R(V,\dot\gamma)\dot\gamma,V\rangle\bigr)dt.
\]
The first Dirichlet-eigenvalue inequality and curvature bound give $E(V),Q(V)\geq0$, and the aid's estimate is
\[
 E(V)+Q(V)\leq1-\frac{\pi^2}{L^2}.

For $L=L_i$, this is $O(1/i)$. The field therefore has small first-mode error and small integrated curvature-one deficit; this is an averaged direction estimate, not a pointwise bound on every normal plane.

The stronger classification in the aid is conditional on convergence sufficient to pass Jacobi equations, for example $C^2$ convergence after diffeomorphic identifications to a smooth complete limit. Then every limiting geodesic has first conjugate time $\pi$, and equality in the curvature comparison supplies a parallel normal direction of curvature $1$, or positive spherical rank.
