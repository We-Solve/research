\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A Yamabe flow, in the usual unnormalized convention, is a family of Riemannian metrics satisfying
\[
\partial_t g(t)=-\operatorname{Scal}_{g(t)}\,g(t).
\]
The source does not explicitly specify this normalization. For a flow on a noncompact manifold \(M\), defined for \(0<t<T\), where \(T>0\), instantaneous completeness means that \((M,g(t))\) is complete for every \(t\in(0,T)\), regardless of whether the initial metric is complete.

To interpret the displayed bound, let \(m=\dim M\) and let \(g_H\) denote a hyperbolic reference metric. The conventional normalization is sectional curvature \(-1\), hence scalar curvature \(-m(m-1)\); this normalization and the completeness of \(g_H\) require confirmation from the source. A conformally hyperbolic flow has
\[
g(t)=u(\cdot,t)g_H,\qquad u:M\times(0,T)\longrightarrow(0,\infty).
\]
The tensor inequality in the source means
\[
g(t)_x(v,v)\geq m(m-1)t\,(g_H)_x(v,v)
\quad\text{for every }x\in M,\ v\in T_xM,\ 0<t<T.
\]
