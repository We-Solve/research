\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(p\) be a prime, let \(k\) be a perfect field of characteristic \(p>0\), and let \(X\) be an irreducible variety over \(k\). Here the usual convention is used that a variety is a reduced, separated scheme of finite type over its ground field; geometric irreducibility is not assumed. There is no restriction on \(\dim X\).

The arc space \(X_\infty\) is the \(k\)-scheme representing the functor that assigns to a \(k\)-algebra \(A\) the set
\[
\operatorname{Hom}_k\bigl(\operatorname{Spec} A[[t]],X\bigr).
\]
Evaluation at \(t=0\) defines the center morphism \(\pi_0:X_\infty\to X\). Write \(\operatorname{Sing}(X)\) for the locus where \(X\) is not regular, equipped with its reduced closed subscheme structure. Since \(k\) is perfect, this is also the locus where \(X\to\operatorname{Spec}k\) is not smooth. Define
\[
S_X:=X_\infty\times_X\operatorname{Sing}(X).
\]
For a field extension \(K/k\), an arc \(\gamma:\operatorname{Spec}K[[t]]\to X\) has center \(\gamma|_{\operatorname{Spec}K}\), and belongs to \(S_X\) precisely when that center lies in \(\operatorname{Sing}(X)\).

\textbf{Question.} For every such \(k\) and \(X\), does the underlying Zariski topological space of \(S_X\) have finitely many irreducible components?

The condition concerns the center of an arc; it does not require the whole arc to factor through \(\operatorname{Sing}(X)\). Components are those of the scheme \(S_X\), rather than components of a set of \(k\)-rational arcs or of an unspecified geometric base change. If \(X\) is smooth, then \(S_X=\varnothing\), which has zero irreducible components. \subsection{Short English statement}
For every irreducible variety over a perfect field of positive characteristic, ask whether the space of all formal arcs whose centers lie in the singular locus has finitely many irreducible components. The question has no dimension bound and allows arcs whose generic points lie outside the singular locus.
