\section{Game Theory at Scale}\label{72}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 72 · Computer Science}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Standard number systems are $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$. Specialized notation and index ranges follow the cited source and the definitions below.


In a differential game a state follows dynamics such as $\dot x=F(t,x,u_1,\ldots,u_m)$, with each player choosing controls or a strategy to optimize its own specified payoff. A strategy may depend on observed histories under the information convention. A Nash equilibrium is a strategy profile at which no single player can improve its payoff by changing only its own strategy. Partial differential equations arise in value-function and equilibrium descriptions under additional assumptions. Scalability concerns how computational resources depend on the state dimension, player count and required accuracy.
