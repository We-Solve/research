\section{Research attempt}
The source agenda does not specify dynamics, information, equilibrium, or scaling. This attempt examines a finite-player differential game for which a mean-field replacement appears tempting, derives the exact equilibrium without a PDE, and identifies the finite-player term that the replacement loses. This is a worked linear-quadratic model, not a novel general theory.

Fix $N\ge1$, $T>0$, $a\ge0$, and real initial values $\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_N$. Player $i$ chooses a deterministic open-loop control $u_i\in L^2([0,T])$. States obey $\dot x_i=u_i$, $x_i(0)=\xi_i$, and the player minimizes
\[
 J_i(u)=\frac12\int_0^T u_i(t)^2dt+
       \frac a2\left(x_i(T)-\frac1N\sum_jx_j(T)\right)^2.
\]
Other players' controls are held fixed when a deviation is evaluated. Put $\bar\xi=N^{-1}\sum_i\xi_i$ and $k_N=aT(1-1/N)$.

\begin{proposition}
There is a unique open-loop Nash equilibrium up to almost-everywhere equality of controls. It has constant controls
\[
 u_i^*(t)=\frac{z_i-\xi_i}{T},\qquad
 z_i=\bar\xi+\frac{\xi_i-\bar\xi}{1+k_N}.
\]
Computing all $z_i$ uses $O(N)$ real arithmetic operations. If $z_i^{\infty}=\bar\xi+(\xi_i-\bar\xi)/(1+aT)$, then
\[
 z_i-z_i^{\infty}=
 \frac{aT(\xi_i-\bar\xi)}{N(1+k_N)(1+aT)}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For a prescribed terminal value $z_i$, integration gives $\int_0^T u_i=z_i-\xi_i$. Expanding the square about the constant mean, or using Cauchy--Schwarz, shows
\[
 \int_0^T u_i^2\ge (z_i-\xi_i)^2/T,
\]
with equality precisely for the constant control, almost everywhere. Therefore every best response is constant and it suffices to consider terminal choices. With $\bar z=N^{-1}\sum_jz_j$, player $i$'s reduced objective is
\[
 \frac{(z_i-\xi_i)^2}{2T}+\frac a2(z_i-\bar z)^2.
\]
Its second derivative in its own variable is $1/T+a(1-1/N)^2>0$. Thus its unique best response is characterized by
\[
 (z_i-\xi_i)/T+a(1-1/N)(z_i-\bar z)=0.
\]
Sum these equations to get $\bar z=\bar\xi$. Substitution gives the displayed $z_i$, and strict convexity proves each is a global best response. Any equilibrium must obey the same equations and hence is this one. The computation requires one sum and one constant-length formula per player. Subtracting the two terminal formulas gives the exact error identity.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Failed mean-field substitution.}
Replacing a player's effect on the mean by zero deletes the factor $1-1/N$. That replacement does not give an exact finite-player Nash equilibrium. For $N=2$, $a=T=1$ and initial values $(1,-1)$, it predicts terminal values $(1/2,-1/2)$, whereas the exact equilibrium is $(2/3,-2/3)$. At the proposed first coordinate the best-response derivative is $-1/2+(1/2)(1/2)=-1/4$, so a small increase improves the objective. The mean-field simplification is asymptotic, and its error is uniformly $O(1/N)$ only when the initial deviations and $aT$ are controlled. No feedback or stochastic equilibrium is inferred from this deterministic open-loop calculation. At $N=1$ the interaction cost vanishes and the formula correctly gives zero control; at $a=0$ every control is likewise zero at equilibrium.
