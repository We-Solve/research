The Graham--Pollak recurrence $u_1=1$, $u_{n+1}=\lfloor\sqrt2(u_n+1/2)\rfloor$ yields the binary digits of $\sqrt2$ through $u_{2n+1}-2u_{2n-1}$. The question asks for analogous results for square roots and other algebraic numbers. We give a binary construction for every positive real number, recovering that recurrence, and then an arbitrary-base construction.
For $w>0$ and integer base $g\ge2$, let $m=\lfloor\log_g w\rfloor$ and $t=w/g^m\in[1,g)$. Its digits, starting with the leading nonzero digit, are
\[d_n=\lfloor tg^{n-1}\rfloor-g\lfloor tg^{n-2}\rfloor\quad(n\ge1).\tag{1}\]
The radix point is restored using $m$. The floor convention chooses terminating expansions when available. The first digit of $\sqrt2$ here is its leading $1$, not the first digit after the point.
