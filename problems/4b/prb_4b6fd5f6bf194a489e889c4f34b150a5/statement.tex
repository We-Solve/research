\section{Explicit Gaussian MLE for monotone block missingness}\label{20001234}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 20001234 · Computer Science}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.


For a statistical model with parameter $\theta$ and observations $D$, the likelihood is $L(\theta;D)=\Pr_\theta(D)$, or the corresponding density. Maximum likelihood seeks a global maximum over the admissible parameter space. Each observation from a Gaussian population may reveal a different subset of coordinates. Its contribution to the observed likelihood is the Gaussian marginal on that subset, with the same underlying mean vector and covariance matrix. A block structure groups coordinates with related missingness patterns.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Problem 57. Gaussian missing data problem. Consider a continuous Gaussian population and a random sample of size n but only some of the characteristics of each individual are observed. Estimate the mean and covariance matrix. Fienberg: First approach the problem using a block structure. Steele: Look at a sequence of regressions as a sequence of polynomials. Can you solve the structure explicitly? \sref{20001234}{2}
