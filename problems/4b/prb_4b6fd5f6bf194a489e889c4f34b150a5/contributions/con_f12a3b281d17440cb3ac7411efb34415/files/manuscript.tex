\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

For a statistical model with parameter $\theta$ and observations $D$, the likelihood is $L(\theta;D)=\Pr_\theta(D)$, or the corresponding density. Maximum likelihood seeks a global maximum over the admissible parameter space. Each observation from a Gaussian population may reveal a different subset of coordinates. Its contribution to the observed likelihood is the Gaussian marginal on that subset, with the same underlying mean vector and covariance matrix. A block structure groups coordinates with related missingness patterns.

\section{Research attempt}
\subsection{Literature and scope}
Problem 57 asks for mean and covariance estimation under arbitrary Gaussian missingness. I tested whether collecting overlapping pairs necessarily determines the full covariance. Even with the mean and variances known, pair patterns $12$ and $23$ leave a continuum of observationally identical covariance completions.
\begin{proposition}
Fix $|a|,|b|<1$. For centered three-dimensional Gaussian distributions with unit variances and covariance
\[
\Sigma(c)=\begin{pmatrix}1&a&c\\a&1&b\\c&b&1\end{pmatrix},
\]
the positive-definite completions are precisely
\[
|c-ab|<\sqrt{(1-a^2)(1-b^2)}.
\]
Every such completion induces the same law for any fixed independent collection of observations of pairs $12$ and $23$ only.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For a quadratic form in coordinates $(u,v,w)$, complete the square in $v$:
\[
(u,v,w)\Sigma(c)(u,v,w)^{\mathsf T}
=(v+au+bw)^2+(1-a^2)u^2+2(c-ab)uw+(1-b^2)w^2.
\]
The remaining two-dimensional form is positive definite exactly when its positive first diagonal coefficient and its determinant are positive; completing its square once more proves this criterion. Its determinant is $(1-a^2)(1-b^2)-(c-ab)^2$, giving the interval. The pair covariance matrices $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&a\\a&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ and $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&b\\b&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ do not involve $c$. Gaussian marginals depend only on these mean and covariance entries, so each observed-pair density, and their independent product, is identical for all allowed $c$.
\end{proof}
\subsection{Verification and failed route}
At $a=b=0$ every $c\in(-1,1)$ is a positive-definite completion with identical observed pair laws. At $a=1/2,b=1/3$ the interval is centered at $1/6$ with squared radius $2/3$. Exact rational arithmetic verifies these endpoints' formula. Thus even exact population pair distributions cannot identify the missing covariance; more samples of the same patterns cannot repair this.

If the identifiable observed parameters have a fitted optimum inside this family, the likelihood is constant across the whole compatible interval. This obstruction does not preclude set-valued estimates, regularization or additional structural assumptions. It neither estimates unknown means in general nor solves all missingness patterns.
