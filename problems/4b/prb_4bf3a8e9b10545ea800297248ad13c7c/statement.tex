\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
This record merges eight topics from the Calibrations workshop list; its research aid treats only a restricted part of topic \(1\). A calibration is a closed differential form of comass at most one; an oriented submanifold is calibrated when its restriction is its volume form. In a normalized Calabi--Yau threefold \((M,\omega,\Omega)\), a special Lagrangian (SLAG) threefold of phase \(\theta\) satisfies
\[
 \omega|_L=0,\qquad\operatorname{Im}(e^{-i\theta}\Omega)|_L=0,\qquad
 \operatorname{Re}(e^{-i\theta}\Omega)|_L=\dd V_L.
\]
For a torsion-free \(G_2\) structure \(\varphi\), associative threefolds and coassociative fourfolds are calibrated by \(\varphi\) and \(*\varphi\), respectively. For a merely closed \(G_2\) structure, \(*\varphi\) need not be closed, a distinction relevant to the source's proposed extensions.

\begin{enumerate}
\item For elliptically or K3-fibred Calabi--Yau threefolds, characterize their SLAGs, decide when they are fibred, develop a theory of the fibred ones, and seek a derived-category construction from Lagrangians respecting the elliptic structure. In particular, for \(M\to\mathbb{CP}^1\) with K3 fibers, can a SLAG \(L^3\) over a base curve \(C\) be assembled from one-parameter families of real \(T^2\)'s in the fibers? For families \(M_t\to B\) collapsing adiabatically to \(B\), with fibred SLAGs \(L_t\), determine the limiting base curve and use the limit to study the SLAGs. The sentence ``What is the curve B in the limit'' appears to confuse the curve \(C\) with the base \(B\); the collapse topology and singular fibers are unspecified.
\item For a K3 surface, exploit hyperk\"ahler rotation, under which a special Lagrangian surface becomes holomorphic for another complex structure, to address Yau--Zaslow counting. The source loosely calls this the number of holomorphic curves in a homology class; genus, rational-curve conditions, multiplicities and an enumerative invariant must be specified before this is a finite counting question.
\item Seek B\"acklund-type transformations or PDE relations between calibrated geometries; investigate integrable systems for SLAGs in \(\C^3\), including index problems; and express calibrated conditions effectively through exterior differential systems or sharp inequalities. The source mentions pseudoholomorphic curves in \(S^6\), great-circle-fibred ``SLAGs,'' and coassociative cones in \(\R^7\), but leaves the relevant dimensions and geometric structures informal. Its extracted heading ``13'' is retained as a numbering defect, not a thirteenth topic.
\item Develop a Floer-type theory in \(G_2\) geometry by counting associative necks with boundary on two nearby coassociatives \(N,N'\). Study intersection circles, small-volume necks when \(N'\) is modelled by a small closed self-dual two-form on \(N\), and a proposed relation to Seiberg--Witten invariants. The source invokes Taubes's ``GW = SW'' in the disjoint, nowhere-zero-form case, suggests closed but not necessarily coclosed \(G_2\) structures for transversality, and asks about an M-theory analogue. These are proposed relationships, with boundary, compactness, orientation and counting data absent, rather than an established general neck-count theorem.
\item Classify entire associative graphs \(h:\R^3\to\R^4\), possibly under polynomial volume growth. The source notes nonlinear examples from a holomorphic graph times a line and from phase-compatible SLAG graphs in \(\R^6\subset\operatorname{Im}\mathbb O\), including potentials harmonic in two variables. Ask whether linear-growth associative graphs are affine, distinguishing growth of \(h\), bounded derivative and volume growth. The source asserts a positive result with a gradient bound but supplies no precise bound or theorem.
\item Study genericity and regularity of calibrated currents under varying calibrations: compare finite-dimensional current families with infinite-dimensional calibration families, ask whether generic cycles lie in families, and specify what improved structure means. Suggested settings are Joyce's almost Calabi--Yau structures and closed, possibly noncoclosed, \(G_2\) structures. Also ask about tangent-cone uniqueness. The source attributes an epiperimetric inequality in complex dimension at most two to Rivi\`ere, mentions Tian--Rivi\`ere, and asserts uniqueness in the complex case, without exact categories or hypotheses.
\item For SLAG cones, especially torus links, investigate analyticity or the source's ``semi-analyticity,'' classify the corresponding \(T^2\) cones in \(\C^3\) using integrable systems and maps from generalized Jacobian varieties, and seek effective two-sided index/area bounds. The source says some torus cones are not analytic, informally explains semi-analytic as ``half'' of a reflection-invariant analytic cone, and asks whether an associative cone over an almost-complex curve in \(S^6\) is a component of an algebraic variety. Its semi-analytic terminology and behavior at the vertex need definition. Finally, ask whether there exist higher-genus special Legendrian surfaces in the unit \(S^5\) with first positive Laplace eigenvalue \(\lambda_1=2\); the index convention in the other subquestion is unspecified.
\item Seek stronger regularity for calibrated cycles satisfying transversality conditions, for example with tangent planes restricted to a geometrically specified open subset of the calibrated Grassmannian. The open subset and intended regularity improvement are not given.
\end{enumerate}

The aid's exact reduction for topic \(1\) is conditional on a semi-flat horizontal torus ansatz over the smooth K3-fibration locus. Let \(T_b^2\) vary smoothly in a Gauss--Manin-flat fiber class, let \(V(b)\) be its area, and let \(h\) be the projectable horizontal base metric. Assume the horizontal torus bundles are Lagrangian and that the volume-form phase is pointwise constant on each torus, with the calibrated normalization required below. Fiber integration gives a closed complex one-form
\[
 Z_b(v)=\int_{T_b^2}\iota_{\widetilde v}\Omega,\qquad
 \dd Z=0,\qquad |Z_b(v)|=V(b)|v|_h.
\]
These ansatz hypotheses, especially pointwise phase control and projectability, are not automatic for every K3 fibration. For the torus bundle \(L_c\) over a regular oriented curve \(c\),
\[
 \operatorname{Vol}(L_c)=\int_c V\,\dd s_h
       =\operatorname{Length}_{V^2h}(c).
\]
The full SLAG phase condition within this ansatz is
\[
 e^{-i\theta}Z(\dot c)>0\text{ real}.
\]
In a local period coordinate \(w\) with \(\dd w=Z\), this means that \(w(c)\) is a straight oriented trajectory. If \(T\) is the \(h\)-unit tangent, its weighted-geodesic equation is
\[
 \nabla^h_TT=(\nabla^h\log V)^\perp.
\]
For a closed fibred SLAG, the torus homology class must also be invariant under Gauss--Manin monodromy around the base curve, and its normalized period phase must be constant. These are required conditions, not a proof of global gluing across singular fibers or an adiabatic convergence theorem. The reduction does not settle topics \(2\)--\(8\), construct the requested category, or show that arbitrary SLAGs respect a given fibration.
