\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(D=(V,E)\) be a finite directed graph, with vertex set \(V\) and arc relation \(E\subseteq V\times V\). For a nonnegative integer \(s\), call \(D\) \(s\)-dominating if
\[
\forall U\subseteq V,\quad |U|\le s\ \Longrightarrow\ \exists v\in V\ \forall u\in U,\ (v,u)\in E.
\]
The witnessing vertex \(v\) is not explicitly required to lie outside \(U\). The quantifier includes \(U=\varnothing\), so this definition requires \(V\ne\varnothing\).

A directed cycle of length \(k\) consists of pairwise distinct vertices \(x_1,\ldots,x_k\) such that \((x_i,x_{i+1})\in E\) for \(1\le i\le k\), where \(x_{k+1}=x_1\).

The question is whether every finite \(100\)-dominating directed graph contains a directed cycle of length at most \(100\). With loops counted as directed cycles of length \(1\), this asks whether
\[
\begin{gathered}
\left[\forall U\subseteq V,\ |U|\le100\ \Longrightarrow\ \exists v\in V\ \forall u\in U,\ (v,u)\in E\right]\\
\Longrightarrow\quad
\exists k\in\{1,\ldots,100\}\ \exists x_1,\ldots,x_k\in V:\quad
\left\{
\begin{aligned}
&x_i\ne x_j &&(1\le i<j\le k),\\
&(x_i,x_{i+1})\in E &&(1\le i\le k),\qquad x_{k+1}=x_1.
\end{aligned}
\right.
\end{gathered}
\]
The supplied statement does not explicitly specify whether loops are permitted or counted as cycles. Under the loopless convention, impose \((v,v)\notin E\) for every \(v\in V\) and use \(2\le k\le100\). These conventions are stated explicitly for review; the intended source convention has not been independently established.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Suppose every set of at most \(100\) vertices in a finite directed graph has a vertex sending an arc to every member of that set. Must the graph contain a directed cycle of length at most \(100\)? The supplied record labels this problem open; loop and cycle conventions require confirmation.
