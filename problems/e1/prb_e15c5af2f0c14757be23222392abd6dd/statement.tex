\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(n\ge 2\) and let \(P\subset\R^2\) be a set of \(n\) points. Write
\[
\operatorname{sep}(P)=\min_{p\ne q\in P}\|p-q\|_2,
\qquad
\operatorname{diam}(P)=\max_{p,q\in P}\|p-q\|_2,
\]
and let \(\mathcal L(P)\) be the set of distinct affine lines through pairs of distinct points of \(P\). For an unoriented line \(\ell\), let \(\theta_\ell\in[0,\pi)\) represent its direction, with angles measured in radians. Define the angular distance between two line directions by
\[
\delta(\ell,m)=\min\{|\theta_\ell-\theta_m|,\pi-|\theta_\ell-\theta_m|\}.
\]
For \(r>0\), define the closed Euclidean neighborhood
\[
N_r(\ell)=\{x\in\R^2:\operatorname{dist}(x,\ell)\le r\}.
\]
Under this neighborhood convention, call two distinct lines \(\ell,m\) essentially different at size \(n\) if
\[
\delta(\ell,m)\ge \frac1n
\quad\text{or}\quad
N_{1/\sqrt n}(\ell)\cap N_{1/\sqrt n}(m)\cap\operatorname{conv}(P)=\varnothing.
\]
Here \(\operatorname{conv}(P)\) is the convex hull of \(P\).

The usual uniform interpretation of the supplied asymptotic question is the following. For every fixed \(C>0\), do there exist constants \(c(C)>0\) and \(n_0(C)\ge2\) such that, for every integer \(n\ge n_0(C)\) and every such \(P\) satisfying
\[
\operatorname{sep}(P)=1,
\qquad
\operatorname{diam}(P)\le C\sqrt n,
\]
there is a family \(\mathcal F\subseteq\mathcal L(P)\) with
\[
|\mathcal F|\ge c(C)n^2
\]
whose distinct members are pairwise essentially different?

\subsection{Short English statement}
Must every planar set of \(n\) points with minimum pairwise distance exactly \(1\) and diameter at most a fixed multiple of \(\sqrt n\) determine a quadratic-size family of lines such that each pair either differs in direction by at least \(1/n\), or has disjoint \(1/\sqrt n\)-neighborhoods within the convex hull? The neighborhood boundary convention and the intended uniformity of the asymptotic constants require review.
