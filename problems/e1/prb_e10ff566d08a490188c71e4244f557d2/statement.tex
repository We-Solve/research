\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(M\) be a closed hyperbolic \(3\)-manifold and \(\Gamma=\pi_1(M)\). Agol's Problem 4.1 asks whether
\[
 \forall M\ \exists H\le\Gamma\ \exists W:\quad
 [\Gamma:H]<\infty,\quad
 W\text{ a word-hyperbolic reflection group},\quad
 H\hookrightarrow W.
\]
Here an embedding is an injective group homomorphism. Word hyperbolicity means that a Cayley graph for a finite generating set has uniformly thin geodesic triangles. No quasiconvexity requirement on the embedding is stated.

If ``reflection group'' means an abstract Coxeter group, its presentation has generators \(s\in S\), relations \(s^2=1\) and \((st)^{m_{st}}=1\) for the specified finite exponents, with absent relations represented by \(m_{st}=\infty\). A right-angled Coxeter group has only \(m_{st}=2\) or \(\infty\) for distinct generators. The source does not say whether the desired group must instead arise as geometric reflections in a particular space, nor require right angles, finite covolume, or a specified dimension. Those interpretations must not be interchanged.

The supplied summary discusses virtual specialness and embeddings in right-angled Coxeter groups that need not be word hyperbolic. Such a target does not meet the extra word-hyperbolic requirement. It also phrases an embedding for \(\Gamma\) itself, whereas the problem only asks for a finite-index subgroup; the scope of the cited implication must be verified.

The summary's claimed criterion that a right-angled Coxeter group is word hyperbolic when its nerve is flag with no triangles is not adopted: a triangle gives a finite commuting-reflection subgroup and is not itself an obstruction, while a triangle-free square gives commuting infinite-dihedral factors and obstructs hyperbolicity. Thus that combinatorial sentence cannot be used as the needed target criterion. The main virtual-embedding question remains the supplied open/unverified problem, not a consequence of that sentence.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every closed hyperbolic three-manifold group have some finite-index subgroup embedding in a reflection group that is itself word hyperbolic? The reflection-group category is unspecified. The supplied virtual-specialness discussion does not by itself provide such a target, and its triangle-based Coxeter criterion needs correction.
