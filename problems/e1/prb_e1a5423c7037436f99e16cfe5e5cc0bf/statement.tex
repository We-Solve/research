\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
In the standard four-dimensional setting, a semitoric system is an integrable map $F=(J,H):(M^4,\omega)\to\R^2$ with $\{J,H\}=0$, where $J$ is a proper momentum map for an effective Hamiltonian circle action and all singularities are nondegenerate without hyperbolic components. A focus-focus point has the local Williamson quadratic pair, in suitable Darboux variables,
\[
 q_1=x\xi+y\eta,\qquad q_2=x\eta-y\xi.
\]
Let $c=F(m)$ be a focus-focus critical value. The source does not state whether the semitoric system is simple, in particular whether a singular fiber has only one focus-focus point.

For the usual single-focus setting, the Taylor series invariant is the formal Taylor expansion
\[
 \widehat S(X,Y)=\sum_{a,b\geq0}s_{ab}X^aY^b
\]
of a smooth regularized action germ, obtained by removing the universal logarithmic focus-focus singularity. Its precise representative requires the standard base-coordinate, cycle, orientation and additive/linear normalization choices. These conventions are not supplied in the record.

A quantum semitoric realization uses commuting semiclassical operators with principal symbols $J,H$. Its joint spectrum is a family $\operatorname{JointSpec}(\widehat J_\hbar,\widehat H_\hbar)\subset\R^2$ as $\hbar\to0$. S. Vũ Ngọc's task is to compute the Taylor series invariant directly from this spectrum ``at'' the focus-focus value. Thus the target is a reconstruction of the full formal invariant from spectral information localized at $c$, rather than merely identifying the singularity type.
