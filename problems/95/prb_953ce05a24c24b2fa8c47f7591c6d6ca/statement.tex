\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $\mathbb D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}$, $\mathbb T=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|=1\}$, and $H^1$ be one-dimensional Hausdorff measure, whose restriction to $\mathbb T$ is arc-length measure up to normalization. Let $g:\mathbb T\to[-\infty,\infty]$ be Borel measurable. A holomorphic function has bounded Taylor coefficients when
\[
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_nz^n\quad(z\in\mathbb D),\qquad
\sup_{n\geq0}|a_n|<\infty.
\]
The bound may depend on $g$ and $f$; no bound uniform over all prescribed functions is requested.

\textbf{Question (M. Ortel and W. J. Schneider).} For every such $g$, does there exist a holomorphic $f$ with bounded Taylor coefficients and a set $E\subset\mathbb T$ with $H^1(E)=0$ such that
\[
\lim_{r\to1^-}f(r\zeta)=g(\zeta)\qquad(\zeta\in\mathbb T\setminus E)?
\]
For finite $g(\zeta)$ this is the ordinary complex limit, so in particular the imaginary part tends to zero. The source permits signed infinite values but does not specify how a complex-valued radial limit is to distinguish $+\infty$ from $-\infty$. Spherical convergence identifies those two values; prescribing limits only of the real part is another, weaker condition. Neither is silently chosen in place of the displayed full-function limit. The finite-valued case is unambiguous, while the full extended-real version requires this convention.

The supplied research summary reports the question open as of the 2018 edition, with no definitive later resolution located.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can every Borel real boundary function be realized almost everywhere as radial limits of a holomorphic disk function with bounded Taylor coefficients? Signed infinite boundary values need a precise limit convention.
