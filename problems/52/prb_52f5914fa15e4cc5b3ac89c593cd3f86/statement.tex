Let $P(m)$ denote the largest prime factor of $m$. We prove
\[
 \frac{P(2^n-1)}n\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
The deep input is Stewart's Theorem 1.2, \emph{On divisors of Lucas and Lehmer numbers}, Acta Mathematica 211 (2013), \url{https://doi.org/10.1007/s11511-013-0105-y}. In the specialization needed here, there is $p_0$ such that for every prime $p>p_0$ and integer $n\ge2$,
\[
 v_p(2^n-1)<p\exp\!\left(-\frac{\log p}{52\log\log p}\right)\log2+v_p(n). \tag{1}
\]
We assume this precise $p$-adic logarithm estimate, rather than the paper's final largest-prime-factor theorem. We reconstruct the remaining argument with a slightly weaker numerical constant, which suffices for the question.
