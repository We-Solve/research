\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(A\) be a finite nonempty alphabet and let \(\sigma:A\to A^+\) be a substitution, extended to finite words by concatenation. Its incidence matrix is \(M_\sigma=(m_{ab})_{a,b\in A}\), where \(m_{ab}\) counts occurrences of \(a\) in \(\sigma(b)\). The substitution is primitive if some positive power of \(M_\sigma\) has every entry positive. Using the usual substitution-language convention, its two-sided hull is
\[
\Omega=\{\omega\in A^{\Z}:\text{every finite contiguous block of }\omega\text{ occurs in some }\sigma^k(a),\ a\in A,\ k\geq0\}.
\]
Let \(\ell^2(\Z)=\{\psi:\Z\to\C:\sum_{n\in\Z}|\psi(n)|^2<\infty\}\). For an arbitrary injective map \(f:A\to\R\) and each \(\omega\in\Omega\), define the bounded self-adjoint operator \(H_{\omega,f}\) on all of \(\ell^2(\Z)\) by
\[
(H_{\omega,f}\psi)(n)=\psi(n+1)+\psi(n-1)+f(\omega_n)\psi(n),\qquad n\in\Z.
\]
These are operators on the whole integer line; no finite-endpoint boundary conditions are imposed. Define
\[
\Omega_c(f)=\{\omega\in\Omega:H_{\omega,f}\text{ has no eigenvalues}\}.
\]
Here absence of eigenvalues means
\[
\forall E\in\R\quad\forall\psi\in\ell^2(\Z),\qquad H_{\omega,f}\psi=E\psi\ \Longrightarrow\ \psi=0.
\]
A Pisot number is a real algebraic integer greater than \(1\) whose other algebraic conjugates all have modulus less than \(1\). However, the supplied record does not define the phrase ``Pisot substitution.'' Requiring the Perron--Frobenius eigenvalue of \(M_\sigma\) to be a Pisot number does not specify the same condition as requiring every other eigenvalue of \(M_\sigma\) to have modulus less than \(1\); additional irreducibility conventions may also matter. No convention is selected here.
