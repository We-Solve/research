# A regular S_n realization from the Morse polynomial X^n−X−T

**Problem:** prb_52d5c122f8fb4781b537d47b2a457adb

**Claim type:** Partial result

**Exact scope:** For every n≥2, the splitting field of X^n−X−T over Q(T) has arithmetic and geometric Galois group S_n and constant field Q; this gives every symmetric group as a regular realization, extending the earlier revision’s S_3 example.

## The family

For every $n\ge2$, let $F_n$ be the splitting field over $\mathbb Q(T)$ of

$$f_n(X,T)=X^n-X-T.$$

Then $\mathrm{Gal}(F_n/\mathbb Q(T))\simeq S_n$ and $F_n\cap\overline{\mathbb Q}=\mathbb Q$. This is a classical symmetric-group family; no novelty of the regular realization is claimed. The following proof makes its geometric monodromy and constant-field condition explicit.

## All finite critical values are simple and distinct

Consider the degree-$n$ polynomial map $p(x)=x^n-x$ over $\mathbb C$. Its derivative is $p'(x)=nx^{n-1}-1$. Its $n-1$ critical points $c$ satisfy

$$c^{n-1}=1/n.$$

They are nonzero and distinct. At each one, $p''(c)=n(n-1)c^{n-2}\ne0$, so its local ramification index is exactly two. Its critical value is

$$p(c)=c/n-c=-\frac{n-1}{n}c.$$

These values are distinct because multiplication by the displayed nonzero scalar is injective. Thus above each finite branch value precisely one pair of sheets comes together, and all other sheets are unramified. A small positively oriented loop around that value interchanges those two sheets and fixes the others: in a local analytic coordinate the map has the form $u\mapsto u^2$ times an invertible analytic coordinate change.

## Why these transpositions generate the whole symmetric group

Delete the finite set $B$ of critical values from the affine $T$-line, and delete $p^{-1}(B)$ from the affine $x$-line. The restriction

$$p:\mathbb C\setminus p^{-1}(B)\longrightarrow\mathbb C\setminus B$$

is an unramified topological covering of degree $n$. Its source is the complex plane minus a finite set, hence path-connected. The action of the fundamental group of the target on a fiber is therefore transitive: a path joining any two points in the source projects to a loop connecting their fiber labels by analytic continuation.

The fundamental group of the plane minus $n-1$ points is generated by small loops around those individual punctures, based along connecting paths. Each generator acts by a transposition, as just checked. Form a graph on the $n$ sheet labels with an edge for each of these transpositions. The orbits of the generated group are precisely the connected components of the graph, because each edge swap stays in its component and swaps along a path allow movement throughout a component. Transitivity makes the graph connected.

Transpositions along a connected graph generate $S_n$. For a path $v_0,v_1,\ldots,v_r$, conjugating its last edge swap by successive earlier edge swaps produces a swap involving $v_0$ and $v_r$; hence all swaps between a fixed vertex and the others lie in the group, and these generate every permutation. Thus the analytic monodromy is $S_n$.

The standard characteristic-zero correspondence between algebraic functions and their analytic continuation identifies this monodromy with the Galois group of the splitting field over $\mathbb C(T)$. This is the branch-cycle method explained in the checked primary text of Serre. The crucial point is generation by **all** finite branch loops. An $n$-cycle plus one unspecified transposition would by itself not justify the same conclusion for arbitrary composite $n$.

## Arithmetic descent and constants

The polynomial is separable because its critical values are nonconstant in $T$, or simply because its discriminant is not the zero rational function. Its arithmetic Galois group embeds in $S_n$ by its faithful action on the roots. Base extension from $\mathbb Q$ to $\overline{\mathbb Q}$ and then to $\mathbb C$ can only decrease the root permutation group. The last group is already $S_n$, so both the geometric group over $\overline{\mathbb Q}(T)$ and the arithmetic group over $\mathbb Q(T)$ are $S_n$.

Let $k_n=F_n\cap\overline{\mathbb Q}$. For a finite Galois function-field extension in characteristic zero, the geometric group is the kernel of the arithmetic action on the algebraic constant extension $k_n/\mathbb Q$. Equality of the two groups forces that action to have trivial quotient, so $k_n=\mathbb Q$. This proves regularity exactly as defined in the prompt, not just an ordinary number-field realization.

For $n=2$ there is one critical point and one transposition, and the argument gives the regular quadratic family. The trivial group can separately be realized by $\mathbb Q(T)$ itself. For $n=3$ the family agrees with the earlier revision's example.

## Added scope and limitation

The prior revision proves only the $S_3$ member explicitly and develops quotient/product closure and a general pullback obstruction. This report supplies a uniform proof for every $S_n$, including the geometric generation step for all $n$. The result does not realize an arbitrary subgroup $H\le S_n$ over $\mathbb Q(T)$: the extension with group $H$ would have base $F_n^H$, which need not be a rational function field. Hence Cayley's subgroup embedding does not turn this family into a solution for every finite group.
