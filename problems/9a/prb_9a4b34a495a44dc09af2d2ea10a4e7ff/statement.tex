The displayed problem asks about the size $L_2$ of the second-largest component of $G(n,1/n)$. Its editorial says $L_2\ll\log n$. That assertion is incompatible with the displayed critical model: the logarithmic second component belongs to the strictly supercritical regime $G(n,c/n)$ with fixed $c>1$.
For the literal model we prove the following precise exponent-scale description, without importing a random-graph component theorem: for every fixed $\epsilon>0$,
\[
 \mathbb P\bigl(n^{2/3-\epsilon}\le L_2\le L_1\le n^{2/3+\epsilon}\bigr)\longrightarrow1. \tag{1}
\]
Here rounding integer thresholds does not change the conclusion. In particular $L_2=n^{2/3+o(1)}$ with high probability and cannot be $O(\log n)$ with high probability. The finer known limiting distribution of $n^{-2/3}L_2$ is not reconstructed below.
