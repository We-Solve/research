\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(G\) be a finite group. An irreducible complex character of \(G\) is the function \(\chi(g)=\operatorname{tr}(\rho(g))\) associated with an irreducible representation \(\rho:G\to\operatorname{GL}(V)\) on a nonzero finite-dimensional complex vector space. Its degree is \(\chi(1)=\dim_{\C}V\). For \(g\in G\), write
\[
g^G=\{xgx^{-1}:x\in G\},\qquad C_G(g)=\{x\in G:xg=gx\}.
\]
The length of this conjugacy class is \(|g^G|=[G:C_G(g)]\). Since a character is constant on conjugacy classes, saying that \(\chi\) vanishes on \(g^G\) means \(\chi(g)=0\). A group is simple if it is nontrivial and has no nontrivial proper normal subgroup.

\textbf{Conjecture (V. Pannone).} For every finite group \(G\), if there exist an irreducible complex character \(\chi\) of \(G\) and an element \(g\in G\) such that
\[
\chi(1)\in\{1,3,5,\ldots\},\qquad |g^G|\in\{1,3,5,\ldots\},\qquad \chi(g)=0,
\]
then \(G\) is not simple.

The supplied source states that this conjecture, if true, implies the solvability of all finite groups of odd order, and therefore identifies a proof independent of the classification of finite simple groups (CFSG) as being of special interest. This contextual assertion is retained as supplied, without independent verification.
\subsection{Short English statement}
V. Pannone conjectures that a finite group admitting an irreducible complex character of odd degree which vanishes on a conjugacy class of odd size cannot be simple. The source highlights interest in a proof independent of CFSG because of the stated implication for solvability of finite groups of odd order.
