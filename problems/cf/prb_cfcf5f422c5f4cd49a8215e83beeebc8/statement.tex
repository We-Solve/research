\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A simplicial complex is a collection of simplices closed under taking faces; a subcomplex is a subcollection that is itself a simplicial complex. An elementary simplicial collapse removes a pair of simplices \(\tau,\sigma\), where \(\tau\) is a nonempty codimension-one face of \(\sigma\) and \(\sigma\) is the unique maximal simplex containing \(\tau\). Write \(K\searrow L\) if \(L\) is obtained from \(K\) by a finite sequence of elementary simplicial collapses, allowing a sequence of length zero.

A subdivision \(D\) of \(C\) triangulates each simplex of \(C\) compatibly with its faces. Under the subdivision identification \(|D|=|C|\), its restriction to a subcomplex \(C'\subseteq C\) is
\[
D'=D|_{C'}:=\{\delta\in D:|\delta|\subseteq |C'|\},
\]
which is a subdivision of \(C'\).

\textbf{Subdivision preservation question.} For every simplicial complex \(C\), every subcomplex \(C'\subseteq C\), and every subdivision \(D\) of \(C\) restricting to a subdivision \(D'\) of \(C'\), is the implication
\[
C\searrow C'\quad\Longrightarrow\quad D\searrow D'
\]
valid?
