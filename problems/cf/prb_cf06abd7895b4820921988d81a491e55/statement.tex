\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a prime $p$, an abstract $p$-group means a group in which every element has finite order equal to a power of $p$; no common exponent bound or local-finiteness assumption is imposed. A group $G$ is finitely generated if $G=\langle S\rangle$ for a finite set $S$. It is residually finite if for every $g\ne1$ there are a finite group $F$ and a homomorphism $\varphi:G\to F$ with $\varphi(g)\ne1$. Its centre is $Z(G)=\{z\in G:zg=gz\text{ for all }g\in G\}$.

The source requests, for every prime $p$, a construction based on a development of E. S. Golod's construction of a finitely generated residually finite $p$-group $G_p$ satisfying
\[1<|Z(G_p)|<\infty.\]
Thus the centre must be nontrivial and finite; a trivial centre or an infinite centre is not the requested case. The method restriction ``based on the development of E. S. Golod's construction'' is part of the source request, although the record does not define a formal class of admissible Golod constructions.
