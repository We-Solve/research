\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(\mathrm{SH}=\operatorname{Ho}(\mathrm{Sp})\) be the classical stable homotopy category, with its distinguished triangles, coproducts and closed symmetric monoidal smash product. The task is to characterize this category up to equivalence. A precise characterization must specify the allowed abstract categories/axioms and whether equivalences preserve the additive, triangulated, coproduct or monoidal structures. The source does not fix that level; the summary's strongest monoidal formulation is not silently imposed on its shorter wording.

A phantom map \(f:X\to Y\) is a stable map such that \(f\circ a=0\) for every map \(a:F\to X\) from a finite spectrum \(F\). Write \(\mathcal P(X,Y)\) for this ideal. The source reports that Margolis characterized the quotient by phantoms and Christensen--Strickland characterized the phantom part, leaving a square-zero-extension issue. In an additive-category description, such an extension involves groups of morphisms fitting
\[0\to\mathcal P(X,Y)\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal C}(X,Y)
\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal Q}(X,Y)\to0,\]
compatible with identities and composition, with composites of two ideal morphisms zero. Knowing the quotient \(\mathcal Q\) and the ideal's data alone need not specify all compatible composition/extension data. The source suggests there might be many inequivalent extensions; it does not assert their existence or a classification. The quotient by an ideal is not automatically endowed with every inherited triangulated structure, so the precise categorical framework is required.

Hovey also says that a characterization within homotopy categories of model categories was given by Schwede, explicitly qualifying this with ``I think,'' and that much general structure is known. These are reported partial results, not precise theorem statements supplied here.

The summary relates the issue to the generating hypothesis, but the notions must be distinguished. A map inducing zero on all stable homotopy groups is tested by sphere spectra, whereas a phantom is tested by all finite spectra. These conditions cannot simply be identified. No generating-hypothesis claim or unrestricted map-detection theorem is inserted as a solution to the categorical extension problem.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Characterize the stable homotopy category and resolve what extra data remain after its phantom ideal and quotient are understood. The possible square-zero extensions and the required kind of equivalence are not specified, and phantoms must not be confused with all homotopy-group-zero maps.
