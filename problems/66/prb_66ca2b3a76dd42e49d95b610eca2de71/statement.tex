\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(n\in\Z\) with \(n\geq 3\). Equip \(\R^n\) with its Euclidean inner product \(\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\), and let \(S^{n-1}=\{\theta\in\R^n:\langle\theta,\theta\rangle=1\}\). A convex body is a compact convex subset of \(\R^n\) with nonempty interior. It is origin-symmetric if \(K=-K\). Define its support function by
\[
h_K(\theta)=\max_{x\in K}\langle x,\theta\rangle,
\]
and define \(\theta^\perp=\{x\in\R^n:\langle x,\theta\rangle=0\}\). Write \(\operatorname{vol}_{n-1}\) for the Euclidean \((n-1)\)-dimensional volume on this hyperplane. An origin-centered ellipsoid is a set of the form
\[
\{x\in\R^n:x^{\mathsf T}Ax\leq 1\},
\]
where \(A\) is a real symmetric positive-definite \(n\times n\) matrix.

\textbf{Question.} For every integer \(n\geq 3\) and every origin-symmetric convex body \(K\subseteq\R^n\), does
\[
\exists c>0\ \ \forall\theta\in S^{n-1},\qquad
h_K(\theta)\operatorname{vol}_{n-1}(K\cap\theta^\perp)=c
\]
imply that \(K\) is an origin-centered ellipsoid? The constant \(c\) may depend on \(K\) and \(n\), but must be independent of \(\theta\). Positivity of \(c\) follows from the convex-body and symmetry assumptions.

\subsection{Short English statement}
If the product of the support function and the volume of the perpendicular central section is constant in every direction, must an origin-symmetric convex body in every dimension \(n\geq 3\) be an ellipsoid?
