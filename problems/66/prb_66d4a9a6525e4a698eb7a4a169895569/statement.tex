\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 800210}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(n\geq 1\) be an integer, and let \(D\subset\C^n\) be a bounded domain, meaning a nonempty connected open set, with smooth boundary (interpreted as \(C^\infty\)). Let \((D^{\sim},p)\) be its envelope of holomorphy, regarded as a connected Riemann domain over \(\C^n\), with canonical locally biholomorphic projection \(p:D^{\sim}\to\C^n\). The canonical inclusion \(j:D\hookrightarrow D^{\sim}\) satisfies \(p\circ j=\operatorname{id}_D\), and each holomorphic function on \(D\) extends holomorphically to \(D^{\sim}\).

\begin{enumerate}
\item[(A)] Must \(D^{\sim}\) be finitely sheeted over \(\C^n\)? Under the interpretation that the number of sheets is uniformly bounded, this asks whether, for every such \(D\), there exists an integer \(N_D\geq 1\) such that
\[
\#p^{-1}(z)\leq N_D\qquad\text{for every }z\in\C^n.
\]
The source does not specify whether ``finitely sheeted'' requires this uniform bound or only that every fiber is finite. These are distinct formulations, and the intended convention requires confirmation. No surjectivity of \(p\) onto \(\C^n\) is assumed.
\item[(B)] Must \(D^{\sim}\) have finite volume? Using the Euclidean volume form on the base and counting all sheets, this asks whether
\[
\operatorname{Vol}_{p}(D^{\sim})
:=\int_{D^{\sim}}p^*(\mathrm{d}V_{\mathrm{Eucl}})<\infty.
\]
Here, writing \(z_k=x_k+i y_k\), the base volume form is \(\mathrm{d}V_{\mathrm{Eucl}}=\mathrm{d}x_1\wedge\mathrm{d}y_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{d}x_n\wedge\mathrm{d}y_n\). The source does not explicitly specify a volume convention; this formulation uses the natural volume induced by the projection, with multiplicity.
\end{enumerate}

Both questions are stated for each positive integer \(n\); the supplied source gives no dimension restriction or uniform bound independent of \(D\).
\subsection{Short English statement}
For a bounded domain with smooth boundary in \(\C^n\), ask whether its envelope of holomorphy has finitely many sheets and finite Euclidean volume counted over all sheets. The meaning of finite sheetedness and the volume convention require confirmation. The supplied registry labels this problem open; this draft does not independently verify that status.
