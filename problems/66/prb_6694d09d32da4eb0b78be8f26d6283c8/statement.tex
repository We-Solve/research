\section{Bounded Local Observables in Factorizing Quantum Field Theories}\label{30000341}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 30000341 · Mathematical Physics}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


\paragraph{Definitions and conventions.} In two-dimensional Minkowski spacetime a wedge is an unbounded causal region and a double cone is the bounded intersection of a future and a past light cone. The report constructs a wedge-local net $W\mapsto A(W)$ on a Hilbert space from a factorizing two-particle scattering matrix $S$. For an operator algebra $A$, its commutant is $A'=\{T:TX=XT\ \forall X\in A\}$. If $W_1\subset W_2$ and $O=W_1'\cap W_2$ is a double cone, the candidate local algebra is $A(O)=A(W_1)'\cap A(W_2)$. Nontrivial means $A(O)\ne\mathbb C I$.

\paragraph{Source scope and qualifications.} The target is the report's admissible factorizing S-matrix family and wedge construction. Its modular-nuclearity subfamily already has nontrivial double-cone observables; those positive cases do not settle every admissible S-matrix.

\paragraph{Catalogue mathematical target.}
\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}
For the wedge-local net associated with a factorizing two-dimensional S-matrix, do nontrivial observables localized in bounded double cones exist? Equivalently, for nested wedges $W_1\subset W_2$, is the double-cone algebra $A(W_1)'\cap A(W_2)$ nontrivial?

\paragraph{Saved literature context.} The supplied catalogue's assessment dated 2026-08-21 records: Modular nuclearity has produced nontrivial bounded-region algebras and full inverse-scattering models for a large class of factorizing S-matrices, but not for every admissible S-matrix. The assessment is retained as dated provenance; it is not a new comprehensive literature or open-status certification.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Determine when a wedge-local theory built from a factorizing scattering matrix contains observables in bounded spacetime regions.
