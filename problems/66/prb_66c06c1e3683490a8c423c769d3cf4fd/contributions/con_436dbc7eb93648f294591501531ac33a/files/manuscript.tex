\section{Removing term order without hiding the extrapolation choice}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.


For a finite design $D\subseteq\mathbb R^d$, its design ideal is $I(D)=\{f:f(v)=0\text{ for every }v\in D\}$. Polynomial functions are compared modulo $I(D)$; evaluating on $D$ identifies their restrictions to the design. A term order selects standard monomials for a Gröbner basis of $I(D)$, hence one particular basis for polynomial functions on the observed design. Different representatives agree on $D$ but can make different predictions elsewhere. The question concerns removing dependence on this arbitrary order when fitting a model.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Question 64 (Laubenbacher). Choosing models modulo the design ideal depends on the term order chosen. How can we eliminate this choice of term order from the problem of finding a model that fits the data? \section{Research attempt}
\subsection{Literature and scope}
Question 64 asks to remove term-order dependence in polynomial model fitting. I tested a strong proposed replacement: a deterministic interpolating polynomial chosen equivariantly under all affine coordinate changes, without additional geometric structure. The following symmetry obstruction rules out that strengthened goal, not the original question in all its interpretations.
\begin{proposition}
There is no deterministic rule assigning to every finite consistent real interpolation data set a polynomial interpolant that is equivariant under every invertible affine change of input coordinates.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Consider the two points $D=\{(0,0),(1,0)\}$ with responses 0 and 1. They have a polynomial interpolant, namely $x$. For every real $c$, the invertible linear shear $T_c(x,y)=(x+cy,y)$ fixes both data points and their responses. A deterministic equivariant rule must assign the same polynomial $F$ before and after this unchanged data set. Equivariance says the transformed polynomial is $F\circ T_c^{-1}$; replacing $c$ by $-c$ gives
\[
F(x+cy,y)=F(x,y)\quad\hbox{for every }c,x,y\in\mathbb R.
\]
Differentiate this polynomial identity in $c$ at zero to obtain $y\,\partial F/\partial x=0$. Since the polynomial ring over $\mathbb R$ has no zero divisors, $\partial F/\partial x=0$. In characteristic zero this means $F$ depends only on $y$. It must then take the same value at $(0,0)$ and $(1,0)$, contradicting the two responses.
\end{proof}
\subsection{Failed route and verification}
The shear fixes each observed point, while it changes the sample interpolant $x$ at the unobserved point $(0,1)$ from 0 to $c$. This direct substitution checks the geometry behind the proof. The unsupported step in the attempted canonical construction is assuming all coordinate symmetries admit a common polynomial representative of the fitted equivalence class.
\subsection{Original-scope comparison and remaining gaps}
The proof does not rule out a canonical quotient class modulo $I(D)$, fitted values alone, a chosen metric or regularizer, a restricted coordinate group, or randomized searches. In particular, term-order independence is weaker than full affine equivariance. No novelty is claimed for this elementary obstruction, and no general fitting method replacing term orders is established here.
