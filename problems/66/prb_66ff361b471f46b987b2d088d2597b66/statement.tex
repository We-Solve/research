\section{Union-Closed Sets Conjecture}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $X$ be a finite set and let $\mathcal F\subseteq2^X$ be a nonempty family of distinct subsets whose union is nonempty. It is union-closed if $A\cup B\in\mathcal F$ for every $A,B\in\mathcal F$. For $x\in X$, write $f_{\mathcal F}(x)=\#\{A\in\mathcal F:x\in A\}$. The family $\{\varnothing\}$ is excluded, as required in the standard formulation.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every such union-closed family, is there an element $x\in\bigcup\mathcal F$ such that
\[
f_{\mathcal F}(x)\geq\tfrac12|\mathcal F|?
\] \sref{statement}{2}

\subsection{Short English statement}
Must some element occur in at least half the sets of any finite union-closed family with a nonempty set?
