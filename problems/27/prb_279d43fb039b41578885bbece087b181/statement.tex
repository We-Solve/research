\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
\textbf{Original question, from Kollár's lecture.} Fix a class of smooth manifolds embedded by a pluricanonical system in some projective space. Take the closure of its Hilbert points and the GIT semistable points there. Reembed using a higher power of the canonical bundle, in the corresponding new projective space. Is there an integer $N$, depending on the fixed class, such that the class of GIT semistable surfaces stabilizes for embeddings by $MK_X$ whenever $M>N$? The source calls this fundamental and states that the curve analogue has $N=5$.

To express the two exponents, for an admissible pluricanonical embedding by $MK_X$ let $k$ denote the Hilbert-point degree; asymptotic Hilbert semistability means the verdict for $k\gg0$, with $M$ fixed. Stabilization then concerns variation of $M$. The original does not fix Hilbert polynomials, admissible divisibility, base field, GIT linearizations or Hilbert degrees, or a rule identifying boundary objects in the different Hilbert-scheme closures. In particular, eventual equality of abstract semistable loci and existence of compatible asymptotic GIT compactifications are distinct interpretations.

\textbf{Attached unreviewed research-aid account.} The aid attributes to published surface counterexamples of Wang and Xu a negative answer in the intended sense of compatible asymptotic pluricanonical GIT compactification. It says their KSBA limits violate a necessary multiplicity bound for weak asymptotic Chow semistability, and uniqueness of the KSBA filling prevents replacing such a limit by a different compatible weakly asymptotically semistable filling. Here KSBA limits refer to stable canonical models with the appropriate $\Q$-Gorenstein family structure. The aid says this Chow obstruction also excludes asymptotic Hilbert semistability recurring for infinitely many sufficiently divisible pluricanonical exponents. No explicit example, multiplicity bound, weak-semistability definition, or publication locator is supplied. These statements do not, without a comparison convention, certify every possible reading of the original stabilization question.

The aid proposes a two-exponent finite-wall reduction. Let $R=\bigoplus_{j\ge0}H^0(X,jK_X)$ be the intended finitely generated canonical ring and $F$ a compatible filtration with finitely generated Rees algebra. After passage to a standard Veronese, put $h(j)=\dim R_j$ and let $w_F(j)$ be the total associated graded weight in degree $j$. For admissible $M,k$ the reported trace-normalized weight is
\[\Phi_F(M,k)=h(M)w_F(Mk)-k\,w_F(M)h(Mk).\]
For each fixed compatible $F$, the aid claims that the eventual lexicographic sign of this polynomial in $k$ changes only finitely many times as $M$ runs along a fixed divisible ray. Here lexicographic sign means the sign of the highest nonzero coefficient in powers of $k$; the choice of which sign means instability depends on the linearization and weight convention.

Its conditional uniform conclusion requires a finite compatible Kempf-detection hypothesis: a finite stratification with finite lists of compatible filtrations detecting every large-$M$ instability, with the necessary uniform weight data on the strata. Under this explicit finiteness and compatibility assumption, the semistable abstract locus is reported to become constant. Failure of stabilization is accordingly said to force unboundedly moving destabilizing filtration types or failure of compatibility with the canonical ring. This is a conditional reduction; the aid does not supply the detecting lists or establish that the finiteness hypothesis holds for the original class.
\subsection{Short English statement}
The problem asks whether pluricanonical GIT semistability eventually stabilizes for a fixed surface class. The aid reports counterexamples for compatible compactifications, while a separate finite-filtration argument gives stabilization only under a uniform finite detection hypothesis. Embedding exponent and Hilbert-point exponent, and the possible meanings of stabilization, are kept distinct.
