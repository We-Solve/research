\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $\mathbb H\mathbb H^n$ denote quaternionic hyperbolic $n$-space, and let $G\leq\operatorname{Isom}(\mathbb H\mathbb H^n)$ be a discrete subgroup. Kazhdan's Property $T$ means that every unitary representation with almost invariant unit vectors has a nonzero invariant vector. Almost invariance means that for every finite $F\subset G$ and $\varepsilon>0$ there is a unit vector $v$ with $\|\pi(g)v-v\|<\varepsilon$ for all $g\in F$.

If $G$ has Property $T$, must there exist a $G$-invariant totally geodesic subspace $H\subseteq\mathbb H\mathbb H^n$ such that $G$ acts on $H$ as a lattice? Here totally geodesic means that geodesics of the subspace are ambient geodesics. In the restriction-action interpretation, the image of $G$ in $\operatorname{Isom}(H)$ must be discrete with finite covolume; torsion is allowed, so the quotient may be an orbifold.

The source does not prescribe the dimension or type of $H$, so no quaternionic-type requirement on $H$ is added. It also does not exclude finite groups or specify whether a point counts as a permissible totally geodesic subspace. These degenerate cases and any faithfulness requirement on the restricted action should be fixed in a complete version.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Must a discrete Property $T$ subgroup of quaternionic-hyperbolic isometries be a lattice on some invariant totally geodesic subspace?
