\section{Sublaminations and superlaminations — Question 6.6}\label{10300015}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 10300015 · Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A surface lamination $\Lambda$ is a closed subset of $M$ locally homeomorphic to a product of a disk with a closed transverse set. It is essential when it has no sphere leaf or torus leaf bounding a solid torus, and its complementary regions are irreducible, have incompressible boundary, and have no compressing monogon. Such a monogon is a compressing disk with one boundary arc on a boundary leaf and the other along a transverse interval fiber. It is genuine if some complementary region is not an interval bundle. A sublamination is a closed union of leaves. A manifold is atoroidal if it has no incompressible embedded torus other than a boundary-parallel one. Write $\widetilde M$ for its universal cover, and put a tilde on a foliation or lamination for its lift. A loosesse lamination may have compressing monogons but satisfies the other essentiality requirements. Realization by minimal surfaces means that all leaves have zero mean curvature for one ambient Riemannian metric.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Construct an atoroidal $3$-manifold $M$ with a lamination $\Lambda$, possibly loosesse, for which no Riemannian metric on $M$ makes every leaf minimal, but whose existence proves an identifiable useful topological property of $M$. Supply both the obstruction to such minimal realization and the certified topological consequence. \sref{10300015}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Find an atoroidal manifold with a lamination that cannot be minimal for any metric but still certifies useful topology.
