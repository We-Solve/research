\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(X\) be a Banach space over its scalar field \(\mathbb K\). The supplied record does not specify whether \(\mathbb K=\R\) or \(\mathbb K=\C\); all scalar coefficients below belong to the same field as \(X\). A sequence \((x_n)\) in \(X\) is normalized and weakly null if \(\|x_n\|=1\) for every \(n\) and \(f(x_n)\to0\) for every \(f\in X^*\).

A normalized basic sequence \((e_i)\) in a Banach space \(E\) is a spreading model generated by \((x_n)\) if, for every \(k\geq1\), every \(a_1,\ldots,a_k\in\mathbb K\), and every \(\varepsilon>0\), there is \(N\geq1\) such that
\[
\left|\left\|\sum_{i=1}^k a_i x_{n_i}\right\|_X-\left\|\sum_{i=1}^k a_i e_i\right\|_E\right|<\varepsilon
\qquad\text{whenever }N\leq n_1<\cdots<n_k.
\]
Here a basic sequence means a Schauder basis for its closed linear span. Write \(\mathrm{SP}_w(X)\) for the spreading models generated by normalized weakly null sequences in \(X\), allowing passage to a subsequence before generation. Two basic sequences \((e_i)\) and \((f_i)\) are equivalent, written \((e_i)\sim(f_i)\), if there is \(C\geq1\) such that, for every \(k\geq1\) and every \(a_1,\ldots,a_k\in\mathbb K\),
\[
 C^{-1}\left\|\sum_{i=1}^k a_i e_i\right\|
 \leq \left\|\sum_{i=1}^k a_i f_i\right\|
 \leq C\left\|\sum_{i=1}^k a_i e_i\right\|.
\]
For \(c_0(\mathbb K)\), the space of scalar sequences tending to zero with the supremum norm, and \(\ell_p(\mathbb K)\), the space of scalar sequences \((a_i)\) with \(\sum_i|a_i|^p<\infty\), let \((u_i)\) denote the standard unit vector basis. Their finite-sum norms are respectively \(\max_i|a_i|\) and \((\sum_i|a_i|^p)^{1/p}\).

\textbf{Unique spreading model problem (S. A. Argyros, source label [AOST]).} Is it true that, for every reflexive Banach space \(X\) satisfying
\[
\bigl|\mathrm{SP}_w(X)/\!\sim\bigr|=1,
\]
the unique equivalence class in \(\mathrm{SP}_w(X)/\!\sim\) is represented by the standard unit vector basis of \(c_0(\mathbb K)\) or of \(\ell_p(\mathbb K)\) for some \(1\leq p<\infty\)? Equivalently, must every representative of that class be equivalent to one of those bases? The exponent \(p\), when applicable, may depend on \(X\).
