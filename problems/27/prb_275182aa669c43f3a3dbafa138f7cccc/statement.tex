\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A finite simple graph is Berge if neither it nor its complement has an induced odd cycle of length at least five. An even pair is a nonadjacent pair such that every induced path joining it has even edge length. An even-pair skew-partition, with the source's nonemptiness convention, is a partition $V(G)=A\mathbin{\dot\cup}B\mathbin{\dot\cup}C\mathbin{\dot\cup}D$ into four nonempty sets such that $A$ is complete to $B$, $C$ is anticomplete to $D$, and every nonadjacent pair within $A$ or within $B$ is an even pair.

The question is whether every Berge graph is basic, has a $2$-join, or has such an even-pair skew-partition. The supplied extract does not define basic or the side-degeneracy convention for a $2$-join. Under the usual Berge-decomposition interpretation, the basic classes are bipartite graphs, their complements, line graphs of bipartite graphs, their complements, and double-split graphs. A double-split graph consists of matching pairs $\{a_i,b_i\}$ with no edges between distinct pairs, complementary-matching pairs $\{c_j,d_j\}$ with all edges between distinct pairs, and exactly two disjoint edges between each pair $\{a_i,b_i\}$ and each pair $\{c_j,d_j\}$, with at least two pairs of each type.

A standard nondegenerate $2$-join interpretation partitions $V(G)=X_1\mathbin{\dot\cup}X_2$, $|X_i|\geq3$, with disjoint nonempty $A_i,B_i\subseteq X_i$, such that the cross edges are exactly all $A_1$--$A_2$ and $B_1$--$B_2$ edges, and neither side is just a three-vertex induced path with its ends in $A_i,B_i$. Some literature imposes further connectedness conditions; the source convention must be checked before choosing among these versions. These explicit conventions clarify the target without claiming they were defined in the isolated question.

The aid supplies a useful exact reduction independent of those two terms: in an even-pair-free graph, an even-pair skew-partition exists if and only if there is a clique cutset of size at least two. A clique cutset is a clique $S$ with $G-S$ disconnected into at least two nonempty parts. The size requirement corresponds to splitting $S$ into nonempty $A,B$; the internal even-pair requirement is then vacuous.

For a reported counterexample, the aid takes $H=\overline W$, where $W$ is the 21-vertex ``Worst Berge Graph Known So Far'' in Trotignon's survey. It reports that $W$ is perfect and nonbasic, has neither a $2$-join nor a complement $2$-join, and every edge of $W$ is the middle edge of an induced four-vertex path. It consequently reports $H$ Berge, nonbasic, with no $2$-join and no even pair. It then reports exhaustive checking of all 3,229 cliques of $H$ with no clique cutset, which would rule out even-pair skew-partitions by the equivalence above and refute the question. The reproduced adjacency list and check certificate are not present in this supplied record, so this is a reported counterexample, not an independently verified explicit graph or exhaustive check here.
