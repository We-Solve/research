Under the usual word-hyperbolic interpretation, a group \(\Gamma\) is hyperbolic if it has a finite generating set whose Cayley graph, with edges of length one, has \(\delta\)-thin geodesic triangles for some \(\delta\geq0\): every side lies in the closed \(\delta\)-neighborhood of the union of the other two sides. For a discrete group \(\Gamma\), Kazhdan property \((T)\) means that every unitary representation \(\pi:\Gamma\to\mathcal U(H)\) on a complex Hilbert space having almost invariant unit vectors has a nonzero invariant vector.
