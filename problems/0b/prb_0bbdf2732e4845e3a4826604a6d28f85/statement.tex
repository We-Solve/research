\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(n\in\N\) with \(n\geq 1\), and write \([n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}\). Suppose that \(A\subseteq\Z\) satisfies
\[
\bigl|A\cap\{k,-k\}\bigr|=1\qquad\text{for every }k\in[n].
\]
Define
\[
A-A=\{a-b:a,b\in A\},\qquad D_n(A)=(A-A)\cap[n].
\]
For \(B\subseteq[n]\), let \(\mathbf 1_B(t)\) equal \(1\) when \(t\in B\) and \(0\) otherwise, and define the ordered-pair count
\[
R_A(B)=\sum_{(a,b)\in A\times A}\mathbf 1_B(a-b).
\]
The literal source condition permits elements of \(A\) outside \(\{-n,\ldots,-1,1,\ldots,n\}\), including infinitely many elements. Alternatively, one may restrict attention to the finite set \(A_0=A\cap\{-n,\ldots,-1,1,\ldots,n\}\) of signed representatives. Both proposed universal lower bounds are equivalent under these two readings, since \(A_0\subseteq A\), and the finite signed-representative sets themselves satisfy the literal condition.

\textbf{First question.} Is it true, uniformly over all such \(A\), that
\[
|D_n(A)|=(1-o(1))n\qquad(n\to\infty)?
\]
Explicitly, does every \(\eta\in(0,1)\) admit an integer \(N_\eta\geq1\) such that, for every integer \(n\geq N_\eta\) and every admissible \(A\),
