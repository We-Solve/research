Suppose $A,B\subseteq\mathbb N$ satisfy $|A\cap[1,N]|\gg\sqrt N$ and $|B\cap[1,N]|\gg\sqrt N$ for every sufficiently large $N$. Must $a_1-a_2=b_1-b_2\ne0$ have infinitely many solutions with $a_1,a_2\in A$, $b_1,b_2\in B$? We construct sets with no such solution at all.
