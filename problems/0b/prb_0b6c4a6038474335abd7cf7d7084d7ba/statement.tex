\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Use the setup identified by the supplied summary: an $n$-dimensional Riemannian manifold $(X,g)$ has scalar curvature $\operatorname{Sc}_g\geq\sigma$. Let $X_{-r}$ denote its $r$-interior, whose exact boundary convention is omitted. For a manifold with boundary, a usual definition is
\[
X_{-r}=\{x\in X:d_g(x,\partial X)\geq r\};
\]
for an incomplete boundaryless manifold an analogous definition uses its metric-completion boundary. These conventions are not interchangeable without specifying the category.

The completion-by-extension conjecture asks whether, for every dimension $n$, there is a sufficiently large constant $C_n>0$ depending only on $n$ such that whenever $\sigma^-<\sigma$ and
\[
r\geq C_n(\sigma-\sigma^-)^{-1/2},
\]
there exists a complete Riemannian $n$-manifold $(X^+,g^+)$ with
\[
\operatorname{Sc}_{g^+}\geq\sigma^-
\]
that isometrically contains $X_{-r}$. In a Riemannian-extension interpretation this requires an embedding $i:X_{-r}\to X^+$ with $i^*g^+=g|_{X_{-r}}$. If distance preservation in the ambient metric-space sense is required, that is stronger and must be specified. The source writes the desired extension radius as $r^+=\infty$, corresponding to a complete extension.

\subsection{Short English statement}
Can the sufficiently deep interior of any manifold with scalar curvature at least $\sigma$ be extended to a complete manifold with scalar curvature at least $\sigma^-<\sigma$? The required depth should be at most a dimensional constant times $(\sigma-\sigma^-)^{-1/2}$.
