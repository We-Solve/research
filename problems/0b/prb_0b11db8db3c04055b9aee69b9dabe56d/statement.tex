\section{Decidability of Universal Factor Languages}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.


$S^*$ is the set of finite concatenations of words from $S$, including the empty word. A factor is a contiguous substring; $\operatorname{Fact}(S^*)$ collects all such substrings and $\Sigma^*$ all finite alphabet words.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For a finite set $S$ of words over an alphabet $\Sigma$, where $\operatorname{Fact}(S^*)$ is the set of factors of $S^*$, is it decidable whether $\operatorname{Fact}(S^*)=\Sigma^*$, and what is the computational complexity of testing this equality? \sref{statement}{2}

The supplied catalogue labels this record partially solved. Its source-status metadata was checked against the supplied snapshot on 2026-10-11. This import does not independently establish current open status or mathematical correctness. The catalogue literature assessment is dated 2026-08-21.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Determine decidability and complexity of whether factors of the generated language include every possible word.
