\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For each integer \(n\geq 1\), let \(\mathcal C_n\) be the collection of pairs \((R,B)\) of disjoint finite subsets of \(\R^2\) such that \(|R|=|B|=n\) and no three points of \(P=R\cup B\) are collinear. Points of \(R\) are red and points of \(B\) are blue; here general position is interpreted as the stated noncollinearity condition.

A triangle determined by \(P\) is the convex hull \(\operatorname{conv}\{x,y,z\}\) of an unordered three-element subset \(\{x,y,z\}\subseteq P\). It is monochromatic if all three vertices belong to \(R\), or all three belong to \(B\). It is empty if
\[
\operatorname{int}\bigl(\operatorname{conv}\{x,y,z\}\bigr)\cap P=\varnothing.
\]
Thus emptiness excludes interior points of either color. The interior is the ordinary planar interior; the general-position assumption also excludes additional points on the edges.

Let \(T(R,B)\) denote the number of monochromatic empty triangles, counting each unordered vertex triple once, and define
\[
f(n)=\min_{(R,B)\in\mathcal C_n}T(R,B).
\]
Equivalently, \(f(n)\) is the largest nonnegative integer such that every configuration in \(\mathcal C_n\) contains at least \(f(n)\) monochromatic empty triangles.

\textbf{Problem.} Prove or disprove that
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{f(n)}{n^2}=0.
\]
Explicitly, the proposed assertion is
\[
\forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists N\geq1\ \forall n\in\Z_{\geq1},\quad
n\geq N\ \Longrightarrow\ \exists(R,B)\in\mathcal C_n:\ T(R,B)<\varepsilon n^2.
\]

\subsection{Short English statement}
Among all general-position configurations with exactly \(n\) red and \(n\) blue points, let \(f(n)\) be the smallest number of monochromatic triangles whose interiors contain no point of either color. Determine whether \(f(n)/n^2\) tends to zero. The supplied registry labels the problem open; this draft preserves that label without independently verifying its current status.
