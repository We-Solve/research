\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a positive integer \(n\), let \(\R P^n\) denote real projective space. Let \(g_{\mathrm{can}}\) denote the canonical metric, interpreted as the quotient of the round metric of sectional curvature \(1\) on \(S^n\) by the antipodal identification. This fixes the length scale associated with the bound \(\pi\).

The question is whether, for every smooth Riemannian metric \(g\) on \(\R P^n\) satisfying
\[
\operatorname{Vol}(\R P^n,g)=\operatorname{Vol}(\R P^n,g_{\mathrm{can}}),
\]
there exist a real number \(L\) and a smooth curve \(\gamma:\R/L\Z\to\R P^n\) such that
\[
0<L\leq\pi,\qquad
\nabla^g_{\dot\gamma}\dot\gamma=0,\qquad
 g(\dot\gamma,\dot\gamma)=1.
\]
Thus \(\gamma\) is a nonconstant closed geodesic of length \(L\). No homotopy class, simplicity condition, or least-period condition is imposed by the supplied statement.

The source does not specify the dimension or any dimension restriction; the formulation above is parameterized by \(n\geq1\), with its intended dimensional scope requiring confirmation. The curvature-\(1\) normalization of the canonical metric and the exclusion of constant geodesics are standard interpretations needed to make the numerical bound and the question substantive, but are not explicitly stated in the supplied excerpt.
