\section{Composites beyond a Growing Gap with Large Least Prime Factor}\label{statement}


\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Unless a section says otherwise, $\N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$,
$\Z$ is the ring of integers, $\Q,\R,\C$ are the rational, real, and complex
fields, $[N]=\{1,\ldots,\lfloor N\rfloor\}$, and $\log$ is the natural
logarithm. For real functions of a parameter tending to infinity,
$f\ll g$ and $f=O(g)$ mean $|f|\leq Cg$ eventually for some fixed $C$;
$f\gg g$ reverses this comparison; $f=o(g)$ means $f/g\to0$;
$f\sim g$ means $f/g\to1$; and $f\asymp g$ means both order bounds.
A subscript on $O$ or $\ll$ specifies allowed constant dependence.
The expression $n^{o(1)}$ in an upper bound means growth smaller than
$n^\epsilon$ for each fixed $\epsilon>0$. Local definitions govern any
exception, finite-field convention, or use of the same symbol for another
object. An asymptotic claim is not automatically an effective algorithm.



For composite $m$, let $p(m)$ be the least prime divisor. The inequalities are equivalent to $f(n)<m-n<p(m)$, so the next candidate must lie inside a gap shorter than its own least prime divisor. One function $f$ tending to infinity must work for every sufficiently large integer $n$, with $m$ allowed to depend on $n$. \sref{statement}{1} \sref{statement}{2}

\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Is there a function $f$ with $f(n)\to \infty$ as $n\to \infty$ such that, for all large $n$, there is a composite number $m$ such that $ n+f(n)<m<n+p(m)? $ (Here $p(m)$ is the least prime factor of $m$.) \sref{statement}{1}

\subsection{Short English statement}

Can every large threshold be followed, beyond a uniformly growing gap, by a composite whose least prime factor exceeds its distance from the threshold?
