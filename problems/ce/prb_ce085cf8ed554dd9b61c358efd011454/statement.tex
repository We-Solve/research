{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 9400141 · Number Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A composite integer is an integer greater than one which is not prime. Congruence $u\equiv v\pmod m$ means $m$ divides $u-v$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Does there exist a composite integer $c>1$ such that $c^2\mid2^{c-1}-1$? \sref{9400141}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can a composite integer satisfy the base-two Wieferich congruence modulo its square?
