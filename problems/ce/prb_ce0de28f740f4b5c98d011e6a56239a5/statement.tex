\section{Ball-Shaped Minimizers of Competing Nonlocal Energies}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


\paragraph{Definitions and conventions.}
An admissible density is a measurable function $\rho:\mathbb R^3\to[0,1]$ with mass $m>0$. Its interaction energy is the displayed double integral, interpreted when finite. A characteristic-function minimizer is $\rho=\mathbf1_B$ almost everywhere for a Euclidean ball $B$, allowing translations of its center. The ball\textquotesingle s volume equals $m$ because of the unit density constraint. The index $m'_{c_2}(\alpha)$ denotes a finite, exponent-dependent large-mass threshold, not an independently quantified geometric constant. The report states the model for $\alpha>0$ and $m>0$, so these are the source parameter ranges.

\paragraph{Target retained from the canonical source catalogue.}
\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}
For
$$
E_\alpha(m)=\inf\left\{E_\alpha[\rho]:0\leq\rho\leq1,\ \int_{\mathbb R^3}\rho=m\right\},
$$
where
$$
E_\alpha[\rho]=\frac12\iint_{\mathbb R^3\times\mathbb R^3}\rho(x)\left(|x-y|^{-1}+|x-y|^\alpha\right)\rho(y)\,dx\,dy,
$$
does there exist $m'_{c_2}(\alpha)<\infty$ such that, whenever $m>m'_{c_2}(\alpha)$, every minimizer is the characteristic function of a ball of volume $m$?

\paragraph{Source scope and qualification.}
The parameter range $\alpha>0$ was recovered from PDF pages 40--42. The target concerns every minimizer for all masses beyond an exponent-dependent finite threshold, not only existence of one radial minimizer. The report\textquotesingle s diameter bound and asymptotic ball energy do not already prove eventual exact ball shape.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Above a finite mass threshold, must every minimizer of this competing-interaction energy be a filled ball?
