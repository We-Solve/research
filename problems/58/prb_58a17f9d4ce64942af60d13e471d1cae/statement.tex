\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Problem 26 asks whether a given manifold admits a metric with prescribed \(Q\), with variants restricting to one conformal class or to conformally flat metrics. The source notes an integral constraint on compact manifolds, depending on conformal class and topology. It does not specify dimension, boundary conditions, or whether \(Q\) is a scalar or a top-degree density. Those two prescription problems must be distinguished.

The aid's fixed-class density statement is in the standard critical even-dimensional closed setting. Let \((M,g)\) be a closed Riemannian manifold of even dimension \(n\), with critical \(Q\)-curvature and formally self-adjoint elliptic critical GJMS operator \(P_g\), normalized by
\[
 Q_{e^{2u}g}\dd V_{e^{2u}g}
 =(Q_g+P_gu)\dd V_g,
 \qquad P_g1=0.
\]
Let \(\mathcal K=\ker P_g\). For a prescribed smooth top-degree density \(\tau\), the reported exact criterion is
\[
 \exists u:\ Q_{e^{2u}g}\dd V_{e^{2u}g}=\tau
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \int_M\psi\,\tau=\int_M\psi Q_g\dd V_g
 \quad\forall\psi\in\mathcal K.
\]
When solvable, its conformal factors form \(u_0+\mathcal K\). This is the linear Fredholm problem for a fixed density. The constant test function includes equality of total \(Q\); other kernel elements give further constraints.

For a prescribed scalar function \(f\), the equation instead is
\[
 P_gu+Q_g=f e^{nu}.
\]
Its necessary kernel moments are
\[
 \int_M\psi f e^{nu}\dd V_g=\int_M\psi Q_g\dd V_g
 \quad(\psi\in\mathcal K).
\]
The aid gives a finite-dimensional feasibility criterion for these moments only. Assume
\(\kappa=\int_MQ_g\dd V_g\ne0\), and \(f\) is smooth and strictly of the sign of \(\kappa\) everywhere. Choose a real basis \(1,\psi_1,\ldots,\psi_d\) of \(\mathcal K\), define
\[
 \Phi(x)=(\psi_1(x),\ldots,\psi_d(x)),\qquad
 b=\kappa^{-1}\left(\int_M\psi_iQ_g\dd V_g\right)_{i=1}^d.
\]
Then the aid reports
\[
 \exists u\in C^\infty(M)\text{ satisfying all moment identities}
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 b\in\operatorname{int}\operatorname{conv}(\Phi(M)).
\]
With this basis the evaluation image has full affine span; equivalently one can use relative interior in its affine hull. For \(d=0\) use the zero-dimensional convention. Indeed the moment formulation uses the positive probability density \(f e^{nu}/\kappa\); the feasibility criterion is independent of the particular strictly sign-compatible \(f\). It is not claimed to be sufficient for the nonlinear scalar PDE itself. Zero-total-\(Q\), sign-changing, and non-strict-sign targets are outside this criterion.

The aid also reports local scalar prescription near a background with
\[
 \ker(P_g-nQ_g)=\{0\},
\]
where \(nQ_g\) denotes multiplication by the function \(nQ_g\). This is the linearization of \(u\mapsto Q_{e^{2u}g}\) at zero; the claim is local surjectivity for nearby scalar targets in suitable elliptic function spaces, not a global prescription theorem. No claim is made here for arbitrary metrics across conformal classes or for all conformally flat manifolds. The broad question's integral constraint is not interpreted as saying total \(Q\) is purely topological for every unrestricted metric.
\subsection{Short English statement}
The record asks for prescribed \(Q\)-curvature under several metric restrictions. The aid exactly solves fixed-density prescription by kernel orthogonality, while scalar prescription remains nonlinear. Its convex-hull test decides joint feasibility of necessary scalar moments, and its separate invertibility condition gives a local scalar result.
