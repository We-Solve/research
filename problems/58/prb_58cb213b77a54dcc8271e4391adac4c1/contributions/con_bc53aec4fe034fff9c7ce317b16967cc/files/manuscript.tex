\subsection{An exactly solvable unbounded measure game}
Let both strategies lie in $\mathcal P_2(\mathbb R)$, with $\rho$ maximizing and $\mu$ minimizing
\[
 F(\rho,\mu)=b m_\rho m_\mu-\frac a2\int z^2d\rho(z)+\frac c2\int x^2d\mu(x),
 \quad m_\rho=\int z\,d\rho,
\]
where $a,c>0$ and $b\in\mathbb R$. There is no entropy term in this restricted example.
\begin{proposition}
The unique saddle point is $(\delta_0,\delta_0)$. The corresponding transport ascent-descent equations converge to it with
\[
 W_2(\rho_t,\delta_0)^2+W_2(\mu_t,\delta_0)^2
 \le e^{-2\min(a,c)t}\left(\int z^2d\rho_0+\int x^2d\mu_0\right).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For fixed $\mu$, completing the square shows that the unique maximizing measure is $\delta_{bm_\mu/a}$. For fixed $\rho$, the unique minimizing measure is $\delta_{-bm_\rho/c}$. Simultaneous best responses therefore require $a m_\rho=b m_\mu$ and $c m_\mu=-b m_\rho$, implying both means zero, and then both measures are $\delta_0$.

The transport velocities are $\dot z=b m_\mu-a z$ and $\dot x=-b m_\rho-c x$. The means solve the same linear coupled system. Its global solution and these affine scalar characteristic flows define global probability measures with finite second moments. Differentiating those moments is justified directly by the affine formulas, and gives, with $E(t)=\int z^2d\rho_t+\int x^2d\mu_t$,
\[
 E'(t)=2b m_\rho m_\mu-2a\int z^2d\rho_t-2b m_\rho m_\mu-2c\int x^2d\mu_t
 \le-2\min(a,c)E(t).
\]
Multiplying by $e^{2\min(a,c)t}$ and integrating proves the bound. The only coupling to a point mass sends every point to zero, so its squared Wasserstein distance is exactly the second moment.
\end{proof}
\subsection{Failed relaxations}
Removing the positive quadratic terms leaves the bilinear game $F=m_\rho m_\mu$. For Dirac strategies its flow is $z'=x,x'=-z$, whence $z(t)^2+x(t)^2$ is constant. Starting at $(1,0)$ gives $(\cos t,-\sin t)$, which does not converge despite the saddle $(0,0)$. Non-strict convex-concave structure alone cannot justify the previous contraction argument.

Unbounded strategy spaces alone do not even ensure existence: the maximizing payoff $\int\arctan z\,d\rho$ has supremum $\pi/2$ but no maximizer. Every probability measure has strictly smaller expectation because $\pi/2-\arctan z$ is everywhere positive. Dirac masses at increasing positive integers approach the supremum and all belong to $\mathcal P_2$. An irrelevant second player turns this into a zero-sum example.
