\section{Nash Equilibria over Unbounded Measure Spaces}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.


A strategy here is a probability measure on the designated action space. A Nash equilibrium is a strategy profile where no player improves its payoff by unilateral change. Gradient descent-ascent minimizes one variable and maximizes another in the stated zero-sum or saddle-point formulation.

For a differentiable loss $L$, gradient flow solves $\dot\theta=-\nabla L(\theta)$, while gradient descent updates $\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\eta_t\nabla L(\theta_t)$. Global optimality refers to the minimum over the whole admissible parameter space.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Establish general conditions for existence of Nash equilibria for games whose strategies are probability measures on unbounded sets. Under those conditions, determine whether gradient descent–ascent converges to an equilibrium, beyond the known displacement-convex–concave cases. \sref{statement}{2}
\paragraph{Scope qualification.}
The payoff regularity, moment restrictions, topology on measures, and exact gradient geometry must be supplied by the source; existence and convergence are separate targets.
