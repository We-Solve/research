\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(n,N\geq 1\) be integers, and let \(X_1,\ldots,X_n\) be independent random vectors in \(\R^N\), without an assumption that they are identically distributed. Each vector is assumed to be isotropic, meaning
\[
\E X_i=0,\qquad \E[X_iX_i^{\mathsf T}]=I_N,
\]
and log-concave, meaning that its distribution has a density \(f_i\) satisfying
\[
f_i((1-t)x+ty)\geq f_i(x)^{1-t}f_i(y)^t
\quad(x,y\in\R^N,\ 0<t<1).
\]
A vector \(X_i\) is unconditional if, for every \(\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^N\), the vectors \(X_i\) and \((\varepsilon_1X_{i1},\ldots,\varepsilon_NX_{iN})\) have the same distribution. Unconditionality is the hypothesis whose removal is being asked about.

Let \(A\in\R^{n\times N}\) have rows \(X_i^{\mathsf T}\). For integers \(1\leq k\leq n\) and \(1\leq m\leq N\), and index sets \(J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\), \(I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\}\) of sizes \(k\) and \(m\), respectively, let \(A(J,I)\) be the corresponding submatrix. Define
\[
A_{k,m}=\max_{\substack{J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\},\ |J|=k\\ I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,N\},\ |I|=m}}

\]
