\textbf{Hamiltonian-norm question.} If a \(G\)-invariant norm on normalized Hamiltonians induces a genuine bi-invariant distance on the Hamiltonian group \(G\), must it be equivalent to the oscillation norm \(\operatorname{osc}(F)=\max F-\min F\)? The source does not supply the symplectic manifold or analytic hypotheses on the norm.\par Under the usual closed connected symplectic convention, let \((M^{2m},\omega)\) be fixed, \(G=\operatorname{Ham}(M,\omega)\), and \(\mathcal H=\{F\in C^\infty(M,\R):\int_MF\,\omega^m=0\}\). Adjoint invariance means \(\nu(F\circ\phi^{-1})=\nu(F)\) for all \(\phi\in G\), \(F\in\mathcal H\). For a smooth normalized time-dependent Hamiltonian \(H_t\), define \(\ell_\nu(H)=\int_0^1\nu(H_t)\,dt\), whenever this integral is meaningful, and\[d_\nu(\phi,\psi)=\inf\{\ell_\nu(H):\phi_H^1=\phi^{-1}\psi\}.\] The genuine-distance hypothesis is \(d_\nu(\phi,\psi)>0\) for \(\phi\ne\psi\), in addition to the standard metric requirements. A nonzero norm on functions can still induce a degenerate path pseudodistance, so these conditions are distinct.\par The requested norm equivalence convention is the existence of constants \(0<c\le C<\infty\), depending on the norm and fixed manifold but not on \(F\), such that\[c\,\operatorname{osc}(F)\le\nu(F)\le C\,\operatorname{osc}(F)\quad\text{for every }F\in\mathcal H.\] It is not equality of norms, and merely asking whether the induced distances are equivalent would be a different conclusion. \subsection{Short English statement}
Does nondegeneracy of the path distance force a Hamiltonian-invariant function norm to satisfy uniform two-sided oscillation bounds? The norm may be nondegenerate even when its induced distance is not. The manifold and continuity assumptions are missing and affect the scope of the question.
