\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Problem 12 asks to calculate transport coefficients or related physical quantities for a variety of billiard systems. It does not specify tables, scatterers, horizon, boundary conditions, driving, invariant measure, observable or transport normalization. The aid gives a general correlation reduction and an exactly solvable persistent-flight surrogate; it does not identify that surrogate with an arbitrary billiard.

For a stationary centered square-integrable collision displacement \(\xi_j\in\R^p\), let \(S_n=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\xi_j\) and \(C_k=\E[\xi_0\xi_k^{\mathsf T}]\). If \(\sum_{k\ge1}\|C_k\|<\infty\), the aid's symmetrized Green--Kubo covariance statement is
\[
\frac1n\operatorname{Cov}(S_n)=C_0+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}(1-k/n)(C_k+C_k^{\mathsf T})\longrightarrow
\Sigma=C_0+\sum_{k\ge1}(C_k+C_k^{\mathsf T}).
\]
This summability statement alone is not a functional central limit theorem. To pass to physical time one additionally needs the stated functional-limit, clock/time-change, remainder and uniform-integrability hypotheses. In a precise conditional framework, if the rescaled collision process converges to Brownian motion of covariance \(\Sigma\), the collision clock has asymptotic rate \(1/\bar\tau\), the unfinished-flight remainder is negligible on the square-root-time scale, and squared normalized physical displacements are uniformly integrable, then physical covariance per unit time is \(\Sigma/\bar\tau\). With the convention \(\operatorname{Cov}(Y(t))\sim2tD\), the physical diffusion tensor is
\[
D=\frac{\Sigma}{2\bar\tau}.
\]
Thus the collision diffusion tensor \(\Sigma/2\) is divided by mean flight time. This explicit factor-two convention avoids conflating covariance tensors and diffusion tensors; applicability of the required hypotheses to a billiard must be proved separately.

The aid's scalar surrogate has stationary signs \(\epsilon_j\in\{-1,1\}\) with equal stationary probabilities and \(\Pr(\epsilon_{j+1}=\epsilon_j)=q\in(0,1)\). Let displacement per step be \(\ell\epsilon_j\), fixed flight length \(\ell>0\), and step time \(\tau>0\). With \(r=2q-1\in(-1,1)\),
\[
C_k=\ell^2r^k,\qquad D=\frac{\ell^2q}{2\tau(1-q)}.
\]
For \(D_n=\operatorname{Var}(S_n)/(2n\tau)\), the exact finite-time bias is
\[
D_n-D=-\frac{\ell^2r(1-r^n)}{\tau n(1-r)^2}.
\]
It is negative for persistent signs \(r>0\), positive for \(r<0\), and zero for \(r=0\). The one-lag ratio \(C_1/C_0=r\), together with \(\ell,\tau\), predicts the complete geometric correlation sequence and finite-time bias in this surrogate, rather than merely its limiting coefficient.

The aid further gives a Perron path-tilt response identity. Normalize the tilt by physical step displacement, so the transition-weight matrix is \(M_h(i,j)=P(i,j)e^{h\ell j}\), \(i,j\in\{-1,1\}\). If \(\lambda(h)\) is its Perron eigenvalue and the associated normalized tilted chain is used, its stationary velocity is \(v(h)=\tau^{-1}(\log\lambda)'(h)\). Then the reported identity is
\[
v'(0)=2D.
\]
Only if a physical forcing convention supplies \(h=\beta F/2\) does this imply the Einstein relation \((\dd v/\dd F)(0)=\beta D\). The matching of field \(F\) to the tilt is an extra hypothesis, not an automatic property of a billiard model.

Finally, for centered scalar currents with positive correlation tail \(C_k\sim c/k\), \(c>0\), the same finite-time covariance sum gives
\[
D_n\sim(c/\tau)\log n.
\]
Thus the usual finite diffusion coefficient fails in that diagnostic regime; summable-correlations formulas for a finite limit cannot be applied there.
