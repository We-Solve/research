\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 700003}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(G\) be a group with identity \(e\). The group \(G\) is torsion-free if, for every \(g\in G\) and every integer \(n\geq 1\), the equality \(g^n=e\) implies \(g=e\). Its integral group ring is
\[
\Z[G]=\left\{\sum_{g\in G}a_g g : a_g\in\Z,\ a_g=0\text{ for all but finitely many }g\right\},
\]
with coefficientwise addition and multiplication determined by the group multiplication and distributivity:
\[
\left(\sum_g a_g g\right)\left(\sum_h b_h h\right)
=\sum_{k\in G}\left(\sum_{gh=k}a_gb_h\right)k.
\]
A zero-divisor pair consists of nonzero elements \(a,b\in\Z[G]\) satisfying \(ab=0\).

\textbf{Problem (Kourovka Notebook 1.3).} Does there exist a torsion-free group \(G\) whose integral group ring has a zero-divisor pair? Equivalently, is it true that, for every torsion-free group \(G\) and all \(a,b\in\Z[G]\),
\[
ab=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad a=0\ \text{or}\ b=0?
\]
No finiteness, finite-generation, or commutativity assumption on \(G\) is imposed. The source labels this a well-known problem and records its status as open; that status is supplied metadata, not an independently verified literature assessment.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can two nonzero finite integer linear combinations of elements of a torsion-free group have product zero in its group ring? The question ranges over all torsion-free groups and is attributed in the supplied record to L. A. Bokut'.
