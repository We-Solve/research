\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Use the standard complex Kleinian setting: a Kleinian group is a discrete subgroup of $\operatorname{PSL}_2(\C)$ acting on $\mathbb H^3$ and its sphere at infinity $\widehat{\C}$. Its limit set $\Lambda(\Gamma)$ is the set of accumulation points on $\widehat{\C}$ of an orbit in $\mathbb H^3$. Write $\dim_H\Lambda(\Gamma)$ for its Hausdorff dimension in the spherical metric.

A Schottky group here means a finite-rank free convex-cocompact Kleinian group. It is \emph{classical} if it admits a Schottky fundamental domain bounded by pairwise disjoint round circles on $\widehat{\C}$ paired by a free generating set. It is \emph{non-classical} if no such circular fundamental-domain presentation exists; non-classicality is not merely the failure of a particular chosen presentation.

Ed Taylor's question is whether
\[
\exists c>0\ \forall\Gamma:\quad
\bigl(\Gamma\text{ is a non-classical Schottky group}\bigr)
\Longrightarrow \dim_H\Lambda(\Gamma)\geq c.
\]
The constant is independent of the group and its rank. The supplied research summary reports an affirmative answer with optimal constant $c=1$, in particular
\[
\dim_H\Lambda(\Gamma)\geq1
\quad\text{for every non-classical Schottky group }\Gamma.
\]
It cites sufficiently-small-dimension classicality theorems of Hou, first for two generators and then in general, and a 2023 sharp classification. Its assertion of optimality would also require that no constant $c>1$ works; the supplied summary does not reproduce the examples or precise sharpness statement establishing that additional assertion. This draft records the literature claim without supplying a new proof.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Taylor asks whether all non-classical Schottky limit sets have a common positive lower bound on Hausdorff dimension. The supplied summary reports the bound $1$ and calls it optimal, following small-dimension classicality results and a later sharp classification.
