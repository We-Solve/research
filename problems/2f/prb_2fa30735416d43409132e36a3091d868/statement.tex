\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(C_n=\Z/n\Z\) and let \(\Z\) denote the infinite cyclic group. Define
\[
H=(C_3\times\Z)*(C_2\times\Z)
 =\langle x,y,z,t\mid x^3=z^2=[x,y]=[z,t]=1\rangle,
\]
where \(*\) denotes the free product and \([a,b]=a^{-1}b^{-1}ab\). The first direct factor is generated by \(x,y\), and the second by \(z,t\).

Let \(F=F(x,y,z,t)\) be the free group on these four symbols, and put
\[
N=\langle\!\langle x^3,z^2,[x,y],[z,t]\rangle\!\rangle_F,
\]
where \(\langle\!\langle S\rangle\!\rangle_F\) denotes the smallest normal subgroup of \(F\) containing \(S\). Thus \(H=F/N\), with its specified generating tuple.

The question is whether
\[
\forall r_1,r_2,r_3\in N,\qquad
\langle\!\langle r_1,r_2,r_3\rangle\!\rangle_F\ne N.
\]
Equivalently, does the kernel of this specified presentation require exactly four normal generators, so that no presentation on the specified generators \(x,y,z,t\) uses at most three defining relators?

The relation module associated with this presentation is \(M=N/[N,N]\), regarded as a left \(\Z H\)-module through conjugation: if \(h=fN\) and \(n\in N\), then
\[
h\cdot(n[N,N])=fnf^{-1}[N,N].
\]
The supplied background reports that this relation module has rank \(3\), citing K. W. Gruenberg and P. A. Linnell, and states that an affirmative answer would solve the relation gap problem. These are supplied background claims. The record does not define its convention for module rank; if it means the minimum number of \(\Z H\)-module generators, the reported claim is that this minimum is \(3\).

The formulation above interprets the question as concerning the displayed generating tuple. The source does not explicitly specify whether presentations on other generating four-tuples are also intended.
\subsection{Short English statement}
For the displayed generators of \(H=(C_3\times\Z)*(C_2\times\Z)\), must every presentation use at least four relators?
