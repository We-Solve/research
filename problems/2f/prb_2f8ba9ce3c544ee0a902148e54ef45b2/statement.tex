\section{Inference and Training Effects of the Softmax Bottleneck}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Standard number systems are $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$. Specialized notation and index ranges follow the cited source and the definitions below.


For logits $z_i$, softmax gives $p_i=e^{z_i}/\sum_j e^{z_j}$. Nucleus sampling restricts sampling to a chosen high-probability prefix. A softmax bottleneck constrains distributions representable through a low-dimensional output projection.

\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
When nucleus sampling is used at inference time, do the probabilities of tokens outside the top one thousand have negligible effects on performance? Does the softmax bottleneck materially limit performance during training even when it does not significantly restrict the probabilities of the most likely tokens at inference time? \sref{statement}{2}
