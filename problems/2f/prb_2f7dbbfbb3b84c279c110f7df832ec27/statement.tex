\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 30000116}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $(A_0,A_1)$ and $(B_0,B_1)$ be compatible couples of complex Banach spaces: each pair is continuously embedded in a common Hausdorff topological vector space so its intersection and sum are defined. Let $T:A_0+A_1\to B_0+B_1$ be one linear operator with
\[
 T(A_j)\subseteq B_j,\qquad T|_{A_j}:A_j\to B_j
 \text{ compact},\qquad j=0,1.
\]
Thus the endpoint restrictions agree on $A_0\cap A_1$. Compact means that the image of the endpoint unit ball has compact closure in the corresponding $B_j$ norm.

For $0<\theta<1$, $[A_0,A_1]_\theta$ denotes the Calder\'on complex interpolation space: its elements are values $F(\theta)$ of bounded strip-analytic functions on $0<\operatorname{Re}z<1$, continuous on the closed strip into $A_0+A_1$, with boundary values $F(j+it)$ in $A_j$ and the usual bounded continuous, vanishing-at-infinity boundary conditions. Its norm is the infimum of
\[
 \max_{j=0,1}\sup_{t\in\R}\|F(j+it)\|_{A_j}
\]
over admissible $F$ with the given value at $\theta$; define the $B$ interpolation space analogously.

The question is whether, for \emph{every} such pair of couples and compatible operator,
\[
 T:[A_0,A_1]_\theta\longrightarrow[B_0,B_1]_\theta
 \quad\text{is compact for every }\theta\in(0,1).
\]
Equivalently, every bounded sequence in the interpolated domain should have a subsequence whose $T$-images converge in the interpolated target norm. Compactness, not merely boundedness furnished by interpolation, is the requested conclusion. Both endpoint compactness assumptions are retained; no Hilbert, lattice, approximation, or dense-intersection hypothesis is added.

The supplied literature assessment checked 21 August 2026 describes this both-endpoints-compact variant as unresolved, with special cases and partial interpolation theorems. Its phrase ``stronger variant requiring compactness at both endpoints'' is retained as a description of stronger \emph{hypotheses}; the present assertion is distinct from the more general problem assuming compactness at only one endpoint and boundedness at the other. No independent current-status determination is made.
\subsection{Short English statement}
If the same linear operator is compact at both endpoints of two Banach couples, must it remain compact at every complex interpolation parameter? Both endpoint assumptions and the full range $0<\theta<1$ are retained. The supplied dated assessment reports this variant unresolved in general.
