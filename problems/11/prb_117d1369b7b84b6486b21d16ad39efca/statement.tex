\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 701450}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A \emph{variety of groups} is the class of all groups satisfying a specified set of group identities. A group is \emph{\(d\)-generated} if it has a generating tuple of length \(d\), allowing repetitions and identity elements; equivalently, it can be generated by at most \(d\) elements. A group is \emph{locally finite} if each of its finitely generated subgroups is finite. A variety is locally finite if every group belonging to it is locally finite.

\textbf{Question.} For every integer \(d\geq 2\) and every variety of groups \(\mathfrak{V}\), does the hypothesis
\[
\forall G\in\mathfrak{V},\qquad
\bigl(\exists g_1,\ldots,g_d\in G:\ G=\langle g_1,\ldots,g_d\rangle\bigr)
\Longrightarrow |G|<\infty
\]
imply that \(\mathfrak{V}\) is locally finite, that is,
\[
\forall H\in\mathfrak{V}\ \forall n\in\Z_{\geq 1}\ \forall h_1,\ldots,h_n\in H,
\qquad |\langle h_1,\ldots,h_n\rangle|<\infty?
\]
The hypothesis requires each such group to be finite; it does not specify a common bound on their orders.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Fix an integer \(d\geq 2\). If every group in a variety that can be generated by at most \(d\) elements is finite, must every finitely generated subgroup of every group in that variety be finite?
