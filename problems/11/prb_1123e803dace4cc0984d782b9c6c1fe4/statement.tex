\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Fix $n\ge1$ and $R=\C[[x_1,\ldots,x_n]]$ with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$. Musta\c t\u a asks whether for every $c\in\R$ there is a truncation level $k=k(n,c)$ such that membership in
\[
\{f\in R:\operatorname{lct}_0(f)\ge c\}
\]
depends only on the $k$th truncation. Explicitly, with degree-$k$ jets defined modulo $\mathfrak m^{k+1}$, ask that $f\equiv h\pmod{\mathfrak m^{k+1}}$ imply $\operatorname{lct}_0(f)\ge c$ if and only if $\operatorname{lct}_0(h)\ge c$. Here the local log canonical threshold is the infimum of log-discrepancy-to-vanishing-order ratios for divisorial valuations relevant at the origin. Use $\operatorname{lct}_0(0)=0$ and $\operatorname{lct}_0(u)=+\infty$ for units; nonzero nonunits have threshold in $(0,1]$.

The weaker question is
\[
\forall f\in R\ \exists k(f)\ \forall g\in\mathfrak m^{k(f)}:\quad
\operatorname{lct}_0(f+g)\ge\operatorname{lct}_0(f).
\]
The source connects the first assertion to the ascending chain condition (ACC) for thresholds on smooth $n$-folds, namely absence of an infinite strictly increasing threshold sequence. Its printed $k(c)$ suppresses the fixed dimension; the jet-degree and perturbation-order conventions differ by one.

The attached unreviewed aid reports affirmative answers from the de Fernex--Musta\c t\u a finite-cylinder equivalence and the proved smooth ACC theorem. For $0<c\le1$, let $J_n(c)$ be the least deciding jet degree, and let $\delta_n(c)$ be the gap between $c$ and the largest threshold below $c$ in the $n$-dimensional hypersurface spectrum, including zero under the displayed convention. It proposes
\[
n\left\lfloor1/c\right\rfloor\le J_n(c)
\le\left\lfloor n/\delta_n(c)\right\rfloor,
\]
with the lower degree sharp for pure-monomial testing and in dimension one. For a nonzero nonunit $f$, it gives
\[
k(f)=\left\lfloor n/\delta_n(\operatorname{lct}_0(f))\right\rfloor+1.
\]
The quantitative formula is not applied to threshold zero or infinity. The cases $c\le0$, $c>1$, zero $f$ and unit $f$ require their stated elementary conventions. The precise spectrum, gap theorem and quantitative proof are not supplied; no numerical gap or new ACC proof is asserted here.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Ask whether bounded jets decide a threshold inequality uniformly in fixed dimension, and whether sufficiently high-order perturbations can only preserve or increase a given threshold. The aid reports affirmative literature results and a quantitative bound in terms of a spectral gap.
