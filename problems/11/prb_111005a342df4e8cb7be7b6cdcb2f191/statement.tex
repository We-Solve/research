\section{Well-Ordered Linearizations}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Work over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$, the subsystem of second-order arithmetic with $\Delta^0_1$ comprehension and $\Sigma^0_1$ induction; all structures and orders are countable and coded by sets of integers. $\mathsf{ACA}_0$ adds comprehension for every arithmetical formula (set parameters allowed), and $\mathsf{ATR}_0$ adds arithmetical transfinite recursion along every coded well-order. Reverse-mathematical equivalence means provability of both implications over this base. A partial order $(P,\le_P)$ is well-founded if it has no infinite strictly descending sequence. A linearization is a linear order on the same underlying set extending $\le_P$. Let $\mathsf{EXT}(\omega^*)$ assert that every well-founded partial order has a well-founded linearization.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Is $\mathsf{EXT}(\omega^*)$ equivalent to $\mathsf{ACA}_0$ over $\mathsf{RCA}_0$? \sref{statement}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does guaranteeing a well-ordered extension of every well-founded countable partial order require exactly arithmetical comprehension?
