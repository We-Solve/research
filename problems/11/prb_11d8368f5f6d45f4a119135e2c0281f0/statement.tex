\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
The source asks for a classification of affinely equivalent Finsler metrics on a common underlying manifold \(M\). It does not explicitly specify the dimension, boundary conventions, regularity class, or convexity assumptions; these qualifications remain subject to review.

Under the usual smooth, strongly convex interpretation, a Finsler metric is a function \(F:TM\to[0,\infty)\) that is continuous on \(TM\), smooth away from the zero section, positive on nonzero tangent vectors, and satisfies
\[
F(x,\lambda v)=\lambda F(x,v)\qquad(\lambda>0),
\]
with positive-definite fundamental tensor
\[
g^F_{ij}(x,v)=\frac12\frac{\partial^2(F^2)}{\partial v^i\partial v^j}(x,v),\qquad v\ne0.
\]
This convention does not require reversibility \(F(x,-v)=F(x,v)\); the supplied source does not explicitly settle whether any additional restrictions are intended.

A nonconstant affinely parametrized \(F\)-geodesic is a curve \(\gamma:I\to M\), with \(I\subset\R\) an open interval, satisfying the Euler--Lagrange equations for \(L_F=\tfrac12F^2\):
\[
\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}\frac{\partial L_F}{\partial v^i}(\gamma(t),\dot\gamma(t))
-\frac{\partial L_F}{\partial x^i}(\gamma(t),\dot\gamma(t))=0.
\]
Its speed \(F(\gamma(t),\dot\gamma(t))\) is constant. If manifolds with boundary are intended, the treatment of geodesics at the boundary is unspecified.

Following the source, two Finsler metrics \(F\) and \(\widetilde F\) on \(M\) are called \emph{affinely equivalent} if every \(F\)-geodesic, parametrized with constant \(F\)-speed, is a \(\widetilde F\)-geodesic with the same parameter. Thus the condition concerns parametrized curves, without changing their parameters. In local spray notation, where the geodesic equations are
\[
\ddot x^i+2G_F^i(x,\dot x)=0,
\]
this is expressed by
\[
G_F^i(x,v)=G_{\widetilde F}^i(x,v)
\quad\text{for every }(x,v)\in TM\setminus\{0\}\text{ and every }i.
\]
The source states that affine equivalence is equivalent to coincidence of the Berwald connections in the natural sense. It also cautions that Finsler connections are nonlinear and that several different connections are commonly used; the named connection here is the Berwald connection.

\textbf{Problem.} Describe all pairs \((F,\widetilde F)\) of affinely equivalent Finsler metrics on a common manifold \(M\). Equivalently, for a given \(F\), describe all Finsler metrics \(\widetilde F\) having the same parametrized geodesics. The source does not prescribe a classification format or specify classification modulo diffeomorphisms, and the conventions identified above require confirmation.
