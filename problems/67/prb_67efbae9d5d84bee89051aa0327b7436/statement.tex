\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Use the usual category of closed, connected, oriented smooth \(3\)-manifolds. A rational homology \(3\)-sphere is such a manifold \(W\) satisfying
\[
H_i(W;\Q)\cong H_i(S^3;\Q)\qquad\text{for every }i\geq 0.
\]
It is irreducible if every smoothly embedded \(2\)-sphere in \(W\) bounds an embedded \(3\)-ball. Its group \(H_1(W;\Z)\) is finite; write \(\lvert H_1(W;\Z)\rvert\) for its order.

For an explicit standard coefficient convention in this draft, put \(\F_2=\Z/2\Z\). An L-space is a rational homology \(3\)-sphere satisfying
\[
\dim_{\F_2}\widehat{HF}(W;\F_2)=\lvert H_1(W;\Z)\rvert,
\]
where \(\widehat{HF}\) denotes hat Heegaard Floer homology. The supplied record does not specify coefficients; this convention requires confirmation against the cited source.

A group \(G\) is left-orderable if it admits a strict total order \(<\) such that
\[
\forall g,a,b\in G,\qquad a<b\Longrightarrow ga<gb.
\]
Under this definition the trivial group is left-orderable.

The supplied conjecture asserts that, for every irreducible rational homology \(3\)-sphere \(W\),
\[
W\text{ is not an L-space}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\pi_1(W)\text{ is left-orderable}.
\]
There is an unresolved boundary qualification: the stated hypotheses include \(S^3\), which is an L-space and has trivial fundamental group. Thus the displayed assertion is incompatible with the definition of left-orderability above at \(S^3\). A consistent intended formulation needs an explicit qualification, such as excluding \(S^3\), or using a convention that requires a left-orderable group to be nontrivial. The supplied record does not specify either qualification, so neither is silently imposed here.
\subsection{Short English statement}
The supplied conjecture says that an irreducible rational homology \(3\)-sphere fails to be an L-space exactly when its fundamental group admits a left-invariant total order. The treatment of \(S^3\) and the coefficient convention for Heegaard Floer homology require clarification before this becomes a fully resolved formulation.
