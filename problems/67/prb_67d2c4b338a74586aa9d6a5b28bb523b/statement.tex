\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $\Sigma_g$ be the genus-$g$ surface in the source convention, and let $\operatorname{Homeo}(\Sigma_g)$ be its homeomorphism group. An action of a discrete group $G$ by homeomorphisms is a homomorphism $\rho:G\to\operatorname{Homeo}(\Sigma_g)$; it is faithful if $\ker\rho=\{1\}$.

Question 6.7 asks whether $\operatorname{Mod}_g$ has such a faithful action on $\Sigma_g$, and whether a finite-index subgroup can have one. The latter existence formulation is
\[
 \exists\Gamma\leq\operatorname{Mod}_g,\quad
 [\operatorname{Mod}_g:\Gamma]<\infty,\quad
 \exists\rho:\Gamma\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Homeo}(\Sigma_g).
\]
The full-group case is the special case $\Gamma=\operatorname{Mod}_g$. The phrase ``any of its finite index subgroups'' is not silently strengthened to a demand for every finite-index subgroup.

No requirement is imposed that the induced mapping-class representation be the standard inclusion. In particular, this faithful-action question is distinct from asking for a section of the natural map $\operatorname{Homeo}^+(\Sigma_g)\to\operatorname{Mod}_g$. The excerpt does not specify a genus range, whether the surface is closed, or whether actions must preserve orientation; these conventions must be fixed before asserting an answer.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can the genus-$g$ mapping class group, or some finite-index subgroup, embed in the homeomorphism group of the same surface? The action is not required to realize the usual mapping classes.
