\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(G\) be an arbitrary countable discrete group. No residual finiteness assumption is imposed. Write \(\Q G\) for the rational group algebra, whose elements are finite sums \(\sum_{g\in G}a_g g\) with \(a_g\in\Q\). Let \(\ell^2(G)\) be the Hilbert space of square-summable complex-valued functions on \(G\), and let \(\lambda(g)\) act by left translation:
\[
(\lambda(g)\xi)(h)=\xi(g^{-1}h).
\]
Let \(\mathcal N(G)\) be the group von Neumann algebra generated by these operators, with canonical trace
\[
\tau_G(T)=\langle T\delta_e,\delta_e\rangle,
\]
where \(e\) is the identity of \(G\).

For positive integers \(m,n\) and \(A=(a_{ij})\in M_{m,n}(\Q G)\), let
\[
T_A:\ell^2(G)^n\longrightarrow\ell^2(G)^m,
\qquad
(T_A\xi)_i=\sum_{j=1}^n\lambda(a_{ij})\xi_j.
\]
Its kernel is closed. If \(P_A\in M_n(\mathcal N(G))\) is the orthogonal projection onto this kernel, define its von Neumann dimension using the unnormalized matrix trace:
\[
\dim_{\mathcal N(G)}\ker T_A
:=\sum_{j=1}^n\tau_G\bigl((P_A)_{jj}\bigr).
\]
Thus the ambient space \(\ell^2(G)^n\) has dimension \(n\). Define
\[
C(G):=\left\{
\dim_{\mathcal N(G)}\ker T_A:
 m,n\geq 1,\ A\in M_{m,n}(\Q G)
\right\}\subseteq\R_{\geq0}.
\]
Here the source's phrase ``\(\ell^2\)-Betti numbers'' refers to these matrix-kernel dimensions. Using square matrices gives the same set, since \(A^*A\in M_n(\Q G)\) and \(\ker T_A=\ker T_{A^*A}\).

\textbf{Problem.} For each fixed countable discrete group \(G\), determine the set \(C(G)\).
