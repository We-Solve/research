\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a full-rank Euclidean lattice $L=\sum_{i=1}^n\Z b_i\subset\R^n$, let $\lambda_1(L)=\min\{\|v\|:0\ne v\in L\}$. An LLL-reduced basis with parameter $\delta\in(1/4,1)$ satisfies the standard size-reduction and Lov\'asz conditions. Writing $a=(\delta-1/4)^{-1}$, a usual shortest-vector approximation guarantee is
\[
\|b_1\|\leq a^{(n-1)/2}\lambda_1(L).
\]
For $\delta=3/4$ this gives $2^{(n-1)/2}$. A determinant-based Hermite bound is a different performance measure and must not be substituted for this ratio without specifying the target.

The source asks for cyclic or ideal lattices on which LLL achieves its worst-case approximation factor. One conventional cyclic class consists of full-rank $L\subseteq\Z^n$ invariant under cyclic coordinate shift. Ideal lattices require an ideal in a specified order/number field or polynomial quotient ring and an embedding/norm; coefficient and canonical Euclidean embeddings need not yield the same geometry. The source does not choose these conventions.

A precise exhibit would give an infinite dimension-indexed family, its structural proof, and input bases/LLL parameters showing the specified output approximation ratio. The existence of a badly approximating reduced basis and the output of a fixed implementation on a prescribed input are related but distinct tasks. The record does not say whether worst-case means exact equality, arbitrarily close to the guarantee, or matching its asymptotic exponential scale, nor whether cyclic and ideal examples are separately required or either suffices. No general-lattice example is automatically an ideal example without proving the ring structure.
