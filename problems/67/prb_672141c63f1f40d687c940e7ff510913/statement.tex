\section{Carmichael's totient function conjecture}\label{9400002}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 9400002 · Number Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Euler's totient is $\varphi(n)=\#\{a\in\mathbb N:1\le a\le n,\ \gcd(a,n)=1\}$ for $n\in\mathbb N$. For $m\in\mathbb N$, its multiplicity is $A(m)=\#\{n\in\mathbb N:\varphi(n)=m\}$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Is $A(m)\ge2$ for every $m\in\varphi(\mathbb N)$? Equivalently, for every $n\in\mathbb N$, does there exist $n'\in\mathbb N$, $n'\ne n$, with $\varphi(n')=\varphi(n)$? \sref{9400002}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every totient value come from at least two distinct positive integers?
