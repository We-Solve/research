\section{Perturbation theory for exactly solvable combinatorial problems}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


\paragraph{Definitions and notation.}Exactly solvable probabilistic models admit algebraic formulae for their distributions. In last-passage percolation, geometric or exponential site-weight laws lead to tractable formulae. A perturbation changes the weight distribution; a universality result would show persistence of a normalized fluctuation limit for a declared class of such changes.

\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}
The asymptotic behavior of a variety of combinatorial problems has now been analyzed in great detail. Here we have in mind Ulam's problem for the length of the longest increasing subsequence, the tiling problem for the Aztec diamond, the hexagon tiling problem, and the last passage percolation problem, amongst many others. In all cases, in an appropriate scaling limit, the statistical fluctuations in the systems at hand are described by random matrix theory. The asymptotic analysis, however, depends in a critical, and rigid way, on the underlying probability measures for the systems. For example, the analysis of the last passage percolation problem requires the waiting time $w_i$ at each site $i$ to be either geometrically or exponentially distributed (Johansson): if the statistics of the waiting times is neither geometric nor exponential, the analysis fails completely. What happens if the geometric distribution, say, is slightly perturbed? The challenge here is to develop an effective perturbation theory for such systems. One expects that the random matrix behavior of the fluctuations should persist. Related work on this problem has been done by Baik-Suidan and Bouchard-Martin (cf. Problem 10).

\paragraph{A coupling estimate, retaining the source-solved label.}The catalogue marks this historical programme solved; the present calculation neither audits that settlement nor reopens it. For \(n\times n\) directed last-passage arrays \(w,\widetilde w\), let \(D_n=\max_{ij}|w_{ij}-\widetilde w_{ij}|\). Every admissible path has \(2n-1\) sites, hence
\[|L_n(w)-L_n(\widetilde w)|\le(2n-1)D_n.
\]
For a deterministic scale \(a_n>0\), if \(nD_n/a_n\to0\) in probability and \((L_n(w)-b_n)/a_n\) has a limiting distribution \(F\), then the normalized perturbed passage time has the same limit at every continuity point of \(F\). Indeed, for each \(\eta>0\), its distribution function is trapped between the unperturbed ones at \(x-\eta\) and \(x+\eta\), up to the probability that \((2n-1)D_n/a_n>\eta\). Let \(n\to\infty\), then \(\eta\downarrow0\).
\paragraph{An explicit vanishing-perturbation regime.}If each perturbed weight is \(\widetilde w_{ij}=w_{ij}+\delta_n\xi_{ij}\), with \(|\xi_{ij}|\le1\) almost surely, then \(D_n\le\delta_n\). At scale \(a_n=n^{1/3}\), the transfer works for \(\delta_n=o(n^{-2/3})\). Independence of the perturbations is unnecessary for this deterministic comparison. A constant shift can instead be absorbed exactly into \(b_n\), since it adds \((2n-1)\delta\) to every path.
\paragraph{First gap.}For a fixed nontrivial change of the weight law, the crude comparison is order \(n\), much larger than the fluctuation scale. Proving cancellation or a new centering does not follow from a sup-norm bound. Thus this elementary conditional transfer lemma explains a limited stability window but does not reconstruct the full effective perturbation theory or establish universality for fixed perturbed geometric/exponential laws. The inherited solved status is preserved separately from this unresolved attempt's limited scope.
