\textbf{Finite-attainment problem.} For an energy density \(W\), find readily checkable sufficient conditions under which its rank-one convex envelope \(W^{\mathrm{rc}}\) is attained by a finite-order laminate; bound the required order \(N\) in terms of the specified energy data and control the radius containing all matrices in the construction. The curated wording asks for a finite prescribed radius, while the original asks for a bound on the radius; the intended dependence and quantifiers need to be fixed.\par In a usual matrix-space formulation, let \(W:\R^{m\times n}\to\R\cup\{+\infty\}\). A function \(h\) is rank-one convex if \(h(\theta A+(1-\theta)B)\le\theta h(A)+(1-\theta)h(B)\) whenever \(0\le\theta\le1\) and \(\operatorname{rank}(A-B)\le1\), with the intended domain and extended-value conventions. The envelope is the greatest such minorant, equivalently \(W^{\mathrm{rc}}(F)=\sup\{h(F):h\le W,\ h\text{ rank-one convex}\}\) when this convention is well defined. It is not the quasiconvex or polyconvex envelope.\par A finite laminate at barycenter \(F\) is a finitely supported probability measure \(\nu=\sum_{i=1}^L\theta_i\delta_{F_i}\) obtainable from \(\delta_F\) by finitely many binary replacements of an atom at \(C\) by atoms at \(A,B\), where \(C=\theta A+(1-\theta)B\) and \(\operatorname{rank}(A-B)\le1\). Its barycenter satisfies \(\sum_i\theta_iF_i=F\). Attainment at a target matrix means\[W^{\mathrm{rc}}(F)=\sum_{i=1}^L\theta_iW(F_i),\] not merely convergence of such averages along laminates of increasing complexity. An order bound must specify whether \(N\) counts tree depth, total splitting steps, or another convention; these are not identical.\par The requested quantitative result should specify the target matrices and whether \(N\) is uniform over them, the admissible hypotheses on \(W\), and the permitted dependence of a radius bound. If a ball \(\overline B_R(0)\) is prescribed for the support, necessarily \(F\in\overline B_R(0)\), since it is a convex barycenter; intermediate splitting matrices then also lie in that ball. A single finite radius cannot contain supports for every barycenter in the whole unbounded matrix space. The source does not fix matrix dimensions, norm and ball center, energy regularity or coercivity, determinant or other constraints, or the domain on which uniform attainment is sought.
