\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(\N_{>0}=\{1,2,3,\ldots\}\). The von Mangoldt function is
\[
\Lambda(n)=\begin{cases}
\log p,&n=p^k\text{ for a prime }p\text{ and an integer }k\geq 1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\]
where \(\log\) denotes the natural logarithm; in particular, \(\Lambda(1)=0\).

In the standard normalized convention, a function \(h\) on \(\N_{>0}\) is multiplicative if \(h(1)=1\) and \(h(ab)=h(a)h(b)\) whenever \(a,b\in\N_{>0}\) satisfy \(\gcd(a,b)=1\). This does not require complete multiplicativity.

The supplied question has the following formulation once a scalar field \(K\) is specified. Do there exist functions
\[
F:\N_{>0}\longrightarrow K,
\qquad
G:\N_{>0}\times\N_{>0}\longrightarrow K
\]
such that \(F\) is multiplicative, and, for every \(n\in\N_{>0}\),
\[
G(1,n)=1,\qquad
G(ab,n)=G(a,n)G(b,n)
\quad\text{whenever }\gcd(a,b)=1,
\]
\[
\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}|G(q,n)|<\infty,
\qquad
\Lambda(n)=F(n)\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}G(q,n)?
\]
