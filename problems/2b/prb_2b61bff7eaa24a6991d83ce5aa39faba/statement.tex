\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For readability let $\mathcal S_n(Y;g)$ denote the source's invariant printed as $\operatorname{Sc}^{[n]}_{g\cap}(Y)$, for $Y\subset W$ with ambient metric $g$. This is only a name for that source invariant, whose full definition is absent. The supplied discussion involves domains $V_\varepsilon\subset W$ approximating $Y$, with their boundaries carrying scalar-curvature lower bounds tending to $+\infty$ and satisfying ambient isometric-embedding requirements; the exact approximation and quantifiers are not given.

The Cartesian-product stabilization proposal is
\[
\mathcal S_n(Y;g)=+\infty\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathcal S_{n+k}(Y\times X^k;g\oplus g_k)=+\infty,
\]
where $(X^k,g_k)$ is a compact Riemannian $k$-manifold and $Y\times X^k\subset W\times X^k$ carries the indicated product geometry. The source qualifies the two directions separately. It calls the forward implication obvious for compact factors without boundary and also for complete noncompact factors with scalar curvature bounded below, especially $\R^k$. It only suggests possible extension to compact factors with boundary. For the reverse implication it says failure is probable for $n=2$, possible for $n=3,4$, and validity plausible for $n\geq5$. Thus the displayed equivalence must not be promoted to an unqualified conjecture in every dimension or boundary category.

The extract also proposes replacing $C^2$ boundaries $\partial V_\varepsilon$ by $C^1$ boundaries with $C^2$ induced metrics, invoking Nash--Kuiper isometric embeddings to address ambient embeddability. It then suggests continuous induced metrics but explicitly says defining/extending $\operatorname{Sc}\geq\sigma$ for them is nontrivial. No such extension or embedding theorem with unprovided hypotheses is asserted here.

A further appended programme defines a local volume comparison for metric-measure spaces. Its printed condition compares
\[
\operatorname{vol}(B_x(\varepsilon))<\operatorname{vol}'(B_{x'}(\varepsilon))
\]
for all $x,x'$ with a continuously chosen positive radius $\varepsilon=\varepsilon(x,x')$, denoted $X\prec_{\mathrm{vol}}X'$. It says all small balls are compared, but does not specify whether the inequality must hold for every radius below that value or only the chosen one. It then claims Cartesian additivity:
\[
X\prec_{\mathrm{vol}}X',\quad Y\prec_{\mathrm{vol}}Y'
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad X\times Y\prec_{\mathrm{vol}}X'\times Y',
\]
with Pythagorean product metrics. The product-volume formula is printed using $\operatorname{vol}_X\oplus\operatorname{vol}_Y$; the operation is undefined and is not silently equated with product measure. This ancillary discussion is not a definition or proof of the stabilization invariant.
