\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(G\) be a group admitting a finite presentation with one defining relation, meaning
\[
G\cong \langle x_1,\ldots,x_d\mid r=1\rangle
=F(x_1,\ldots,x_d)/\langle\!\langle r\rangle\!\rangle,
\]
where \(r\) is a word in the free group on the displayed generators and \(\langle\!\langle r\rangle\!\rangle\) denotes its normal closure. Let \(S\subseteq G\) be a finite generating set. For \(g\in G\), define its word length \(\ell_S(g)\) to be the least number of letters from \(S\cup S^{-1}\) whose product is \(g\), with \(\ell_S(1)=0\). Define the spherical and ball growth series by
\[
A_{G,S}(t)=\sum_{n\geq0}\bigl|\{g\in G:\ell_S(g)=n\}\bigr|t^n,
\qquad
B_{G,S}(t)=\sum_{n\geq0}\bigl|\{g\in G:\ell_S(g)\leq n\}\bigr|t^n.
\]
These are formal power series in \(\Z[[t]]\), and
\[
B_{G,S}(t)=\frac{A_{G,S}(t)}{1-t}.
\]
Consequently, algebraicity over \(\Q(t)\), and likewise rationality over \(\Q(t)\), is equivalent for these two conventions. A series \(H(t)\) is algebraic here if there is a nonzero polynomial \(P(t,X)\in\Q[t,X]\) such that \(P(t,H(t))=0\); it is rational if \(H(t)=p(t)/q(t)\) for polynomials \(p,q\in\Q[t]\) with \(q(0)\neq0\).

The supplied question asks whether the growth series of every finitely generated group with one defining relation is algebraic, and asks the stronger question whether it is always rational. The generating-set quantifier is unresolved in the supplied record.
