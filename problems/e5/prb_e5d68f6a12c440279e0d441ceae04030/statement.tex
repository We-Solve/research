\section{Are all Euclid numbers square-free}\label{9400138}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 9400138 · Number Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $p_1=2<p_2=3<\cdots$ enumerate primes and $P_n=\prod_{j=1}^{n}p_j$ for $n\ge1$. Put $E_n=P_n+1$, the $n$th Euclid number. A positive integer is squarefree when no prime square divides it.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Is $q^2\nmid E_n$ for every $n\ge1$ and every prime $q$? \sref{9400138}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every primorial plus one have no repeated prime factor?
