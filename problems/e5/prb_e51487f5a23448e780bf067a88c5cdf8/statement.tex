\section{Do any Taxicab(5, 2, n) exist for n > 1}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For positive integers $k,j,n$, $\operatorname{Taxicab}(k,j,n)$ denotes the least positive integer, if any, expressible as a sum of $j$ positive integer $k$th powers in $n$ distinct ways, counting permutations of summands as the same way. For two fifth powers, sort each pair: $1\le a\le b$, $1\le c\le d$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Does there exist any $\operatorname{Taxicab}(5,2,n)$ with $n>1$? Equivalently, do there exist positive integers $a\le b$, $c\le d$ with $(a,b)\ne(c,d)$ and $a^5+b^5=c^5+d^5$? \sref{statement}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can a positive integer be represented as a sum of two positive fifth powers in two different ways?
