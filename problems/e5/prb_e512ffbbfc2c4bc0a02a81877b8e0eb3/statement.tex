\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
In a standard edge first-passage model on the nearest-neighbor graph of $\Z^2$, assign i.i.d. nonnegative passage times $t_e$ with common law $\nu$, and put
\[
T(x,y)=\inf_{\pi:x\to y}\sum_{e\in\pi}t_e,\qquad T_n=T(0,(n,0)).
\]
The source asks whether boundary fluctuations have a Tracy--Widom limit and whether passage-time variance has order $n^{2/3}$. The latter, under a fixed law with finite relevant variances, means existence of constants $0<c_\nu\leq C_\nu<\infty$ such that
\[
c_\nu n^{2/3}\leq\operatorname{Var}(T_n)\leq C_\nu n^{2/3}
\]
for all sufficiently large $n$.

The infected set at time $t$ is $B(t)=\{x:T(0,x)\leq t\}$. A boundary-fluctuation limit needs a specified boundary observable, direction, centering, scaling and Tracy--Widom variant. Boundary height, passage-time fluctuations and geodesic transverse displacement are different quantities; the variance exponent corresponds to an $n^{1/3}$ standard-deviation scale, not an asserted $n^{2/3}$ standard deviation.
