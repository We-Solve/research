\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $\Sigma$ be a compact surface, $X$ a connected four-manifold, and $I=[0,1]$. For an embedding $f:\Sigma\to X$, the source defines $\pi_1$-negligibility by requiring the complement inclusion to induce an isomorphism
\[
 \pi_1(X\setminus f(\Sigma),*)\longrightarrow\pi_1(X,*),
\]
with a compatible basepoint outside the image. No orientability assumption on $\Sigma$ or $X$ is stated in the general problem.

Suppose $f_0,f_1:\Sigma\to X$ are $\pi_1$-negligible embeddings with $f_0|_{\partial\Sigma}=f_1|_{\partial\Sigma}$, and there is a homotopy $h:\Sigma\times I\to X$ satisfying
\[
 h(x,0)=f_0(x),\quad h(x,1)=f_1(x),\quad
 h(b,t)=f_0(b)\quad(b\in\partial\Sigma).
\]
A boundary-fixed concordance in the natural product convention is an embedding
\[
 F:\Sigma\times I\longrightarrow X\times I
\]
with $F(x,i)=(f_i(x),i)$ for $i=0,1$ and $F(b,t)=(f_0(b),t)$ on $\partial\Sigma\times I$. It is $\pi_1$-negligible if
\[
 \pi_1\bigl((X\times I)\setminus F(\Sigma\times I),*\bigr)
       \longrightarrow\pi_1(X\times I,*)
\]
is an isomorphism. The concordance is not required to preserve every interior level; that stronger condition would impose an isotopy-type requirement absent from the source.

The task is to construct computable invariants deciding whether such an $F$ exists. An obstruction-package formulation would specify a target and a distinguished vanishing value for $\mathcal O(X;f_0,f_1)$ and prove
\[
 \mathcal O(X;f_0,f_1)=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \text{a boundary-fixed $\pi_1$-negligible concordance exists}.
\]
This is a schema for the requested complete obstruction theory, not a proposed new invariant. The source does not specify a uniform algorithmic model, a finite encoding of the ambient manifold/embeddings, or what access to fundamental-group data ``computable'' permits. It also does not explicitly choose smooth versus locally flat topological embeddings, or proper/neat boundary conventions. These choices must be fixed rather than replaced by an unqualified all-input algorithm claim.

The background says $\pi_1$-negligibility is equivalent to having an immersed dual sphere. A dual sphere is an immersed $S^2\to X$ meeting the embedded surface transversely in one point and otherwise disjoint; ``framed'' requires a trivialized normal bundle. The stated equivalence is retained as a source assertion with its usual connected-surface scope: if disconnected surfaces are allowed, the appropriate componentwise dual conditions need clarification. A dual need not be framed merely because it exists.

The supplied literature triage reports a complete Freedman--Quinn concordance obstruction for compact orientable surfaces under the immersed framed-dual hypotheses in Klug--Miller [KM21]. It reports that for embedded two-spheres with an immersed dual, Freedman--Quinn and Stong invariants give a complete package under mild additional hypotheses, as developed by Freedman--Quinn, Stong and Klug--Miller [KM22]. The record does not reproduce every auxiliary hypothesis or target group in those theorems, so no unrestricted version or explicit formula for them is invented. Stong's paper is specifically a correction to the relevant Freedman--Quinn theorem.

The source's remaining general case concerns arbitrary compact surfaces and unframed duals beyond those established regimes. It suggests extending the Freedman--Quinn/Stong invariants to that setting. The literature triage checked 17 August 2026 classifies partial progress but reports no full computable obstruction theory in this generality; the main registry label is open. OCR forms such as ``4manifold'' and the malformed indefinite article do not change the mathematical question.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Find complete computable obstructions to concordance of homotopic, boundary-agreeing surface embeddings whose complements preserve the ambient fundamental group. Known sphere and framed-dual results do not cover every compact surface with an unframed dual, and category/computability conventions still need specification.
