\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Work in \(\C[x_1^{\pm1},\ldots,x_n^{\pm1}]\), and put
\[
 \operatorname{Log}(z)=(\log|z_1|,\ldots,\log|z_n|),\qquad
 \mathcal A(I)=\operatorname{Log}(V(I)\cap(\C^*)^n).
\]
For a nonzero Laurent polynomial \(f=\sum_{\alpha\in S}c_\alpha x^\alpha\), with distinct exponents and nonzero coefficients, set
\[
 r_\alpha(a)=|c_\alpha|e^{\langle\alpha,a\rangle},\quad
 S_f(a)=\sum_\alpha r_\alpha(a),\quad
 \rho_f(a)=\frac{2\max_\alpha r_\alpha(a)-S_f(a)}{S_f(a)}.
\]
The magnitudes are constant on \(\operatorname{Log}^{-1}(a)\). The polygon condition allows degenerate polygons and means \(2\max r_\alpha\leq\sum r_\alpha\). Thus
\[
 P(f)=\{a:\rho_f(a)\leq0\},\qquad P(0)=\R^n.
\]
The source's amoeba Nullstellensatz is
\[
 \mathcal A(I)=\bigcap_{f\in I}P(f).
\]
Its hypersurface approximation uses the cyclic resultant
\[
 f_m(x)=\prod_{\substack{\zeta_1^m=\cdots=\zeta_n^m=1}}
                   f(\zeta_1x_1,\ldots,\zeta_nx_n),
 \qquad m\geq1,
\]
after collecting like monomials when forming \(P(f_m)\). The reported theorem gives uniform Euclidean convergence of these outer approximations to \(\mathcal A(f)\), with worst-case order \(O(\log m/m)\). The extracted terminal 10 and malformed summation/root indices are source-layout artifacts, not extra factors or powers.

Kevin Purbhoo's four questions are retained: (A) find an explicit analogous higher-codimension approximation family; (B) seek a context generalizing the complex theorem and its known nonarchimedean analogue; (C) determine typical/random-polynomial convergence rates and constants in an error bound \((a\log m+b)/m\); and (D) identify open problems these methods can address. No probability distribution, support ensemble or general category is specified in (B)--(C), and (D) is an open research direction rather than a quantified assertion.

The aid supplies a robust compact answer toward (A). For every compact \(K\subset\R^n\setminus\mathcal A(I)\), there exist finitely many nonzero \(g_1,\ldots,g_s\in I\) and \(0<\eta<1\) such that
\[
 \max_j\rho_{g_j}(a)\geq\eta\qquad(a\in K).
\]
For an empty \(K\) the assertion is vacuous. If all coefficients of each \(g_j\) are perturbed with unchanged support by
\[
 |c'_\alpha-c_\alpha|\leq\delta|c_\alpha|,
 \qquad0\leq\delta<\eta,
\]
the perturbed family still excludes \(K\) from its joint \(P\)-intersection. Indeed a dominant-term excess loses at most \(\delta S_{g_j}\). A spatial version uses exponent-support diameter \(D\), the maximum over this finite family: when \(D>0\), every point at distance strictly less than
\[
 D^{-1}\log\frac{1+\eta}{1-\eta}
\]
from \(K\) is still excluded by at least one original polynomial. If all supports are single monomials, exclusion is global. This states the normalization needed for the claimed quantitative margins; arbitrary support changes are not covered by the relative-coefficient claim.

For fixed nonzero generators \(f_1,\ldots,f_q\) of a nonzero ideal, consider all nonzero combinations
\[
 h=\sum_{i=1}^q a_i f_i,
 \qquad a_i\in\Q(i)[x_1^{\pm1},\ldots,x_n^{\pm1}].
\]
They form a countable family \((h_j)\). With \(E_N=\bigcap_{j\leq N}P(h_j)\), the aid states
\[
 E_{N+1}\subseteq E_N,\qquad
 \mathcal A(I)\subseteq E_N,\qquad
 \bigcap_NE_N=\mathcal A(I).
\]
For a proper ideal, so \(\mathcal A(I)\ne\varnothing\), every compact window \(W\) satisfies the directed-excess convergence
\[
 \sup_{a\in W\cap E_N}\operatorname{dist}(a,\mathcal A(I))\to0,
\]
with empty supremum taken as zero. For the unit ideal, each compact window is eventually excluded; the zero ideal has amoeba \(\R^n\) and needs no nonzero-generator hierarchy. Rational multipliers suffice by coefficient continuity of a strict lopsidedness certificate, not by assuming the original coefficients are rational. This is compact-window convergence, with no global rate or computability guarantee for arbitrary exact complex coefficients.

Cyclic resultants of the displayed generators alone need not solve (A): \(\mathcal A((f_i)_m)=\mathcal A(f_i)\), so every point in \(\bigcap_i\mathcal A(f_i)\setminus\mathcal A(I)\) survives all those approximations. The aid reports a proper-ideal example with such a false component, but its defining generators are absent. Including ideal combinations is essential to the stated hierarchy; the report does not resolve the probabilistic constants or all broader-context/application questions.
\subsection{Short English statement}
The aid extends pointwise amoeba certificates to finite robust certificates on compact complement sets. Enumerating rational-coefficient combinations of fixed generators gives monotone outer approximations converging on compact windows. Using only cyclic resultants of the original generators can retain false points, and typical random rates remain undefined without an ensemble.
