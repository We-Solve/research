\subsection{Why rarity under the uniform law does not settle Heuristica}
The primary discussion asks whether worst-case NP hardness yields average-case hardness under an efficiently samplable distribution. The 2026 Hirahara--Nanashima report studies a sharp characterization of Pessiland, a different implication involving one-way functions; its revised abstract does not claim an unconditional elimination of Heuristica. We test a direct worst-case-to-uniform-average inference with an explicit padding construction.
For any language $L\subseteq\{0,1\}^*$ define
\[
L'=\{1^{|x|}0x:x\in L\}.
\]
\begin{proposition}
There is a polynomial-time many-one reduction from $L$ to $L'$, and if $L\in\mathrm{NP}$ then $L'\in\mathrm{NP}$. Nevertheless the constant algorithm answering NO has error at most $2^{-(m+1)}$ under the uniform law on strings of length $2m+1$, and zero error at every even length.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The map $x\mapsto1^{|x|}0x$ is computable in linear time and is a membership equivalence by definition. To verify membership in $L'$, check its form, extract $x$, and use an NP witness and verifier for $x\in L$; lengths grow only linearly. At length $2m+1$, there are only $2^m$ strings of the required form, among $2^{2m+1}$ strings total. The set of YES instances is a subset of these, so the constant-NO error is at most their fraction $2^{-m-1}$. No even-length string is in $L'$.
\end{proof}
Thus if $L$ is hard in the worst case, that hardness is preserved, while uniform-distribution error becomes exponentially small. This is a heuristic-error statement, not an errorless algorithm or an assertion that $\mathrm P\ne\mathrm{NP}$ has been proved.
The same construction also explains why it does not demonstrate Heuristica. For any efficiently samplable law $D_m$ on length-$m$ inputs, draw $x\sim D_m$ and output $1^m0x$. This is still efficiently samplable and puts all its mass on the rare prefix. An algorithm for $L'$ under that pushed-forward law would, by the membership-preserving map, give an algorithm for $L$ under $D_m$ with exactly the same error probability. The rarity argument has vanished. It supplies no hard $D_m$ and no efficient algorithm for every such law.
