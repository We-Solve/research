\section{The actual source setting}
Gribling's contribution, printed pages 2689--2691, concerns
symmetry-reduced noncommutative moment relaxations for mutually
unbiased bases. Its table contains very few scalar variables but
large matrix blocks, and its final question asks whether that
further reduction can be exploited algorithmically. Thus a generic
claim that a small variable count makes an SDP small would miss
the feature motivating the question. We attempt the log-determinant
Newton route, count the costs that survive, and check feasibility
issues relevant to proving nonexistence. No improvement for the
source moment hierarchy is claimed.

\section{What a small variable count does reduce}
Suppose a reduced real symmetric pencil is explicitly supplied as
\[
 F(x)=A_0+\sum_{j=1}^p x_jA_j
       =\bigoplus_{k=1}^K F_k(x),\qquad F_k(x)\in\mathbb R^{b_k\times b_k}.
\]
The same arguments use adjoints for complex Hermitian pencils.
At a strictly feasible point set $\Phi(x)=-\log\det F(x)$.
Its gradient and Hessian are
\[
 \partial_i\Phi=-\operatorname{tr}(F^{-1}A_i),\qquad
 \partial_i\partial_j\Phi=
 \operatorname{tr}(F^{-1}A_iF^{-1}A_j).
\]
To justify them, diagonalize
$F^{-1/2}AF^{-1/2}$ in
$\log\det(F+tA)=\log\det F+\log\det(I+tF^{-1/2}AF^{-1/2})$.
Differentiating the resulting sum of logarithms gives the first
formula. Differentiating $F^{-1}F=I$ gives
$\partial_jF^{-1}=-F^{-1}A_jF^{-1}$ and hence the second.
For $A_v=\sum_i v_iA_i$,
\[
 v^T\nabla^2\Phi\,v
 =\operatorname{tr}((F^{-1/2}A_vF^{-1/2})^2)\ge0,
\]
since a symmetric matrix has real eigenvalues and the trace of
its square is their squared sum.

A direct dense-arithmetic implementation factors the blocks and
forms $F_k^{-1}A_{j,k}$ by triangular solves with matrix right-hand
sides. This costs
$O((p+1)\sum_k b_k^3)$ operations. The $p^2$ trace products
cost $O(p^2\sum_k b_k^2)$, and a nonsingular Newton system in
$p$ variables costs $O(p^3)$ by Gaussian elimination. These are
straightforward upper counts for this implementation, not lower
bounds or an optimal algorithm. Small $p$ helps the last two
terms but does not by itself remove block factorization. Also,
linear dependence among pencil directions can make the Hessian
singular, requiring redundant directions to be removed or an
additional optimization prescription. No bit-complexity or
iteration bound follows from these local formulas.

\section{A tractable subcase and a surviving input cost}
If a simultaneous orthogonal diagonalizing basis for all $A_j$
is supplied, positivity of $F(x)$ is equivalent to one scalar
linear inequality for each diagonal coordinate. This follows
because conjugation preserves quadratic-form positivity and a
diagonal matrix is PSD exactly when every diagonal entry is
nonnegative. Thus this promised case becomes an LP in $p$
variables, with $\sum_kb_k$ inequalities. The source's noncommuting
blocks do not come with this promise.

Even one variable can retain a linear dependence on the input
length. Given $a_i\in\{0,1\}$ for $1\le i\le B$, minimizing
$t$ subject to $\operatorname{diag}(t-a_1,\ldots,t-a_B)\succeq0$
has optimum $\max_i a_i$. A deterministic exact entry-query
algorithm must inspect all $B$ entries on the zero input:
otherwise changing an unread entry to one leaves its transcript
unchanged and changes the answer. For randomized bounded-error
algorithms, choose the zero input with probability $1/2$ and a
uniformly chosen single-one input with probability $1/2$.
Fix the algorithmic coins. On the all-zero transcript at most
$q$ locations are inspected; with probability $1-q/B$ a single
one avoids them. Conditional on avoiding them, the same answer
cannot correctly distinguish both inputs. Minimizing the average
error on that transcript gives an unconditional error at least
$(1-q/B)/2$. Averaging the coins preserves the bound, so error
at most $1/3$ on every input requires $q\ge B/3$.
This is a classical input-access obstruction, not a quantum
lower bound or a lower bound for the specially structured MUB
coefficients. It rules out a complexity guarantee depending only
on the number of scalar variables for arbitrary supplied pencils.

\section{Why the barrier route does not decide all source-style tasks}
A feasible SDP can have no strictly feasible point: the pencil
$\operatorname{diag}(t,-t)$ is PSD only at $t=0$, while its
log-determinant barrier is undefined everywhere. Thus feasibility
cannot simply be equated with finding a barrier minimizer.

An elementary strict infeasibility certificate consists of a PSD
matrix $Y$ such that
\[
 \operatorname{tr}(YA_j)=0\ (1\le j\le p),\qquad
 \operatorname{tr}(YA_0)<0.
\]
For a PSD $F(x)$ this is impossible, since
$\operatorname{tr}(YF(x))=\operatorname{tr}(Y^{1/2}F(x)Y^{1/2})\ge0$,
whereas the certificate makes it negative. But these certificates
need not exist for an infeasible pencil. Consider the one-variable
example
\[
 F(t)=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&t\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its determinant is $-1$, so it is never PSD. Yet if
$Y=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\b&c\end{smallmatrix}\right)\succeq0$
annihilates the coefficient of $t$, then $c=0$, and PSD forces
$b=0$: otherwise $a+2bs$ takes a negative value on vectors
$(1,s)$. Hence $\operatorname{tr}(YA_0)=2b=0$, so no strict
certificate of the displayed form exists. The pencil approaches
PSD matrices arbitrarily closely: replacing its $(1,1)$ entry
by $\delta>0$ and choosing $t=1/\delta$ gives nonnegative
principal diagonal entries and determinant zero, hence a PSD
matrix, at entrywise distance $\delta$. This verifies the
weak-infeasibility mechanism directly.

\section{Audit and precise gap}
The attempted Newton reduction supplies a small linear system
but still requires large-block matrix work and additional handling
of singular or empty interiors. The audit checked that arithmetic
counts are upper counts, that the classical lower bound uses an
explicit input family, and that a strict dual certificate is not
assumed complete. No claim is made that the source's particular
MUB pencils realize the pathological examples. Exploiting their
extra representation structure, obtaining a runtime improvement,
