\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $X_{\mathrm{fl}}=\mathbb R^n/\Gamma$ be a complete flat manifold, where $\Gamma$ is a discrete group acting by translations. Let $(X,g)$ be a complete Riemannian $n$-manifold with $\operatorname{Sc}_g\geq0$. Isometric at infinity is understood to mean that there are compact sets $K\subset X$ and $K_{\mathrm{fl}}\subset X_{\mathrm{fl}}$ and an exact Riemannian isometry
\[
X\setminus K\ \cong\ X_{\mathrm{fl}}\setminus K_{\mathrm{fl}}.
\]
Must $X$ be flat, meaning its full Riemann curvature tensor vanishes identically?

The intended noncompact setting requires clarification: a full-rank translation quotient is a compact flat torus, and the complements-of-compact-sets condition can then become empty and impose no actual end geometry. The source does not explicitly exclude this case. Translation groups also exclude rotational-holonomy flat manifolds, so the summary's references to general flat manifolds and Klein bottles concern a broader class than the actual hypothesis.

The exact exterior isometry is retained; it is not weakened to asymptotic agreement of metrics. The supplied summary describes related nonnegative-scalar-curvature rigidity and splitting results but gives no precise hypotheses or verified complete resolution of this statement.
\subsection{Short English statement}
If a complete nonnegative-scalar-curvature manifold agrees exactly outside compact sets with a translation quotient of Euclidean space, must it be flat? Clarify noncompactness and retain the translation-only hypothesis.
