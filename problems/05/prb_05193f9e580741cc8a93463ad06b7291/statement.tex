\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For notation, a linear group is a subgroup \(G\leq \mathrm{GL}_n(K)\), where \(K\) is a field and \(n\geq 1\). A free basis of a subgroup \(H\leq G\) is a subset \(B\subseteq H\) that generates \(H\) and for which every nonempty reduced word in the formal letters \(b^{\pm1}\), \(b\in B\), represents a nonidentity element. A subgroup is free if it has such a basis.

The supplied problem asks for an algorithm that, given a linear group \(G\) and a matrix \(M\in G\), decides whether
\[
\exists H\leq G:\qquad M\in H,\qquad H\neq\{1\},\qquad H\text{ is free},
\]
and, in an affirmative case, constructs a free basis \(B\) of a witnessing subgroup \(H\). Membership of \(M\) in \(H\) is required; the statement does not require \(M\) to be a basis element or exclude \(M=1\).
