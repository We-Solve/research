\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Fix a prime number \(p\). A group \(G\) is locally finite if every subgroup generated by a finite subset of \(G\) is finite. Let
\[
\mathcal K_p=\{G: G\text{ is locally finite and }g^p=1\text{ for every }g\in G\}.
\]
The source calls \(\mathcal K_p\) the Kostrikin variety. Here ``exponent \(p\)'' is understood as satisfying the identity \(x^p=1\), so the trivial group is included; every nontrivial group satisfying this identity has exponent exactly \(p\).

A group identity is an equation \(w(x_1,\ldots,x_d)=1\), where \(w\) is a word in the free group on \(d\) generators. It holds in \(G\) if \(w(g_1,\ldots,g_d)=1\) for every \((g_1,\ldots,g_d)\in G^d\).

The question is whether, for each fixed prime \(p\), there exist a positive integer \(m\), positive integers \(d_1,\ldots,d_m\), and group words \(w_i(x_1,\ldots,x_{d_i})\), such that for every group \(G\),
\[
G\in\mathcal K_p
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\forall i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\;\forall(g_1,\ldots,g_{d_i})\in G^{d_i},\quad w_i(g_1,\ldots,g_{d_i})=1.
\]
The finite list may depend on \(p\); no common list or uniform bound across primes is requested. The source prefixes the question with ``(H. Neumann)'' while listing G. M. Bergman as proposer; their respective attribution roles are not further explained.
\subsection{Short English statement}
For each fixed prime \(p\), can finitely many group identities characterize exactly the locally finite groups satisfying \(x^p=1\)? The finite list may depend on \(p\). The supplied registry labels the problem open; this draft does not independently verify that status.
