\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(M\) be a continuous metric structure, and work on the bounded sorts of its language. A formula \(\varphi(\bar x,\bar y)\) has the order property in \(M\) if there are real \(r<s\) such that for every \(n\) there are tuples \(\bar a_i,\bar b_i\) in \(M\), \(i<n\), with
\[
 \varphi(\bar a_i,\bar b_j)\leq r,\qquad
 \varphi(\bar a_j,\bar b_i)\geq s\quad(i<j<n).
\]
Call it stable when it has no such property; the analogous theory condition requires the absence of these configurations in models of the complete theory. Quantifier-free stability restricts this condition to quantifier-free formulas. A stable complete continuous theory has all its formulas stable. The phrase ``stability of structures'' in the source is not defined: stability of a chosen formula on one structure, stability of all formulas there, and Banach-space stability must be distinguished.

\textbf{Primary Question 3.} Determine the implications among quantifier-free stability, the intended stability of metric structures, and stability of their complete theories. For a Banach space \(B\) in the pure Banach language, the relevant Banach-space condition is the Krivine--Maurey double-limit property
\[
 \lim_i\lim_j\|x_i+y_j\|=\lim_j\lim_i\|x_i+y_j\|
\]
for bounded sequences whenever both iterated limits exist. The research aid reports its equivalence with stability on \(B\) of every quantifier-free formula. It also reports that, for complete continuous theories, quantifier-free stability together with quantifier elimination implies full stability; quantifier elimination here means uniform approximation by quantifier-free formulas on the specified bounded sorts. Language, bounded-sort conventions and the structure/theory distinction must be retained when applying these statements.

The aid's reported counterexample outside this Banach setting is the following. Start with a random bipartite graph with vertex sorts \(A,B\), and introduce a discrete sort \(W\) with exactly one witness \(w\) for each edge. Take the language consisting of equality, discrete metrics, and \(\{0,1\}\)-valued incidences \(P(a,w),Q(w,b)\), whose value is zero exactly when \(a,b\) are the corresponding endpoints of \(w\). The aid reports that the complete theory of this three-sorted subdivision has stable quantifier-free formulas, while
\[
 \psi(a,b)=\inf_{w\in W}\max\{P(a,w),Q(w,b)\}
\]
is zero exactly on the original edges and has the order property. This is a reported counterexample to the unrestricted implication from quantifier-free stability to theory stability, not a Banach-space counterexample.

The workshop background attributes to Krivine the statement that stability of the norm formula gives an isometric copy of \(\ell_p\), for some real \(1\leq p<\infty\), in an ultrapower of the normed space. The supplied wording says ``\(p\geq1\)'' and does not specify all conventions or auxiliary hypotheses; no assertion about an actual subspace of the original space is substituted for this ultrapower statement. The group ``Definable norms and stable Banach spaces'' is mentioned.
