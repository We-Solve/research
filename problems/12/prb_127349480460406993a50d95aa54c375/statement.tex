\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
The historical AIM problem asks for a construction of moduli of stable $n$-folds avoiding the minimal model program (MMP), and lists three tasks, including varieties not assumed smoothable. In the stable-variety interpretation, an $n$-fold $X$ is projective, demi-normal (in particular $S_2$ with ordinary double crossings in codimension one), has $K_X$ $\Q$-Cartier and ample, and is semi-log-canonical (slc): its normalization with the conductor boundary is log canonical. The ampleness/stability qualification is essential; boundedness of arbitrary slc varieties from a fixed number $K_X^n$ alone is not silently asserted.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Prove boundedness of stable slc $n$-folds with fixed canonical volume $K_X^n=v$. Bounded means containment of all such objects in fibers of finitely many finite-type families.
\item Prove preservation of slc singularities under sufficiently small $\Q$-Gorenstein deformations, with the appropriate flatness and canonical-sheaf base-change conditions. The source states the corresponding semi-log-terminal assertion as known.
\item Verify the valuative properness condition for stable $n$-folds without smoothability: stable generic fibers should acquire stable extensions after the permitted finite base change, with the requisite uniqueness/separatedness for the moduli interpretation.
\end{enumerate}
The source attributes construction in the smoothable case to Karu's use of semistable MMP in dimension $n+1$ and Abramovich--Karu weak semistable reduction. The previous-problem and later-Alexeev references are not included in this record.

It explicitly says these solutions use MMP methods and therefore do not achieve the original MMP-avoidance aim. Complete references and theorem hypotheses are absent.
