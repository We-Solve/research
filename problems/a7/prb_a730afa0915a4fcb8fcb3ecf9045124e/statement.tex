\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 700220}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(G\) be a finitely generated torsion-free nilpotent group. Here torsion-free means that \(g^m=1\), for \(g\in G\) and an integer \(m\geq 1\), implies \(g=1\). Nilpotence means that the lower central series \(\gamma_1(G)=G\), \(\gamma_{k+1}(G)=[\gamma_k(G),G]\), reaches the trivial subgroup after finitely many steps, where \([H,G]\) is generated by the commutators \(h^{-1}g^{-1}hg\) with \(h\in H\) and \(g\in G\).

Write \(\operatorname{Aut}(H)\) for the group of all automorphisms of a group \(H\), with composition as its operation. Define
\[
\alpha^1G=\operatorname{Aut}(G),\qquad
\alpha^{n+1}G=\operatorname{Aut}(\alpha^nG)\quad(n\geq 1).
\]
Is it true that for every such \(G\), the sequence \((\alpha^nG)_{n\geq 1}\) contains only finitely many abstract group isomorphism types? Equivalently, must there exist an integer \(N\geq 1\), depending on \(G\), such that
\[
\forall n\geq 1\quad\exists j\in\{1,\ldots,N\}\quad
\alpha^nG\cong\alpha^jG?
\]
The hypotheses of finite generation, torsion-freeness and nilpotence apply to the initial group \(G\); no such hypotheses are imposed on its iterated automorphism groups.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Starting with any finitely generated torsion-free nilpotent group, repeatedly take its full automorphism group. Must only finitely many group isomorphism types occur? This is the question attributed to G. Baumslag in the supplied record.
