\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(n\geq 1\) be an integer, and let \(K\subset\R^n\) be a convex body, meaning a compact convex set with nonempty interior. A random vector \(X\) is uniformly distributed on \(K\) if its probability density is \(\mathbf{1}_K/\operatorname{vol}_n(K)\), where \(\operatorname{vol}_n\) denotes Lebesgue measure. Write \(\langle x,y\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^n x_i y_i\), \(|x|_2=\sqrt{\langle x,x\rangle}\), and \(S^{n-1}=\{\theta\in\R^n:|\theta|_2=1\}\). For a square-integrable real random variable \(Y\), define \(\operatorname{Var}(Y)=\E[(Y-\E Y)^2]\).

Here isotropic means that \(\E X=0\) and \(\E[XX^{\mathsf T}]=\sigma^2 I_n\) for some \(\sigma>0\), where \(I_n\) is the identity matrix. The supplied record does not specify whether isotropy additionally requires unit covariance or unit volume. The displayed conjecture is invariant under scalar dilation of \(K\), since both sides scale by the fourth power of the dilation factor; consequently these usual normalization choices give equivalent statements.

\textbf{Variance conjecture.} Does there exist a constant \(C>0\), independent of \(n\), \(K\), and \(\sigma\), such that for every integer \(n\geq1\) and every isotropic convex body \(K\subset\R^n\), a random vector \(X\) uniformly distributed on \(K\) satisfies
\[
\operatorname{Var}(|X|_2^2)
\leq C\left(\sup_{\theta\in S^{n-1}}\E\bigl[\langle X,\theta\rangle^2\bigr]\right)\E[|X|_2^2]?
\]
Under the isotropy convention above, the right-hand side is \(C n\sigma^4\).

The source labels this problem open. This is a supplied status label, not an independently verified literature conclusion.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is there a universal constant bounding the variance of the squared Euclidean norm of a uniform point in every isotropic convex body by the product of its largest directional second moment and its expected squared norm? The constant must work in every dimension. The source leaves the isotropic scale normalization unspecified; the inequality is invariant under that choice.
