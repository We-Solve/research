\paragraph{Definitions and conventions.}
In the hard-sphere model, configurations whose particle centers are closer than the sphere diameter are forbidden; allowed configurations otherwise have no pair energy. The thermodynamic limit sends volume and particle number to infinity at fixed density or fixes chemical potential. A fluid--solid phase transition concerns distinct macroscopic equilibrium phases and a thermodynamic singularity or coexistence criterion; crystalline order and symmetry breaking require specified observables and Gibbs-state notions.

\paragraph{The hard-rod boundary case.} To test whether packing alone produces a thermodynamic singularity, take \(N\) indistinguishable rods of length \(a>0\) in a one-dimensional interval of length \(L>Na\). Ordered left endpoints obey \(0\le x_1\), \(x_{i+1}\ge x_i+a\), \(x_N\le L-a\). Set \(y_i=x_i-(i-1)a\). The allowed region becomes the ordered simplex \(0\le y_1\le\cdots\le y_N\le L-Na\), whose volume is
\[
 Z_N^{\rm conf}(L)=\frac{(L-Na)^N}{N!}.
\]
The simplex volume follows by partitioning the cube into its \(N!\) orderings; coincidence sets have zero volume. Differentiation gives \(\beta P_N=\partial_L\log Z_N^{\rm conf}=N/(L-Na)\), with inverse temperature \(\beta>0\).
\paragraph{Thermodynamic diagnostic.} For \(N/L\to\rho\in(0,1/a)\), this tends to \(\beta P=\rho/(1-a\rho)\), an analytic function throughout the allowed open density interval. Close packing is a boundary divergence, not an interior coexistence transition in this calculation. Kinetic factors independent of \(L\) do not change the pressure.
\paragraph{Remaining target.} This exact elementary one-dimensional result cannot be extrapolated to three-dimensional hard spheres, where crystalline order and coexistence require a different argument. It shows that excluded volume and a packing limit alone are insufficient mechanisms. The first gap is construction of distinct infinite-volume phases or a free-energy singularity in a specified higher-dimensional ensemble.
