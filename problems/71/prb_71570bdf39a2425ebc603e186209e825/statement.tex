\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 701535}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(p\) be a prime. A group \(G\) is a \(p\)-group if every element has finite order equal to a power of \(p\). It is residually finite if, for every \(g\in G\setminus\{1\}\), there exist a finite group \(F\) and a homomorphism \(\varphi:G\to F\) such that \(\varphi(g)\ne 1\). It is finitely generated if \(G=\langle S\rangle\) for some finite subset \(S\subseteq G\). For \(H\le G\), the index \([G:H]\) is the number of left cosets of \(H\) in \(G\), and \(H\) is cyclic if \(H=\langle h\rangle\) for some \(h\in H\).

For the prime parameter \(p\), does there exist a group \(G\) such that
\[
\begin{gathered}
G\text{ is infinite, finitely generated, residually finite, and a }p\text{-group},\\
\forall H\le G,\qquad [G:H]=\infty\ \Longrightarrow\ H\text{ is cyclic}?
\end{gathered}
\]
The subgroup quantifier ranges over all subgroups, with no finite generation hypothesis on \(H\). The source does not explicitly specify whether its overarching existence question concerns at least one prime or every prime; the formulation above retains \(p\) as a parameter.

The supplied source reports that the answer is negative for \(p=2\). It also attributes to M. Ershov and A. Jaikin-Zapirain the following background result: for every prime \(p\), there exists an infinite, finitely generated, residually finite \(p\)-group \(K\) such that
\[
\forall L\le K,\qquad
\bigl(L\text{ is finitely generated and }[K:L]=\infty\bigr)
\ \Longrightarrow\ |L|<\infty.
\]
These are supplied source assertions, not independently verified conclusions of this draft.
\subsection{Short English statement}
For a prime \(p\), ask whether an infinite finitely generated residually finite \(p\)-group can have every subgroup of infinite index cyclic. The source reports a negative answer for \(p=2\), and separately reports examples for every prime when only finitely generated subgroups of infinite index are required to be finite. The intended quantification over primes is not explicit.
