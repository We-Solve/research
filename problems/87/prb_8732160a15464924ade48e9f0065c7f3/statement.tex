\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For scalars \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\), define
\[
A_\alpha=\begin{pmatrix}1&\alpha\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
B_\beta=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\\beta&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
C_\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}1-\gamma&-\gamma\\\gamma&1+\gamma\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Each matrix has determinant one. Let \(F_3=\langle x,y,z\rangle\) denote the free group on three specified generators.

The supplied record attributes to Yu. I. Merzlyakov the result that, for \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\C\) satisfying \(|\alpha|,|\beta|,|\gamma|\geq 3\), these matrices generate a free group of rank three. This background result is reported from the supplied source and has not been independently verified here.

The question is whether there exist \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\Q\) satisfying
\[
|\alpha|<3,\qquad |\beta|<3,\qquad |\gamma|<3
\]
such that the homomorphism
\[
\varphi_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}:F_3\longrightarrow\operatorname{SL}_2(\Q),\qquad
x\longmapsto A_\alpha,\quad y\longmapsto B_\beta,\quad z\longmapsto C_\gamma
\]
is injective. Equivalently, every nonidentity freely reduced word in \(x,y,z\) evaluates to a matrix different from \(I_2\). Thus the three displayed matrices must freely generate their subgroup, which has rank three.

The mathematical question is explicit in the supplied record; the unresolved source qualification concerns the truncated spelling of the proposer's name.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can three rational parameters, each strictly between \(-3\) and \(3\), make the displayed matrices freely generate a group of rank three? The supplied background states that complex parameters of absolute value at least \(3\) have this property.
