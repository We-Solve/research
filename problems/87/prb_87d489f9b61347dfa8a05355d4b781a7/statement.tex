\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(d\geq3\) be an integer, let \(\mathbb T=\R/\Z\) carry normalized Haar measure, and put \(e(t)=\exp(2\pi i t)\). For \(N\geq1\) and \(a=(a_1,\ldots,a_N)\in\C^N\), define
\[
 E_da(x,t)=\sum_{n=1}^N a_n e(nx+n^d t),\qquad (x,t)\in\mathbb T^2.
\]
The intended reconstruction of Li's OCR-damaged Question 9 is: for every \(d\geq3\), every real \(p\geq2(d+1)\), and every \(\epsilon>0\), there exists \(C_{d,p,\epsilon}<\infty\), independent of \(N,a\), such that
\[
 \|E_da\|_{L^p(\mathbb T^2)}\leq C_{d,p,\epsilon}
 N^{1/2-(d+1)/p+\epsilon}\left(\sum_{n=1}^N|a_n|^2\right)^{1/2}.
\]

The supplied partial theorem concerns coefficient-dependent phase rotations. Set \(s=d+1\). For each prescribed \(b\in\C^N\), each integer \(q>s\), and independent variables \(\omega_n\) uniformly distributed on \(\{\zeta\in\C:\zeta^q=1\}\), it states
\[
 \E\left\|\sum_{n=1}^N\omega_n b_n e(nx+n^d t)\right\|_{L^{2s}(\mathbb T^2)}^{2s}
 \leq s!\left(\sum_{n=1}^N|b_n|^2\right)^s.
\]
In particular, taking \(q=d+2=s+1\), for each \(b\) there is a choice of phases with
\[
 \|E_d(\omega b)\|_{2s}\leq(s!)^{1/(2s)}\|b\|_2.
\]
More quantitatively, for every \(\lambda\geq1\), outside a fraction at most \(\lambda^{-2s}\) of the \(q^N\) choices, the same phase vector obeys, simultaneously for all finite real \(p\geq2s\),
\[
 \|E_d(\omega b)\|_p\leq
 \bigl(\lambda(s!)^{1/(2s)}\bigr)^{2s/p}
 N^{1/2-s/p}\|b\|_2.
\]
The corresponding \(p=\infty\) inequality is the elementary \(\sqrt N\|b\|_2\) bound. The good phase vector may depend on \(b\), and no single choice is asserted to work for every coefficient vector. Hence this does not prove the deterministic estimate for arbitrary \(a\).

\subsection{Short English statement}
The intended question asks for a uniform discrete restriction bound for the curve \(n\mapsto(n,n^d)\). The partial theorem supplies a good choice of finite-alphabet phase rotations for each prescribed coefficient vector, with the desired high-\(p\) power and no epsilon loss. Choosing phases depending on the coefficients leaves the arbitrary-coefficient deterministic problem unresolved.
