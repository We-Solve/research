\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $S_0=\mathbb H^2/\Gamma_0$ be the intended reference hyperbolic surface and $\mathcal T_{\mathrm{qc}}(\Gamma_0)$ its quasiconformal Teichm\"uller space of marked structures. The source asks whether a Thurston metric can be defined there, but supplies neither the hypotheses on $\Gamma_0$ nor the meaning of extending the finite-type metric. Fix the intended nonempty family $\mathcal C$ of marked simple curve classes of positive hyperbolic length, with boundary/peripheral conventions required for marked-length rigidity. The following construction requires that these length functions separate points; otherwise it produces only a pseudometric before taking the length-spectrum quotient.

For $X,Y\in\mathcal T_{\mathrm{qc}}(\Gamma_0)$ define
\[
A(X,Y)=\log\sup_{\alpha\in\mathcal C}\frac{\ell_Y(\alpha)}{\ell_X(\alpha)},\qquad
\Delta(X,Y)=\max\{A(X,Y),A(Y,X)\}.
\]
Quasiconformal length distortion makes both directed quantities finite. One has $A(X,X)=0$ and the directed triangle inequality, but these do not alone give nonnegativity and separation. With the strong asymmetric-metric convention $d(X,Y)>0$ for $X\ne Y$, the naive formula $A$ is an asymmetric metric exactly when strict marked-length non-domination holds:
\[
\forall X\ne Y,\qquad
\sup_{\alpha\in\mathcal C}\frac{\ell_Y(\alpha)}{\ell_X(\alpha)}>1,
\]
equivalently some $\alpha$ satisfies $\ell_Y(\alpha)>\ell_X(\alpha)$, for every ordered pair. This differs from the weaker separation convention requiring only that $d(X,Y)=d(Y,X)=0$ imply $X=Y$.

The aid's regularization avoids assuming that unresolved condition. In the convention without a factor of one half, $\Delta=\sup_\alpha|\log(\ell_Y(\alpha)/\ell_X(\alpha))|$ is the symmetric length-spectrum metric, under the stipulated rigidity hypothesis. Since $-\Delta\leq A\leq\Delta$, for any real $c>1$ put
\[
D_c(X,Y)=A(X,Y)+c\Delta(X,Y).
\]
Then $D_c$ is a possibly asymmetric metric satisfying
\[
(c-1)\Delta\leq D_c\leq(c+1)\Delta,
\qquad\max\{D_c(X,Y),D_c(Y,X)\}=(c+1)\Delta(X,Y),
\]
\[
D_c(X,Y)-D_c(Y,X)=A(X,Y)-A(Y,X).
\]
Its triangle inequality follows by adding those for $A$ and $\Delta$, and the lower bound gives strong positivity off the diagonal. Thus it has exactly the same directional defect as $A$ and an explicitly controlled symmetrization. If the chosen length family does not separate the quasiconformal markings, the same inequalities give a pseudometric and do not repair that defect.

The construction is an asymmetric length-spectrum regularization. It is not asserted to coincide with an infimal Lipschitz metric, preserve the finite-type Thurston formula, be geodesic, be canonical independently of $c$, or induce the quasiconformal topology. Deciding the non-domination condition and the desired additional meaning of a Thurston metric remains outside the supplied partial result.
