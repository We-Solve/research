\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 20001880}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Klingenberg's conjecture asks: if a closed smooth manifold \(M\) admits a Riemannian metric whose geodesic flow is Anosov, must \(M\) admit some metric of strictly negative sectional curvature? The latter metric may be different. Anosov means the tangent bundle of the unit-speed flow splits into the flow direction and uniformly exponentially contracted/expanded invariant bundles. A given Anosov metric having some positive-curvature regions does not refute this manifold-existence conclusion; nor does an abstract Anosov flow on another manifold automatically give an Anosov geodesic metric.

The source reports the conjecture true in dimensions two and three via uniformization and Thurston geometrization. It attributes to Klingenberg [Klingenberg1974] seven negative-curvature properties extending to Anosov geodesic manifolds, including the no-\(\Z\times\Z\) conclusion of Preissman's theorem [Preissman1943]. Its example discussion cites [Ba-Br-Eb1985] and Heintze's construction gluing suitable truncated cusped hyperbolic three-manifolds along boundary tori and smoothing to nonpositive curvature. Those examples contain \(\Z^2\) and therefore are not candidate Anosov-geodesic counterexamples. The statement that all then-known examples were perturbations of negative metrics is retained as historical source context, not a fresh classification.

The aid's newer obstruction is explicitly conditional on Butler's Corollary 1.2, arXiv:2512.21308v1, December 2025, asserting word-hyperbolicity of the fundamental group of an Anosov geodesic manifold. Under that assumption it reports, for every connected closed smooth \(n\)-manifold, \(n\ge2\), admitting such a metric:
\begin{itemize}
\item \(M\) is aspherical;
\item \(\pi_1(M)\) is torsion-free, non-elementary, and word-hyperbolic;
\item \(\|M\|>0\), where simplicial volume is the infimum of \(\ell^1\) norms of real fundamental cycles (for nonorientable \(M\), normalized via its orientation double cover);
\item \(M\) has no direct-product decomposition into two closed connected positive-dimensional factors.
\end{itemize}
The aid combines Klingenberg's no-conjugate-points theorem with Mineyev's surjectivity of the bounded-to-ordinary group-cohomology comparison map and the bounded-cohomology pairing with the fundamental class. No full theorem hypotheses or proof artifacts are supplied, so the preprint-dependent positivity claim is not stated unconditionally.

Thus, conditional on that exact Butler version, zero simplicial volume obstructs an Anosov Riemannian geodesic metric, and any counterexample to Klingenberg must survive all the listed tests. These necessary conditions do not construct a negatively curved metric or a counterexample; group hyperbolicity and positive simplicial volume alone are not asserted sufficient. The aid reports the conjecture still open in dimensions at least four, and notes that recent positive-curvature regions in Anosov metrics and abstract Anosov graph-manifold flows do not change its logical conclusion. That is a dated unreviewed aid assessment, not independent current verification.
\subsection{Short English statement}
The conjecture asks for existence of some negative-curvature metric, not negativity of the given Anosov metric. Conditional on Butler's specified 2025 preprint theorem, the aid reports positive simplicial volume and other necessary manifold/group properties; it does not settle the higher-dimensional conjecture.
