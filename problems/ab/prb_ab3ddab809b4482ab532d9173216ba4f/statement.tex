\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 700921}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A variety of groups \(\mathfrak M\) is the class of all groups satisfying some fixed collection of group identities, with group homomorphisms as morphisms. A group \(P\in\mathfrak M\) is projective in \(\mathfrak M\) if, for every surjective homomorphism \(q:A\twoheadrightarrow B\) between groups in \(\mathfrak M\) and every homomorphism \(f:P\to B\), there exists a homomorphism \(\widetilde f:P\to A\) such that \(q\circ\widetilde f=f\).

A group is countably generated if it has a generating set of cardinality at most \(\aleph_0\), including the possibility of a finite generating set. It is infinitely generated if it has no finite generating set. For a family \((P_i)_{i\in I}\) in \(\mathfrak M\), its \(\mathfrak M\)-free product is its coproduct in \(\mathfrak M\): a group \(C\in\mathfrak M\) with homomorphisms \(\iota_i:P_i\to C\) such that, for every \(H\in\mathfrak M\) and family of homomorphisms \(f_i:P_i\to H\), there is a unique homomorphism \(f:C\to H\) satisfying \(f\circ\iota_i=f_i\) for all \(i\in I\).

\textbf{Question (V. A. Artamonov).} For every variety of groups \(\mathfrak M\) and every infinitely generated group \(P\in\mathfrak M\) that is projective in \(\mathfrak M\), do there exist a set \(I\) and countably generated groups \(P_i\in\mathfrak M\), each projective in \(\mathfrak M\), such that
\[
P\cong\coprod_{i\in I}^{\mathfrak M}P_i\,?
\]
Here projectivity is relative to the variety, and the displayed coproduct is the free product in that variety. The supplied record labels this question open; that status is not independently verified here.
\subsection{Short English statement}
For an arbitrary variety of groups, must every infinitely generated projective group be a free product within that variety of projective groups having at most countably many generators? Projectivity and the free product are both interpreted within the same variety.
