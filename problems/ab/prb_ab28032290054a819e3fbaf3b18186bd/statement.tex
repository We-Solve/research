\section{O24 — Finding a point on an elliptic curve}\label{8400028}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 8400028 · Graph Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Integers are supplied in binary; polynomial time means a polynomial number of classical bit operations in total input length. Given a prime $p$ and positive integer representatives $a,b$ with $4a^3+27b^2\not\equiv0\pmod p$, an affine point is a pair $x,y\in\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z$ satisfying $y^2=x^3+ax+b$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Is there a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm which outputs an affine point on every promised curve? \sref{8400028}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can one deterministically find finite coordinates of a point on the given nonsingular elliptic curve in polynomial time?
