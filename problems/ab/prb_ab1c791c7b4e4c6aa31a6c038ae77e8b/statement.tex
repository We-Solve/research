\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 700966}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \((G,\tau)\) be a topological group: multiplication \(G\times G\to G\) and inversion \(G\to G\) are continuous, with the product topology on \(G\times G\). Write \(e\) for its identity. A subset \(D\subseteq G\) is dense if \(\overline{D}^{\tau}=G\). The group is \emph{irresolvable} if
\[
\forall D_1,D_2\subseteq G,\qquad
\bigl(\overline{D_1}^{\tau}=G\ \land\ \overline{D_2}^{\tau}=G\bigr)
\Longrightarrow D_1\cap D_2\ne\varnothing.
\]
It is non-discrete if \(\tau\ne\mathcal P(G)\). An infinite subgroup of exponent \(2\) means an algebraic subgroup \(H\le G\) such that \(|H|\) is infinite and \(h^2=e\) for every \(h\in H\); no closedness requirement is stated.

\textbf{Question.} Does every non-discrete irresolvable topological group \((G,\tau)\) contain an infinite subgroup of exponent \(2\)? Equivalently, must there exist \(H\le G\) with
\[
|H|\text{ infinite}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\forall h\in H,\quad h^2=e?
\]
The source does not specify separation axioms for its topological groups. In particular, whether the universal quantifier is restricted to Hausdorff topological groups remains unresolved in this draft.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Must every non-discrete topological group in which any two dense subsets intersect contain an infinite subgroup all of whose nonidentity elements have order \(2\)? The source's separation convention needs clarification.
