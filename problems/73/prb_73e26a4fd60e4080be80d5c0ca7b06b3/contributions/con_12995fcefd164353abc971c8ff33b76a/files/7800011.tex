\section{Short-Range Spin Glasses}\label{7800011}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 7800011 · Mathematical Physics}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


\paragraph{Definitions and notation.}In the Edwards--Anderson model, $\sigma_x\in\{-1,1\}$ and $H_\Lambda(\sigma)=-\sum_{\langle x,y\rangle}J_{xy}\sigma_x\sigma_y$ at zero external field. The nearest-neighbour $J_{xy}$ are independent and identically distributed, with mean zero and finite variance. Infinite-volume Gibbs measures satisfy the conditional Gibbs laws; pure states are their extreme points. Spin flip sends all spins to their negatives. A metastate is a probability measure on Gibbs measures, encoding possible volume-dependent limiting states.

\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}
For almost every coupling realization on $\mathbb Z^d$, prove or disprove a thermodynamic transition above a lower critical dimension $d_c$, and determine $d_c$ if a transition exists. Decide whether some low-temperature phase breaks spin-flip symmetry. For each applicable $d,T$, determine the number of pure-state pairs. If there are infinitely many, establish or refute the source’s proposed metastate picture: infinitely many mixed states, each the equal-weight mixture of a single spin-flip pair, with the visible pair changing with volume. If multiple Gibbs states exist, determine whether that phase persists under a sufficiently small external magnetic field.

\paragraph{A one-dimensional boundary-influence calculation.}At zero field on a finite chain, write \(t_i=\tanh(\beta J_{i,i+1})\), with \(0<\beta<\infty\). Summing the Boltzmann weight over one spin shows that its conditional transition kernel with a free forward endpoint is \(K_i(s,s')=e^{\beta J_i ss'}/(2\cosh\beta J_i)=(1+ss't_i)/2\). Multiplication of these two-by-two kernels gives
\[\mathbb E(\sigma_n\mid\sigma_0=s)=s\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}t_i.\]
Thus the effect of a single fixed endpoint decays with the absolute product. With both endpoints fixed, the same expansion gives, for an interior spin \(\sigma_k\),
\[\mathbb E(\sigma_k\mid\sigma_0=s,\sigma_n=u)=
\frac{s\prod_{i<k}t_i+u\prod_{i\ge k}t_i}{1+su\prod_{i<n}t_i}.
\]
The analogous finite-block conditional probabilities follow by multiplying the kernels through each exterior segment.
\paragraph{Almost-sure disappearance of the boundaries.}For any real-valued iid coupling law, choose finite \(M\) with \(\mathbb P(|J|\le M)>0\). Almost surely infinitely many bonds in either half-line satisfy this bound: independence makes the probability of no such bond in an arbitrarily long tail zero. At those bonds \(|t_i|\le r=\tanh(\beta M)<1\), while all other factors are at most one. Therefore each long product tends to zero. The displayed endpoint formulas and their block versions then show that all finite-volume boundary choices converge to the same local law. Any infinite-volume Gibbs measure obeys these finite-interval conditional laws, so its finite-dimensional marginals equal that limit. Hence the one-dimensional zero-field Gibbs state is unique at every positive temperature under the stated iid assumptions.
\paragraph{Limits of the attempt.}This is an elementary known one-dimensional test, not a determination of the lower critical dimension. The limit \(\beta=\infty\) invalidates \(r<1\), and higher-dimensional loops prevent the chain-product argument.
