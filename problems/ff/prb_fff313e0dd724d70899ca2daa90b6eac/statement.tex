\section{Lang and Trotter's conjecture}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $E/\mathbb Q$ be an elliptic curve without complex multiplication, meaning $\operatorname{End}_{\overline{\mathbb Q}}(E)=\mathbb Z$. Let $N_E$ be its conductor. For a good prime $p\nmid N_E$ define $a_p(E)=p+1-\#E(\mathbb F_p)$, counting the point at infinity. For $p\ge5$, good reduction is supersingular precisely when $a_p(E)=0$. Put
\[
S_E(X)=\#\{p\text{ prime}:5\le p<X,\ p\nmid N_E,\ a_p(E)=0\}.
\]
Writing $f(X)\sim g(X)$ means $f(X)/g(X)\to1$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Does $S_E(X)$ satisfy the Lang--Trotter prediction
\[S_E(X)\sim C_{E,0}\frac{\sqrt X}{\log X}\]
for the nonnegative constant $C_{E,0}$ associated to $E$? As in the cited formulation, when $C_{E,0}=0$ this statement means that $S_E(X)$ is bounded. When $C_{E,0}>0$, it asks for the usual asymptotic and hence bounds above and below by positive multiples of $\sqrt X/\log X$. \sref{statement}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does the number of supersingular good primes of a non-CM rational elliptic curve have the Lang--Trotter square-root-over-logarithm asymptotic, using the source convention that a zero leading constant means bounded count?
