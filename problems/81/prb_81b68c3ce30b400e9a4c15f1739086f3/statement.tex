\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
The original problem asks which consequences of strictly positive sectional curvature persist when positivity holds on an open dense set. For a smooth connected \(n\)-manifold, \(n\geq2\), define the positive-point set
\[
 P=\{p\in M:\sec_p(\sigma)>0\text{ for every two-plane }\sigma\subset T_pM\}.
\]
The supplied discussion assumes \(P\) contains an open dense set of points, not merely a dense set of positively curved planes. Smoothness then forces \(\sec\geq0\) everywhere, and there is at least one point where all sectional curvatures are positive. Let
\[
 B=M\setminus P=\{p:\text{some sectional two-plane at }p\text{ has curvature }0\}.
\]
This is the bad set relevant to the reported localized statements.

The aid records two carryovers with different global hypotheses: closed examples have finite fundamental group, and complete noncompact examples are diffeomorphic to \(\R^n\). The latter is a topological/smooth conclusion about the underlying manifold, not an isometry assertion or a claim without completeness.

Its sharper partial result is a localized Frankel criterion. Let \(M\) be complete with \(\sec\geq0\), and let \(A,D\subset M\) be disjoint compact embedded totally geodesic submanifolds without boundary, with dimensions \(a,b\) satisfying \(a+b\geq n\). For every unit-speed shortest connector \(\gamma:[0,L]\to M\) from \(A\) to \(D\), the aid asserts a vector space of parallel normal fields along \(\gamma\) of dimension at least
\[
 r=a+b-n+1,
\]
with endpoint values tangent to \(A\) and \(D\), respectively, and with
\[
 R(V,\dot\gamma)\dot\gamma=0
\]
for every such field \(V\) along the entire connector. These parallel radial-null fields are Jacobi fields. In particular, the image of every shortest connector lies in \(B\), since at each of its points at least one radial two-plane is flat.

Consequently, if \(B\) contains no nonconstant geodesic segment, two such compact totally geodesic submanifolds with \(a+b\geq n\) must intersect. A sufficient special case is
\[
 \dim_{\mathrm H}B<1,
\]
since a nonconstant geodesic segment has Hausdorff dimension one. Open dense positivity alone only says that \(B\) has empty interior and does not supply this additional condition.

In contrast, the aid attributes to Wilking odd-dimensional nonorientable projectivized tangent-bundle examples with the relevant dense positivity, showing that the orientability conclusion from the odd-dimensional positive-curvature Synge theorem need not persist. It does not provide their base manifolds, dimension range, metric formulas, or bibliographic reference. This reported obstruction is retained without inventing a specific bundle or asserting failure of every part of Synge's theorem. The finite-fundamental-group, complete noncompact, localized Frankel, and Synge-orientability discussions are partial answers to the broad source request.
\subsection{Short English statement}
The supplied partial answer retains some global consequences of nonnegative curvature plus a positive point, but a Frankel extension needs extra control of the zero-curvature bad set. Its shortest-connector lemma produces parallel radial-null Jacobi fields. Reported nonorientable examples show that the odd-dimensional Synge conclusion does not transfer wholesale.
