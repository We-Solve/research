\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 701421}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A group \(G\) is \emph{residually finite} if, for every \(g\in G\setminus\{1\}\), there exist a finite group \(F\) and a homomorphism \(\varphi:G\to F\) such that \(\varphi(g)\ne 1\). For a subgroup \(H\le G\), write \([G:H]\) for its index. A group is \emph{cyclic-by-finite} if it has a cyclic normal subgroup of finite index; the cyclic subgroup may be finite or infinite.

\textbf{Question.} Let \(G\) be any residually finite group satisfying
\[
\forall H\le G,\qquad [G:H]=\infty\ \Longrightarrow\ |H|<\infty.
\]
Must there exist a subgroup \(C\trianglelefteq G\) such that \(C\) is cyclic and \([G:C]<\infty\)? No finite generation assumption is imposed.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Must every residually finite group whose subgroups of infinite index are all finite have a cyclic normal subgroup of finite index?
