{\small UnsolvedMath ID 30004804 · Mathematical Physics}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


A vacuum spacetime is a Lorentzian manifold $(M,g)$ satisfying $\operatorname{Ric}(g)=0$. Its Cauchy data are a Riemannian metric and second fundamental form satisfying the vacuum constraint equations. The maximal globally hyperbolic development is the largest Cauchy evolution. Relative to future null infinity $\mathcal I^+$, the black-hole region is $M\setminus J^-(\mathcal I^+)$; a naked singularity is visible from the exterior.

\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}
Weak Cosmic Censorship for the Einstein vacuum equations: for generic complete, regular, asymptotically flat Cauchy data, are all singularities in the corresponding maximal development hidden inside a black hole (so that naked singularities do not occur)?

\paragraph{Rejecting a tempting naked-singularity counterexample.} Put \(M=-a\), \(a>0\), in the Schwarzschild form, so \(f(r)=1+2a/r>0\) for every \(r>0\). Radial null curves satisfy \(dt/dr=\pm f^{-1}\). Since
\[
 \int_0^{r_0}\frac{dr}{1+2a/r}<\infty,
\]
there are outgoing radial rays from arbitrarily close to the singular boundary reaching a regular exterior in finite coordinate time, and no positive root of \(f\) supplies a horizon. The radial curvature component contains \(f''(r)/2=2a/r^3\), which diverges at zero.
\paragraph{The excluded initial-data hypothesis.} On a static spatial slice, the radial proper distance to zero is
\[
 \int_0^{r_0}\frac{dr}{\sqrt{1+2a/r}}<\infty,
\]
by comparison near zero with a constant times \(r^{1/2}\). Removing the singular center therefore leaves an incomplete initial manifold; adjoining it does not give regular data. This candidate violates the source's complete regular Cauchy-data requirement before genericity is even considered.
