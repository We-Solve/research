Choose \(\mu\) distinct from every \(\epsilon_j\), fill all modes below \(\mu\), and call this vector \(\Omega_F\). Define \(b_j=c_j\) for empty modes and \(b_j=c_j^*\) for filled modes. Direct use of the canonical anticommutation relations shows that the \(b_j\) again obey those relations and annihilate \(\Omega_F\). With \(N=\sum_jc_j^*c_j\),
\[
 H_0-\mu N-E_F=\sum_j|\epsilon_j-\mu|b_j^*b_j,
 \qquad E_F=\sum_{\epsilon_j<\mu}(\epsilon_j-\mu).
\]
Thus free excitations are exactly fermionic in this finite grand-canonical reference. At fixed particle number the allowed excitations have matching particle and hole counts.
\paragraph{Testing interaction closure.} Add \(\lambda c_1^*c_1c_2^*c_2\), with modes 1 and 2 distinct. Writing \(n_2=c_2^*c_2\), direct multiplication gives
\[
 [H_0+\lambda n_1n_2,c_1^*]
 =\epsilon_1c_1^*+\lambda c_1^*n_2.

The extra cubic expression is not a scalar multiple of \(c_1^*\) on the whole Fock space: its energy shift depends on the other occupation. Merely shifting one-particle energies therefore cannot be an exact universal interacting description.
