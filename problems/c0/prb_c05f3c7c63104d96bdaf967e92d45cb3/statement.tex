\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A translation structure on a connected oriented surface is a Euclidean coordinate atlas on its regular locus whose transition maps are translations \(z\mapsto z+c\). It induces a flat area measure, denoted by \(\operatorname{Area}(S)\). The surface has infinite genus if, for every \(n\in\N\), it contains a compact connected subsurface of genus at least \(n\).

Using the orientation-preserving convention, let \(\operatorname{Aff}^{+}(S)\) be the group of self-homeomorphisms preserving the translation structure affinely: in translation coordinates each map has the form \(z\mapsto Az+b\), with a constant matrix \(A\in\operatorname{GL}^{+}(2,\R)\). Its derivative group is the Veech group
\[
\Gamma(S)=\{Df:f\in\operatorname{Aff}^{+}(S)\}.
\]
When \(0<\operatorname{Area}(S)<\infty\), area scaling implies \(\det Df=1\), so \(\Gamma(S)\subseteq\operatorname{SL}(2,\R)\). A subgroup \(\Gamma\subseteq\operatorname{SL}(2,\R)\) is a lattice if it is discrete and \(\operatorname{SL}(2,\R)/\Gamma\) has finite invariant Haar volume.

\textbf{Existence question.} Does there exist a translation surface \(S\) such that
\[
\operatorname{genus}(S)=\infty,\qquad
0<\operatorname{Area}(S)<\infty,\qquad
\Gamma(S)\text{ is a lattice in }\operatorname{SL}(2,\R)?
\]
