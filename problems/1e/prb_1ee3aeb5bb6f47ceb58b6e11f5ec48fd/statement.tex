\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 10900039}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a group \(G\), set
\[
 \mathcal C(G)=\{[Q]:Q\text{ finite and there is a surjective homomorphism }G\twoheadrightarrow Q\},
\]
where \([Q]\) denotes abstract isomorphism class, without multiplicity. Let \(\Gamma\leq\operatorname{PSL}(2,\C)\) be discrete of finite covolume, so \(\mathbb H^3/\Gamma\) has finite hyperbolic volume; torsion is not excluded by the source.

Reid asks two rigidity questions, naturally read as abstract group determination:
\begin{enumerate}
\item For every Kleinian group \(\Lambda\), does \(\mathcal C(\Lambda)=\mathcal C(\Gamma)\) imply \(\Lambda\cong\Gamma\)? The comparison group is not explicitly required to have finite covolume or to be finitely generated.
\item For every compact \(3\)-manifold \(N\), does \(\mathcal C(\pi_1N)=\mathcal C(\Gamma)\) imply \(\pi_1N\cong\Gamma\)? No closedness, orientation or irreducibility restriction on \(N\) is printed.
\end{enumerate}
The profinite completion is \(\widehat G=\varprojlim_{K\triangleleft G,,[G:K]<\infty}G/K\). For finitely generated comparison groups, equality of these finite-quotient isomorphism-class sets can be expressed as isomorphism of profinite completions as topological groups. That equivalence is not assumed without qualification for arbitrary infinitely generated Kleinian comparison groups. If determination was intended up to geometric conjugacy rather than abstract group isomorphism, the target must be specified separately.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does the collection of finite quotients of a finite-covolume Kleinian group determine its abstract group, first among Kleinian groups and then among compact \(3\)-manifold groups?
