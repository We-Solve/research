\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A continuous flow on \(\R^3\) is a continuous map \(\varphi:\R\times\R^3\to\R^3\) such that \(\varphi(0,x)=x\) and \(\varphi(s+t,x)=\varphi(s,\varphi(t,x))\) for every \(s,t\in\R\) and \(x\in\R^3\). Write \(\varphi_t(x)=\varphi(t,x)\). A set \(B\subseteq\R^3\) is invariant if \(\varphi_t(B)=B\) for every \(t\in\R\).

For this draft, interpret a compact set \(K\subseteq\R^3\) as movable in the usual shape-theoretic neighborhood sense: for every open neighborhood \(O\) of \(K\), there exists an open neighborhood \(V\) of \(K\), with \(V\subseteq O\), such that for every open neighborhood \(W\) of \(K\) satisfying \(W\subseteq V\), there is a continuous homotopy
\[
H:V\times[0,1]\longrightarrow O,
\qquad H(x,0)=x\quad(x\in V),
\qquad H(V,1)\subseteq W.
\]
This homotopy need not fix \(K\) pointwise and is not required to arise from the flow. The supplied record does not define movability or specify the regularity of the flow; the shape-theoretic interpretation and the continuous-flow convention above require confirmation against the cited source.

The question is whether, for every such flow \(\varphi\), every compact invariant set \(A\subseteq\R^3\), and every open set \(U\subseteq\R^3\) with \(A\subseteq U\), there exists a compact set \(K\subseteq\R^3\) such that
\[
A\subseteq K\subseteq U,\qquad
\varphi_t(K)=K\quad\text{for every }t\in\R,
\qquad K\text{ is movable}.
\]
Considering open neighborhoods suffices to express the question for arbitrary neighborhoods. The inclusions are not required to be strict, and the source does not require \(K\) itself to be a neighborhood of \(A\).
