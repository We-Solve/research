\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(X\) be a smooth Mori dream space of dimension \(n\). In the usual projective convention, this means a smooth projective variety for which \(\operatorname{Pic}(X)\otimes_{\Z}\Q\cong N^1(X)_{\Q}\) and the Cox ring, the algebra of global sections graded by divisor classes, is finitely generated. Here \(N^1(X)_{\R}\) denotes the real vector space of Cartier divisors modulo numerical equivalence, and \(N^1(X)_{\Q}\) its rational structure. The source does not specify the ground field or its characteristic; the intended field hypotheses remain unresolved.

An admissible flag is a chain of irreducible closed subvarieties
\[
Y_\bullet:\quad X=Y_0\supset Y_1\supset\cdots\supset Y_n=\{x\},
\qquad \operatorname{codim}_X(Y_i)=i,
\]
with every \(Y_i\) nonsingular at \(x\). For a Cartier divisor \(D\) and a nonzero section \(s\in H^0(X,\mathcal O_X(D))\), let \(\nu_{Y_\bullet}(s)\in\Z_{\geq 0}^{n}\) be its flag valuation: successively take the order of vanishing along the next member of the flag, divide out that vanishing, and restrict to that member.

Using the standard closed-cone convention, define the global Newton--Okounkov body by
\[
\Delta_{Y_\bullet}(X)
=
\overline{\operatorname{cone}\!\left\{
\bigl(\nu_{Y_\bullet}(s),[D]\bigr):
D\text{ a Cartier divisor on }X,\quad
0\ne s\in H^0(X,\mathcal O_X(D))
\right\}}
\subseteq \R^n\times N^1(X)_{\R}.
\]
Here \(\operatorname{cone}\) means finite nonnegative real linear combinations, and the bar denotes closure. This global object is a cone, despite the customary name ``body.'' It is rational polyhedral if there are finitely many vectors \(v_1,\ldots,v_r\in\Q^n\times N^1(X)_{\Q}\) such that
\[
\Delta_{Y_\bullet}(X)=\sum_{j=1}^{r}\R_{\geq0}v_j.
\]

\textbf{Question.} Does every smooth Mori dream space \(X\) admit an admissible flag \(Y_\bullet\) for which \(\Delta_{Y_\bullet}(X)\) is rational polyhedral?
