\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 700273}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a finite group \(H\), a finite presentation \(P=\langle x_1,\ldots,x_{m_P}\mid R_1,\ldots,R_{r_P}\rangle\) has \(m_P\) generators and \(r_P\) defining relations. Define its deficiency by
\[
\operatorname{def}(H)=\max_{P\text{ a finite presentation of }H}(m_P-r_P).
\]
Write \(G'=[G,G]\) for the commutator subgroup of \(G\), and write \(M(G)=H_2(G,\Z)\) for its Schur multiplier (called the multiplicator in the source). For \(n\geq 1\), let \(G^n=\prod_{i=1}^{n}G\) denote the direct product of \(n\) copies of \(G\).

\textbf{Statement (J. Wiegold).} For every finite group \(G\) such that \(G=G'\neq\{1\}\) and \(M(G)\) is trivial, prove that
\[
\operatorname{def}(G^n)\longrightarrow-\infty\qquad(n\longrightarrow\infty).
\]
Explicitly, for every integer \(k\geq1\), there exists an integer \(N\geq1\) such that, for every integer \(n\geq N\),
\[
\operatorname{def}(G^n)\leq-k.
\]
\subsection{Short English statement}
J. Wiegold asks whether the deficiencies of the successive direct powers of every nontrivial finite perfect group with trivial Schur multiplier tend to minus infinity. Deficiency is maximized over all finite presentations.
