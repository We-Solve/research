\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 30000798}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
An integral binary cubic form is a homogeneous polynomial
\[
 F(X,Z)=aX^3+bX^2Z+cXZ^2+dZ^3,
 \qquad a,b,c,d\in\Z.
\]
It is primitive when \(\gcd(a,b,c,d)=1\). A primitive representation of an integer \(m\) means a solution \(F(x,z)=m\) with \(x,z\in\Z\) and \(\gcd(|x|,|z|)=1\). In particular, primitiveness of the representation is a condition on \((x,z)\), not on \((x,y,z)\) or on the represented integer alone.

The question is whether
\[
 \forall(a,b,c,d)\in\Z^4\text{ with }\gcd(a,b,c,d)=1,
 \quad\exists x,z,y\in\Z:
 \gcd(|x|,|z|)=1,
 \quad ax^3+bx^2z+cxz^2+dz^3=y^2.
\]
The pair \((x,z)=(0,0)\) is excluded by coprimality; zero in one coordinate is permitted. As written, \(y=0\) is permitted, so a square includes zero. The supplied statement imposes neither irreducibility nor nonzero discriminant, and no such restriction has been added.

The supplied assessment dated 2026-08-21 says the report proves the weaker alternative that a primitive binary cubic form primitively represents a square or twice a square. In symbols, this reported alternative permits
\[
 F(x,z)=\varepsilon y^2,\qquad \varepsilon\in\{1,2\},\qquad \gcd(|x|,|z|)=1.
\]
The question asks for the case \(\varepsilon=1\) in every instance; the reported alternative does not by itself give that conclusion.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every integral cubic form in two variables whose coefficients have no common factor take a square value at some coprime integer pair? The supplied report assessment gives only the weaker square-or-twice-a-square alternative.
