\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
In the stable homotopy category, let $\mathbb S$ denote the sphere spectrum and define the stable homotopy groups by
\[
 \pi_n(X)=[\Sigma^n\mathbb S,X],\qquad n\in\Z.
\]
A finite spectrum is understood in the usual sense of the finite stable category generated from the sphere by finite cofiber constructions, suspensions, and retracts. The source does not specify integral versus a fixed-prime localized conventions.

The generating-hypothesis question is:
\[
 \forall X,Y\text{ finite spectra}\ \forall f\in[X,Y],
 \quad\bigl(\forall n\in\Z:\pi_n(f)=0\bigr)
       \quad\Longrightarrow\quad f=0\text{ in }[X,Y]\ ?
\]
Here $\pi_n(f)$ is the induced homomorphism on the entire stable homotopy group, and $f=0$ means null-homotopic as a map of spectra. The hypothesis is not merely vanishing in one degree or vanishing on ordinary homology. The supplied assessment identifies this as Freyd's generating hypothesis for finite spectra.

The source also asks: when is such a map nilpotent under composition? For an endomorphism $f:X\to X$, composition nilpotence has the precise meaning
\[
 \exists r\ge1:\qquad f^{\circ r}=0\text{ in }[X,X].
\]
For the general map $f:X\to Y$ in the first question, self-composition need not be defined. The record does not say whether the second question is restricted to endomorphisms, concerns a suitably specified composable sequence of homotopy-annihilating maps, or uses another construction. It therefore cannot be given a unique composition-nilpotence target as written. Smash-product nilpotence would be a different notion and is not silently substituted. No nilpotence exponent, uniformity, or characterization of the missing case is asserted.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Freyd's question asks whether a map between finite spectra that induces zero on every stable homotopy group must itself vanish. A second composition-nilpotence question is retained, but requires an endomorphism or another explicit way to compose maps.
