\paragraph{A conservation-compatible coupling calculation.} For $m$ scalar model states $x_i$ let the coupling alone be $\dot x_i=\sum_jk_{ij}(x_j-x_i)$ with symmetric nonnegative weights $k_{ij}=k_{ji}$. Pairwise cancellation gives $d(\sum_ix_i)/dt=0$. Writing $\bar x=m^{-1}\sum_ix_i$, direct differentiation yields
\[
 \frac d{dt}\frac12\sum_i(x_i-\bar x)^2
 =-\frac12\sum_{i,j}k_{ij}(x_i-x_j)^2\leq0.
\]
If the graph is connected, the right side vanishes only when every state agrees. A positive spectral gap of the finite graph Laplacian then gives exponential decay of disagreement, by diagonalizing its symmetric matrix on the zero-sum subspace.

\paragraph{Synchronization does not remove common bias.} Add identical model drift $\dot x_i=b$ to every component while the true scalar state has zero drift. The coupling still drives disagreement to zero, but $\dot{\bar x}=b$. Thus all models can synchronize perfectly and share a linearly growing forecast error. Training only agreement between models cannot detect this common missing tendency; observations or an additional model component are necessary.

\paragraph{Scope of the design result.} Symmetric exchange gives a precise sufficient conservation constraint for one scalar total; for physical energy or momentum, the coupling must use the appropriate variables and weights. This does not choose connection count, observation frequency, training sample size or slow-ocean treatment, nor does it ensure prediction under changing climate or truth outside the model class. The first gap is identifiability and generalization from observations subject to the actual physical balance constraints. All those source design questions remain separate from consensus.
