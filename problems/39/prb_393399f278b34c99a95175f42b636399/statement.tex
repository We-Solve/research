\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For integers $n\geq2$ and $N\geq1$, let
\[
 S^{n-1}=\{x\in\R^n:\|x\|=1\},\qquad
 \mathcal C_{N,n}=\{C\subset S^{n-1}:|C|=N\}.
\]
The source asks for the best way to distribute $N$ points on this sphere. ``Best'' is not defined in its one-sentence statement; finite energy minimization, maximal separation, covering, and discrepancy are different optimization problems. The case $N=1$ is degenerate for pair interactions; the usual nontrivial problem has $N\geq2$. No asymptotic limit in $N$ or $n$ is specified.

The supplied literature assessment interprets the question through spherical energy. For a chosen pair potential $f:(0,4]\to\R$, one can define
\[
 E_f(C)=\sum_{\{x,y\}\subset C}f(\|x-y\|^2),\qquad
 \text{and seek }C_*\in\mathcal C_{N,n}\text{ minimizing }E_f.
\]
The unordered-pair convention fixes the irrelevant factor of two. Distinct points avoid zero arguments, and the potential's regularity or blowup at zero controls possible limiting collisions. A packing formulation would instead maximize $\min_{x\ne y\in C}\|x-y\|$. Neither objective is silently asserted as the unique source meaning.

The cited universal-optimality perspective asks for configurations minimizing $E_f$ simultaneously for every completely monotone function of squared distance, meaning a smooth function with $(-1)^j f^{(j)}\geq0$ for all $j\geq0$ on the distance interval, with the needed endpoint convention. Such universal optimality is a stronger property of special configurations; it is not the assertion that one common optimizer exists for every $N,n$ and every unrelated criterion. Rotations and permutations do not change these energies, so classification should at least account for those symmetries if it is requested.
