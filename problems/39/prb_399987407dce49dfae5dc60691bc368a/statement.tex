For \(k\in\{3,4\}\), a \(k\)-holed sphere in \(S_g\) is a compact embedded subsurface \(H\subset S_g\) homeomorphic to a sphere with the interiors of \(k\) pairwise disjoint closed disks removed. Require every component of \(\partial H\) to be essential in \(S_g\), meaning that it is not null-homotopic in \(S_g\). No requirement that distinct boundary components be nonisotopic is added. For each \(k\in\{3,4\}\), consider the assertion
\[
\begin{gathered}
\text{For every pair of integers }h>g\geq 2
\text{ and every finite covering map }f:S_h\longrightarrow S_g,\\
\text{there exists a }k\text{-holed sphere }H\subset S_g
\text{ whose boundary components are essential in }S_g\\
\text{such that }\operatorname{int}_{S_h}\!\bigl(f^{-1}(H)\bigr)
\text{ is disconnected.}
\end{gathered}
\]
The source asks whether this assertion holds for \(k=4\), and asks the corresponding question for \(k=3\). The subsurface \(H\) may depend on the covering map \(f\). These are questions, not asserted conclusions.
\subsection{Short English statement}
For every finite cover \(S_h\to S_g\) with \(h>g\geq2\), must there be an embedded four-holed sphere with essential boundary whose full preimage has disconnected interior? Ask the same question for a three-holed sphere. This formulation uses the usual closed, connected, orientable surface convention, which the supplied record does not explicitly specify.
