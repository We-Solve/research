\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(\zeta(s)\) denote the Riemann zeta function, let \(f\) denote a Hecke--Maass cusp form, and let \(\chi\) denote a Dirichlet character. Write \(L(s,f\otimes\chi)\) for the standard twisted \(L\)-function, in the normalization with central point \(s=\tfrac12\). For any finite family \(\mathcal X\) of Dirichlet characters, define the unweighted sixth moment by
\[
M_6(f;\mathcal X)=\sum_{\chi\in\mathcal X}\left|L\!\left(\tfrac12,f\otimes\chi\right)\right|^6.
\]
This notation does not prescribe the family intended by the source.

The supplied background attributes to Heath-Brown the bound
\[
\forall\varepsilon>0,\qquad
\int_0^T\left|\zeta\!\left(\tfrac12+it\right)\right|^{12}\,\dd t
\ll_{\varepsilon}T^{2+\varepsilon}
\qquad(T\to\infty),
\]
where the implied constant may depend on \(\varepsilon\). It attributes an analogous sixth-moment result in the \(t\)-aspect for \(L(\tfrac12+it,f)\) to Jutila, and complementary twelfth-moment results for Dirichlet \(L\)-functions in the level aspect, obtained independently, to Milicevic--White and Nunes. These are supplied background attributions, not independently verified assessments.

\textbf{Problem (Peter Humphries, Virginia; source Problem 16).} Prove analogous level-aspect bounds for the sixth moment of \(L(\tfrac12,f\otimes\chi)\), with \(\chi\) varying through an appropriate family of Dirichlet characters.

\textbf{Unresolved formulation.} The source does not specify that character family or a quantitative target inequality. In particular, it does not determine whether the moment uses all or only primitive characters, a fixed modulus or a range of moduli, any restrictions on those moduli, or weights or normalization of the average. Consequently, the supplied question cannot be converted into a unique quantified bound for \(M_6(f;\mathcal X)\) without additional hypotheses. No particular modulus exponent is asserted here.

The source asks for sixth-moment bounds for central values of Dirichlet twists of a Hecke--Maass cusp form, analogous to the cited moment bounds for zeta and other \(L\)-functions.
