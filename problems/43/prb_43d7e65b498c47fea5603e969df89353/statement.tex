\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a group \(G\), define the requested fixed-point threshold by
\[\delta(G)=\min\{d\in\Z_{\geq0}:\exists\text{ a complete CAT}(0)\text{ space }X,
\ \dim_{\mathrm{cov}}X=d,\ \exists G\curvearrowright X\text{ isometrically with }X^G=\varnothing\},\]
with no finite value if such a space does not exist. The dimension is topological covering dimension, as the source's footnote specifies. Actions need not be faithful, proper, cocompact or semisimple unless separately required.

The problem asks to determine \(\delta(\operatorname{Out}(F_n))\), where \(F_n\) is the rank-\(n\) free group, and the analogous least dimension for \(\operatorname{Mod}^{\pm}(S_g)\), the extended mapping class group of a closed orientable surface. Rank/genus ranges and low-complexity exceptions are not stated in the excerpt.

The source gives this context for \(\operatorname{Out}(F_n)\):
\begin{itemize}
\item The first-homology action gives \(\operatorname{Out}(F_n)\to GL(n,\Z)\) and an action without a global fixed point on the symmetric space of dimension \(n(n+1)/2-1\). A standard model is determinant-one positive-definite real quadratic forms with the appropriate linear action. This supplies the stated upper bound in its intended rank range.
\item It reports property FA and hence \(\delta\geq2\). The relevant rank hypotheses and passage from tree fixed-point properties to this complete finite-dimensional CAT(0) convention must be retained from the missing context; no low-rank universal assertion is added.
\item Motivated by Farb's \(GL(n,\Z)\) work, Bridson [14] is reported to obtain a lower bound growing linearly with \(n\), using a Helly-type theorem and finite subgroup structure. No explicit constants are supplied.
\item A lower bound \(3n-3\) is said to exclude a complete \(\operatorname{Out}(F_n)\)-equivariant nonpositively curved metric on Outer Space, under the source's model/dimension conventions. Such a bound is proposed, not asserted established.
\end{itemize}
Finally, the source reports that isometries of a CAT(0) polyhedral complex with finitely many cell isometry types are elliptic or hyperbolic: they fix a point or have a positive-length translation axis. Finite-dimensional unit cube complexes are its example. For \(n\geq4\), a variation of Gersten's argument [36] is said to prevent any Nielsen generator from being hyperbolic in an action on such \(X\). A standard Nielsen representative sends \(x_1\mapsto x_1x_2\) and fixes the other basis elements. The precise cell/action and completeness hypotheses belong to [15] and the omitted argument. Individual generators being elliptic is not itself a common-fixed-point conclusion, and this restricted polyhedral statement is not substituted for the question about all complete CAT(0) spaces.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Find the smallest covering dimension of a complete CAT(0) space supporting a fixed-point-free action of each stated group. The source gives symmetric-space upper bounds, reported linear lower bounds and a separate Nielsen-generator restriction for finite-shape polyhedral actions.
