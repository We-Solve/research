\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $R$ be a fixed standard-chess ruleset specifying legal moves, terminal wins, stalemate and other draws, repetition rules, move-count rules, and any draw-claim actions. Let $p_0$ be the ordinary initial position with White to move. A history records every state variable needed by $R$, including whose turn it is, castling and en-passant rights, and the past information relevant to repetition and move-count rules. A pure strategy for a player assigns a legal action to every nonterminal legal history at which that player moves.

For a ruleset under which every legal play terminates, let $\Sigma_W$ and $\Sigma_B$ be the sets of White and Black strategies and define the payoff of the resulting play by
\[
 u_R(p_0,\sigma,\tau)=
 \begin{cases}
 1,&\text{White wins},\\
 0,&\text{the game is drawn},\\
 -1,&\text{Black wins}.
 \end{cases}
\]
The game-theoretic value from White's perspective is
\[
 v_R(p_0)=\max_{\sigma\in\Sigma_W}\min_{\tau\in\Sigma_B}
             u_R(p_0,\sigma,\tau)\in\{-1,0,1\}.
\]
The requested determination is which of these three values standard chess has. Value $1$ means White has a strategy winning against every Black strategy; value $-1$ means Black has a strategy winning against every White strategy; and value $0$ means each player has a strategy avoiding defeat against every opponent strategy. This concerns play from the initial position, not merely the evaluation of selected endgames.

\subsection{Short English statement}
Determine whether optimal play from the standard initial chess position gives White a forced win, Black a forced win, or a draw. This is a strategy-level game value; the rules for draws and game termination must be fixed, and solved small endgames do not by themselves determine it.
