\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 800053}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Fix integers \(k\geq 0\) and \(0\leq r\leq k\). In a smooth interpretation, let \(M\) be a real smooth manifold of dimension \(2k+1\). A nonsingular Pfaffian equation on \(M\) is a smooth hyperplane distribution \(H\subset TM\), locally expressible as \(H|_U=\ker\alpha_U\) for a nowhere-zero smooth real one-form \(\alpha_U\). Local defining forms differ on overlaps by multiplication by nowhere-zero smooth functions.

The equation has constant class \(2r+1\) if, on every such defining neighborhood,
\[
\alpha_U\wedge(\mathrm d\alpha_U)^r\neq 0
\quad\text{at every point},\qquad
\alpha_U\wedge(\mathrm d\alpha_U)^{r+1}=0.
\]
Here \((\mathrm d\alpha_U)^0=1\); the second condition is automatic when its degree exceeds \(\dim M\). These conditions are invariant under replacement of \(\alpha_U\) by a nowhere-zero multiple. The corresponding local normal form, as stated in the source, is
\[
\mathrm d x_{2k+1}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}x_i\,\mathrm d x_{k+i}=0,
\]
where the sum is empty when \(r=0\). The source identifies \(r\) as the only local invariant and calls \(2r+1\) the class of the local Pfaffian equation.

\textbf{Problem.} For fixed \(r\), give a necessary and sufficient criterion for \(M^{2k+1}\) to admit a globally defined nonsingular Pfaffian equation of class \(2r+1\) at every point.

\textbf{Unresolved scope.} The phrase ``a Pfaffian equation in the large'' does not explicitly distinguish a hyperplane distribution defined by local forms from an equation defined by one global nowhere-zero one-form. Under the latter interpretation, the requested object is \(\alpha\in\Omega^1(M;\R)\) satisfying
\[
\alpha\wedge(\mathrm d\alpha)^r\neq 0
\quad\text{everywhere},\qquad
\alpha\wedge(\mathrm d\alpha)^{r+1}=0.
\]
This requires the defining line bundle to be trivial, equivalently the hyperplane distribution to be coorientable. Both interpretations are recorded pending clarification. The smooth category used here is an explicit interpretation: the supplied record does not specify differentiability, compactness, boundary conditions, or additional global hypotheses on \(M\).
\subsection{Short English statement}
For a fixed integer \(0\leq r\leq k\), characterize the \((2k+1)\)-dimensional manifolds that carry a nonsingular Pfaffian equation whose local normal form has exactly \(r\) paired terms everywhere. The source leaves unclear whether one global defining one-form is required and which global manifold hypotheses are intended.
