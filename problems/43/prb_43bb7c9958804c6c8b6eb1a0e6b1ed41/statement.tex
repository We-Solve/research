\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A group \(G\) is finitely generated if \(G=\langle S\rangle\) for some finite subset \(S\subseteq G\). It is left-orderable if it admits a strict total order \(<\) such that, for all \(a,b,g\in G\), \(a<b\) implies \(ga<gb\). Let \(F_2\) denote the free group on two generators, and let \(\operatorname{Hom}(G,\Z)\) denote the set of group homomorphisms from \(G\) to the additive group of integers. Here \(0\) denotes the homomorphism sending every element to zero.

Under the interpretation that ``without free subgroups'' means without nonabelian free subgroups, the supplied question asks whether there exists a group \(G\) such that
\[
\begin{gathered}
G\text{ is finitely generated and left-orderable},\\
\nexists\,H\leq G\text{ with }H\cong F_2,\\
\operatorname{Hom}(G,\Z)=\{0\}.
\end{gathered}
\]
Excluding \(F_2\) excludes every nonabelian free subgroup, since every nonabelian free group contains a subgroup isomorphic to \(F_2\).

\textit{Unresolved qualifications.} The supplied wording does not specify whether ``free subgroups'' means nonabelian free subgroups or also includes infinite cyclic subgroups. Under the latter reading, the subgroup condition would instead exclude every subgroup isomorphic to a free group of positive rank, including \(\Z\). Furthermore, the supplied statement does not require \(G\) to be nontrivial or infinite. Neither requirement has been added here; the intended target requires clarification.
