\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $\mathcal F$ be a finite family of sets, let $m=|\mathcal F|$, and let $U=\bigcup_{S\in\mathcal F}S$. Union-closure means
\[
 \forall A,B\in\mathcal F,\qquad A\cup B\in\mathcal F.
\]
For $x\in U$, define its frequency count by
\[
 d_{\mathcal F}(x)=|\{S\in\mathcal F:x\in S\}|.
\]
The literal stored question is whether every such union-closed family satisfies
\[
 \exists x\in U,\qquad 2d_{\mathcal F}(x)\ge|\mathcal F|.
\]
The supplied literature triage records that this exact formulation is false for $\mathcal F=\{\varnothing\}$: it is finite and union-closed, while $U=\varnothing$ provides no candidate element. If an empty family is also permitted by ``finite family,'' it likewise has empty union; the record does not separately state nonemptiness.

The intended nondegenerate Frankl union-closed sets conjecture is the same implication subject to $\mathcal F\ne\varnothing$ and $\mathcal F\ne\{\varnothing\}$, equivalently $U\ne\varnothing$. In the customary finite-ground-set version one additionally takes all members to be finite, so $U$ is finite. The stored sentence itself specifies only finiteness of the family, not of its members; that scope should be checked when aligning the record with the named conjecture and cited theorems. The nondegenerate conjecture is a separately identified intended target, not a silent replacement of the literal false statement.

\subsection{Short English statement}
Frankl's intended conjecture says that a nonempty finite union-closed family with nonempty union has an element in at least half its sets. The stored sentence omits the nondegeneracy condition and is refuted by the family containing only the empty set; the supplied literature still treats the intended conjecture as open.
