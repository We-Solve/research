\subsection{Checking embeddings and the loss from keeping only their equations}
The primary report supplies the definition omitted from the short extract: a nontrivial Abelian embedding of a predicate $P$ consists of maps $\sigma_i:\Sigma_i\to A$ into an Abelian group, not all constant, whose sum is zero on every satisfying tuple. Its precise conjecture is further qualified through integrality-gap structure. We test what the embedding equations alone can and cannot decide, without replacing that conjecture by a blanket tractability claim.
\begin{proposition}
The even-parity predicate on three bits has a nontrivial Abelian embedding. The three-bit OR predicate has none. Nevertheless a collection of predicates all sharing a nontrivial Abelian embedding can have an unsatisfiable instance whose associated Abelian equations are jointly satisfiable.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For even parity, use $A=\mathbb F_2$ and $\sigma_i(x)=x$. For OR, fix a coordinate $i$ and set some other coordinate to one. The tuples with $x_i=0$ and $x_i=1$ both satisfy OR. Subtracting their embedding equations gives $\sigma_i(0)=\sigma_i(1)$. Repeating for each $i$ makes every map constant, contradicting nontriviality, in every Abelian group.
For the last claim take the alphabet $\Sigma=\{0,1\}^2$ and, for each $s\in\{0,1\}^3$, define
\[
P_s((a_1,b_1),(a_2,b_2),(a_3,b_3))=1
\]
exactly when $a_1+a_2+a_3=0$ over $\mathbb F_2$ and at least one $b_i\ne s_i$. Every $P_s$ has the common nonconstant embedding $\sigma_i(a,b)=a$. On three variables impose all eight predicates $P_s$. Setting all $a_i=0$ satisfies every embedding equation. Yet each choice of the three $b_i$ fails the constraint indexed by $s=(b_1,b_2,b_3)$, so the full instance is unsatisfiable. If the parity of the $a_i$ is even it satisfies exactly seven of the eight constraints; if odd it satisfies none. Thus its exact optimum is $7/8$.
\end{proof}
This gives a concrete failure of the route ``solve the embedding equations and read off a satisfying assignment.'' Those equations retain only a necessary condition on each support. The source's proposed hybrid method also uses a semidefinite relaxation and rounding; the example neither analyzes nor contradicts that combined method. Conversely the lack of an embedding for OR by itself is not a proof of any approximation threshold.
