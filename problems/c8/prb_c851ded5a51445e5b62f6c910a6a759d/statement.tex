\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A Frobenius category is an exact category \(\mathcal C\) with enough projective objects and enough injective objects, in which the projective and injective objects coincide. Its stable category \(\underline{\mathcal C}\) is the quotient by morphisms factoring through projective-injective objects; it is triangulated, with suspension denoted by \(\Sigma\). Under the usual additional convention that this stable category is linear over a field \(k\) and has finite-dimensional morphism spaces, the stably \(2\)-Calabi--Yau condition means that there are bifunctorial isomorphisms
\[
\operatorname{Hom}_k\!\left(\operatorname{Hom}_{\underline{\mathcal C}}(X,Y),k\right)
\cong \operatorname{Hom}_{\underline{\mathcal C}}(Y,\Sigma^2X)
\qquad (X,Y\in\underline{\mathcal C}).
\]

For an object \(T\), let \(\operatorname{add}(T)\) consist of the objects isomorphic to direct summands of finite direct sums of copies of \(T\). A standard cluster-tilting convention requires
\[
\operatorname{add}(T)
=\{X\in\mathcal C:\operatorname{Ext}^1_{\mathcal C}(T,X)=0\}
=\{X\in\mathcal C:\operatorname{Ext}^1_{\mathcal C}(X,T)=0\},

A right approximation \(A\to X\), with \(A\in\operatorname{add}(T)\), is a morphism through which every morphism from an object of \(\operatorname{add}(T)\) to \(X\) factors; a left approximation is defined dually.
