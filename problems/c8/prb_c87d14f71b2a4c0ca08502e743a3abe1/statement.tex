\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(A\) be a unital commutative \(\R\)-algebra. Its character space is
\[
X(A)=\{\alpha:A\to\R:\alpha\text{ is a unital }\R\text{-algebra homomorphism}\}.
\]
Assume \(X(A)\ne\varnothing\). For \(a\in A\), define \(\widehat a(\alpha)=\alpha(a)\). Equip \(X(A)\) with the weakest topology making every evaluation map \(\widehat a:X(A)\to\R\) continuous; this topology is Hausdorff.

Here a Radon measure means a nonnegative measure on the Borel \(\sigma\)-algebra of this topology that is locally finite and inner regular with respect to compact subsets. Its support \(\operatorname{supp}\nu\) is the smallest closed subset \(C\subseteq X(A)\) such that \(\nu(X(A)\setminus C)=0\).

\textbf{Characterization problem.} For every such algebra \(A\), characterize the \(\R\)-linear maps \(L:A\to\R\) with \(L(1)=1\) for which there exists a Radon measure \(\nu\) on \(X(A)\) satisfying
\[
\operatorname{supp}\nu\text{ is compact},\qquad
L(a)=\int_{X(A)}\widehat a(\alpha)\,\dd\nu(\alpha)
\quad\text{for every }a\in A.
\]
Such a measure is called a representing Radon measure for \(L\), and a functional admitting a representing Radon measure is called a moment functional. The normalization forces \(\nu(X(A))=1\).

No positivity or growth hypothesis on \(L\) is imposed in the question: the requested characterization must determine which normalized linear functionals admit this representation. This is not an assertion that every normalized linear functional does so.
