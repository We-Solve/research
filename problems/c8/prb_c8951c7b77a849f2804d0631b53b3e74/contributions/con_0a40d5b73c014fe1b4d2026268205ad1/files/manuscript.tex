\subsection{A coordinate split and an objective-scaling obstruction}
The original asks how to strengthen the basic lift-and-project procedure and how to assess its performance. We investigate one elementary instance and one potential comparison pitfall. Consider minimizing $z=x_1+x_2+x_3$ over
\[
P=\{0\le x_i\le1:x_i+x_j\ge1\quad(i\ne j)\}.
\]
The relaxation has value $3/2$ at the half vector, since summing the three covering inequalities gives $2z\ge3$. The binary optimum is 2.
\begin{proposition}
The convex hull of the two branches $P\cap\{x_1=0\}$ and $P\cap\{x_1=1\}$ is exactly $\{x\in[0,1]^3:\sum_i x_i\ge2\}$, the integer hull of $P$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
In the zero branch the edge constraints force $x_2=x_3=1$, so the sum is 2. In the one branch, $x_2+x_3\ge1$, so the sum is at least 2. Thus the branch hull lies in the claimed set. Conversely, for any point in that set define a weight $\lambda_{111}=x_1+x_2+x_3-2$ on $(1,1,1)$ and weight $\lambda_i=1-x_i$ on the binary vector with zero only at position $i$. All weights are nonnegative and sum to one. The $i$th coordinate of their weighted sum is $\lambda_{111}+\sum_{j\ne i}\lambda_j=x_i$. Each of these four binary vectors belongs to one of the two branches. This proves both the reverse inclusion and integrality of the hull.
\end{proof}
Now add a constant $C\ge0$ to the objective. Neither the feasible region nor the cuts or optimizer changes, but the ratio of the integer value to the relaxed value becomes
\[
\frac{2+C}{3/2+C}\longrightarrow1\quad(C\to\infty).
\]
The absolute gap stays $1/2$, and the fraction of this gap closed by the coordinate split stays one. For $C=0$ and $C=100$ the ratios are respectively $4/3$ and $204/203$.
