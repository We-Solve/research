\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For the closed oriented genus-\(g\) surface convention, a pseudo-Anosov mapping class has stretch factor \(\lambda(f)>1\). In the unlogged least-dilatation convention set
\[
 \delta_g=\inf\{\lambda(f):f\in\operatorname{Mod}_g
                       \text{ pseudo-Anosov}\}.
\]
The source writes its least-dilatation quantity as \(L(\operatorname{Mod}_g)\), without repeating the preceding definition. It asks whether the sequence is nonincreasing and whether it is strictly decreasing. In the unlogged convention these are respectively
\[
 \forall g\geq1:\ \delta_{g+1}\leq\delta_g,
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad
 \forall g\geq1:\ \delta_{g+1}<\delta_g.
\]
If \(L\) is instead the logarithm of least dilatation, the same monotonicity questions are equivalent, although numerical values differ.

The source's torus discussion reduces the unlogged minimization to the largest root of \(x^2-bx+1\) over integers \(b\geq3\), with minimum at \(b=3\). It also reports that Zhirov [Zh] found the genus-two minimum restricted to pseudo-Anosovs with orientable invariant foliation, and says it was unclear whether that restricted minimum equals the unrestricted \(L(\operatorname{Mod}_2)\). The claim that explicit values were known only in genus one is a dated source statement, not a present assertion. The genus range and exact surface and normalization conventions should be checked against the missing definition.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does least pseudo-Anosov dilatation decrease, or strictly decrease, at every increase of genus? Keep the source's restricted orientable-foliation genus-two result distinct from the unrestricted minimum.
