\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
In the commuting-transformation setting identified by the research summary, let $(X,\mathcal B,\mu)$ be a probability space, let $T_1,\ldots,T_\ell$ be commuting measure-preserving transformations, and let $f_0,\ldots,f_\ell$ be bounded measurable complex-valued functions. Put $T_i^nf_i=f_i\circ T_i^n$ and
\[
 a(n)=\int_X f_0\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}T_i^nf_i\,\mathrm d\mu,
 \qquad n\ge1.
\]
These are the usual system and function hypotheses needed to read the extracted formula; the formula itself does not explicitly list them. Invertibility and any further source-wide assumptions require confirmation; no ergodicity hypothesis is added.

Under the standard uniform-limit convention, an $\ell$-step nilsequence is a uniform limit of basic sequences $n\mapsto F(g^nx_0)$, where $G/\Gamma$ is a compact nilmanifold with $G$ nilpotent of step at most $\ell$, $g\in G$, $x_0\in G/\Gamma$, and $F$ continuous. No connectedness restriction is added. The record does not spell out whether this or a basic-nilsequence convention is intended.

The question is whether for every such $\ell$, system, and choice of functions there exist an $\ell$-step nilsequence $\psi$ and a sequence $e$ such that, for every $n\ge1$,
\[
 a(n)=\psi(n)+e(n),\qquad
 \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\sum_{n=1}^N|e(n)|=0.
\]
The error condition is absolute Cesàro nullity along initial intervals. It is stronger than merely having a signed mean zero and does not assert uniform nullity over all translated intervals.
