\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For subsets \(B,C\subseteq\R\), define their sum and difference by
\[
B+C=\{b+c:b\in B,\ c\in C\},\qquad B-C=\{b-c:b\in B,\ c\in C\}.
\]
All neighborhoods and interiors in the following question are taken in the usual topology of \(\R\). For \(A\subseteq\R\), write
\[
4A=A+A+A+A=\{a_1+a_2+a_3+a_4:a_1,a_2,a_3,a_4\in A\},
\]
where the four elements need not be distinct.

\textbf{Question (Banakh; supplied Problem 11).} Is it true that for every compact set \(A\subseteq\R\),
\[
\bigl(\exists\varepsilon>0\;(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\subseteq A-A\bigr)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{int}_{\R}(4A)\ne\varnothing?
\]
Equivalently, under the displayed hypothesis, must there exist \(x\in\R\) and \(\delta>0\) such that
\[
(x-\delta,x+\delta)\subseteq A+A+A+A?
\]
