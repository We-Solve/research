\section{Are there infinitely many Fibonacci primes}\label{9400149}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 9400149 · Number Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Define Fibonacci numbers by $F_0=0$, $F_1=1$ and $F_{n+2}=F_{n+1}+F_n$ for $n\ge0$. A Fibonacci prime is a prime value $F_n$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Are there infinitely many integers $n\ge0$ for which $F_n$ is prime? \sref{9400149}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Do infinitely many Fibonacci numbers take prime values?
