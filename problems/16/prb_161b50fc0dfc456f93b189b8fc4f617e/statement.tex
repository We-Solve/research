\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $Q$ be a positive-definite real binary quadratic form, as specified in the preceding section 6.4 setup in companion permanent ID $20003337$. For $T>0$ and Borel $B\subset\R^2$, define the raw ordered-vector count
\[
 N_T(B)=\#\left\{(x_1,x_2,x_3)\in(\Z^2)^3:
 \begin{array}{l}
 x_i\ne x_j\ (i\ne j),\quad\|x_i\|<T\ (i=1,2,3),\\
 (Q(x_1)-Q(x_2),Q(x_2)-Q(x_3))\in B
 \end{array}\right\}.
\]
J. Marklof's Question 45 asks for its asymptotics as $T\to\infty$, in particular for $B=(a,b)\times(c,d)$ with $a<b$, $c<d$. Distinctness is of lattice vectors, not of the values $Q(x_i)$; the count keeps multiplicity. The following source continuation predicts a suitably normalized area law for generic $Q$, but does not specify genericity or normalization in the assigned extract. Its closing Berry--Tabor sentence is truncated. Here the Euclidean cutoff is used for the quantitative model below; its numerical coefficient depends on that convention.

The attached aid reports an exact $S_3$ symmetry, obtained by permuting the ordered vectors. The six transformations of a gap pair $(u,v)$ are
\[
 (u,v),\ (u+v,-v),\ (-u,u+v),\ (v,-u-v),\ (-u-v,u),\ (-v,-u).
\]
For every corresponding integral linear map $w$ and every Borel $B$,
\[
 N_T(wB)=N_T(B).
\]
This is the $A_2$ gap action; no asymptotic or genericity assumption is needed. If the coefficients of $Q$ have a common integer denominator $M\ge1$, all gaps lie in $M^{-1}\Z^2$, so the raw support is discrete.

Evenness $Q(x)=Q(-x)$ also creates collision strata on
\[
 u=0,\qquad v=0,\qquad u+v=0.
\]
For example, every ordered pair $(x,y)$ with $x\ne0$, $y\notin\{x,-x\}$ and both vectors inside the cutoff gives the distinct-vector triple $(x,-x,y)$ with gap $(0,Q(x)-Q(y))$. Permutations give the other collision lines. Thus ordinary pair-count mass can contribute singular triple-gap mass; vector distinctness does not remove equal-value collisions.

For the special positive diagonal form $Q(m,n)=\alpha m^2+\gamma n^2$, $\alpha,\gamma>0$, the aid reports an unconditional origin atom under the Euclidean cutoff:
\[
 N_T(\{(0,0)\})\ge6\pi T^2-CT
 \quad\text{for all sufficiently large }T
\]
for some constant $C$. Each sign orbit with both coordinates nonzero has four vectors of the same value and contributes $4\cdot3\cdot2$ ordered distinct triples. This is a lower bound; further equal values may add triples. The symmetry and collision results explain why the literal raw statistic must be distinguished from a desymmetrized Poisson statistic. They do not solve the generic fixed-form asymptotic problem.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Determine the triple-gap distribution for distinct lattice vectors of a positive binary quadratic form. The supplied results preserve ordering and multiplicity: permutations give an exact $S_3$ symmetry, while sign symmetries force collision lines and, for diagonal forms, a large atom at the origin.
