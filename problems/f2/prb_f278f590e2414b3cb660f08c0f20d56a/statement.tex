\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
The supplied question is: ``Can there be an indecomposable ultrafilter on a cardinal that is neither measurable nor the limit of countably many measurable cardinals?'' The record does not define ``indecomposable'' or specify uniformity, a lower bound on the cardinal, or the intended consistency assumptions. Consequently, the following is a conditional formalization requiring confirmation of these conventions.

Let \(\kappa\) be an uncountable cardinal. An ultrafilter on \(\kappa\) is a family \(U\subseteq\mathcal P(\kappa)\) such that \(\kappa\in U\), \(\varnothing\notin U\), \(U\) is closed under finite intersections and supersets within \(\kappa\), and exactly one of \(A\) and \(\kappa\setminus A\) belongs to \(U\) for every \(A\subseteq\kappa\). It is uniform if \(|A|=\kappa\) for every \(A\in U\).

One convention for indecomposability of a uniform ultrafilter \(U\) on \(\kappa\) is
\[
\forall\text{ cardinals }\lambda<\kappa\quad
\forall f:\kappa\longrightarrow\lambda\quad
\exists A\in U\quad |f[A]|\leq\aleph_0.
\tag{*}
\]

Interpret ``the limit of countably many measurable cardinals'' as the existence of a strictly increasing sequence \(\langle\mu_n:n<\omega\rangle\) of measurable cardinals below \(\kappa\) such that \(\sup_{n<\omega}\mu_n=\kappa\).
