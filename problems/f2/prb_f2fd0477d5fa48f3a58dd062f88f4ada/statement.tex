\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A group \(G\) is \emph{periodic} if every element of \(G\) has finite order. For a class \(X\) of groups, say that \(G\) is \emph{saturated with groups from \(X\)} if
\[
\forall K\leq G,\quad |K|<\infty\ \Longrightarrow\ \exists L\leq G\ \exists T\in X:\quad K\leq L\ \text{and}\ L\cong T.
\]
For a positive integer \(r\), let \(\mathcal L_r\) denote the class of finite simple groups of Lie type of rank at most \(r\), with rank understood in the source's terminology.

The question proposed by A. K. Shl\"epkin is whether every periodic group \(G\) satisfying
\[
\exists r\in\Z_{\geq 1}\ \forall K\leq G,\quad
|K|<\infty\ \Longrightarrow\ \exists L\leq G\ \exists T\in\mathcal L_r:\quad K\leq L\ \text{and}\ L\cong T
\]
is itself a simple group of Lie type of finite rank. The bound \(r\) is independent of \(K\), but may depend on \(G\). The question does not require the rank of the conclusion to equal \(r\).

The supplied source does not specify the rank convention for twisted groups or the precise scope of the concluding phrase ``simple group of Lie type of finite rank,'' including the permitted fields and infinite forms. These qualifications remain unresolved here. No assumption that \(G\) is finite or locally finite is added.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Must a periodic group in which every finite subgroup lies inside a finite simple subgroup of Lie type, with one uniform bound on the ranks of these containing groups, itself be a simple group of Lie type of finite rank? The source leaves the rank convention and the precise scope of the conclusion unspecified.
