\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


For observables $B_i$ and density matrix $\Gamma$, the expectation vector is $b_i=\operatorname{Tr}(B_i\Gamma)$. Constrained search fixes $b$ while minimizing energy. Multipliers $\beta=(\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_m)$ enter a Hamiltonian of the form $H_0+\sum_i\beta_iB_i$, with the sign convention fixed by the source. The report initially assumes a finite-dimensional Hilbert space, self-adjoint operators $H_0,B_i$, commuting $B_i$, and linear independence of $\{I,B_1,\ldots,B_m\}$. It proves uniqueness of the multiplier vector for regular expectation values and treats critical or degenerate exceptions.

\paragraph{Question recovered from the primary report.}Extend this constrained-search and multiplier theory to an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space, potentially retaining a finite family of observables $B_i$, with appropriate domains and regularity assumptions.

\paragraph{An infinite-dimensional loss-of-attainment test.} Let the Hilbert space be \(\ell^2(\mathbb N)\), with basis \(e_n\), bounded self-adjoint \(H_0e_n=n^{-1}e_n\), and observable \(Be_n=e_n\) for even \(n\), \(Be_n=0\) for odd \(n\). The operators commute and \(I,B\) are linearly independent, as required by the finite-dimensional source assumptions. Prescribe \(\operatorname{Tr}(B\Gamma)=1/2\) for trace-one positive density operators.
\paragraph{The infimum is not a ground state.} The admissible states
\[
 \Gamma_k=\tfrac12|e_{2k}\rangle\langle e_{2k}|+
 \tfrac12|e_{2k+1}\rangle\langle e_{2k+1}|
\]
have energy \(\tfrac12((2k)^{-1}+(2k+1)^{-1})\to0\). Every admissible density operator has strictly positive energy: its diagonal probabilities are nonnegative, sum to one, and each coefficient \(1/n\) is positive. Hence the constrained infimum zero is not attained. For any real multiplier \(\beta\), \(H_0+\beta B\) has spectral infimum \(\min(0,\beta)\), approached along one parity but never attained by an eigenvector. No multiplier repairs the missing ground state.
\paragraph{Implication and limit.} This counterexample disproves an assumption-free infinite-dimensional transfer of finite-dimensional existence, even with bounded commuting independent observables. It does not refute the source programme, which explicitly asks for suitable domains and regularity assumptions. The first missing ingredient is compactness or coercivity ensuring attainment; only then can uniqueness of multipliers and representations, regular/critical values and degeneracies be addressed. No claim to supersede the already developed finite-dimensional theory is made.
