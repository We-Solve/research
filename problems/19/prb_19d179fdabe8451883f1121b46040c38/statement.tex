\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A Coxeter system consists of a group \(W\) with a generating set \(S\) and a presentation
\[
W=\left\langle S\ \middle|\ (st)^{m(s,t)}=1\text{ whenever }m(s,t)<\infty\right\rangle,
\]
where \(m(s,s)=1\) and \(m(s,t)=m(t,s)\in\{2,3,\ldots\}\cup\{\infty\}\) for distinct \(s,t\in S\). The value \(m(s,t)=\infty\) imposes no relation on \(st\). A Coxeter group is a group admitting such a presentation. Its rank relative to this presentation is \(|S|\). Write \(\operatorname{Aut}(W)\) for its group of abstract automorphisms. A group \(G\) virtually maps onto a group \(V\) if there are a subgroup \(H\leq G\) of finite index and a surjective homomorphism \(H\twoheadrightarrow V\).

The supplied conjecture asks whether, for every infinite Coxeter group \(W_\Gamma\), there exist an infinite Coxeter group \(V\), a subgroup \(H\leq\operatorname{Aut}(W_\Gamma)\), and a surjective homomorphism \(\varphi:H\twoheadrightarrow V\) such that
\[
[\operatorname{Aut}(W_\Gamma):H]<\infty.
\]
The group \(V\), the subgroup \(H\), and the homomorphism \(\varphi\) may depend on \(W_\Gamma\).
