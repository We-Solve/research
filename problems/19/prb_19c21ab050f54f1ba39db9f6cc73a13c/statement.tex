\section{Excess Argument Sums in Dividing Factorial Products}\label{statement}


\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Unless a section says otherwise, $\N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$,
$\Z$ is the ring of integers, $\Q,\R,\C$ are the rational, real, and complex
fields, $[N]=\{1,\ldots,\lfloor N\rfloor\}$, and $\log$ is the natural
logarithm. For real functions of a parameter tending to infinity,
$f\ll g$ and $f=O(g)$ mean $|f|\leq Cg$ eventually for some fixed $C$;
$f\gg g$ reverses this comparison; $f=o(g)$ means $f/g\to0$;
$f\sim g$ means $f/g\to1$; and $f\asymp g$ means both order bounds.
A subscript on $O$ or $\ll$ specifies allowed constant dependence.
The expression $n^{o(1)}$ in an upper bound means growth smaller than
$n^\epsilon$ for each fixed $\epsilon>0$. Local definitions govern any

For a nonnegative integer $n$, $n!=\prod_{j=1}^n j$ with $0!=1$. Factorial arguments and any factors or denominator indices below are integers in the explicitly stated ranges. Distinctness is imposed only where specified. \sref{statement}{1} \sref{statement}{2}

\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For any $k\geq 2$ let $g_k(n)$ denote the maximum value of $ (a_1+\cdots+a_k)-n $ where $a_1,\ldots,a_k$ are integers such that $a_1!\cdots a_k! \mid n!$. Can one show that $ \sum_{n\leq x}g_k(n) \sim c_k x\log x $ for some constant $c_k$? Is it true that there is a constant $c_k$ such that for almost all $n<x$ we have $ g_k(n)=c_k\log x+o(\log x)? $ \sref{statement}{1}

{\small\emph{Transcription note.} Only unambiguous escape/formatting damage and nonmathematical serialization debris were repaired in the displayed source statement.}

\subsection{Short English statement}

How large, on average and typically, can the sum of arguments of a fixed number of factorials be when their product divides another factorial?
