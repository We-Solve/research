\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A group \(H\) is \emph{polycyclic} if it admits a finite series
\[
H=H_0\trianglerighteq H_1\trianglerighteq\cdots\trianglerighteq H_n=\{1\},
\]
where \(H_{i+1}\) is normal in \(H_i\) and \(H_i/H_{i+1}\) is cyclic for every \(0\leq i<n\). Here \emph{almost polycyclic} is interpreted in its standard sense: a group containing a polycyclic subgroup of finite index.

For a group \(G\), its integral group ring \(\Z[G]\) consists of the formal sums \(\sum_{g\in G}a_g g\), where \(a_g\in\Z\) and only finitely many coefficients are nonzero. Addition is coefficientwise and multiplication extends the group multiplication distributively. A ring is left Noetherian if every ascending chain of left ideals stabilizes, and right Noetherian if every ascending chain of right ideals stabilizes. For \(\Z[G]\), these conditions are equivalent because \(\sum_g a_g g\mapsto\sum_g a_g g^{-1}\) is an involutive ring anti-automorphism; thus the source's unspecified handedness does not affect the question.

Does there exist a group \(G\) such that \(\Z[G]\) is Noetherian and \(G\) contains no polycyclic subgroup of finite index? Equivalently, is there a group \(G\) satisfying
\[
\Z[G]\text{ is Noetherian}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\forall H\leq G,\qquad [G:H]<\infty\ \Longrightarrow\ H\text{ is not polycyclic}?
\]
No restriction on the group is imposed beyond these conditions. The interpretation of ``almost polycyclic'' above is conventional; the supplied excerpt does not explicitly define it.
\subsection{Short English statement}
