\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
The supplied question is: \emph{When does symplectic reduction produce a symplectic singularity?}

Under the usual complex algebraic convention, a symplectic singularity is a normal complex algebraic variety \(X\) whose smooth locus \(X_{\mathrm{reg}}\) admits a regular two-form \(\omega\) satisfying \(\mathrm d\omega=0\), such that \(\omega\) is nondegenerate at every point of \(X_{\mathrm{reg}}\), and such that for every resolution of singularities \(\pi:\widetilde X\to X\), the form \(\pi^*\omega\), initially defined on \(\pi^{-1}(X_{\mathrm{reg}})\), extends to a regular two-form on all of \(\widetilde X\). The extension is not required to be nondegenerate.
