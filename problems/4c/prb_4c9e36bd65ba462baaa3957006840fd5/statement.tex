\section{Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 5.28}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Write $\mathbb D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}$ and $\mathbb T=\partial\mathbb D$. All Taylor coefficients below are taken at $0$.Let $f$ be a nonconstant function holomorphic on $\mathbb D$ and continuous on $\overline{\mathbb D}$. For $0<\delta<2$, define
\[\omega(f,\delta)=\sup_{\substack{z,w\in\mathbb D\\|z-w|\le\delta}}|f(z)-f(w)|,\qquad
\widetilde\omega(f,\delta)=\sup_{\substack{z,w\in\mathbb T\\|z-w|\le\delta}}|f(z)-f(w)|.
\]
Nonconstancy makes the denominator positive; constant functions have zero modulus in both definitions.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Must every such $f$ satisfy $\lim_{\delta\downarrow0}\omega(f,\delta)/\widetilde\omega(f,\delta)=1$? \sref{statement}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Historical question: are the small-scale interior and boundary moduli of continuity asymptotically equal for every disc-algebra function? The source update records a negative answer.
