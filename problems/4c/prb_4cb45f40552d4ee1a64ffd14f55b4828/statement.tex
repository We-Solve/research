\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A group \(G\) is finitely generated if \(G=\langle S\rangle\) for some finite subset \(S\subseteq G\). Define its derived series by \(G^{(0)}=G\) and
\[
G^{(n+1)}=[G^{(n)},G^{(n)}]\qquad(n\geq 0),
\]
where \([H,H]\) is the subgroup generated by the commutators \(x^{-1}y^{-1}xy\) with \(x,y\in H\). The group \(G\) is soluble if \(G^{(d)}=\{1_G\}\) for some integer \(d\geq 0\). It is Hopfian if every surjective group homomorphism \(G\to G\) is injective, and hence an automorphism.

The direct product \(A\times B\) has multiplication \((a,b)(a',b')=(aa',bb')\).

\textbf{Question (P. M. Neumann, 1976; Kourovka Notebook Problem 5.38).} For every pair of finitely generated soluble Hopfian groups \(A\) and \(B\), is \(A\times B\) Hopfian? Equivalently, must every surjective group homomorphism
\[
\varphi:A\times B\longrightarrow A\times B
\]
be injective?
