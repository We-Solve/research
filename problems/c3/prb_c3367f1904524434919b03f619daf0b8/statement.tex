\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $G$ be the Hamiltonian group of the symplectic manifold intended by the source. A bi-invariant distance is a genuine metric $\rho$ satisfying
\[
 \rho(afb,agb)=\rho(f,g)\qquad(a,b,f,g\in G).
\]
For a symplectic ball $B$ define its displacement energy with respect to this distance by
\[
 e(\rho,B)=\inf\{\rho(\operatorname{id},f):f\in G,
                                      \ f(B)\cap B=\varnothing\},
\]
with $\inf\varnothing=+\infty$. A symplectic ball of capacity $a$ conventionally means a symplectic image of a standard Euclidean ball of radius $r$ with $a=\pi r^2$; the report's normalization and boundary convention should be checked.

The question asks for the quantifier pattern
\[
 \forall\rho\text{ bi-invariant distance on }G\ \exists C_\rho>0
 \ \forall B\text{ an admissible symplectic ball of capacity }a:
 \qquad e(\rho,B)\ge C_\rho a.
\]
The constant may depend on the distance and the fixed group/ambient data, but must be independent of the ball and its capacity. It is not a single constant shared by every metric. Nondisplaceable balls satisfy the displayed lower bound vacuously under the infinite-energy convention; the substantive issue is the displaceable case.

The ambient symplectic manifold, dimension, support restrictions on $G$, permitted capacities, and metric regularity are not supplied. The source says every bi-invariant distance; it does not authorize restriction to the Hofer distance, to continuous metrics, or to metrics induced by a particular norm. Conversely, no unbounded-capacity ambient setting is silently imposed. These missing scope conditions matter for an assertion uniform over all balls. The inequality is retained as a source question without supplying unmentioned hypotheses or a proof.
\subsection{Short English statement}
For each bi-invariant Hamiltonian-group metric, the question asks for a positive metric-dependent constant giving a linear lower bound on the energy needed to displace every symplectic ball. The constant must be uniform over balls, with the ambient manifold and capacity range still unspecified.
