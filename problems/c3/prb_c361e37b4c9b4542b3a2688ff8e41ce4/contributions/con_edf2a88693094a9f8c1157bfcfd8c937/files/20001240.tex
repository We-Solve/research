\section{Literature and context check}

\section{Research attempt: A finite grid construction and the failure of rank as a statistical criterion}
Let $L=\{x^{\alpha_1},\ldots,x^{\alpha_N}\}$ be distinct monomials in $d\ge1$ variables, with $N\ge1$, and set $m=\max_{i,j}\alpha_{i,j}$. Evaluate them on $G=\{0,1,\ldots,m\}^d$. The resulting matrix has full column rank over $\mathbb Q$. To prove this, a linear combination vanishing on $G$ is a polynomial of degree at most $m$ in each variable. Such a polynomial must be zero: for $d=1$, a nonzero degree-at-most-$m$ polynomial has at most $m$ roots, since each root gives a linear factor by division and repeated division cannot exceed the degree. For $d>1$, fix the first $d-1$ grid coordinates and view the polynomial in the last variable. Its $m+1$ zero values force all its coefficient polynomials to vanish on the smaller grid. Induction makes all coefficients zero. Distinct formal monomials then force the proposed linear combination to be trivial.

One can therefore scan the rows, retaining a row whenever it increases rank, until $N$ rows have been retained. Failure to reach $N$ would make the span of all rows have dimension less than $N$, contradicting full column rank. The corresponding $N$ distinct rational grid points form a design $D$ for which the monomials of $L$ are independent modulo $I(D)$: a combination in that ideal evaluates to zero on the invertible $N$-row submatrix. This gives a terminating exact construction. Its candidate grid has $(m+1)^d$ points, so the argument is no claim of a polynomial-time algorithm in the sparse binary encoding of the exponents.

The first tempting shortcut, sampling on a single diagonal, fails already for $L=\{x,y\}$: at every point $(t,t)$ the two evaluation columns coincide, regardless of how many points are sampled. The second shortcut, accepting any full-rank design as statistically good, fails for $L=\{1,x\}$ and $D_\varepsilon=\{0,\varepsilon\}$. The determinant is $\varepsilon\ne0$, so interpolation is valid. But if the two observed responses have independent mean-zero errors of variance $\sigma^2>0$, the fitted slope error is $(e_2-e_1)/\varepsilon$ and has variance $2\sigma^2/\varepsilon^2$, which diverges as $\varepsilon\to0$. Independence of the polynomial classes therefore supplies no uniform estimation precision.
