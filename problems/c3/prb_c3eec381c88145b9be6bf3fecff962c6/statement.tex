\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(F\) range over fields of characteristic \(2\). Interpret a quadratic form over \(F\) as a map \(q\colon V\to F\) on a finite-dimensional \(F\)-vector space satisfying \(q(av)=a^2q(v)\), with bilinear polar form
\[
b_q(v,w)=q(v+w)-q(v)-q(w).
\]
The form is anisotropic if \(q(v)=0\) implies \(v=0\), and totally singular if \(b_q=0\). Let \(Q'(F)\) denote the anisotropic quadratic forms over \(F\) that are not totally singular.

To make the supplied terminology explicit, use its standard interpretation: put
\[
R_q=\operatorname{rad}(b_q)=\{v\in V:b_q(v,w)=0\text{ for every }w\in V\},
\qquad
\operatorname{ql}(q)=q|_{R_q},
\]
and define
\[
\operatorname{rdim}(q)=\operatorname{rank}(b_q)=\dim V-\dim R_q.
\]
Thus \(\operatorname{ql}(q)\) is the quasilinear part, of dimension \(\dim R_q\), and the regular dimension is even because \(b_q\) is alternating. These definitions explicate terms that the supplied record names without defining internally; their agreement with the cited report requires review.
