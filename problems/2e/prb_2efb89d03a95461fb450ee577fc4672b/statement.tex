\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(I\subset\R\) be a finite interval of positive length and \(m\) Lebesgue measure. Real measurable functions \(f,g\) on \(I\) are rearrangements of each other when
\[
m\{x\in I:g(x)<y\}=m\{x\in I:f(x)<y\}
\qquad\text{for every }y\in\R.
\]
Equivalently, they have equal pushforward measures on \(\R\) under \(m|_I\). For a class of real functions \(f\) with a measurable derivative \(f'\) defined at least almost everywhere, Rubel asks to classify those satisfying
\[
m\{x\in I:f'(x)<y\}=m\{x\in I:f(x)<y\}
\qquad(y\in\R).
\]
Values of \(f'\) at the interval endpoints or other null exceptional sets do not affect this condition.

The source examples are \(f(x)=ke^x\) on any finite interval and \(f(x)=k\sin x\) on \([0,\pi/2]\), with real constant \(k\).
