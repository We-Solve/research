\section{Research Problems in Function Theory — Problem 6.25}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Write $\mathbb D=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|<1\}$ and $\mathbb T=\partial\mathbb D$.Let $p\ge1$ be an integer. A nonconstant holomorphic $f(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}a_nz^n$ is $p$-valent if $f(z)=w$ has at most $p$ solutions in $\mathbb D$, counted with multiplicity, for every $w\in\mathbb C$. For $1\le k\le p<n$, put
\[D(p,k,n)=\frac{2kn\prod_{j=1}^p(n^2-j^2)}{(p+k)!(p-k)!(n^2-k^2)}.\]
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Is Goodman's coefficient bound
\[|a_n|\le\sum_{k=1}^p|a_k|D(p,k,n)\qquad(n>p)\]
valid for every $p$-valent $f$? Determine its sharpness for all $p,k,n$ if it holds. \sref{statement}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does Goodman’s proposed coefficient inequality hold sharply for all holomorphic p-valent disc functions?
