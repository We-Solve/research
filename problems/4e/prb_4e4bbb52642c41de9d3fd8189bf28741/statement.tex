\section{Extremal Exchangeability Beyond Natural Noncommutative Products}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


\paragraph{Definitions and conventions.} An algebraic probability space is a unital $*$-algebra $A$ with a positive normalized state $\phi$. Write $A^{*\,\mathbb N}$ for the unital free product of countably many copies, with canonical embeddings $j_n$. A state on it is exchangeable if invariant under every finite permutation of the copies, and extremal if not a nontrivial convex combination of exchangeable states. For $B,C\subseteq A$, the source's independence requires an extremal exchangeable state $\phi_\infty$ whose restriction to separate copies of $B$ and $C$ agrees with their original joint distribution under $\phi$. The five universal product notions named in the source are tensor, free, Boolean, monotone, and antimonotone independence. In a represented von Neumann setting the tail algebra is the intersection of algebras generated by copies with indices at least $n$ as $n\to\infty$; triviality means that this intersection is only the scalars.

\paragraph{Source scope and qualifications.} The curated statement speaks of replacing extremal shift-invariance by shift-invariance with a trivial tail. The primary item (5) actually says extremal S\_infinity-invariance and S\_infinity-invariance with trivial tail field, meaning exchangeability, not mere shift invariance. These conditions differ and are recorded separately rather than silently conflated.

\paragraph{Primary-source invariance convention.} In the report's comparison, replace extremality among $S_\infty$-invariant states by $S_\infty$-invariance with trivial tail field. $S_\infty$ acts by finite permutations; the one-sided shift is a weaker symmetry.
