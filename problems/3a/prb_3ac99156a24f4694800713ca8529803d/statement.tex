\section*{A Markus–Yamabe problem for differential equations}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a smooth map $X:\mathbb R^3\to\mathbb R^3$, consider the autonomous
differential equation $\dot z=X(z)$. Assume that $X$ has an equilibrium,
which after translation is $X(0)=0$, and that its Jacobian is Hurwitz at
every point:
\[
 \operatorname{Re}\lambda<0\quad
 \text{for every eigenvalue }\lambda\text{ of }DX(z),
 \quad z\in\mathbb R^3.
\]
The question is whether some such vector field has a nonconstant periodic
solution. Precisely, does there exist $T>0$ and a nonconstant differentiable
map $\gamma:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R^3$ with
$\gamma(t+T)=\gamma(t)$ and $\gamma'(t)=X(\gamma(t))$?

The spectral assumption is pointwise, with no uniform negative bound on
the real parts required. Smooth means $C^\infty$; the vector field need
not be polynomial. The periodic orbit need not be attracting, hyperbolic,
or isolated. Its existence is the requested obstruction, so a counterexample
to global stability caused solely by an escaping trajectory does not
answer this particular question.

For context, global asymptotic stability of the equilibrium would mean
Lyapunov stability together with existence for all positive time and
convergence to zero for every initial condition. The general
Markus–Yamabe implication from the stated Jacobian condition to that
conclusion fails in dimension three. The source asks whether failure can
specifically be witnessed by recurrent motion along a closed nonconstant
trajectory under the same everywhere-Hurwitz hypothesis.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can an everywhere-Hurwitz smooth vector field on three-dimensional space have a nonconstant periodic orbit?
