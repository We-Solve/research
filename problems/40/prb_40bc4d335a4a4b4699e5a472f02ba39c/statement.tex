\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 700479}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A variety of groups is a class \(\mathcal V\) consisting of all groups satisfying some set of group identities. An identity is an equation \(w(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=1\), where \(w\) is a group word, required to hold for every substitution of group elements for its variables. Groups are not assumed to be finite.

Let \(\mathsf{Var}(\mathrm{Grp})\) be the lattice of all varieties of groups, ordered by inclusion. Its meet is intersection, and \(\mathcal V\vee\mathcal W\) is the least variety containing both \(\mathcal V\) and \(\mathcal W\). A lattice automorphism is a bijection \(\Phi\colon\mathsf{Var}(\mathrm{Grp})\to\mathsf{Var}(\mathrm{Grp})\) such that, for all varieties \(\mathcal V,\mathcal W\),
\[
\mathcal V\subseteq\mathcal W
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\Phi(\mathcal V)\subseteq\Phi(\mathcal W).
\]
It is non-trivial if it is not the identity map.

\textbf{Question (A. V. Kuznetsov, 1982).} Does there exist such an automorphism \(\Phi\) with
\[
\exists\,\mathcal V\in\mathsf{Var}(\mathrm{Grp})\qquad
\Phi(\mathcal V)\ne\mathcal V?
\]
The supplied registry labels this problem open; that label is supplied curation and is not an independently verified assessment of its current status.
\subsection{Short English statement}
A. V. Kuznetsov asks whether the lattice of all group varieties has an automorphism that moves at least one variety. Here varieties are classes defined by group identities, and the lattice is ordered by inclusion.
