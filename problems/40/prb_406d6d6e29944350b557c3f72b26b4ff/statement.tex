\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $T_n$ be the source's uniformly sampled spherical triangulation with $n$ faces, for admissible $n$. Under a circle-packing model, place disks on $S^2$ with tangency graph $T_n$, using the source's conformal-barycenter normalization. If $z_e$ is the contact point for an edge $e$, the natural empirical probability measure is
\[
\mu_n=\mu_{P T_n}=\frac1{|E(T_n)|}\sum_{e\in E(T_n)}\delta_{z_e}.
\]
This equal-contact weighting is the intended empirical interpretation; the precise model and normalization are not reproduced.

The compound problem asks to characterize subsequential limits $\mu_\infty$, including: their support dimension and singularity; uniqueness in law; a possible relation to the Gaussian free field and KPZ; and their relationship with the limiting random metric $d_\infty$.

One precise weak-limit setting is convergence in distribution of the random measures in $\mathcal P(S^2)$ with its weak topology. Support dimension then means $\dim_H(\operatorname{supp}\mu_\infty)$ in the round metric, while singularity would ordinarily be relative to spherical area. Dimension of the support and dimension of the measure are different; a singular measure can have full support. Uniqueness in law would mean all subsequential distributional limits have the same law after fixing the normalization. No deterministic limit measure is presupposed.

The triangulation class (rooted or unrooted, simple or allowing multiple edges), uniform weighting, and admissible face counts are unspecified. The barycenter construction and residual rotations must also be fixed: a Möbius normalization can leave rotational freedom, which affects a law on embedded measures unless treated consistently. Almost-sure limits in a coupling differ from weak limits in law.

The GFF/KPZ request does not specify a field normalization, parameter or proposed transformation, and the summary's undefined CP/LSAT terminology does not supply one. The metric $d_\infty$ needs its graph-distance rescaling, topology of convergence and joint identification with the packed sphere before a measure--metric relation can be formulated. No particular Liouville parameter, metric-area identity or coupling is invented.
