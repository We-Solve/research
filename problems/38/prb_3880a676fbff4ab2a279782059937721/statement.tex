\section{Are there infinitely many Pierpont primes}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A Pierpont prime is a prime $p$ of the form $p=2^a3^b+1$ for integers $a,b\ge0$. Equivalently, $p-1$ has no prime divisors other than $2$ and $3$; the empty factorization $p-1=1$ is allowed.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Are there infinitely many primes of this form? \sref{statement}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Are there infinitely many primes one more than a product of powers of two and three?
