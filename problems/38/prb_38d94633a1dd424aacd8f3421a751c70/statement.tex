\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $T$ be a countable first-order theory in the intended countable language, and let $\Gamma=\{p_i:i\in\N\}$ be a countable family of types. Under a standard finite-tuple, parameter-free convention, $p_i$ is a set of formulas in a fixed finite tuple of variables, and $M$ omits $p_i$ when no tuple in $M$ satisfies all formulas of $p_i$. The source does not state whether types are partial or complete, over parameters, or possibly in infinitely many variables.

Define
\[
 K=\{M:M\models T\text{ and }M\text{ omits every }p_i\in\Gamma\}.
\]
Write $\omega_1$ for the first uncountable ordinal, $\aleph_{\omega_1}$ for the corresponding aleph-indexed cardinal, and $\mathfrak c=2^{\aleph_0}$ for the continuum.

The proposed transfer question is whether, for every such $T$ and $\Gamma$,
\[
 \bigl(\exists M\in K:\ |M|=\aleph_{\omega_1}\bigr)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \bigl(\exists N\in K:\ |N|=\mathfrak c\bigr).
\]
No equality between $\mathfrak c$ and a particular aleph, continuum-size inequality, cofinality assumption, or form of Martin's Axiom is imposed. The countable language and type conventions need matching to the intended source before a cardinal-transfer theorem is fully specified.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does a countable theory with countably many omitted types transfer model existence from $\aleph_{\omega_1}$ to the continuum? The continuum is left as the actual set-theoretic cardinal $2^{\aleph_0}$.
