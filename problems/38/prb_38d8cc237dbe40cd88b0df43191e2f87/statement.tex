\section{New Mersenne conjecture}\label{9400133}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 9400133 · Number Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For an odd positive integer $p$, consider these three conditions: (i) for some integer $k\ge1$, $p$ equals one of $2^k-1$, $2^k+1$, $4^k-3$, $4^k+3$; (ii) $2^p-1$ is prime; (iii) $(2^p+1)/3$ is prime. The quotient in (iii) is an integer because $p$ is odd. The variable $p$ is not assumed prime in advance.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Whenever any two of conditions (i), (ii) and (iii) hold for such $p$, does the remaining condition also hold? \sref{9400133}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
For every odd positive exponent, do any two of these special-exponent, Mersenne-prime and Wagstaff-prime properties force the third?
