\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A closed manifold is a compact manifold without boundary. In the Riemannian interpretation of the source, a geodesic is closed if its unit-speed parametrization \(\gamma:\R\to M\) is periodic: there exists \(T_\gamma>0\) such that \(\gamma(t+T_\gamma)=\gamma(t)\) for every \(t\in\R\). The period may depend on the geodesic; no common period is assumed. A CROSS is a compact rank-one Riemannian symmetric space, equipped with its standard symmetric metric.

The supplied question is as follows. Let \(M\) be a closed manifold not covered by a sphere, and let \(g\) be a metric on \(M\) for which all geodesics are closed. Must \((M,g)\) be isometric to a CROSS equipped with its standard metric?

Here, ``not covered by a sphere'' is understood in the covering-space sense: there is no covering map \(S^{\dim M}\to M\). The source does not explicitly specify connectedness, the class of metrics, or the normalization of the standard metric. The periodic-geodesic formulation above uses the customary smooth Riemannian interpretation. Whether the conclusion permits an overall rescaling remains unresolved in the supplied wording, so the question does not specify a unique metric-normalized assertion.
