\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 800240}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A domain \(\Omega\subset\C^2\) is a nonempty connected open set. It is pseudoconvex if it admits a continuous plurisubharmonic exhaustion function \(\rho:\Omega\to\R\): for every \(c\in\R\), the set \(\{z\in\Omega:\rho(z)\le c\}\) is compact in \(\Omega\). Here plurisubharmonic means that the restriction to each complex affine line is subharmonic on every connected component of its intersection with \(\Omega\).

Let \(S^2=\{x\in\R^3:\|x\|=1\}\). Does there exist a pseudoconvex domain \(\Omega\subset\C^2\) homotopy equivalent to \(S^2\)? Explicitly, do there exist such an \(\Omega\) and continuous maps
\[
f:\Omega\longrightarrow S^2,\qquad g:S^2\longrightarrow\Omega
\]
with
\[
g\circ f\simeq\operatorname{id}_{\Omega},\qquad f\circ g\simeq\operatorname{id}_{S^2},
\]
where \(\simeq\) denotes homotopy through continuous maps? The supplied question specifies neither boundedness nor any boundary regularity condition.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does a nonempty connected pseudoconvex open subset of \(\C^2\) have the homotopy type of the two-dimensional sphere? The question asks for existence, with no specified boundedness or boundary regularity.
