\paragraph{Definitions and conventions.} Thermostated dynamics augments resolved variables with auxiliary feedback or reservoir variables to reproduce a prescribed statistical ensemble. A time correlation of observables $A,B$ has the form $\langle A(X_t)B(X_0)\rangle$ under the chosen ensemble. In a PDE or gridpoint model, subgrid variables are unresolved degrees of freedom and their energetics form an effective reservoir. A perturbed vector field specifies the feedback force on the resolved dynamics.

\paragraph{Source scope and qualifications.} Correct equilibrium averages do not by themselves guarantee correct transient dynamics or correlations. The source asks both dynamical validation and reservoir/vector-field modeling questions; no particular universal thermostat or numerical tolerance is fixed.

\paragraph{Equilibrium matching does not identify correlations.} For each $\gamma>0$, take the scalar Ornstein--Uhlenbeck thermostat $dX_t=-\gamma X_tdt+\sqrt{2\gamma}\,dW_t$. Its explicit solution is $X_t=e^{-\gamma t}X_0+\sqrt{2\gamma}\int_0^te^{-\gamma(t-s)}dW_s$. If $X_0$ is an independent centered Gaussian of variance one, the variance remains $e^{-2\gamma t}+2\gamma\int_0^te^{-2\gamma(t-s)}ds=1$. Hence every $\gamma$ has the same stationary Gaussian law, but
\[
 \mathbb E[X_tX_0]=e^{-\gamma t},\qquad
 \left.\frac d{dt}\mathbb E[X_tX_0]\right|_{t=0}=-\gamma.
\]
Even the initial correlation slope can be arbitrary while all equilibrium moments agree.

\paragraph{Eliminating a reservoir produces memory.} In the deterministic linear pair $\dot x=-ax+by$, $\dot y=-cy+dx$, $c>0$, solve $y(t)=e^{-ct}y(0)+d\int_0^te^{-c(t-s)}x(s)ds$. Substitution yields an exact convolution term $bd\int_0^te^{-c(t-s)}x(s)ds$ in the resolved equation. Replacing it by an instantaneous feedback requires a time-scale approximation, not merely fitting a stationary variance.
