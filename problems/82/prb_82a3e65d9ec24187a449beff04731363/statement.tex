\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(F\) be a field and let \(G\) be an absolutely almost simple linear algebraic group over \(F\): thus \(G\) is connected and semisimple, and over an algebraic closure of \(F\) it has no nontrivial proper connected normal algebraic subgroup. For an extension \(L/F\), write \(G_L=G\times_F L\). The group \(G_L\) is anisotropic over \(L\) if it contains no nontrivial \(L\)-split torus, that is, no \(L\)-subgroup isomorphic to \((\mathbb G_{m,L})^r\) for any integer \(r\geq 1\).

The supplied statement uses \(S(G)\) as a set of primes but does not define that set. Retaining this unresolved source notation, the question is: whenever \(G\) is anisotropic over \(F\), is it true that, for every finite field extension \(K/F\),
\[
\bigl(\forall p\in S(G),\ p\nmid [K:F]\bigr)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
G_K\text{ is anisotropic over }K?
\]
Here \([K:F]=\dim_F K\). The source imposes no restriction on the characteristic of \(F\) and does not require \(K/F\) to be separable. A complete formulation requires the intended definition of \(S(G)\); it is not being quantified over arbitrary sets of primes.
