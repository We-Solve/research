\section{Parameters admitting infinitely many rational D(q)-quintuples}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For $q\in\mathbb Q$, a rational $D(q)$-$m$-tuple is a set $A=\{a_1,\ldots,a_m\}\subset\mathbb Q\setminus\{0\}$ of distinct numbers such that $a_i a_j+q$ is a square in $\mathbb Q$ for every $i<j$. Elements may have either sign; zero squares are allowed.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Determine precisely which $q\in\mathbb Q$ admit infinitely many distinct rational $D(q)$-quintuples. \sref{statement}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Classify the rational parameters for which infinitely many rational five-element tuples exist.
