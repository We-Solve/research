\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
The supplied Problem 5.11 asks to classify functions $f(x,y)$ near $(0,0)\in\R^2$ satisfying the three displayed conditions
\[
f_y(0,0)\not\equiv0,\qquad f_y(0,0)=0,\qquad
\frac{f_x(x-f_x)-f}{f_y}\text{ extends to a locally smooth function}.
\]
The first two conditions are incompatible if both refer to the scalar value $f_y(0,0)$: a scalar nonzero condition and equality to zero cannot both hold. The symbol $\not\equiv$ normally applies to a function, so a plausible intended first condition is that $f_y$ is not identically zero as a local function, while its value at the origin is zero. That repair is not established by the supplied record and is not silently substituted for the literal text.

For precision about the third condition, under the product interpretation of the numerator write
\[
N_f(x,y)=f_x(x,y)\bigl(x-f_x(x,y)\bigr)-f(x,y),
\qquad U_f=\{(x,y)\in U:f_y(x,y)\ne0\},
\]
where $U$ is a neighborhood of the origin on which the required derivatives exist. A smooth-extension condition for the quotient means that, after possibly shrinking $U$, there is $g\in C^\infty(U)$ such that
\[
g(x,y)=\frac{N_f(x,y)}{f_y(x,y)}
\quad\text{for all }(x,y)\in U_f.
\]
It does not automatically require $N_f=f_yg$ at points outside the closure of $U_f$. In particular, the hypothesis that $f_y$ is not identically zero does not by itself make $U_f$ dense. A global smooth-divisibility formulation would require additional information and cannot simply replace the extension wording.

The source does not specify the regularity class of $f$ itself or whether ``locally smooth'' means a smooth germ at the origin. It also does not explicitly expand $f_x(x-f_x)$; the product interpretation above is natural, whereas reading it as evaluation would need additional arguments.
