\paragraph{Testing a direct on-site realization of nonpointed fusion.} Suppose a simple object \(x\) obeys the nonpointed fusion rule \(x\otimes x\cong\mathbf1\oplus x\). Any additive strong tensor realization assigning a nonzero finite-dimensional Hilbert space \(H_x\) to \(x\) and \(\mathbb C\) to the unit would force, by taking dimensions,
\[
 n^2=1+n,\qquad n=\dim H_x\in\mathbb N.
\]
There is no such integer: the positive root is \((1+\sqrt5)/2\). Equivalently \(n(n-1)=1\) is impossible for an integer \(n\). This explicitly obstructs the naive recipe of treating each nonpointed simple object as an independent on-site Hilbert space whose ordinary tensor products exactly realize its fusion.
\paragraph{What survives the test.} Fusion-path spaces or constrained lattice subspaces can have ordinary integer dimensions while their abstract simple-object dimensions are nonintegral, so the argument is not a QCA no-go theorem. For actual QCAs, stacking two bounded-range automorphisms has range bounded by the maximum of their ranges, while composition has range bounded by the sum; both statements follow by tracking support neighborhoods. These operations can combine existing realizations, but starting with only pointed Witt classes and applying group operations stays inside the pointed subgroup by its definition.
\paragraph{Full-Witt gap.} To reach the full source Witt group, one needs a nonpointed construction respecting fusion and braiding through local constrained spaces and then a bounded-range automorphism on the permitted full observable algebra. The first missing step is that extension with a proved Witt correspondence, independent of presentation and compatible with the source automaton equivalence. Orders of the resulting classes and dimensional realization questions are also not determined. The integer-dimension obstruction rules out one simple recipe, not every desired realization.
