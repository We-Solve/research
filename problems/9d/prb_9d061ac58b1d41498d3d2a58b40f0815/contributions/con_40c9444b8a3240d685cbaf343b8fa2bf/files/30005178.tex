\paragraph{An exact zero-mode scaling calculation.} Let \(V>0\) denote volume, \(g>0\) a quartic coefficient and \(t\in\mathbb R\) a mass parameter. In the scalar constant-mode integral with density proportional to \(\exp[-V(tm^2/2+gm^4/4)]\), define susceptibility \(\chi_0=V\langle m^2\rangle\). Substituting \(m=(Vg)^{-1/4}s\) gives
\[
 \chi_0=V^{1/2}g^{-1/2}\,F(t\sqrt{V/g}),\qquad
 F(z)=\frac{\int_{\mathbb R}s^2e^{-zs^2/2-s^4/4}ds}{\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-zs^2/2-s^4/4}ds}.
\]
Both integrals converge for every real \(z\), since the quartic term dominates; the denominator is strictly positive. The candidate window is therefore \(t\) of order \(\sqrt{g/V}\), without changing any integration contour.
\paragraph{The missing comparison.} Integrating out nonconstant Euclidean lattice modes generally changes the effective mass, quartic coupling and higher interactions. Even a small pointwise remainder need not be small after rescaling throughout the growing integration region. A rigorous transfer requires an error bound dominated by an integrable quartic tail and uniform in the source scaling variable, together with the correct running-coupling normalization.
\paragraph{Scope.} This calculation reproduces only the standard constant-mode mechanism; it is not the hierarchical theorem or its Euclidean extension. It neither identifies the report's universal function with \(F\) under its full conventions nor proves its logarithmic renormalization factors. The first gap is a controlled effective Euclidean zero-mode measure with the exact source normalization and uniform scaling-window errors.
