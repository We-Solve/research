\section{Transferring Resonant Blowup to the Full Einstein System}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


\paragraph{Definitions and conventions.}
A resonant system retains the secular mode interactions of a weakly nonlinear evolution on a long, amplitude-dependent timescale. Finite-time blowup means loss of boundedness or regularity in the norm used for that effective system. Transferring blowup to a full Einstein evolution requires comparison estimates that remain meaningful as the resonant solution approaches its singular time, rather than only convergence on fixed intervals before it.

\paragraph{Target retained from the canonical source catalogue.}
\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}
How can the finite-time blowup result for the resonant system be transferred to the full system?

\paragraph{Source scope and qualification.}
The extraction does not supply the resonant equations, matter model, gauge or blowup norm. These must be recovered from the report; the phrase full system does not identify a standalone PDE by itself.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can a blowup mechanism in the resonant approximation be proved to occur in the full Einstein evolution?
