\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.


With logits $Z=WH$, balanced one-hot targets give cross-entropy $L=-k^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^k\log(e^{Z_{jj}}/\sum_{i=1}^ke^{Z_{ij}})$. Gradient flow differentiates both factor matrices. Neural-collapse geometry concerns aligned class features and classifiers with centered simplex-like class means.

A feed-forward neural network composes affine maps and specified nonlinear activations. ReLU is $\sigma(t)=\max\{0,t\}$. Empirical risk averages a loss on training samples; population risk is its expectation on the target distribution, and generalization compares these risks.

For a differentiable loss $L$, gradient flow solves $\dot\theta=-\nabla L(\theta)$, while gradient descent updates $\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\eta_t\nabla L(\theta_t)$. Global optimality refers to the minimum over the whole admissible parameter space.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For the multiclass two-layer linear unconstrained-features model with balanced standard-basis inputs, weights $W_t\in\mathbb R^{k\times d}$ and $H_t\in\mathbb R^{d\times k}$, and cross-entropy loss, determine the gradient-flow dynamics under general initialization. Do the normalized parameters converge to neural-collapse geometry, which quantities are monotone along the trajectory, and how do the answers depend on the initialization and the number of classes $k$? \sref{statement}{2}
