\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 701715}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A group \(G\) is \emph{periodic} if every element of \(G\) has finite order. It is \emph{locally finite} if every subgroup generated by finitely many elements is finite. For a positive integer \(n\), let \(A_n\) denote the alternating group of even permutations of \(\{1,\ldots,n\}\).

Let \(G\) be a periodic group such that
\[
\forall H\leq G,\qquad |H|<\infty\ \Longrightarrow\ \exists K\leq G\ \exists n\in\Z_{\geq1}\quad (H\leq K\ \text{and}\ K\cong A_n).
\]
Must \(G\) be locally finite? Equivalently, must \(\langle S\rangle\) be finite for every finite subset \(S\subseteq G\)?

The supplied wording imposes no lower bound such as \(n\geq5\) on the alternating groups and no requirement that \(H\) be properly contained in \(K\).
\subsection{Short English statement}
Must a group in which every element has finite order be locally finite if each of its finite subgroups lies in a finite alternating subgroup? This is the question proposed by V. D. Mazurov; the supplied registry labels it open.
