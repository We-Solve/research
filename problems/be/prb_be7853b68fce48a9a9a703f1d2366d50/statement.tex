\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(G\) be a group with identity \(1\). Its derived subgroup is
\[
G'=[G,G]=\langle x^{-1}y^{-1}xy:x,y\in G\rangle.
\]
Define \(G^{(0)}=G\) and \(G^{(n+1)}=[G^{(n)},G^{(n)}]\) for integers \(n\geq 0\). The group \(G\) is \emph{soluble} if \(G^{(n)}=\{1\}\) for some integer \(n\geq 0\). It is \emph{right-orderable} if there exists a strict total order \(<\) on \(G\) such that, for every \(x,y,z\in G\),
\[
x<y\quad\Longrightarrow\quad xz<yz.
\]
The quotient \(G/G'\) is its abelianization.

The supplied question asks whether there exists a soluble right-orderable group \(G\) such that
\[
|G/G'|=[G:G']<\infty.
\]
