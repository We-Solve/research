Write $u_y(t)$, periodic in $t$ with period $1$, for the corresponding closed horocycle of length $y^{-1}$ in $T^1X$, and $\lambda$ for probability Liouville measure. Conjecture 21 of J. Marklof and A. Str\"ombergsson asks, for a fixed badly approximable $\alpha$ and fixed $0<c_1<c_2$, whether
\[
\sup_{c_1M^{-2}\leq y\leq c_2M^{-2}}
\left|\frac1M\sum_{m=1}^M f(u_y(m\alpha))-\int f\,\dd\lambda\right|\longrightarrow0.
\]
The continuous function $f$ must satisfy the cusp-growth condition referred to as [136], whose exact exponent range and definition are not supplied. Bad approximation means $\|k\alpha\|_{\R/\Z}\geq c_\alpha/|k|$ for every nonzero integer $k$.

The aid gives a conditional Fourier reduction. Set $F_y(t)=f(u_y(t))$, let $\widehat F_y(k)$ be its period-one Fourier coefficient, and assume
\[
K(y)=\sum_{k\ne0}|k|\,|\widehat F_y(k)|<\infty.
\]
Then the sampling error relative to the continuous horocycle mean satisfies
\[
\left|\frac1M\sum_{m=1}^M F_y(m\alpha)-\int_0^1F_y(t)\,\dd t\right|
\leq\frac{K(y)}{2c_\alpha M}.
\]
Hence uniform $K(y)=o(M)$ across the full critical band, together with continuous-horocycle equidistribution for the test function, proves the displayed conclusion. A bound $K(y)=O(y^{-\beta})$ gives sampling error $O(M^{2\beta-1})$, sufficient for $\beta<1/2$. At $\beta=1/2$ this only yields $O(1)$ and is inconclusive, not a counterexample. The aid does not establish the required Fourier bound for every test function in the conjecture.
