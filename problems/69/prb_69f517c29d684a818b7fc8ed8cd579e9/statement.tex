\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
This record is the end of a sentence followed by the heading ``7. Polygonal billiards,'' not an independent Conjecture 45. Neighboring local records recover its antecedent as Question 45 of J. Marklof on the two-gap set of a positive-definite binary quadratic form. The source's later ``Conjecture 45'' label is an internal cross-reference mismatch.

Let
\[
 Q(u,v)=a u^2+buv+c v^2,\qquad a,c>0,\quad4ac-b^2>0,
\]
and define
\[
 S_Q=\{(Q(x_1)-Q(x_2),Q(x_2)-Q(x_3)):
        x_i\in\Z^2,\ x_i\ne x_j\ (i\ne j)\}.
\]
The recovered continuation says density of this set in $\R^2$ was not known, in the preceding generic-form discussion; it does not assert density for rational forms. Distinctness is of vectors, not necessarily of their quadratic values. The broader neighboring question concerns normalized triple-count asymptotics, which is stronger than density and is not included as a new problem here.

The aid's shared-anchor criterion is exact. Density is equivalent to the following property: for every pair of bounded open intervals $I,J$ with rational endpoints, and every $H>0$, there is an anchor $x_2\in\Z^2$ with $Q(x_2)>H$ and pairwise distinct vectors $x_1,x_2,x_3$ such that
\[
 Q(x_1)\in Q(x_2)+I,\qquad
 Q(x_3)\in Q(x_2)-J.
\]
The same middle value must serve both conditions. Positive definiteness makes all bounded-height lattice sets finite, so bounded anchors yield only finitely many gap pairs in a fixed bounded rectangle. This explains the arbitrarily-large-anchor formulation; conversely such recurrence meets every rational open rectangle and gives density.

There is also a coefficient-group obstruction. Put $G_Q=a\Z+b\Z+c\Z$. Every value $Q(x)$ lies in $G_Q$, so
\[
 S_Q\subset G_Q\times G_Q.
\]
If the coefficients have rational rank one, a common denominator gives $G_Q\subset\rho\Z$ for some $\rho>0$. The two-gap set is then contained in a discrete planar lattice and is not dense. A dense coefficient group in higher rational rank is only a necessary ambient-support condition, not a proof that shared-anchor triples exist.

Finally the aid gives a nonquadratic diagnostic showing why dense ordinary differences do not suffice. Enumerate positive rationals $d_j$, choose $b_1=10$, $b_{j+1}=b_j+d_j+10$, and let
\[
 \mathcal A=\bigcup_{j\geq1}\{b_j,b_j+d_j\}\subset\R_{>0}.
\]
This set is locally finite and $\mathcal A-\mathcal A$ contains every positive and negative rational, hence is dense in $\R$. But its shared-middle two-gap set misses $(1,2)\times(1,2)$: those two gaps would require three distinct points within a total span below $4$, whereas different two-point blocks are separated by at least $10$. This refutes only the implication from dense one-gap differences to dense shared-anchor two-gap differences. It is neither a quadratic-form example nor a counterexample to a Berry--Tabor prediction.
