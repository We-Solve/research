\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(H\) be an infinite-dimensional complex Hilbert space with norm \(\|\cdot\|\), and write \(B_H=\{z\in H:\|z\|<1\}\). A domain is a nonempty connected open subset of \(H\). A domain \(D\subset H\) is bounded if \(\sup_{z\in D}\|z\|<\infty\), and convex if \((1-t)x+ty\in D\) for all \(x,y\in D\) and \(t\in[0,1]\).

For a precise formulation, adopt the following Hilbert-ball convention. Holomorphic maps are complex Fr\'echet-holomorphic maps. A biholomorphic embedding \(f:D\to B_H\) means a biholomorphism from \(D\) onto an open subset \(f(D)\subset B_H\). For \(p\in D\), define the squeezing function by
\[
 s_D(p)=\sup\Bigl(\{0\}\cup\bigl\{r\in(0,1]:\text{there exists a biholomorphic embedding }f:D\to B_H,
 \ f(p)=0,\ rB_H\subset f(D)\bigr\}\Bigr).
\]
The domain \(D\) is called \emph{holomorphic homogeneous regular} (HHR) if
\[
 \inf_{p\in D}s_D(p)>0.
\]
Equivalently, there exists \(r_D\in(0,1)\) such that for every \(p\in D\) there is such an embedding \(f_p\) satisfying
\[
 r_DB_H\subset f_p(D)\subset B_H,\qquad f_p(p)=0.
\]
The radius may depend on \(D\), but must be independent of \(p\). This definition does not require a single radius valid for all domains.

\textbf{Principal question.} Is every bounded convex domain \(D\) in every infinite-dimensional complex Hilbert space \(H\) HHR? In the above convention, is
\[
 \forall H\ \forall D\subset H\text{ bounded convex domain},\qquad
 \inf_{p\in D}s_D(p)>0
\]
true, where \(H\) ranges over infinite-dimensional complex Hilbert spaces?

For each bounded convex domain in an infinite-dimensional complex Hilbert space, must there be a positive radius, independent of the chosen point, such that the domain can be mapped biholomorphically into the unit ball with that point at the origin and with the image containing the ball of that radius?
