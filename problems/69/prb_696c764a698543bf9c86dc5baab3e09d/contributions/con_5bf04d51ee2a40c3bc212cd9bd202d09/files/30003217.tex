\section{Zero-Velocity Lieb–Robinson Bounds for Disordered Spin Systems}\label{30003217}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 30003217 · Mathematical Physics}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


\paragraph{Definitions and conventions.}
A quantum spin model on $\mathbb Z^d$ has finite-dimensional local Hilbert spaces and a Hamiltonian built from finite-range interaction terms. Its Heisenberg evolution is $\tau_t(A)=e^{itH}Ae^{-itH}$. A zero-velocity Lieb--Robinson estimate bounds the commutator of distant local observables by a decaying function of their separation uniformly in time, rather than with a light cone widening linearly in $|t|$. The disorder averaging or probability quantifiers are part of the model.

\paragraph{An exactly localized commuting class.}
In a finite spin volume let $H_0=\sum_X h_X$, where all self-adjoint interaction terms commute, every $X$ has diameter at most $R$, and $h_X$ acts only on $X$. Their sizes and coefficients may be random. If $A$ is supported in a finite set $S$, split
\[
 H_0=H_S+H_{S^c},\qquad
 H_S=\sum_{X:X\cap S\ne\varnothing}h_X.
\]
Every term of $H_{S^c}$ commutes with $A$, and by hypothesis $[H_S,H_{S^c}]=0$. Hence
\[
 e^{itH_0}Ae^{-itH_0}=e^{itH_S}Ae^{-itH_S}.
\]
The right side is supported in $S_R=\{x:d(x,S)\leq R\}$ for every real $t$. If $B$ is supported in $T$ with $d(S,T)>R$, the commutator is exactly zero. Together with the universal bound $2\|A\|\|B\|$, this gives, for any $\mu>0$,
\[
 \|[\tau_t^{H_0}(A),B]\|
 \leq 2e^{\mu R}\|A\|\|B\|e^{-\mu d(S,T)},
 \qquad t\in\mathbb R.
\]
For fixed local supports the proof is volume independent. Diagonal finite-range Ising Hamiltonians with arbitrary random coefficients supply examples in every dimension. Commutativity, rather than a probabilistic localization estimate, is doing the work.

\paragraph{Testing perturbative stability.}
Put $H=H_0+\varepsilon V$ with $V$ bounded and self-adjoint in a finite volume. Duhamel's formula and unitary invariance of the norm imply
\[
 \|\tau_t^H(A)-\tau_t^{H_0}(A)\|
 \leq 2|\varepsilon|\,|t|\,\|V\|\,\|A\|.
\]
For distant $B$, this gives only a commutator bound
$4|\varepsilon|\,|t|\,\|V\|\,\|A\|\,\|B\|$ in addition to the trivial norm bound. It neither decays with separation nor is useful uniformly in time; moreover an extensive $V$ has volume-growing norm. Thus ordinary small-perturbation continuity cannot extend the exact commuting calculation to the requested regime.

\paragraph{Remaining scope.}
One must replace this time-growing expansion by a localization mechanism with volume-uniform, time-uniform spatial estimates, together with stated disorder probability or averaging quantifiers. No such estimate is supplied for noncommuting many-body interactions here. As the definition emphasizes, the target cannot mean every disorder distribution at every strength. This calculation gives a restricted exact model and identifies the failed perturbative step, not a universal many-body-localized phase.
