\section{Zero-Velocity Lieb–Robinson Bounds for Disordered Spin Systems}\label{30003217}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 30003217 · Mathematical Physics}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


\paragraph{Definitions and conventions.}
A quantum spin model on $\mathbb Z^d$ has finite-dimensional local Hilbert spaces and a Hamiltonian built from finite-range interaction terms. Its Heisenberg evolution is $\tau_t(A)=e^{itH}Ae^{-itH}$. A zero-velocity Lieb--Robinson estimate bounds the commutator of distant local observables by a decaying function of their separation uniformly in time, rather than with a light cone widening linearly in $|t|$. The disorder averaging or probability quantifiers are part of the model.

\paragraph{Target retained from the canonical source catalogue.}
\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}
Can zero-velocity Lieb–Robinson bounds be proved for general disordered finite-range quantum spin models on $\mathbb Z^d$, thereby establishing a universal many-body-localized phase?

\paragraph{Source scope and qualification.}
The word general cannot be interpreted as every disorder distribution at every disorder strength: those classes include delocalized systems. A proposed localized regime and quantitative disorder hypotheses require source-specific specification; no unconditional universal theorem is created here.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can time-uniform locality bounds establish a many-body localized regime for general disordered finite-range spin systems?
