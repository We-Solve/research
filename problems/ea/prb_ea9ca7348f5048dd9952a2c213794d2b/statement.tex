\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
In the standard probability-space interpretation, let \(T=(T_t)_{t\in\R}\) be a measurable measure-preserving flow on a standard nonatomic probability space \((X,\mathcal B,\mu)\). Thus \(T_0\) is the identity and \(T_{t+u}=T_tT_u\) as measure-preserving transformations modulo null sets. The source does not explicitly specify this space class, finiteness of measure, or ergodicity.

For \(s\in\R^\times=\R\setminus\{0\}\), write \(T^{(s)}_t=T_{st}\). Define the self-similarity group, in the nonzero-real convention, by
\[
 I(T)=\{s\in\R^\times:\exists R\in\operatorname{Aut}(X,\mu)
 \text{ with }RT_tR^{-1}=T_{st}\text{ for every }t\in\R\}.
\]
The same measure-space isomorphism \(R\) must implement all times. Composition and inverse conjugacies show that \(I(T)\) is a multiplicative subgroup and contains \(1\). An alternative convention restricts to positive \(s\); then \(I_+(T)=I(T)\cap\R_{>0}\). The value \(s=-1\) concerns time reversal and makes these conventions materially different.

The first question asks whether \(I(T)\) is always a Borel subset of \(\R^\times\) with its usual topology, hence a Borel subgroup with the inherited operations. If ``Borel group'' instead means an abstract standard-Borel or Polishable group structure, that is a different specification not provided by the record.

The second question asks whether absence of nontrivial self-similarity is generic. Under the nonzero-real convention this means the class

is comeagre in the intended space of measure-preserving flows; under the positive convention replace \(I(T)\) by \(I_+(T)\). A usual topology is uniform convergence on compact time intervals for the weak topology on \(\operatorname{Aut}(X,\mu)\). The source does not fix that topology or whether genericity is in all flows, ergodic flows, or another subclass.
