\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 700693}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a prime \(p\), the quasicyclic \(p\)-group is
\[
C_{p^\infty}=\{x\in\Q/\Z : p^n x=0\text{ for some integer }n\geq 0\}.
\]
A group \(H\) is called a Chernikov group if it has a normal subgroup \(D\) of finite index such that
\[
D\cong\prod_{i=1}^{r}C_{p_i^\infty}
\]
for some integer \(r\geq 0\) and primes \(p_1,\ldots,p_r\), which need not be distinct. The empty product is the trivial group, so finite groups are included.

Does there exist an abstract group \(G\) and subgroups \(A,B\leq G\) such that \(A\) and \(B\) are Chernikov groups, \(G\) is not a Chernikov group, and
\[
G=AB=\{ab:a\in A,\ b\in B\}?
\]
Here the product is a setwise product: every element of \(G\) must have such a factorization. Neither normality of the factors nor uniqueness of the factorization is assumed.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can a group that is not Chernikov be the setwise product of two Chernikov subgroups? This is the question attributed to V.\ P.\ Shunkov in Kourovka Notebook Problem 10.78.
