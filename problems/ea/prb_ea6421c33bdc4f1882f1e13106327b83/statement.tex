\section{10 Lectures and 42 Open Problems — Certifying the Restricted Isometry Property}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Standard number systems are $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$. Specialized notation and index ranges follow the cited source and the definitions below.


An $M\times N$ matrix $A$ has the $(s,\delta)$ restricted isometry property (RIP) if $(1-\delta)\lVert x\rVert_2^2\leq\lVert Ax\rVert_2^2\leq(1+\delta)\lVert x\rVert_2^2$ for every vector with at most $s$ nonzero coordinates. Its nullspace is $\ker A=\{x:Ax=0\}$. A nonzero $s$-sparse null vector is incompatible with RIP for $\delta<1$. A certifier must be sound for its positive certificates, while the probability of producing a certificate is evaluated over the source Gaussian matrix law. The Gaussian scaling, matrix encoding and time model must be specified for a complexity bound.

\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Let $N = 2M$ . For which $s$ is there a polynomial time algorithm that is guaranteed to, with high probability, certify that a gaussian matrix $A$ is $\left(s,\frac13\right)$ -RIP? In particular, a $\left(s,\frac13\right)$ -RIP matrix has to not have $s$ sparse vectors in its nullspace. This motivates a second question: Let $N = 2M$ . for which $s$ is there a polynomial time algorithm that is guaranteed to, with high probability, certify that a gaussian matrix $A$ does not have $s$ -sparse vectors in its nullspace? \sref{statement}{2}
