\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Write
\[
\operatorname{SL}_3(\Z)=\{A\in\operatorname{Mat}_{3\times3}(\Z):\det A=1\}.
\]
Under the usual convention, a hyperbolic \(3\)-manifold is a connected, complete Riemannian \(3\)-manifold without boundary whose sectional curvature is constantly \(-1\). Finite volume means \(\operatorname{Vol}(M)<\infty\). For a base point \(m_0\in M\), let \(\pi_1(M,m_0)\) denote its fundamental group.

Does there exist a finite-volume hyperbolic \(3\)-manifold \(M\) and a group homomorphism
\[
\rho:\pi_1(M,m_0)\longrightarrow\operatorname{SL}_3(\Z)
\qquad\text{such that}\qquad
\ker\rho=\{1\}?
\]
The existence of such a homomorphism is independent of the choice of base point. The supplied statement imposes no orientability or compactness restriction; both compact and noncompact finite-volume manifolds are within the stated scope. Completeness and absence of boundary are included here as the usual hyperbolic-manifold convention, but are not explicitly specified in the supplied wording and require confirmation against the report.
