\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 3100063}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
The literal question attributed in the source to ``Erd\H{o}s--Strauss'' asks whether
\[
\forall n\in\Z_{\geq1}\ \exists a,b,c\in\Z_{\geq1},\qquad
\frac4n=\frac1a+\frac1b+\frac1c.
\]
The denominators need not be distinct: no distinctness condition appears. Clearing denominators gives the equivalent equation $4abc=n(ab+ac+bc)$ for positive integers $a,b,c$.

There is a boundary discrepancy in the source. At $n=1$ the right side of the fractional equation is at most $3$, whereas the left side is $4$. The nontrivial conjecture identified by the supplied research summary as the Erd\H{o}s--Straus conjecture therefore has the corrected domain
\[
\forall n\in\Z_{\geq2}\ \exists a,b,c\in\Z_{\geq1},\qquad
\frac4n=\frac1a+\frac1b+\frac1c.
\]
This second display states the conventional intended target while exposing the source's inclusion of $n=1$; the two assertions must not be equated.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can $4/n$ be written as a sum of three positive unit fractions for every integer $n\geq2$? The source says every positive integer, which incorrectly includes $n=1$.
