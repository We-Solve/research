\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
The AIM question is ``Local statistics for zeros for \(L\)-functions of orthogonal type,'' literally attributed in the source to ``Chantal Davis.'' Its verification identifies workshop organizer Chantal David and her March 2014 Arizona Winter School notes as the likely intended attribution, explicitly calling this a source-level typo rather than an extraction error. No family, scaling, or limiting regime is fixed in the original.

The attached report interprets the question as low-lying zero statistics in orthogonal function-field families, for example quadratic twists of a fixed elliptic curve over \(\F_q(t)\). It says existing fixed-\(q\) one-level results with Fourier support in \((-1,1)\), including later trace/lower-order refinements, do not distinguish \(O\), \(SO(\mathrm{even})\), and \(SO(\mathrm{odd})\) when the forced central zero is counted. Precise family hypotheses and references are not supplied.

Its candidate finite-rank formula concerns Haar orthogonal matrices. Choose with probability one half a matrix from \(SO(2N)\), and with probability one half from \(SO(2N+1)\), deleting the odd matrix's forced \(+1\) eigenvalue. Both resulting multisets have \(2N\) eigenangles. For an even trigonometric polynomial
\[
F(\theta)=f_0+2\sum_{k=1}^Kf_k\cos(k\theta),\qquad0\leq K\leq2N-2,
\]
the report asserts
\[
\E\sum_{\theta_j}F(\theta_j)
=2Nf_0+\sum_{k=1}^K(-1)^kf_k.
\]
The stable Fourier range and equal mixture weights are part of the statement.

For an even test function \(\varphi\) with Fourier convention \(\widehat\varphi(u)=\int_{\R}\varphi(x)e^{-2\pi iux}\,dx\), take the canonical periodization
\[
F_N(\theta)=\sum_{m\in\Z}\varphi\!\left(\frac{2N}{2\pi}(\theta+2\pi m)\right),\qquad
f_k=\frac1{2N}\widehat\varphi\!\left(\frac{k}{2N}\right).
\]
Under regularity and Fourier-support assumptions making this a polynomial in the preceding range, the claimed expectation is
\[
\widehat\varphi(0)+\mathcal L_N,\qquad
\mathcal L_N=\frac1{2N}\sum_{k=1}^K(-1)^k\widehat\varphi\!\left(\frac{k}{2N}\right),\qquad
|\mathcal L_N|\leq\frac{\|\widehat\varphi'\|_1}{2N}.
\]
Thus the deflated balanced ensemble has the unitary leading term with a controlled alternating leakage. Adding back the forced eigenvalue contributes \(F_N(0)/2\) at finite rank; in the usual decaying-test large-\(N\) regime this recovers the reported orthogonal law \(\widehat\varphi(0)+\varphi(0)/2\). The limiting law is not substituted for an exact finite-rank identity. Nor does the matrix identity alone establish a zero-statistics theorem for an unspecified \(L\)-function family.
