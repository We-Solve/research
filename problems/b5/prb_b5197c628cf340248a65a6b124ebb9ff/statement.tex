\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(B^d=\{x\in\R^d:\|x\|_2\leq1\}\), let \(d\geq1\), and let \(1\leq k\leq d\). Let \(P_{d+k}\subset B^d\) be a full-dimensional polytope with exactly \(d+k\) vertices. ``Inscribed'' conventionally requires its vertices to lie on \(S^{d-1}\), though the source also writes only containment and does not expand that convention.

Under a standard affine-sandwich normalization of multiplicative Banach--Mazur distance to the ball, write
\[
\delta_{\mathrm{BM}}(P,B^d)=\inf\{\lambda\geq1:\exists A\text{ an invertible affine map},\
B^d\subseteq A(P)\subseteq\lambda B^d\}.
\]
The exact affine/translation convention must be checked for these nonsymmetric bodies; it is not a logarithmic distance or distance minus one. The first source conjecture is
\[
\exists\widetilde c>0\quad\forall d\geq1\quad\forall k\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
\quad\forall P_{d+k}\text{ as above},\qquad
\delta_{\mathrm{BM}}(P_{d+k},B^d)\geq\widetilde c\,\frac d{\sqrt k}.
\]
The positive constant is absolute, independent of dimension, vertex count, and the polytope. No value for it is supplied.

For the second question, define the support function \(h_P(u)=\max_{x\in P}\langle x,u\rangle\), and, with normalized spherical measure \(\sigma\), the mean width
\[
w(P)=\int_{S^{d-1}}\bigl(h_P(u)+h_P(-u)\bigr)\,\dd\sigma(u).
\]
Let \(\Delta_d\) be a regular simplex inscribed in \(B^d\), whose unit vertices have pairwise inner product \(-1/d\). Is it true that
\[
\forall P_{d+1}\text{ inscribed},\qquad w(P_{d+1})\leq w(\Delta_d)?
\]
The question asks maximality, not an equality-case classification or uniqueness theorem. The spherical-measure normalization multiplies all mean widths by a common factor and does not change the comparison. The two questions are separate; no implication from the Banach--Mazur bound to the width maximum is asserted.

\subsection{Short English statement}
Seek the universal \(d/\sqrt k\) lower bound on distance from a \((d+k)\)-vertex inscribed polytope to the ball, and separately ask whether the regular simplex maximizes mean width among inscribed simplices. The nonsymmetric distance and inscription conventions need confirmation.
