\subsection{Attempted potential for the repeated finite-strategy question}
The primary report defines $\delta$-distance by vertex altitudes and demands crossing of the retained face. A tempting argument says that every such crossing decreases the distance of the simplex from the origin. The following legal move disproves strict decrease in that unrestricted form.
Take $\delta=1/10$ and, in the unit disk, let
\[
 p=(2/5,0),\quad q=(3/5,2/5),\quad l=(4/5,0),\quad e=(2/5,2/5).
\]
Peter removes $l$ from $\operatorname{conv}(p,q,l)$, and Roland replaces it by $e$.
\begin{proposition}
Both triangles satisfy the source altitude condition, the replacement is legal, and both triangles have distance exactly $2/5$ from the origin.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
All four squared norms are at most one. The signed planar cross products relative to the directed line $p q$ are
$\det(q-p,l-p)=-4/25$ and $\det(q-p,e-p)=2/25$.
Hence $l$ and $e$ lie on opposite sides, and the two triangles meet exactly in the common edge $p q$. The first triangle has altitudes $4/(5\sqrt5),2/5,4/(5\sqrt5)$; the second has altitudes $2/5,1/5,2/(5\sqrt5)$. Each exceeds $1/10$. Every point of either triangle has first coordinate at least $2/5$, while both contain $p$, so their distances are exactly $2/5$.
\end{proof}
Thus strict descent does not follow just from the two legal-move axioms. This is not an infinite legal play, and does not show that Peter lacks a strategy: Peter might select a different leaving vertex or use a more refined potential.
