\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A closed strictly convex real projective $n$-manifold is a compact manifold without boundary with a projective structure modeled on a properly convex domain $\Omega\subset\mathbb{RP}^{n}$ whose boundary contains no nontrivial projective line segment. In the usual complete convex description,
\[
 M=\Omega/\Gamma,
\]
where $\Gamma\le\operatorname{PGL}(n+1,\R)$ preserves $\Omega$ and acts freely, properly discontinuously, and cocompactly; $\pi_1(M)\cong\Gamma$ for a connected $M$.

A group is \emph{cubulated}, in the standard geometric-action sense, if it admits a proper cocompact action by cubical automorphisms on a CAT$(0)$ cube complex. A cube complex is built by gluing Euclidean cubes along faces and is CAT$(0)$ when its intrinsic metric satisfies the CAT$(0)$ comparison inequality.

\textbf{Question (Agol).} For every closed manifold $M^n$ admitting a strictly convex projective structure, does $\pi_1(M)$ admit a proper cocompact action on a CAT$(0)$ cube complex? No dimension restriction is given in the supplied statement. The wording ``is it cubulated?'' is interpreted here in the customary fundamental-group sense; if a particular cubical structure on the manifold itself is intended, that stronger geometric requirement must be specified separately.

The source invokes Benoist's theorem to state that $\pi_1(M)$ is Gromov hyperbolic and emphasizes the question even for closed hyperbolic $n$-manifolds. The registry summary reports a solved closed hyperbolic three-dimensional special case via virtual specialness, while leaving the general strictly convex projective question open. These are attributed source and registry reports, not an independent status audit.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Must the fundamental group of every closed strictly convex real projective manifold act properly and cocompactly on a CAT$(0)$ cube complex? The question includes closed hyperbolic manifolds and has no stated dimension bound.
