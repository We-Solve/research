\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Conjecture 9, attributed to A. Katok, says that the only ergodic probability measures for ``this example'' are the smooth measure and measures supported on periodic orbits. The preceding definition of the example is missing. The aid identifies the intended context as Katok's nonreversible, Liouville-ergodic 1973 Finsler geodesic-flow construction on $S^2$, with two oriented periodic orbits in phase space. Parameter choices in that construction are not specified by the extracted statement.

For a fixed such flow $\phi_t$ on its compact unit tangent or cotangent phase space, let $m$ be normalized Liouville measure and $\gamma_\pm$ its two oriented periodic phase-space orbits, of periods $P_\pm$. Their invariant probabilities are
\[
\nu_\pm=\frac1{P_\pm}\int_0^{P_\pm}\delta_{\phi_t(x_\pm)}\,\dd t.
\]
The target is that the ergodic invariant probabilities are exactly $m,\nu_+,\nu_-$. Equivalently, under the usual ergodic-decomposition hypotheses, every invariant probability is uniquely
\[
a m+b\nu_++c\nu_-,\qquad a,b,c\geq0,\quad a+b+c=1.
\]
These are three measures on the flow phase space, not three atoms or necessarily three different base-surface geodesic images.

The aid's return-map reduction assumes a genuine global annular-section suspension model for the complement of $\gamma_\pm$. Let $T:A^\circ\to A^\circ$ be its interior return map and $r>0$ its return roof. In a standard finite-measure correspondence setting, for example $r$ bounded below by a positive constant, each ergodic $T$-invariant probability $\mu$ with $\int r\,\dd\mu<\infty$ gives
\[
\nu_\mu(E)=\frac{\displaystyle\int_{A^\circ}\int_0^{r(x)}\mathbf1_E(x,s)\,\dd s\,\dd\mu(x)}{\displaystyle\int_{A^\circ}r\,\dd\mu}.
\]
The asserted three-measure classification is exactly uniqueness of the finite-mean ergodic return-map probability corresponding to $m$. Invariant return measures of infinite roof mean do not automatically give a flow probability. If a roof can approach zero, the finiteness of transverse measures needs additional care; the precise suspension hypotheses must be recovered before using this probability-normalized correspondence.

The aid also gives an independent atomicity statement: a fixed-point-free continuous flow on a compact metric space has no atomic invariant probability. An atom would have the same positive mass at every point of its nontrivial continuous orbit. In particular $\nu_\pm$ are non-atomic circle-orbit measures. A time-one-map invariant measure is a different notion and is not substituted for full flow invariance.

This supports the existential construction reading according to the aid, not a universal assertion for every member or an unspecified fixed member of the 1973 family.
