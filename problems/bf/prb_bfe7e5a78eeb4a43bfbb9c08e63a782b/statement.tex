\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Use the standard convention that a locally compact group is a Hausdorff topological group whose identity has a compact neighbourhood. Such a group \(G\) is \emph{compactly generated} if there is a compact subset \(K\subseteq G\) with \(G=\langle K\rangle\), where \(\langle K\rangle\) denotes the subgroup generated algebraically by \(K\). A nontrivial topological group \(G\) is \emph{topologically simple} if its only closed normal subgroups are \(\{1\}\) and \(G\). It is \emph{abstractly simple} if its only normal subgroups, without any closedness requirement, are \(\{1\}\) and \(G\).

Does there exist a nontrivial compactly generated locally compact group \(G\) such that
\[
\forall H\trianglelefteq G,\qquad H\text{ closed in }G\ \Longrightarrow\ H=\{1\}\text{ or }H=G,
\]
while
\[
\exists N\trianglelefteq G,\qquad \{1\}\subsetneq N\subsetneq G?
\]
The Hausdorff convention is made explicit here; the supplied statement does not specify a separation convention. The supplied attribution is unresolved: the statement names P.-E. Caprace and N. Monod, whereas the proposer metadata names C. Reid and S. M. Smith.
