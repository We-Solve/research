\section{An exponential number of facets need not prevent an extension}
A possible negative route is to exhibit exponentially many essential projected
inequalities and conclude that no compact extended formulation exists. The
inference is false. For $d\ge2$, consider
\[
C_d=\{x\in\mathbb R^d:\sum_{i=1}^d|x_i|\le1\}
 =\{x:s^Tx\le1\ \text{for every }s\in\{-1,1\}^d\}.
\]
Equality of the descriptions follows by choosing $s_i$ with the sign of $x_i$;
all other sign sums are no greater than the sum of absolute values.
For each $s$, the $d$ points $s_i e_i$ belong to the hyperplane $s^Tx=1$
and to $C_d$. They are affinely independent: a relation
$\sum_i a_i s_i e_i=0$ forces every $a_i=0$, in particular every affine
relation with $\sum_i a_i=0$ is trivial. Thus this is a facet, since its
affine dimension is $d-1$. The polytope is full dimensional because it
contains a neighborhood of zero. Different signs give different facets,
so there are $2^d$ of them. None can be deleted from this sign description:
for any $1<t<d/(d-2)$ when $d>2$, or any $t>1$ when $d=2$,
$x=(t/d)s$ violates its own inequality while every different sign vector
$u$ has $u^Tx\le t(d-2)/d<1$.

Yet $C_d$ is the projection of just $2d+1$ inequalities in $2d$ variables:
\[
t_i\ge x_i,\quad t_i\ge-x_i\quad(1\le i\le d),\qquad\sum_it_i\le1.
\]
These imply $\sum_i|x_i|\le\sum_it_i\le1$, and conversely one may set
$t_i=|x_i|$. Thus facet counting in the original variables alone cannot
settle the source's unrestricted compact-formulation question. This familiar
example is a proof of a failed inference, not a facility-location lower bound.

\section{Why one residual total-flow condition loses a source constraint}
The MFN network in the source includes a commodity-specific terminal arc
of capacity $y_i d_j$. Replacing it by an unlimited terminal arc appears to
simplify each residual feasibility check. The following exact scenario shows
what information is lost, even before attempting to compress all scenarios.
There are $N=U+1$ clients, $U\ge1$, and facilities $A,B$ of capacities $U,N$.
Take $y_A=1,y_B=1/N$, $x_{Aj}=U/N,x_{Bj}=1/N$ for every client.
Partially assign $U$ clients fully to $A$, leaving $j_0$ unassigned.
Facility $A$ then has zero residual capacity, while $B$ has residual capacity
$Ny_B=1$. If terminal bounds are removed, the one residual unit is routable:
send $1/N$ directly from $j_0$ to $B$, and send $1/N$ along each of the $U$
paths $j_0,A,j,B$ through a preassigned client $j$. The total first-arc load
$j_0\to A$ is $U/N$, every reverse arc $A\to j$ has capacity one,
and every $j\to B$ arc carries $1/N$, exactly its assignment capacity.
The resulting unit crosses $B\to B'$ and reaches $j_0^t$.

In the actual MFN system, however, the arc $B'\to j_0^t$ has capacity
$y_Bd_{j_0}=1/N<1$, and the $A$ bridge has zero capacity. The residual
unit cannot reach its sink. Thus this weakened check accepts a scenario
rejected by the source relaxation. The example does not prove that the
weakened formulation passes every other partial assignment, nor that all
single-commodity formulations have a large gap. It pinpoints the failure of
the proposed deletion in a specific required check.
