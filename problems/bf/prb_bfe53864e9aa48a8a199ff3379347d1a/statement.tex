\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(\Delta_d\) be the supremal density of congruent-ball packings in \(\mathbb R^d\), among all packings rather than only lattices. With Fourier convention \(\widehat f(\xi)=\int f(x)e^{-2\pi i x\cdot\xi}\,dx\), a real radial Schwartz function satisfying
\[
 \widehat f\geq0,\quad\widehat f(0)>0,\qquad
 f(x)\leq0\quad(\|x\|\geq r_0)
\]
gives the Cohn--Elkies bound
\[
 \Delta_d\leq\operatorname{vol}_d B(r_0/2)\,
                       \frac{f(0)}{\widehat f(0)}.
\]
The question asks whether this method proves optimality of \(E_8\) and the Leech lattice in dimensions eight and twenty-four.

The aid reports the known affirmative solution: Viazovska's magic function makes the bound sharp for \(E_8\), and the Cohn--Kumar--Miller--Radchenko--Viazovska function does so for the Leech lattice. In covolume-one normalizations their minimum distances are \(\sqrt2\) and \(2\), respectively, and the resulting densities are
\[
 \Delta_8=\frac{\pi^4}{384},\qquad
 \Delta_{24}=\frac{\pi^{12}}{12!}.
\]
The report also states uniqueness of the optimal periodic packings, up to the usual congruence and scale conventions. Literal uniqueness among all packings is not asserted: removing a density-zero set of balls preserves optimal density and changes the packing.

The paired attempt additionally records an equality condition. For a sharp covolume-one self-dual lattice certificate, every nonzero lattice-shell radius is a root of both radial functions \(f\) and \(\widehat f\). Every positive Fourier-shell root is stationary, as is every physical-shell root strictly greater than the cutoff \(r_0\). Thus
\[
 \widehat f'(r)=0\quad(r>0\text{ a forced Fourier root}),\qquad
 f'(r)=0\quad(r>r_0\text{ a forced physical root}).
\]
The first physical root at \(r_0\) lies at the boundary of the sign region and need not be stationary. These derivative conditions do not assert exact multiplicity two or analyticity of arbitrary Schwartz certificates.

For an equality packing \(P=\bigcup_{j=1}^m(a_j+\Lambda)\) with distinct cosets, define its structure factor at \(w\in\Lambda^*\) by
\[
 A(w)=\sum_{j=1}^m e^{2\pi i w\cdot a_j}.
\]
The reported Fourier equality condition forces \(\widehat f(w)=0\) for nonzero \(w\) only when \(A(w)\ne0\). All nonzero physical pair distances that actually occur must still lie in the zero set of \(f\). Cancellation of \(A(w)\) is therefore the obstruction to transferring every lattice Fourier-shell zero condition directly to a multi-coset equality argument. This is an additional equality lemma, not a replacement for the magic-function optimality proofs.
