\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(n,d\in\N\) with \(n,d\geq1\), and let \(P_n\subset[0,1]^d\) be a set of exactly \(n\) distinct points. The following formulation uses normalized discrepancy of boxes anchored at the origin, consistent with the background's reference to star discrepancy. For \(t=(t_1,\ldots,t_d)\in[0,1]^d\), put
\[
[0,t):=\prod_{j=1}^d[0,t_j),\qquad
\Delta_{P_n}(t):=\frac{\#(P_n\cap[0,t))}{n}-\prod_{j=1}^d t_j.
\]
Thus the boxes are half-open; a coordinate \(t_j=0\) makes the box empty, and points with a coordinate equal to \(1\) are excluded from every such box. Define
\[
D_1(P_n):=\int_{[0,1]^d}|\Delta_{P_n}(t)|\,\mathrm{d}t,
\qquad
D_\infty(P_n):=\sup_{t\in[0,1]^d}|\Delta_{P_n}(t)|,
\]
where integration is with respect to Lebesgue measure. The latter quantity is the star discrepancy under this convention. For \(p\in\{1,\infty\}\), define the least attainable discrepancy by
\[
\delta_p(n,d):=\inf_{\substack{P\subset[0,1]^d\\\#P=n}}D_p(P).
\]
The intended extremal problem is to determine sharp asymptotic orders of \(\delta_1(n,d)\) and \(\delta_\infty(n,d)\). Under a fixed-dimension interpretation, this means finding explicit positive comparison functions \(f_{p,d}(n)\) and constants \(0<c_{p,d}\leq C_{p,d}<\infty\) such that, for all sufficiently large integers \(n\),
\[
c_{p,d}f_{p,d}(n)\leq\delta_p(n,d)\leq C_{p,d}f_{p,d}(n),
\qquad p\in\{1,\infty\}.
\]
Here a lower bound must hold for every admissible point set, while an upper bound requires the existence of suitable point sets.

\subsection{Short English statement}
Determine the smallest possible \(L^1\) discrepancy and star discrepancy of an \(n\)-point set in the unit cube, by proving matching asymptotic lower and upper bounds.
