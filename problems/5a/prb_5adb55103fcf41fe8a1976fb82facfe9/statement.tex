\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(M\) be either \(S^3\) or \(S^2\times S^1\), and let \(K\subset M\) be a knot admitting a solid-torus tubular neighbourhood \(\nu K\). Write \(X_K=M\setminus\operatorname{int}(\nu K)\). A slope on \(\partial X_K\) is an unoriented isotopy class of essential simple closed curves. The meridional slope \(\mu_K\) bounds a disk in \(\nu K\). For a slope \(\alpha\), let \(M_K(\alpha)\) denote the manifold obtained by attaching a solid torus to \(X_K\) so that its meridian represents \(\alpha\). In particular, \(M_K(\mu_K)\cong M\). The usual meaning of a nontrivial surgery is \(\alpha\ne\mu_K\); the supplied record does not explicitly define this term.

A genus-two Heegaard splitting is a decomposition \(M=H_1\cup_\Sigma H_2\), where each \(H_i\) is a genus-two handlebody and \(\Sigma=\partial H_1=\partial H_2\). An essential simple closed curve \(c\subset\partial H_i\) is primitive if there is a properly embedded disk \(D_i\subset H_i\) such that \(c\) and \(\partial D_i\) meet transversely in exactly one point. Call \(K\) doubly primitive if it is ambient isotopic to a curve \(c\subset\Sigma\) for some genus-two Heegaard splitting of \(M\), with \(c\) primitive in both handlebodies. A nontrivial knot in \(S^3\) means a knot not ambient isotopic to the unknot.

Let \(\mathcal L\) denote the class of manifolds counted as lens spaces under the intended source convention. A common convention uses \(L(p,q)\), with integers \(p\ge1\) and \(\gcd(p,q)=1\): these are the quotients of \(S^3\subset\C^2\) by the cyclic action generated by \((z_1,z_2)\mapsto(e^{2\pi i/p}z_1,e^{2\pi iq/p}z_2)\). This convention includes \(S^3=L(1,q)\). Other conventions exclude \(S^3\) or additionally allow \(S^2\times S^1\). The record does not settle these boundary cases, so \(\mathcal L\) is left explicit below.

The supplied record asks the following two questions, the first named the Berge conjecture:
\begin{enumerate}
\item For every nontrivial knot \(K\subset S^3\), does
\[
\bigl(\exists\text{ a slope }\alpha\text{ on }\partial X_K:\ (S^3)_K(\alpha)\in\mathcal L\bigr)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad K\text{ is doubly primitive}
\]
hold?
\item Interpreting ``nontrivial surgery'' as nonmeridional surgery, for every knot \(K\subset S^2\times S^1\), does
\[
\bigl(\exists\text{ a slope }\alpha\ne\mu_K\text{ on }\partial X_K:\
(S^2\times S^1)_K(\alpha)\in\mathcal L\bigr)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad K\text{ is doubly primitive}
\]
hold?
\end{enumerate}
No nontrivial-knot hypothesis is imposed in the second question, and no integrality restriction on the surgery slope is supplied in either question.

There is an unresolved qualification in the first question: its wording does not explicitly exclude meridional filling. If \(S^3\in\mathcal L\), meridional filling satisfies its antecedent for every knot. A reading that requires nonmeridional surgery would instead quantify over \(\alpha\ne\mu_K\) in the first question as well. The intended restriction must be checked against the cited source; it has not been silently inserted here.
\subsection{Short English statement}
The record asks whether every nontrivial knot in \(S^3\) admitting a lens-space surgery is doubly primitive, and asks the analogous question for knots in \(S^2\times S^1\) admitting nontrivial lens-space surgery. Doubly primitive means that the knot can lie on a genus-two Heegaard surface as a primitive curve on each side. The lens-space convention and the missing explicit nonmeridional restriction in the first question require review.
