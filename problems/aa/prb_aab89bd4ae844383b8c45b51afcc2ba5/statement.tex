\textbf{Supplied pure bounded-metric setting.} Work in the distance-only language with complete metric structures of diameter at most one. Code separable spaces by infinite symmetric distance matrices on $\omega$, with zero diagonal, entries in $[0,1]$ and triangle inequalities, in the product topology; pseudometric codes may be quotiented before completion. Vershik genericity here refers to the Baire-category universal-request construction in this coding space, not automatically to a probabilistic or expanded-language genericity.

A vector $r=(r_i)_{i=1}^n\in[0,1]^n$ over a finite metric space $(a_i,d_{ij})$ is Kat\v etov if
\[
 |r_i-r_j|\le d_{ij}\le r_i+r_j\quad\text{for all }i,j.
\]
The approximate one-point extension property says that for every such finite request and every $\eps>0$ there is a point $x$ with $|d(x,a_i)-r_i|<\eps$ for every $i$. In this setting the aid reports Usvyatsov's 2008 theorem identifying the completions of Vershik-generic universal distance matrices with the unique separable existentially closed metric space, the Urysohn sphere. Existential closure means that every quantifier-free approximate existential condition over the space which is achievable in an extension is already arbitrarily well achievable in the space. The approximate extension property is the concrete bridge. The bounded sphere is distinguished from the unbounded Urysohn space named in the broad source question.

\textbf{Supplied quantitative repair.} For a nonempty finite diameter-at-most-one metric space and $q\in[0,1]^n$, define the two-sided defect
\[
 \eta=\max_{i,j}\max\bigl(0,\ |q_i-q_j|-d_{ij},\ d_{ij}-q_i-q_j\bigr).
\]
The aid's construction is
\[
 s_i=\min_j(q_j+d_{ij}),\qquad
 r_i=\min(1,s_i+3\eta/2).
\]
It asserts that $r$ is an exact Kat\v etov vector and
\[
 0\le r_i-q_i\le3\eta/2\quad\text{for every }i.

The aid uses this repair to obtain a countable dense-open request scheme equivalent to approximate one-point extension, with antecedent/witness tolerance margin $\delta=\eps/4$. This quantitative local statement supports the stated bounded-metric bridge; it does not establish that existential closure and every notion of genericity coincide for arbitrary metric theories, extra structure, perturbation systems or measures.
