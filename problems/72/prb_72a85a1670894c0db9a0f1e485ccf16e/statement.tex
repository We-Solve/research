\section*{Strong colouring of matroid-graph pairs}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $G=(V,E)$ be a finite simple graph of maximum degree $\Delta\ge2$,
and let $M=(V,\mathcal I)$ be a matroid on the same vertex set.
Its independent sets are hereditary and satisfy exchange: if
$I,J\in\mathcal I$ with $|I|<|J|$, some $e\in J\setminus I$ can be
added to $I$ while remaining independent. The maximal independent
sets, called bases, all have the same size $r$.

Assume that $M$ contains $2\Delta$ pairwise disjoint bases.
Must it contain bases $B_1,\ldots,B_{2\Delta}$ such that
\[
 B_i\cap B_j=\varnothing\quad(i\ne j),\qquad
                      E(G[B_i])=\varnothing\quad(1\le i\le2\Delta)?
\]
Thus every selected basis must also be a stable vertex set in the
graph. The conclusion may use entirely different bases from those
witnessing the initial packing assumption.

The entire ground set need not be exhausted by the selected bases:
the hypothesis says that disjoint bases exist, not that $V$ is their
union. Stability is imposed separately within each basis, so edges
joining two different selected bases are permitted. The matroid is
arbitrary and is not assumed to be a partition matroid or representable
over a field. The same family must satisfy the graph restrictions
and the basis-packing requirement simultaneously.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does packing $2\Delta$ disjoint matroid bases suffice to pack
that many bases that are each stable in a graph of maximum degree $\Delta$?
