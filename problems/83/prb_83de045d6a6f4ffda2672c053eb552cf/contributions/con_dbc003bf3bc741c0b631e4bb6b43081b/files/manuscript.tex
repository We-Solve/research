\section{Recovered task and retained composite scope}
The primary PDF supplies the well-formed first target: nonnegative integer
$I$ by $J$ tables with fixed row sums, column sums and
$Q(T)=\sum_{i,j}(i-2)(j-2)T_{ij}$. Although described as quadratic,
this is linear in the table entries; the weights are quadratic in labels.
The preserved extraction also contains cone-testing, p-value-comparison
and multiway binary-margin questions. These are additional unresolved
directions here, not silently replaced by a zero-row-face theorem.

\paragraph{Why the standard margin basis fails.}
The usual rectangle move adds one at $(i,j),(k,l)$ and subtracts one at
$(i,l),(k,j)$, with $i\ne k$ and $j\ne l$. It changes $Q$ by
\[
(i-2)(j-2)+(k-2)(l-2)-(i-2)(l-2)-(k-2)(j-2)
=(i-k)(j-l)\ne0.
\]
Thus none of these nonzero moves preserves the extra statistic. In fact
there are no nonzero degree-two moves at all: equality of row and column
sums for two multisets of two cells either forces the multisets to agree
or switches the pairing between two distinct rows and columns. The latter
is exactly a rectangle and fails the statistic. This argument works in
every nontrivial size, not just the displayed $3$ by $4$ example.

\paragraph{A necessary cubic in the displayed format.}
In $3$ by $4$ tables fix row sums $(1,1,1)$ and column sums $(1,1,1,0)$.
Every feasible table is a permutation matrix in the first three columns.
For permutations written by their successive column choices, the six
$Q$ values are
\[
Q(123)=2,\quad Q(132)=Q(213)=1,\quad
Q(231)=Q(312)=-1,\quad Q(321)=-2.
\]
Each follows by direct substitution into $Q$; a Python 3.14.2 enumeration
of all six permutations gave exactly these values. In the $Q=1$ fiber
there are precisely the two matrices $P_{132}$ and $P_{213}$, with
disjoint positive supports. Consequently any Markov basis connecting
every fiber must contain their difference up to sign: there is no third
state through which a path could pass, and a step joining the two states
must be precisely their difference. Its positive and negative parts each
have total three, so this cubic is indispensable. The same argument
applies to the $Q=-1$ pair. All claims here concern these explicitly fixed
fibers; they are not a complete basis for the full model.

\paragraph{Attempted global extension and gap.}
A tempting route is to combine two rectangle switches so their changes
in $Q$ cancel. Cancellation gives a lattice move, but does not prove
that a path through such moves stays nonnegative or connects every
fiber. The two-point cubic fibers already exclude any proposal restricted
to degree two. A full bounded-degree move classification with a
nonnegative-path argument remains missing. The additional source questions
do not specify one null distribution, approximation metric or arbitrary
multiway-margin format, so the cubic computation cannot answer them.
