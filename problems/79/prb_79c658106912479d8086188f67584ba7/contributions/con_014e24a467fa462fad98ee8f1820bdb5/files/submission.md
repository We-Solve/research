# Quadratic graph diameter for every simple graphical zonotope

Problem: TOP-30001930

Claim type: Partial result

Exact scope: Every graphical zonotope of a connected simple graph on d+1 vertices, and every invertible affine image, has vertex-edge diameter at most |E| <= d(d+1)/2.

Let G be any connected simple graph on vertices 0,...,d. In the d-dimensional subspace sum(x_i)=0 form
\[
 Z_G=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E}[-(e_i-e_j),e_i-e_j].
\]
Connectivity makes its affine dimension d: a spanning tree's edge differences span the subspace. The result is a uniform polynomial bound in the literal parameters (n,d) for this infinite family; n is the number of facets. It covers arbitrary d and arbitrary graph density, but only bounded polytopes of this specified form.

Here is a general lemma, with its relevant cost parameter visible. A zonotope generated by m nonzero, pairwise nonparallel vectors has graph diameter at most m. For an objective u not perpendicular to any generator, its unique maximizing vertex is
\[
 z(u)=\sum_{j=1}^m\operatorname{sgn}(u\cdot g_j)g_j.
\]
Every vertex has such an objective: its full-dimensional open normal cone avoids a finite union of hyperplanes. Choose objectives u,v for two given vertices so that their line segment avoids intersections of two distinct generator hyperplanes and is not contained in any such hyperplane. These choices exist within the two open cones: the finitely many simultaneous-crossing conditions are proper algebraic conditions, and a small independent perturbation avoids them. In dimension one there is only one generator direction and the claim is immediate.

Along this segment each scalar product changes sign at most once. At a crossing of exactly one generator hyperplane, the maximizing face consists of the fixed sum of all other selected endpoints plus the entire segment [-g_j,g_j]. It is an exposed edge, whose endpoints are precisely the two successive maximizing vertices. Thus the objective segment produces a walk of at most m bounded edges. Repeated vertices, if any, can simply be omitted from a shortest-path comparison.

For a simple graph the generators e_i-e_j are pairwise nonparallel and m=|E|<=binom(d+1,2), proving the advertised bound. Invertible affine maps preserve the face lattice and vertex-edge graph. The proof is combinatorial; it uses no coordinate-bit-size hypothesis, pivot rule, or algorithm for navigating a general polyhedron. It also explains why merely writing a bound in the number of arbitrary zonotope generators would not automatically settle the original facet-parameter question: here simplicity of the underlying graph supplies the explicit polynomial generator bound.
