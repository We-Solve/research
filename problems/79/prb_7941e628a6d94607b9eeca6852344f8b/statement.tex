\section{Freeness of Two-Generator Integral Matrix Groups}\label{30006351}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 30006351 · Computer Science}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.


Free generation by $A,B$ means no nonempty reduced word in $A^{\pm1},B^{\pm1}$ evaluates to the identity, where reduction cancels adjacent inverse pairs. Uniform decidability requires one terminating procedure for all encoded dimensions.

The group $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb Z)$ consists of integer matrices with integer inverses, and $\mathrm{SL}_n$ imposes determinant one. The subgroup generated by matrices consists of finite products of them and their inverses. A centralizer consists of elements commuting with the specified element.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Given two integral matrices $A,B\in\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{Z})$, decide whether the subgroup $\langle A,B\rangle$ is freely generated by $A$ and $B$. Is this decision problem solvable uniformly in $n$? \sref{30006351}{2}
