\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(n,k\) be positive integers. Using the convention that \(+1\) represents truth, a linear threshold function is a function \(h\colon\{-1,1\}^n\to\{-1,1\}\) admitting weights \(w\in\R^n\) and a threshold \(t\in\R\) such that
\[
h(x)=\begin{cases}+1,&\langle w,x\rangle\ge t,\\-1,&\langle w,x\rangle<t.\end{cases}
\]
An intersection of \(k\) such functions \(h_1,\ldots,h_k\) is the Boolean function \(f\) defined by
\[
f(x)=+1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad h_i(x)=+1\text{ for every }i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}.
\]
For an explicit conventional definition of noise sensitivity, let \(X\) be uniform on \(\{-1,1\}^n\). For \(\delta\in[0,1]\), independently flip each coordinate of \(X\) with probability \(\delta\) to obtain \(Y\), and set
\[
\operatorname{NS}_{\delta}[f]=\Pr\bigl(f(X)\ne f(Y)\bigr).
\]
The supplied source proposes the uniform estimate
\[
\operatorname{NS}_{\delta}[f]\le C\sqrt{\log k}\sqrt{\delta},
\]
where the \(O(\sqrt{\log k})\) notation is understood to require an absolute constant \(C>0\), independent of the dimension, the halfspaces, \(k\), and the admissible noise rate. The intended assertion concerns every intersection of \(k\) halfspaces in every dimension.

Two qualifications prevent a fully specified universal statement. First, the supplied record does not define its noise convention or the claimed range of \(\delta\); the independent coordinate-flip definition above is an explicit conventional interpretation, and its definability on \([0,1]\) does not establish that the source claims the estimate throughout that interval. Second, the displayed expression vanishes when \(k=1\), so it cannot literally bound the noise sensitivity of every single halfspace at positive noise rates. The record does not specify whether \(k\ge2\), an asymptotic restriction on \(k\), or a modification such as \(\sqrt{\log(2k)}\) is intended. No such repair is asserted here.

The supplied background reports an \(O(k)\sqrt{\delta}\) bound from Peres's theorem and describes it as the best known bound in that source context. It also reports that a Gaussian special case follows from Nazarov's work \([\mathrm{Naz03}]\), and that sufficiently regular halfspaces satisfy a bound of the form \(\operatorname{polylog}(k)\delta^{\Omega(1)}\) by \([\mathrm{HKM10}]\). These are supplied contextual claims; the Gaussian formulation, regularity hypothesis, and exponents are not specified in the record.
\subsection{Short English statement}
The proposed bound says that the probability that an intersection of \(k\) Boolean halfspaces changes value under random coordinate noise is at most a universal constant times \(\sqrt{\log k}\sqrt{\delta}\). The noise convention, noise range, and treatment of \(k=1\) require clarification. The registry labels the problem open; this is a statement-only draft requiring human mathematical review.
