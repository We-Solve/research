\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a topological group \(G\), call \(G\) \emph{inductively compact} if
\[
\forall F\subseteq G\text{ finite},\quad \exists K\leq G\quad \bigl(F\subseteq K\ \text{and}\ K\text{ is compact in the subspace topology}\bigr).
\]
The source asks whether this property is preserved under lattice isomorphisms between locally compact groups. The subgroup lattice intended by the source is not specified. Two possible readings are \(\operatorname{Sub}(G)\), the lattice of all subgroups ordered by inclusion, and \(\operatorname{ClSub}(G)\), the lattice of closed subgroups ordered by inclusion.

For a fixed choice \(\Lambda=\operatorname{Sub}\) or \(\Lambda=\operatorname{ClSub}\), the corresponding precise question is: for every pair of locally compact topological groups \(G,H\), if there is a bijection
\[
\Phi:\Lambda(G)\longrightarrow\Lambda(H),\qquad
A\subseteq B\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \Phi(A)\subseteq\Phi(B)
\quad(A,B\in\Lambda(G)),
\]
does inductive compactness of \(G\) imply inductive compactness of \(H\)? The choice of lattice remains unresolved; these are alternative readings, not two separately attributed conjectures. The supplied record also does not specify whether the term locally compact group includes a Hausdorff assumption, so that convention requires confirmation before fixing the domain.
\subsection{Short English statement}
If every finite subset of a locally compact group lies in a compact subgroup, must the same hold for every locally compact group with an isomorphic subgroup lattice? The source leaves the intended subgroup lattice and the Hausdorff convention unspecified.
