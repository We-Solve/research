\paragraph{Definitions and notation.}In the circular restricted three-body problem, two primaries follow prescribed circular motion and a test particle has negligible effect on them. In rotating coordinates the Jacobi constant combines kinetic energy and the effective gravitational/centrifugal potential. The Hill region is the set where the zero-velocity inequality permits motion. Its necks occur near collinear equilibria. In the spatial restricted problem, halo orbits are the named three-dimensional periodic families near the collinear libration-point region, with characteristic symmetries and out-of-plane motion. Bifurcation from a planar periodic family and existence of a numerically observed family are different assertions.

\paragraph{An exact local symmetry reduction, conditionally.}Suppose a smooth reversible shooting construction for the spatial restricted problem yields residuals \(F_1(a,b,c)\), \(F_2(a,b,c)\), where c is an initial vertical displacement and a,b are two planar shooting variables. Assume vertical reflection gives \(F_1(a,b,-c)=F_1(a,b,c)\) and \(F_2(a,b,-c)=-F_2(a,b,c)\). Then
\[F_2(a,b,c)=cK(a,b,c),\qquad K(a,b,c)=\int_0^1\partial_cF_2(a,b,tc)dt,
\]
and K is even in c. At c=0 it equals the vertical linearized residual, so the planar trivial factor is removed without division at zero.
\paragraph{Conditional branch calculation.}Let \(G=(F_1,K)\), and suppose \(G(a_*,b_*,0)=0\) and \(J=D_{(a,b)}G(a_*,b_*,0)\) is invertible. The local implicit-function theorem gives unique smooth a(c),b(c) solving G=0. Reflection and uniqueness make them even, so their first derivatives vanish at zero; differentiating twice yields
\[\binom{a''(0)}{b''(0)}=-J^{-1}\partial_c^2G(a_*,b_*,0).
\]
For nonzero c the full residuals vanish and vertical displacement is nonzero. If the shooting setup's two endpoints are fixed by the reversing involution, time reversal joins the orbit segment to a periodic orbit. This last geometric correspondence is a required hypothesis, not inferred from arbitrary residual names.
