{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 30002996 · Computer Science}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.


A learning problem specifies a data distribution, a hypothesis class and a loss. Statistical consistency means that the estimator approaches its population target as the sample size increases; statistical and computational requirements are distinct.

A statistical query is an expectation $\mathbb E_{x\sim P}[q(x)]$ for a bounded function $q$. Adaptive queries may depend on earlier answers, so accuracy must account for the dependence between the sample and the selected queries.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Given a population distribution P on X and an i.i.d. sample S of size n, can a possibly randomized procedure M(S) answer every adaptively chosen sequence of statistical queries accurately with high probability over S and M's randomness? \sref{30002996}{2}
