\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $k$ be totally real, let $n\geq2$ be odd (hence $n\geq3$), and let $p$ be any prime. The source's Conjecture 2 has three parts, all of which are retained:
\begin{enumerate}
\item There exists $q_p(n,k)\in\Q^*$ such that
\[
 L_p(n,\omega_p^{1-n},k)D_k^{1/2,p}
 =q_p(n,k)\operatorname{Eul}_p(n,k)R_{n,p}(k).
\]
\item With compatible choices, $q_p(n,k)=q(n,k)$, where $q(n,k)$ is the rational factor in the source's Borel complex special-value formula.
\item Both $L_p(n,\omega_p^{1-n},k)$ and $R_{n,p}(k)$ are nonzero.
\end{enumerate}
Here $L_p$ is the specified $p$-adic $L$-function branch with the indicated cyclotomic finite-order character twist $\omega_p^{1-n}$, $D_k$ is the field discriminant, $D_k^{1/2,p}$ its chosen $p$-adic square-root realization, $\operatorname{Eul}_p(n,k)$ the prescribed Euler-factor term, and $R_{n,p}(k)$ the determinant regulator in degree $2n-1$. In the usual totally real odd-$n$ setting this regulator uses rank $[k:\Q]$ rational $K$-theory data. The source does not reproduce its basis, target embeddings, regulator normalization, exact Euler product or the complex formula defining $q(n,k)$.

Those choices are essential: rational factors and square-root signs cannot be compared after independently rescaling complex and $p$-adic regulators. The equality is in the intended $p$-adic coefficient field, with $\Q^*$ embedded there. The phrase any prime includes $p=2$, for which character/branch conventions also need specification; it is not silently excluded.

The nonvanishing part is separate. An equality with a nonzero rational factor does not rule out both the special value and regulator vanishing, so it must not be omitted or treated as an automatic consequence of the formula. Conversely, numerical agreement in examples is not a general proof of the rational-factor equality.
