\section{Research attempt: A knockout identifiability test with an invisible interaction}
Use the state space $\mathbb F_2^3$ with coordinatewise polynomial maps. A single knockout clamps one selected coordinate to zero at every update; the initial state is the baseline $b=(1,1,1)$ with that coordinate zero. Also observe the unclamped baseline trajectory. Consider
\[
F_0(x)=x,\qquad F_1(x)=x+(0,0,g(x)),\qquad
g(x)=(1-x_1)(1-x_2).
\]
At the baseline and each of the three single-knockout initial states, at least one of $x_1,x_2$ equals one. Thus $g=0$ there, and both update maps fix that state. Applying the specified clamp afterward leaves it fixed as well. By induction every time point in every one of the four observed trajectories agrees exactly for $F_0$ and $F_1$. Infinite observation time for those interventions cannot distinguish these two models.

At the double-knockout state $(0,0,1)$, clamping coordinates one and two, $g=1$. The next third coordinate is one under $F_0$ and zero under $F_1$, so the double knockout distinguishes them in one step. The invisible perturbation is a quadratic interaction; it is not a failure caused by observation noise or insufficient observation duration.

There is a complementary exact interpolation result for a restricted class, conditional on observing the un-clamped scalar update at every indicated design state. This requires recording that update before the clamp, or using a separate function-evaluation protocol; the forced-zero output of a clamped coordinate cannot be substituted for its missing function value. For any affine scalar coordinate function $f(x)=a_0+a_1x_1+a_2x_2+a_3x_3$ over a field, its values at $b$ and $b-e_i$ determine $a_i=f(b)-f(b-e_i)$, and then $a_0=f(b)-\sum_i a_i$. These equations prove uniqueness and reconstruct the function. Over $\mathbb F_2$ the same formulas hold with subtraction equal to addition. Consequently the baseline and single-knockout design states can remove ambiguity in an affine dictionary when those un-clamped function values are available, while the actual clamped trajectories in the displayed quadratic example remain ambiguous.

In a general fixed finite dictionary $\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_r$, adding evaluated states stacks rows of the evaluation matrix. Its coefficient nullspace can only shrink: a vector satisfying every old and new homogeneous row equation satisfies every old one. Strict shrinkage, however, requires a new row not already implied by the old rows, and the example shows that even many observations need not achieve full rank.
