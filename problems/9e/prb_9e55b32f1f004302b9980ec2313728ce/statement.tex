\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(G\) be a semisimple Lie group and let \(\Gamma\leq G\) be a lattice, meaning a discrete subgroup for which \(G/\Gamma\) has finite \(G\)-invariant volume. Regard \(\Gamma\) as a discrete group. A model for the classifying space for proper \(\Gamma\)-actions, denoted \(\underline{E}\Gamma\), is a \(\Gamma\)-CW complex \(Y\) such that, for every subgroup \(H\leq\Gamma\),
\[
Y^H=\{y\in Y:hy=y\text{ for every }h\in H\}
\quad\text{satisfies}\quad
\begin{cases}
Y^H\text{ is contractible},&H\text{ is finite},\\
Y^H=\varnothing,&H\text{ is infinite}.
\end{cases}
\]
Use \emph{cofinite} here to mean that \(Y\) has finitely many \(\Gamma\)-orbits of cells. This convention requires confirmation against the source's intended use of the term.

\textbf{Question.} For every lattice \(\Gamma\) in a semisimple Lie group \(G\), does there exist an analogue of the Borel--Serre compactification that provides a cofinite model \(Y\) for \(\underline{E}\Gamma\)? In particular, the requested construction must satisfy the fixed-point conditions above and have finitely many \(\Gamma\)-orbits of cells.
