\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(n\) be a positive integer and let \(p\) be a prime with \(p>n\). The positive-integer range for \(n\) is the usual Engel convention; the supplied source does not explicitly specify its range. A group \(G\) is locally finite if every finitely generated subgroup of \(G\) is finite, and it is a \(p\)-group if every element has finite order a power of \(p\). Define
\[
[x,{}_1y]=[x,y]=x^{-1}y^{-1}xy,\qquad [x,{}_{k+1}y]=[[x,{}_ky],y]\quad(k\geq 1).
\]
The group \(G\) is \(n\)-Engel if \([x,{}_ny]=1\) for all \(x,y\in G\).

For a group \(H\), put \(\gamma_1(H)=H\) and \(\gamma_{k+1}(H)=[\gamma_k(H),H]\), where \([A,B]\) denotes the subgroup generated by the commutators \([a,b]\) with \(a\in A\) and \(b\in B\). The group \(H\) is nilpotent if \(\gamma_{c+1}(H)=\{1\}\) for some integer \(c\geq 0\). Here a \emph{Fitting group} means a group generated by its nilpotent normal subgroups:
\[
G=\left\langle N\mid N\trianglelefteq G\text{ and }N\text{ is nilpotent}\right\rangle.
\]
Equivalently, for each \(g\in G\), its normal closure
\[
\langle g^G\rangle=\left\langle h^{-1}gh\mid h\in G\right\rangle
\]
is nilpotent. No uniform bound on these nilpotency classes is required. The supplied source does not explicitly define the term Fitting group.

\textbf{Question (G. Traustason, Kourovka Notebook 16.96).} For every positive integer \(n\), every prime \(p>n\), and every locally finite \(n\)-Engel \(p\)-group \(G\), must \(G\) be a Fitting group?

The source attributes to N. Gupta and F. Levin examples showing that the restriction \(p>n\) is necessary in general. It supplies neither the examples nor a bibliographic reference for them. This qualification is retained as a source attribution, without asserting a counterexample for every pair \((n,p)\) with \(p\leq n\).
\subsection{Short English statement}
G. Traustason asks whether every locally finite \(n\)-Engel \(p\)-group with \(p>n\) is generated by nilpotent normal subgroups. Equivalently, must every element have nilpotent normal closure? The source states that examples of N. Gupta and F. Levin make the restriction \(p>n\) necessary in general.
