\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 30003649}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For each integer \(n\geq 1\), let \(\mathbb S^n\) denote the real symmetric \(n\times n\) matrices. A matrix \(A\in\mathbb S^n\) is \emph{completely positive} if there exist an integer \(r\geq 1\) and vectors \(v_1,\ldots,v_r\in\R_{\geq 0}^n\) such that
\[
A=\sum_{k=1}^{r}v_kv_k^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Equivalently, \(A=BB^{\mathsf T}\) for an entrywise nonnegative real matrix \(B\in\R_{\geq 0}^{n\times r}\). Write \(\mathcal{CP}_n\) for the cone of these matrices. A \emph{rational completely positive factorization} requires \(B\in\Q_{\geq 0}^{n\times r}\); an \emph{integral completely positive factorization} requires \(B\in\Z_{\geq 0}^{n\times r}\). The number \(r\) may depend on \(A\), and no bound on it is imposed.

The supplied record asks the following two questions.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Is it true that
\[
\forall n\geq 1\;\forall A\in\mathcal{CP}_n\cap\Q^{n\times n},\quad
\exists r\geq 1\;\exists B\in\Q_{\geq 0}^{n\times r}
\quad A=BB^{\mathsf T}?
\]
\item Is it true that
\[
\forall A\in\mathcal{CP}_2\cap\Z^{2\times 2},\quad
\exists r\geq 1\;\exists B\in\Z_{\geq 0}^{2\times r}
\quad A=BB^{\mathsf T}?
\]
\end{enumerate}
The first question includes matrices on the boundary of \(\mathcal{CP}_n\), and both questions include singular matrices and the zero matrix.

\textbf{Supplied dated qualification.} The record's literature assessment dated 2026-08-21 reports an affirmative answer to the first question for rational matrices in \(\operatorname{int}_{\mathbb S^n}(\mathcal{CP}_n)\), and an affirmative answer to the second question for every integral \(2\times 2\) completely positive matrix. Here the interior is taken in the usual Euclidean topology on \(\mathbb S^n\). The assessment reports that the first question remains unresolved for arbitrary rational completely positive matrices on the boundary. Accordingly, the record labels the combined problem \emph{partially solved}. These are supplied dated curation claims, not independently verified mathematical or current-status conclusions.
\subsection{Short English statement}
A completely positive matrix is a finite sum of outer products of nonnegative real vectors. The questions ask whether rational entries always permit rational vectors, and whether integral entries in dimension \(2\) always permit integral vectors. The supplied assessment dated 2026-08-21 reports affirmative results for rational interior matrices and for the integral \(2\times 2\) question, while leaving the general rational boundary case unresolved; this assessment has not been independently verified.
