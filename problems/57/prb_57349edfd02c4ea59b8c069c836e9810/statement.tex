\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $k$ be a field of characteristic $p>3$, and let $\Sigma_d$ be the symmetric group on $d$ letters. Let $M,N$ be indecomposable self-dual $k\Sigma_d$-modules, each having both a Specht filtration and a dual Specht filtration. In the usual finite-dimensional setting, self-dual means isomorphic to $\operatorname{Hom}_k(M,k)$, respectively $\operatorname{Hom}_k(N,k)$, with the contragredient group action.

A Specht filtration is a finite submodule filtration whose successive quotients are Specht modules $S^\lambda$ for partitions $\lambda\vdash d$; a dual Specht filtration has quotients $(S^\lambda)^*$. Each module must admit both kinds, not just one filtration on $M$ and the other on $N$. The two filtrations need not have identical section orders.

The restored questions, identified in the verification as Problems 4 and 5, are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Must $M\otimes_kN$, with diagonal action $\sigma(m\otimes n)=(\sigma m)\otimes(\sigma n)$, be a direct sum of signed Young modules?
\item Must
\[
 \operatorname{Ext}^i_{k\Sigma_d}(M,N)=0
 \qquad\text{for every integer }1\le i\le p-2\ ?
\]
\end{enumerate}
Here Ext is taken in the $k\Sigma_d$-module category. No vanishing at $i=0$ or $i\ge p-1$ is asserted.

In the standard convention, signed Young modules are the indecomposable summands of signed Young permutation modules induced from a Young subgroup with the trivial character on one block of factors and the sign character on the other; symbolically these permutation modules have the form
\[
 \operatorname{Ind}_{\Sigma_\alpha\times\Sigma_\beta}^{\Sigma_d}
       (k\boxtimes\operatorname{sgn}),\qquad |\alpha|+|\beta|=d.
\]
