\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Fix a real transcendental $\alpha$ and define
\[
f_\alpha(n)=\min_{\substack{A\subset\R\ \mathrm{finite}\\|A|=n}}|A+\alpha A|,\qquad
A+\alpha A=\{a+\alpha b:a,b\in A\}.
\]
The source question, attributed to Izabella \L aba, asks for the best lower bound. Its historical remark credits Konyagin--\L aba with $|A+\alpha A|\gg n\log n/\log\log n$ and examples of size $n\exp(c\sqrt{\log n})$. The real domain is supplied by the aid; the original sentence does not explicitly state it.

The attached aid attributes to Conlon--Lim the matching-scale lower bound
\[
|A+\alpha A|\ge |A|\exp(c\sqrt{\log|A|})
\]
for a positive absolute constant in the stated setting. Together with the construction scale it reports
\[
\log\bigl(f_\alpha(n)/n\bigr)=\Theta(\sqrt{\log n})\quad(n\to\infty).
\]
Thus the growth scale is settled as an attributed literature result, while the optimal exponential constant and finer asymptotics remain open in the aid's assessment.

For integers $h\ge2$ and $m\ge1$, its exact construction is
\[
B_{h,m}=\left\{\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}a_i\alpha^i:0\le a_i<h\right\},\qquad |B_{h,m}|=h^m,
\]
\[
|B_{h,m}+\alpha B_{h,m}|=h^2(2h-1)^{m-1}.
\]
Transcendence gives uniqueness of the integer coefficient expansions; the first and last sum coefficients have $h$ choices and each interior coefficient has $2h-1$. Optimizing the containing box and selecting a subset yields, for every sufficiently large integer $N$, an $N$-point example with
\[
|A+\alpha A|\le N\exp\left((2\sqrt{\log2}+o(1))\sqrt{\log N}\right),
\]
using natural logarithms. This is a construction upper bound, not an assertion that its leading constant is optimal.
