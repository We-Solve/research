\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $G$ be an infinite group and let $A\subseteq G$ be a finite generating set. For $j\geq0$, write $A^j=\{a_1\cdots a_j:a_i\in A\}$, with $A^0=\{1\}$. For $t\geq0$, define the source's counting function by \[B_A(t)=\left|\bigcup_{j=0}^{\lfloor t\rfloor}A^j\right|.\] It counts distinct group elements, not words or representations of those elements. The question is: how does $B_A(t)$ grow as $t\to\infty$? If the intended generating set is symmetric, $A=A^{-1}$, this is the usual word-metric ball growth. The statement does not expressly require symmetry or allow inverse letters, so the literal product definition above is retained; replacing $A$ by $A\cup A^{-1}$ would change the definition for an arbitrary given $A$. The source does not specify a particular group class, a candidate bound, or whether the target is an exact asymptotic or a classification up to rescaling the argument. Thus it poses a broad group-dependent growth-classification question, not one common rate asserted for every infinite finitely generated group. The supplied 2026-08-21 literature triage labels the record open, emphasizes dependence on the group, and calls the broad classification question unresolved.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Classify the growth of the number of distinct elements representable by at most $t$ generators. The group and the intended level of asymptotic precision are unrestricted; whether inverse generators are included must be made explicit.
