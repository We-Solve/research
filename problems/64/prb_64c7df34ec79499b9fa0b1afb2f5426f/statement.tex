\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $d\geq1$, let $e_1,\ldots,e_d$ be the standard basis of $\R^d$, and let $\|X\|=\sup_{\|v\|_2=1}\|Xv\|_2$ be the Euclidean operator norm. The source asks for a universal comparison
\[
\mathbb E\|X\|\leq C\,\mathbb E\max_{1\leq k\leq d}\|Xe_k\|_2,
\]
where $C<\infty$ is independent of $d$ and of the entry variances, and $X$ is a real symmetric Gaussian matrix whose upper-triangular entries are independent. The expectation remains outside the column maximum.

The intended centered arbitrary-variance model is
\[
X_{ij}=b_{ij}g_{ij}\quad(i\leq j),\qquad X_{ji}=X_{ij},
\]
where $b_{ij}\geq0$ are arbitrary deterministic numbers and the $g_{ij}$, $i\leq j$, are independent standard real Gaussians. Zero variances are permitted in this model. Centering is not explicitly stated in the extracted question, and is an essential missing qualification: arbitrary deterministic means can yield matrices with operator norm much larger than their maximum column norm as dimension grows. Thus the centered model is identified as the intended conjectural setting, not silently claimed to be present in the source wording.

The reverse comparison holds pointwise, $\max_k\|Xe_k\|_2\leq\|X\|$, so the requested upper bound gives equivalence up to a universal constant. The supplied research summary reports this comparison and its reverse settled for centered arbitrary-variance symmetric Gaussian matrices by Lata\l a--van Handel--Youssef (2018). No explicit universal constant or independent proof is supplied here.
\subsection{Short English statement}
The question compares the expected norm of a symmetric Gaussian matrix with the expected largest column norm, uniformly over dimension and variances. The supplied summary reports the centered Gaussian conjecture solved. The original extraction omits centering, which is necessary for the universal statement.
