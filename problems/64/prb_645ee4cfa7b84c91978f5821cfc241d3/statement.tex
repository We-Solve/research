\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A convex body in \(\R^n\) means a compact convex set with nonempty interior. For the usual nondegenerate interpretation of the question, let \(n\geq 2\) be an integer and let \(t>0\). B(c,t)=\{x\in\R^n:\|x-c\|\leq t\},\qquad S(c,t)=\partial B(c,t).
\]
Suppose that \(K,L\subset\R^n\) are convex bodies and
\[
S(c,t)\subset\operatorname{int}(K)\cap\operatorname{int}(L).
\]
By convexity, this also implies \(B(c,t)\subset\operatorname{int}(K)\cap\operatorname{int}(L)\), so interpreting the enclosed sphere as its solid ball gives the same containment condition. For each unit vector \(u\in\R^n\), let
\[
H(u)=\{x\in\R^n:\langle x-c,u\rangle=t\}.
\]
These are precisely the hyperplanes tangent to the common sphere \(S(c,t)\). Write \(\operatorname{vol}_{n-1}\) for Euclidean \((n-1)\)-dimensional volume in each such hyperplane. The question is whether, for every choice of the parameters and bodies above,
\[
\bigl[\operatorname{vol}_{n-1}(K\cap H(u))=\operatorname{vol}_{n-1}(L\cap H(u))\text{ for every }u\in\R^n\text{ with }\|u\|=1\bigr]
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad K=L.
\]

\subsection{Short English statement}
If two convex bodies contain the same sphere strictly inside them, and their sections by every hyperplane tangent to that sphere have equal \((n-1)\)-dimensional volumes, must the bodies coincide?
