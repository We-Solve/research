\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(F_3=\langle x_1,x_2,x_3\mid\ \rangle\) be the free group of rank \(3\). Let \(\operatorname{Aut}(F_3)\) be its automorphism group, and let
\[
\operatorname{Inn}(F_3)=\{c_g:g\in F_3\},\qquad c_g(h)=ghg^{-1}.
\]
Its outer automorphism group is
\[
\operatorname{Out}(F_3)=\operatorname{Aut}(F_3)/\operatorname{Inn}(F_3).
\]
A group \(G\) is called linear if there exist a commutative field \(K\), a positive integer \(n\), and an injective group homomorphism \(G\hookrightarrow\operatorname{GL}_n(K)\), where \(\operatorname{GL}_n(K)\) denotes the group of invertible \(n\times n\) matrices over \(K\).

\textbf{Question (Kourovka Notebook 15.103).} Is \(\operatorname{Out}(F_3)\) linear? Equivalently, do there exist a field \(K\) and an integer \(n\geq 1\) admitting a faithful representation
\[
\rho:\operatorname{Out}(F_3)\longrightarrow\operatorname{GL}_n(K)?
\]
The source does not specify a field or characteristic; the formulation above uses the conventional meaning of linearity over some field. The source describes this as a well-known problem and lists V. E. Shpilrain as proposer.
