\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Question 1.13 asks how unique the open books associated with a contact manifold are. The original background reports Giroux's statement that open books from Donaldson's construction, depending on a positive integer \(k\), are unique up to stabilization for sufficiently large \(k\). The supplied verification specifies positive stabilization, together with conjugations/isotopies and the appropriate Weinstein homotopies, not stabilization of arbitrary sign.

For a closed cooriented contact manifold \((M^{2n+1},\xi)\), \(n\geq1\), an abstract supporting open book has compact \(2n\)-dimensional page \(W\) and boundary-trivial monodromy, with the relevant exact symplectic data. In the Weinstein setting the page has a Liouville structure and a compatible gradient-like Morse function. The aid reports Breen--Honda--Huang's stronger modern uniqueness result: all strongly Weinstein open books supporting fixed \((M,\xi)\) are strongly stably equivalent. The precise strong/weak Weinstein data and equivalence definitions are not included in this record and must be taken from that theorem, rather than replaced by the sole requirement that a page be Liouville.

The aid reports the weakly Weinstein comparison as still open in its account; arbitrary Liouville pages are not asserted to satisfy the same uniqueness. Thus the strongly Weinstein result extends the source's high-\(k\) Donaldson subclass, but does not settle every possible unrestricted reading of the question.

The supplied stabilization invariant is purely topological once the operation is fixed. A positive stabilization attaches a Weinstein handle of index \(n\) to \(W\), then changes monodromy by the positive Dehn twist prescribed by the stabilization data. Consequently
\[
 H_j(W',W;\R)=\begin{cases}\R,&j=n,\\0,&j\ne n,\end{cases}
\]
and the inclusion induces
\[
 H_j(W;\R)\simeq H_j(W';\R)
 \qquad(j\notin\{n-1,n\}).
\]
The Euler characteristic changes by
\[
 \chi(W')=\chi(W)+(-1)^n.
\]
Thus \(h(W)=(-1)^n\chi(W)\) increases by exactly one at each positive stabilization. Conjugations, isotopies and Weinstein homotopies preserve these topological quantities.

For any stable-equivalence chain from \(W_0\) to \(W_1\), with \(N_+\) positive stabilization steps and \(N_-\) inverse positive-stabilization steps,
\[
 h(W_1)-h(W_0)=N_+-N_-.
\]
The inverse steps are destabilizations of the positive operation, not negative-contact stabilizations. If common positive stabilizations are reached after \(a\) steps from \(W_0\) and \(b\) from \(W_1\), their equality forces
\[
 a-b=h(W_1)-h(W_0).
\]
This is a necessary count constraint, not a claim that every two books have such a common model or that the count determines monodromy.

In particular, unequal page homology in any degree outside \(\{n-1,n\}\) obstructs equivalence under these stabilizations. For arbitrary Liouville pages this can be an obstruction independent of contact-support information; the record supplies no concrete pair to classify. The invariant and count audit do not resolve the missing technical comparison in the weakly Weinstein setting.
\subsection{Short English statement}
The aid reports stable uniqueness for strongly Weinstein supporting open books, extending the source's Donaldson subclass. Positive stabilization changes a page by one middle-index handle, preserving other homology and giving an exact signed Euler-characteristic count along any stabilization chain. Broader Liouville and weakly Weinstein claims retain their qualifications.
