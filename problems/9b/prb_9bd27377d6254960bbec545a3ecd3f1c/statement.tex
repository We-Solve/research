\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
The source asks which lattice minimizes \(E(L,s)\) for fixed \(s>0\), and relates large \(s\) to densest lattice packing. The earlier definition of \(E\) and its normalization are absent. Following the aid's explicit pole convention, take \(L\subset\R^n\) full rank with covolume one and use the Epstein convention
\[
 E(L,s)=\sum_{v\in L\setminus\{0\}}|v|^{-2s}
 \qquad(\operatorname{Re}s>n/2),
\]
continued meromorphically elsewhere. A different fixed positive overall factor would change the identities accordingly but not the minimizers. For \(0<s\leq n/2\), the divergent series itself is not the energy being compared. At \(a=n/2\), use its finite part, since all covolume-one lattices have the same simple-pole residue \(\pi^a/\Gamma(a)\).

Put
\[
 \theta_L(t)=\sum_{v\in L}e^{-\pi t|v|^2},\qquad
 L^*=\{y:y\cdot v\in\Z\text{ for every }v\in L\}.
\]
For covolume-one \(L_0,L\), let
\(\Delta(t)=\theta_L(t)-\theta_{L_0}(t)\) and
\(\Delta^*(t)=\theta_{L^*}(t)-\theta_{L_0^*}(t)\).
The aid's two-sided theta-transfer identity is
\[
 E(L,s)-E(L_0,s)=\frac{\pi^s}{\Gamma(s)}
 \int_1^\infty\left[
 \Delta(t)t^{s-1}+\Delta^*(t)t^{a-s-1}\right]\dd t
\]
for every real \(s>0\), \(s\ne a\). At the pole it gives
\[
 \operatorname{FP}_{s=a}E(L,s)-\operatorname{FP}_{s=a}E(L_0,s)
 =\frac{\pi^a}{\Gamma(a)}\int_1^\infty
 \left[\Delta(t)t^{a-1}+\Delta^*(t)t^{-1}\right]\dd t.
\]
Both integrals converge exponentially at infinity. Consequently the simultaneous inequalities
\[
 \theta_{L_0}(t)\leq\theta_L(t),\qquad
 \theta_{L_0^*}(t)\leq\theta_{L^*}(t)
 \quad\text{for every }t\geq1
\]
imply the corresponding energy inequalities for all positive parameters and finite-part inequality at the pole.

If \(L_0\) is self-dual, or isometric to its dual, global theta minimality among \emph{all} covolume-one competitors on \(t\geq1\) supplies both inequalities, by applying it to \(L\) and \(L^*\). A comparison for one competitor alone cannot silently supply the second dual comparison. Nonnegative theta slack gives a sufficient condition; energy inequalities for all parameters are not asserted to be equivalent to pointwise theta domination. Equality in these integral identities, under the two sign hypotheses, forces both theta slacks to vanish identically on the half-line, but in arbitrary dimension equal theta series do not by themselves prove lattice isometry.

The original workshop account attributes unique hexagonal minimization in dimension two for all \(s>0\) to Rankin and Cassels and reports local optimality of \(D_4,E_8\) and the Leech lattice in dimensions \(4,8,24\). The aid updates this as a supplied literature report: dimensions \(2,8,24\) have unique minimizers up to isometry after unit-covolume normalization, with the common pole interpreted by finite parts; normalized \(D_4\) is a strict local minimizer, while its global optimality remains open in the literature checked. The transfer criterion supplies the sufficient target of global theta domination for \(D_4\) on \(t\geq1\), using its isoduality. No proof of that domination or full classification in other dimensions is supplied. The large-\(s\) packing connection concerns shortest-vector length under fixed covolume, not a finite-parameter equivalence of the two optimization problems.
\subsection{Short English statement}
The energy is reconstructed as analytically continued Epstein zeta at fixed covolume, with finite parts at its common pole. Two nonnegative theta comparisons, for a lattice and its dual, give exact positive slack formulas. The aid reports global minimizers in dimensions \(2,8,24\), while the dimension-four global question remains unresolved.
