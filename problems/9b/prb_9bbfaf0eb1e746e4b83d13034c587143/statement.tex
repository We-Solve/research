\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 700075}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A group \(G\) is polycyclic if it admits a finite subnormal series
\[
1=G_0\triangleleft G_1\triangleleft\cdots\triangleleft G_m=G
\]
whose factors \(G_i/G_{i-1}\) are cyclic, either finite or infinite.

Let \(F=F(x_1,x_2,\ldots)\) be the free group on countably many generators. A word \(w\in F\) is an identity (an identical relation) of \(G\) if \(h(w)=1_G\) for every homomorphism \(h:F\to G\). Write
\[
\operatorname{Id}(G)=\bigcap_{h\in\operatorname{Hom}(F,G)}\ker h.
\]
A finite basis for the identities of \(G\) means a finite set \(W\subseteq\operatorname{Id}(G)\) such that every identity of \(G\) follows from the identities \(w=1\), \(w\in W\), together with the group axioms. Equivalently,
\[
\operatorname{Id}(G)=
\left\langle\!\left\langle
\varphi(w):w\in W,\ \varphi\in\operatorname{End}(F)
\right\rangle\!\right\rangle_F,
\]
where the right-hand side is the normal closure in \(F\) of all substitutions into the words in \(W\).

\textbf{Question (A. L. Shmel'kin, 1965).} Does every polycyclic group \(G\) have a finite basis for its identities in this sense? The finite basis may depend on \(G\). Here the source phrase ``identical relations'' is interpreted in its standard sense of universally valid group identities.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every polycyclic group have a finite collection of identities from which all of its identities follow using substitutions and the group axioms? The collection may depend on the group.
