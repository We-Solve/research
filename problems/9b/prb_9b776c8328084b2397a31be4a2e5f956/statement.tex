\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Jim Propp's original question asks whether Andrea Sportiello's inverse-toppling model [12] is the same as a particle creation/deletion model. Neither model nor ``the same'' is defined in the extracted question. The attached aid identifies two plausible readings: comparison of Caracciolo--Paoletti--Sportiello (CPS) addition/removal with rotor-routing particles/antiparticles, or comparison with the Karmakar--Manna particle--hole protocol. It says CPS explicitly identify a chain with the latter protocol, but that does not answer the rotor-routing comparison.

For the first reading, work on a finite undirected graph with a sink reachable from every vertex. Let $L$ be its reduced Laplacian and $J=\Z^{V\setminus\{t\}}/L\Z^{V\setminus\{t\}}$. In a zero-based convention, stable CPS heights satisfy $0\le\eta(v)<\deg(v)$; addition stabilizes $\eta+e_v$, and removal applies the corresponding inverse-toppling stabilization to $\eta-e_v$. The aid states that their images modulo $L\Z^{V\setminus\{t\}}$ are translations by $+[e_v]$ and $-[e_v]$. With chosen cyclic rotor orders, particle and antiparticle addition induce those same signed translations on rotor-equivalence classes, a $J$-torsor. Identifying that torsor with $J$ requires a base class. Agreement of this quotient action does not assert agreement of the full states or trajectories.

The aid's explicit full-state obstruction uses nonsinks $c,l,r$, sink $t$, edges $ct,cl,cr,lt,rt$, and rotor orders
\[
c:(t,l,r),\qquad l:(t,c),\qquad r:(t,c).
\]
There are $3\cdot2\cdot2=12$ stable-height states and $12$ unrestricted rotor states. Under particle routing with advance-then-move convention, the center addition maps are reported to have fiber-size multisets, including zeros,
\[
\{0,0,0,1,1,1,1,1,1,2,2,2\}\quad\text{(CPS)},
\]
\[
\{0,0,0,0,1,1,1,1,1,2,2,3\}\quad\text{(rotor particle)}.
\]
Their image sizes are respectively $9$ and $8$, so no bijection of the full state sets conjugates these labeled center maps. Nevertheless their common critical group has order $8$, and both quotient maps translate by $[e_c]$. This disproves that specific full-dynamics conjugacy, while preserving the signed critical-group comparison. It does not rule out other equivalence notions or replace the second protocol reading with a rotor claim.
