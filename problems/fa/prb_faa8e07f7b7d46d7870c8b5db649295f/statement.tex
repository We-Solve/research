\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
The original question is: for a discrete-time Lorentz gas with finite horizon, does a measure of maximal entropy always exist? The supplied aid interprets this as the collision map of a planar periodic dispersing Sinai billiard. More precisely, let \(Q\) be a flat two-dimensional torus with the interiors of finitely many pairwise disjoint \(C^3\), strictly convex scatterers removed, each boundary having strictly positive curvature. Assume finite horizon, meaning that all nonsingular free-flight times are bounded above. Let \(T\) be the billiard collision map, sending an outgoing collision position and direction to the next such collision after specular reflection. Restrict to the phase points for which every forward and backward collision is defined and nongrazing; denote this invariant regular collision space by \(M_{\mathrm{reg}}\).

Let \(\mathcal M_T\) be the \(T\)-invariant Borel probability measures carried by \(M_{\mathrm{reg}}\), and let \(h_\mu(T)\) denote Kolmogorov--Sinai entropy. With the variational interpretation
\[
 H(T)=\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal M_T}h_\mu(T),
\]
the question is whether there is \(\mu_*\in\mathcal M_T\) satisfying \(h_{\mu_*}(T)=H(T)\) for every table of this kind. If maximal entropy is instead defined by equality to a topological entropy, the appropriate definition of topological entropy for the singular collision map and its variational principle must also be specified.

The research summary attributes an affirmative answer, and uniqueness for every such finite-horizon table, to Climenhaga and Day, arXiv:2604.25881v1, dated 28 April 2026. It also reports that the measure is multiply mixing, fully supported and has local product structure. These are supplied literature claims about the stated reconstruction, not independently checked conclusions of this draft. The excerpt does not define its local product structure or singularity conventions.

The aid additionally reports the following direct-method sufficient criterion, which is separate from the billiard theorem. Let \(X\) be a compact metric space, \(D\subseteq X\) closed, and \(S:X\to X\) Borel measurable and continuous at every point of \(X\setminus D\). Suppose \(\mathcal M_S\ne\varnothing\) and
\[
 H=\sup_{\nu\in\mathcal M_S}h_\nu(S)<\infty.
\]
A precise uniform-avoidance formulation of the stated hypothesis is that for some \(\eta>0\), with \(D_r=\{x:d(x,D)<r\}\),
\[
 \lim_{r\downarrow0}\ \sup_{\substack{\nu\in\mathcal M_S\\h_\nu(S)>H-\eta}}\nu(D_r)=0.
\]
Assume also that whenever \(\nu_j\in\mathcal M_S\), \(h_{\nu_j}(S)\to H\), and \(\nu_j\) converges weakly to \(\nu\), entropy is upper semicontinuous along this limit, namely \(h_\nu(S)\geq\limsup_jh_{\nu_j}(S)\) once invariance of \(\nu\) is established. The reported criterion says that a maximizing weak limit is invariant and attains \(H\). The aid reports a compact stacked-shift counterexample showing that compactness without singularity avoidance is insufficient: finite-type mixing layers have entropies increasing to \(\log2\), their limits lie on a discontinuously collapsed zero-entropy layer, and the invariant entropy supremum \(\log2\) is not attained. The actual layer spaces and map are not supplied, so this counterexample is recorded descriptively rather than as a fully specified construction.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Ask whether every finite-horizon discrete-time Lorentz collision map has an invariant measure attaining the entropy supremum. For the standard planar periodic Sinai billiard interpretation, the aid cites a 2026 existence and uniqueness theorem. Its separate compact-system criterion requires both control near singularities and upper semicontinuity of entropy.
