# The tangent module is not free at any coprime monomial plane cusp

Claim type: Partial result

Exact scope: For every pair of coprime integers 2≤p<q, the singular affine curve C[t^p,t^q] has a derivation module that is not free even locally at its cusp.

Let R=C[t^p,t^q], S={ap+bq:a,b≥0}, and c=(p−1)(q−1). We first record the precise semigroup facts. For every residue class modulo p choose the unique b∈{0,…,p−1} for which bq has that residue. It is the least element of S in that residue: any other representation has q-coefficient congruent to b modulo p and hence at least b. The largest missing integer in that residue is bq−p. Maximizing gives pq−p−q=c−1. Thus c−1∉S and every integer at least c lies in S.

The coordinate ring is the plane cusp C[x,y]/(y^p−x^q), via x=t^p,y=t^q. It is singular at m=(t^p,t^q): its local dimension is one while m/m² has the independent classes of t^p,t^q and dimension two. Independence follows directly since neither p nor q is a sum of two positive elements of S. Thus the local ring is not regular.

Every C-derivation δ of R extends uniquely to its fraction field by the quotient rule. That field is C(t), because Bezout for p,q expresses t as a quotient of monomials in t^p,t^q. Consequently δ=a(t)d/dt. The condition δ(t^p)∈R implies a(t) is a Laurent polynomial. At the cusp the same statement holds with local coefficients, and valuations still obey the constraints below. Write k=ord_t a for a nonzero derivation. Since p a t^{p−1} and q a t^{q−1} belong to R, or to R_m, we must have
\[
 k+p-1\in S,\qquad k+q-1\in S.
\]
These force k≥1. Indeed k<1−p makes the first exponent negative; 1−p<k≤0 makes it one of 1,…,p−1, all gaps. At k=1−p the other exponent is q−p, which is a gap: it is nonnegative and smaller than q, and if it were a multiple of p then p would divide q. Elements of R_m have leading valuation in S, because their denominators have nonzero constant term, so the same exclusion holds locally.

There are two particular derivations preserving R. The Euler derivation E=t d/dt clearly does. Also T=t^c d/dt does: its images on the two generators have exponents c+p−1 and c+q−1, both at least c and hence in S.

Suppose Der_C(R)_m were free. It has rank one after tensoring with C(t), so it would be generated by some δ=a(t)d/dt over R_m. From E=rδ with r∈R_m we get 1=ord_t r+ord_t a. Both terms on the right are respectively nonnegative and at least one, so ord_t a=1 and ord_t r=0. Therefore r is a unit of R_m and E itself generates the derivation module. But T=t^{c−1}E would then imply t^{c−1}∈R_m, impossible because c−1 is a semigroup gap. This contradiction proves nonfreeness.

This establishes the implication asked in the prompt for this entire two-parameter singular family by ruling out its antecedent at the singular point. It does not supply a singular variety with free derivations. More general plane curves, normal surfaces and higher-dimensional varieties are outside the computation; no unrestricted Zariski–Lipman conclusion follows.

Relation to earlier contributions. The only listed earlier text announces an unverified normal affine surface counterexample. That construction is not adopted. The present explicit valuation argument addresses a different, infinite class of nonnormal plane cusps and proves the local tangent obstruction from first principles.
