\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let
\[
\operatorname{SL}(3,\Z)=\{A\in M_3(\Z):\det A=1\}.
\]
For a finite list \(A_1,\ldots,A_k\in\operatorname{SL}(3,\Z)\), let
\[
H=\langle A_1,\ldots,A_k\rangle
=\{A_{i_1}^{\varepsilon_1}\cdots A_{i_m}^{\varepsilon_m}:m\geq0,\ i_j\in\{1,\ldots,k\},\ \varepsilon_j\in\{-1,1\}\}.
\]
The empty product is the identity matrix; an empty generating list generates the trivial subgroup. Matrix entries are given exactly as integers.

\textbf{Question.} Does there exist a single algorithm which, for every finite generating list \(A_1,\ldots,A_k\in\operatorname{SL}(3,\Z)\) and every \(B\in\operatorname{SL}(3,\Z)\), terminates and correctly decides whether
\[
B\in\langle A_1,\ldots,A_k\rangle?
\]

The formulation above interprets decidability uniformly over generating lists. \subsection{Short English statement}
Given finitely many determinant-one integer \(3\times3\) matrices and another such matrix, can one algorithm always decide whether the latter belongs to the subgroup generated by the former? The intended uniformity should be confirmed against the source.
