\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For each integer \(n>1\), the source asks two parallel generation questions for
\[
 \operatorname{Aut}(\operatorname{Aut}(W_n))
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 \operatorname{Aut}(\operatorname{Sympl}(\C^{2n})).
\]
The title identifies a Weyl-algebra context, but the record does not define \(W_n\), the symplectic group symbol or the category of outer automorphisms. In the usual interpretation, \(W_n\) is the complex Weyl algebra with generators \(q_i,p_i\) satisfying \([p_i,q_j]=\delta_{ij}\), \([q_i,q_j]=[p_i,p_j]=0\); \(G_1=\operatorname{Aut}_{\C\text{-}\mathrm{alg}}(W_n)\). A customary meaning of \(G_2=\operatorname{Sympl}(\C^{2n})\) here is the group of polynomial automorphisms preserving \(\omega=\sum_i dq_i\wedge dp_i\), not merely the linear symplectic group. These meanings require confirmation rather than being inferred from the word group alone.

For a group \(G\), inner conjugation is \(c_g(h)=ghg^{-1}\), and \(\operatorname{Inn}(G)=\{c_g:g\in G\}\). For \(\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}(\C)\), coefficientwise application of \(\sigma\), fixing the named generators or coordinates, gives the usual induced automorphism \(s_\sigma\) of either intended group. In the Weyl case the coefficient map itself is semilinear, but its conjugation action sends complex-linear algebra automorphisms to complex-linear ones; the rational defining relations are preserved. In the polynomial symplectic case the same coefficient operation preserves the displayed rational symplectic form relations.

For both the Weyl-algebra automorphism group and the intended polynomial symplectic group, ask whether every automorphism of the group is generated by conjugations and coefficient-field automorphisms. The two claims are separate and apply only to \(n>1\). The outer-automorphism category and meaning of conjugation are not specified.
