The supplied record does not explicitly define the measures or the entropy. The following formulation uses the conventional interpretation of symmetric probability measures and asymptotic random-walk entropy; this interpretation requires human confirmation against the source.

Let \(G\) be a finitely generated discrete group. A finitely supported probability measure on \(G\) is a function \(\mu:G\to[0,1]\) such that \(\sum_{g\in G}\mu(g)=1\) and \(\operatorname{supp}(\mu)=\{g\in G:\mu(g)>0\}\) is finite. It is symmetric if \(\mu(g)=\mu(g^{-1})\) for every \(g\in G\), and it generates \(G\) if \(\langle\operatorname{supp}(\mu)\rangle=G\). No aperiodicity or positive mass at the identity is assumed.

For probability measures \(\mu,\nu\) on \(G\), define convolution by
\[
(\mu*\nu)(g)=\sum_{x\in G}\mu(x)\nu(x^{-1}g).
\]
Write \(\mu^{*n}\) for the \(n\)-fold convolution, the distribution after \(n\) independent increments with law \(\mu\), starting at the identity. Define
\[
H(\mu^{*n})=-\sum_{g\in G}\mu^{*n}(g)\log\mu^{*n}(g),
\qquad
h(\mu)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{H(\mu^{*n})}{n},
\]
where \(0\log0=0\) and logarithms are natural. Finite support makes each displayed Shannon entropy finite, and subadditivity gives existence of the limit. Choosing another logarithm base greater than one does not change positivity.

\textbf{Question.} For every finitely generated discrete group \(G\) and every pair of symmetric finitely supported probability measures \(\mu_1,\mu_2\) whose supports each generate \(G\), is the implication
\[
h(\mu_1)>0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad h(\mu_2)>0
\]
valid? The quantification includes amenable groups; no nonamenability assumption is supplied.
