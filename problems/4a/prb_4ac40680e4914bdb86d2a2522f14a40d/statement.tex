\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A group \(G\) is a \(2\)-group if every element of \(G\) has finite order equal to a power of \(2\). It is finitely generated if \(G=\langle g_1,\ldots,g_d\rangle\) for some positive integer \(d\) and elements \(g_1,\ldots,g_d\in G\). A group \(H\) is locally finite if \(\langle S\rangle\) is finite for every finite subset \(S\subseteq H\). A \(2\)-group has finite exponent if there is an integer \(e\geq 1\) such that \(g^{2^e}=1\) for every \(g\in G\).

The source asks whether there exists an infinite finitely generated \(2\)-group \(G\) such that
\[
\forall H\leq G,\qquad H\neq G\ \Longrightarrow\ \bigl(\forall S\subseteq H,\ |S|<\infty\ \Longrightarrow\ |\langle S\rangle|<\infty\bigr),
\]

\[
\exists e\in\Z_{\geq 1}\ \forall g\in G,\qquad g^{2^e}=1.

The supplied wording does not explicitly distinguish between requiring finite exponent throughout and asking both the unrestricted question and its finite-exponent strengthening. Both readings are therefore retained here for review.
