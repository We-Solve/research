Yes. For each fixed $c>0$ there is $\beta(c)>0$ such that every sufficiently large $n$-vertex graph with no clique or independent set larger than $c\log n$ has at least $2^{\beta(c)n}$ pairwise nonisomorphic induced subgraphs. We reconstruct Shelah's proof from \emph{Erd\H{o}s and R\'enyi conjecture}, \url{https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9707226}, with the separation and counting steps expanded. The proof below uses only elementary Ramsey bounds and probability; it does not import the desired exponential theorem.

Use base-two logarithms. Increase the given constant slightly, and choose
\[
 L=\lceil C\log n\rceil
\]
so that the hypothesis forbids homogeneous $L$-sets. Here and below ``homogeneous'' means complete or independent. Choose a fixed integer $k\ge2$ sufficiently large that
\[
 \frac Ck\log\bigl(e(k+2)\bigr)<\frac13.
\]
For all sufficiently large $n$, the elementary Ramsey bound gives
\[
 R(L,\lceil L/k\rceil)\le n^{1/2}.\tag{1}
\]
Indeed $R(s,t)\le\binom{s+t-2}{t-1}$ follows by induction using a vertex and its neighbours and non-neighbours. With $t=\lceil L/k\rceil$, the binomial bound $\binom ab\le(ea/b)^b$ bounds its base-two logarithm by $(L/k+1)\log(e(k+2))$, which is less than $(\log n)/2$ eventually. The same estimate holds with the two arguments exchanged. Set
\[
 m=R(k,k),\qquad \alpha=\beta=\frac1{8m}.
\]
Suppose, towards a contradiction, that the number $I(G)$ of induced isomorphism types is less than $2^{\beta n}$.
