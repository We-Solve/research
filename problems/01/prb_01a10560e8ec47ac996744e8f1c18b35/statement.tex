\]
where $q$ is integer. Define $\mathrm{Bad}=\{\xi:\inf_{q\geq1}q\|q\xi\|>0\}$.

The aid asserts that $(\alpha,\beta)\notin C_s$ implies Littlewood:
\[
\liminf_{q\to\infty}q\|q\alpha\|\|q\beta\|=0.
\]

If only bounded denominators witness infimum zero, both coordinates are rational and multiples of a common denominator give the required unbounded sequence. Thus the finite-infimum formulation is not silently confused with a limit at infinity.

The endpoint identities under this literal formula are
\[
C_0=\R\times\mathrm{Bad},\qquad C_1=\mathrm{Bad}\times\R.
\]
The aid's quantitative multiples lemma says that for $0<\delta=q\|q\beta\|\leq1$, some $Q=kq$ satisfies
\[
\max\{\|Q\alpha\|,Q\|Q\beta\|\}\leq2\delta^{1/3}.
\]
A suitable $k$ can be chosen by a one-dimensional pigeonhole approximation with size on the order of $\delta^{-1/3}$. If $\delta=0$, one instead uses multiples with vanishing second error and arbitrarily small first error; no finite exact-zero conclusion is inferred for irrational $\alpha$. Swapping coordinates gives the other endpoint.

For a finite integer scale $Q\geq1$, put
\[
c_s(Q)=\min_{1\leq q\leq Q}\max\{q^s\|q\alpha\|,q^{1-s}\|q\beta\|\}.
\]
The stated weight-window estimate is
\[
Q^{-|s-t|}c_s(Q)\leq c_t(Q)\leq Q^{|s-t|}c_s(Q).
\]
Thus finite-scale continuity in the weight is quantitative, but its constants deteriorate with $Q$. The aid notes that results on countable weighted intersections do not automatically determine the uncountable intersection, and a point lying in all rational-weight sets need not be promoted to all weights without the needed uniform bounds. Emptiness of the all-weights intersection would suffice for Littlewood by the first implication; it is not proved here or asserted equivalent to the conjecture.
