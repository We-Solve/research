\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $(X^n,g)$ be a closed orientable Riemannian manifold with
\[
 \operatorname{Sc}_g(x)\ge n(n-1)\qquad(x\in X),
\]
and give $S^n$ its unit round metric $g_{\mathrm{sph}}$. Let $f:X\to S^n$ be $C^1$ with nonzero topological degree. For $n\ge2$, the usual pointwise area-nonincreasing condition is
\[
 \|\Lambda^2\mathrm df_x\|_{g,g_{\mathrm{sph}}}\le1
 \qquad(x\in X),
\]
expressing that the area of every tangent two-plane, hence every parametrized surface with multiplicity, does not increase. This is distinct from requiring the operator norm $\|\mathrm df_x\|\le1$ on tangent vectors.

The supplied conjecture asks whether these hypotheses alone force $f$ to be an isometry:
\[
 f^*g_{\mathrm{sph}}=g
 \quad\text{and }f\text{ is a bijective Riemannian isometry},
\]
without the additional assumption that $TX$ restricts trivially to every closed surface. The original degree hypothesis is nonzero degree, not only degree one. No spin or tangent-bundle condition is restored as an assumption.

The source does not specify the dimension range or explicitly state connectedness. Both require checking for the proposed conclusion. In particular area control and length control differ in low dimensions, and the conclusion that this particular map is an isometry is stronger than saying only that $X$ is isometric to a round sphere. The exact intended low-dimensional scope and rigidity conclusion are not silently repaired. The omitted surface-bundle condition also lacks a convention on embedded versus mapped surfaces and actual versus stable triviality.
