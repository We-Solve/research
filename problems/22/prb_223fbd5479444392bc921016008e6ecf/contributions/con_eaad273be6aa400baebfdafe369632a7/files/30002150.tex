\section{Resonant Mode Mixing in Anti-de-Sitter Instability}\label{30002150}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 30002150 · Mathematical Physics}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


\paragraph{Definitions and conventions.}
Anti-de Sitter spacetime is the maximally symmetric Lorentzian solution with negative cosmological constant. Its linearized field modes have frequencies; resonant interactions occur when combinations of those frequencies match. A transfer of energy to increasing frequencies is a turbulent cascade. An apparent horizon is a marginally outer trapped surface on a spacelike slice; its formation is a geometric diagnostic, distinct from a proof of linear mode instability.

\paragraph{Target retained from the canonical source catalogue.}
\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}
Is the instability of anti-de-Sitter space caused by resonant mode mixing that transfers energy from low to high frequencies until an apparent horizon forms?

\paragraph{Source scope and qualification.}
This asks for a mechanism in the source evolution problem. Resonance in a truncated mode system is not by itself proof that a horizon forms in the full field equations.

\paragraph{A resonant triad transfers energy reversibly.} Consider complex amplitudes $a,b,c$ obeying $i\dot a=\overline b c$, $i\dot b=\overline a c$, $i\dot c=ab$, a basic exactly resonant three-wave system. With $z=\overline a\,\overline b\,c$, differentiation gives
\[
 \frac d{dt}|a|^2=2\operatorname{Im}z,\quad
 \frac d{dt}|b|^2=2\operatorname{Im}z,\quad
 \frac d{dt}|c|^2=-2\operatorname{Im}z.
\]
Therefore $|a|^2+|c|^2$ and $|b|^2+|c|^2$ are conserved. All amplitudes remain bounded for all time, since the polynomial vector field cannot cease to exist while its finite-dimensional solution stays bounded. Transfer can reverse because the sign depends on the phase of $z$, not solely on mode frequencies.

\paragraph{What resonance does not force.} With $b=c=0$, $a$ is constant: exact resonance allows a stationary single-mode state. More generally a fixed finite truncation has no arbitrarily high frequencies to receive energy. Neither frequency matching nor growth of one selected high mode proves a monotone unbounded cascade.

\paragraph{Einstein/AdS gap.} The triad is not the source resonant system and is not a counterexample to its instability mechanism. It tests the inference from resonant terms alone to horizon formation. One needs control of an infinite-mode transfer, comparison of the resonant approximation with the full field equations on the transfer time scale, and a geometric trapped-surface estimate. Those are separate missing steps; an apparent horizon cannot be deduced solely from modal occupation numbers without reconstructing and controlling the spacetime.
