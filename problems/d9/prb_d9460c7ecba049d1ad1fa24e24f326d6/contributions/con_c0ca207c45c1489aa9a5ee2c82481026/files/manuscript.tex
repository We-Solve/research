\section*{Ecological scaling across space and time}
\label{prob:181}
\noindent\textbf{Status: restricted result or obstruction; full target not established.}
\subsection{Target and scope}
\paragraph{Catalogue definitions and assumptions.}
Let $X(x,t)$ be a vector of local densities or fluxes on a bounded landscape, governed by a specified reaction--dispersal model with finite-parameter rates. For spatial cell $B$ of diameter $\ell$ and temporal window $\tau$, define $\bar X_{B,\tau}(t)=|B|^{-1}\tau^{-1}\int_t^{t+\tau}\int_BX(x,s)\,\,\mathrm{d} x\,\,\mathrm{d} s$. Local environmental variation and boundary flux are observed or assigned bounded stochastic models. Candidate coarse states may include means, covariances and lagged values; their dimension is fixed before fitting. Spatial support and time averaging are fixed before the scale comparison. Changing them together can conceal compensating errors. A successful rule must preserve relevant physical quantities, such as total biomass under transport, as well as matching empirical forecasts.
\paragraph{Original target.}
Find a reduced transition rule $S_{\ell,\tau}$ with an explicit approximation envelope across declared scales:
\[
 \sup_{(\ell,\tau)\in\mathcal S}
 \mathbb{E}\|\bar X_{B,\tau}(t+\tau)
       -S_{\ell,\tau}(Z_{B,\tau}(t))\|^2\le\varepsilon^2,
\]
where $Z$ contains the chosen coarse summaries and $\mathcal S$ is a finite scale range. Determine whether variance or temporal memory is essential to meet the bound.
\paragraph{Catalogue alternative formulations.}
(i) Under linear local dynamics and closed boundaries, derive exact mean aggregation as a benchmark. (ii) Under nonlinear resource limitation add within-cell covariances or a memory kernel and compare Markov versus history-dependent coarse dynamics. Exact closure of the mean is then not assumed.
\subsection{Attempted answer}
\begin{proposition}
For deterministic competition $\dot N_i=N_i(r_i-\sum_jA_{ij}N_j)$, assume symmetric positive-definite $A$ and strictly positive equilibrium $N_*=A^{-1}r$. Every strictly positive initial state converges to $N_*$; this gives one sufficient coexistence regime.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Use $V=\sum_i[N_i-N_{*,i}-N_{*,i}\log(N_i/N_{*,i})]$. Differentiation gives $\dot V=-(N-N_*)^TA(N-N_*)$. Its sublevel sets stay bounded and away from coordinate zero, and the derivative vanishes only at the equilibrium. The invariant-set argument gives convergence.
\end{proof}
\subsection{What remains unresolved}
Noise, immigration, spatial structure and nonsymmetric interactions are absent. The interaction matrix and positive equilibrium require independent estimation; no universal coexistence criterion follows.

\section{Shared mathematical bounds}\label{app:bounds}
\begin{lemma}[Independent bounded sums]\label{lem:bounded}
Let independent real variables $X_i$ satisfy $a_i\leq X_i\leq b_i$ almost surely,
and let $S=\sum_{i=1}^n(X_i-\mathbb{E} X_i)$. If
$V=\sum_i(b_i-a_i)^2>0$, then for every $t>0$,
\[
 \mathbb{P}(|S|\geq t)\leq 2\exp(-2t^2/V).
\]
In particular, independent losses in $[0,1]$ have mean-error bound
$\mathbb{P}(|\overline X-\mathbb{E}\overline X|\geq\epsilon)
\leq2e^{-2n\epsilon^2}$. The same expression for variables in $[-1,1]$
is $2e^{-n\epsilon^2/2}$. If $V=0$ then $S=0$ almost surely.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $X\in[a,b]$ with mean $\mu$, the nonnegative product
$(X-a)(b-X)$ gives
$\operatorname{Var} X\leq(\mu-a)(b-\mu)\leq(b-a)^2/4$.
Let $K(s)=\log\mathbb{E}\exp[s(X-\mathbb{E} X)]$.
Boundedness permits two differentiations. Its second derivative is the variance
under the exponentially tilted law, which has the same support, so
$K''(s)\leq(b-a)^2/4$ for every real $s$.
Because $K(0)=K'(0)=0$, integration yields
$K(s)\leq s^2(b-a)^2/8$.
Independence adds these log moment-generating bounds for $S$.
For $s>0$, Markov's inequality gives
$\mathbb{P}(S\geq t)\leq\exp(-st+s^2V/8)$.
Choosing $s=4t/V$ yields the upper-tail bound $e^{-2t^2/V}$.
Apply it also to $-S$ and add. The stated mean versions follow by substituting
$t=n\epsilon$ and the respective support widths.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Finite-family extension.}
For $M$ prespecified bounded-loss comparisons, apply the relevant inequality to
each and use a union bound. Independence between comparisons is not required;
independence of the biological units within each comparison is. Repeated cells,
reads or time points from one dependent biological unit cannot automatically be
counted as independent units.

\paragraph{No data-dependent shortcut.}
The bound does not justify choosing a model or validation loss using the same
outcomes and then reporting single-model coverage. A prespecified finite family,
a separate validation sample, or an appropriate complexity or sequential argument
is needed. Marginal predictive coverage likewise should not be read as coverage
for every subgroup or under arbitrary distribution shift.
