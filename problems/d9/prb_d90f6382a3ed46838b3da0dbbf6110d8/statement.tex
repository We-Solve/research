\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 701565}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a finitely generated group \(H\), define its first Betti number by
\[
b_1(H)=\max\{n\in\Z_{\geq 0}:\text{there exists a surjective homomorphism }H\twoheadrightarrow\Z^n\}.
\]
Here \(\Z^0\) is the trivial group. Every finite-index subgroup of a finitely presented group is finitely generated, so this definition applies to all subgroups considered below. Let \(F_2\) denote the free group on two generators. A group \(G\) is soluble if its derived series, defined by \(G^{(0)}=G\) and \(G^{(i+1)}=[G^{(i)},G^{(i)}]\), satisfies \(G^{(r)}=\{1\}\) for some integer \(r\geq 0\).

Suppose that \(G\) is a finitely presented group satisfying
\[
\forall M\in\Z_{\geq 0}\quad \exists H\leq G\quad [G:H]<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad b_1(H)>M.
\]
The source describes the following as a well-known problem.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Must there exist a subgroup \(K\leq G\) with \([G:K]<\infty\) and a surjective homomorphism from \(K\) onto a non-abelian free group? Equivalently, must such a \(K\) admit a surjective homomorphism \(K\twoheadrightarrow F_2\)?
\item[(b)] Does there exist a soluble finitely presented group \(G\) satisfying the displayed unboundedness condition?
\end{enumerate}
The supplied registry labels the problem open; that label is supplied source information, not an independently verified assessment.
\subsection{Short English statement}
For a finitely presented group whose finite-index subgroups have unbounded first Betti numbers, must some finite-index subgroup map onto a non-abelian free group? Can a soluble finitely presented group have this unboundedness property?
