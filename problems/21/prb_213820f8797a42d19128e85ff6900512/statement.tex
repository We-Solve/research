\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A \emph{left brace} is a set \(B\) with operations \(+\) and \(\circ\) such that \((B,+)\) is an abelian group, \((B,\circ)\) is a group, and
\[
a\circ(b+c)=a\circ b-a+a\circ c\qquad(a,b,c\in B).
\]
Write \(\lambda_a(b)=-a+a\circ b\). An \emph{ideal} is an additive subgroup \(I\leq (B,+)\) invariant under every \(\lambda_a\) and normal in \((B,\circ)\). The brace is \emph{simple} if its only ideals are \(\{0\}\) and \(B\). A brace is \emph{trivial} if \(a\circ b=a+b\) for all \(a,b\in B\). A group \(G\) is \emph{metabelian} if its commutator subgroup \([G,G]\) is abelian. The condition on Sylow subgroups means that, for every prime \(p\mid |G|\), every Sylow \(p\)-subgroup of \(G\) is abelian.

For an explicit interpretation of the requested construction, the usual bilinear asymmetric product of two trivial braces can be described as follows. Let \(T\) and \(S\) be finite abelian groups, regarded as trivial braces. Let \(b:T\times T\to S\) be symmetric and additive in each argument, and let \(\alpha:(S,+)\to\operatorname{Aut}(T,+)\) be a group homomorphism such that
\[
b(\alpha_s(t),\alpha_s(u))=b(t,u)
\qquad(s\in S,\ t,u\in T).
\]
On \(T\times S\), put
\[
\begin{aligned}
(t,s)+(u,v)&=(t+u,\ s+v+b(t,u)),\\
(t,s)\circ(u,v)&=(t+\alpha_s(u),\ s+v).
\end{aligned}
\]
These operations describe the bilinear asymmetric-product convention used in the following formulation. The supplied record does not define asymmetric product, so agreement of this convention with the intended source construction requires review.
