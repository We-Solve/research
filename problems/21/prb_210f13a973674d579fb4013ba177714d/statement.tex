\section{Karchmer–Raz–Wigderson Composition Conjecture}\label{30004010}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 30004010 · Computer Science}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.


For $f:\{0,1\}^a\to\{0,1\}$ and $g:\{0,1\}^b\to\{0,1\}$, block composition applies $g$ to each of $a$ disjoint blocks and then applies $f$ to the resulting bits. The standard depth measure uses fan-in-two AND/OR gates and NOT gates; it minimizes the longest gate path of a Boolean circuit computing the function.

A Boolean function maps $\{0,1\}^n$ to $\{0,1\}$. A Boolean circuit is an acyclic network of logical gates; a formula is a circuit with no sharing of intermediate gate outputs. Size and depth measure its number of gates and longest gate path.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For nonconstant Boolean functions $f$ and $g$, is the circuit-depth complexity of their composition equal, up to lower-order terms, to the sum of their individual depth complexities? \sref{30004010}{2}

The supplied phrase up to lower-order terms is retained. Its precise asymptotic comparison must come from the source; no unrestricted-fan-in gate convention is substituted.
