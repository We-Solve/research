\section{Errors in Long Equatorial Wave Models}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


\paragraph{Definitions and conventions.} Equatorial wave dynamics are affected by the variation and vanishing of the Coriolis parameter near the equator. Long-wave and short-wave asymptotic models retain different balances. The source discusses a global short-wave theory whose longest equatorial waves have significant errors, and possible missing ingredients from equatorial long-wave theory. Errors mean the discrepancies in that model comparison, not an unspecified error in all equatorial-wave observations.

\paragraph{Source scope and qualifications.} The governing approximation, comparison solutions, and wave-scale regime must be inherited from the report. A universal error estimate for every equatorial-wave model is not supplied; the question is to identify missing dynamics and possible extensions across scales.

\paragraph{Catalogue mathematical target.}
\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}
How can the significant errors in the longest equatorial waves be explained or reduced?

\paragraph{Saved literature context.} The supplied catalogue's assessment dated 2026-08-21 records: The extracted question concerns unexplained long equatorial wave errors; later observational and modeling work still documents unresolved equatorial wave representation errors, without resolving this specific issue. The assessment is retained as dated provenance; it is not a new comprehensive literature or open-status certification.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Explain and reduce the long-equatorial-wave errors in the report's otherwise globally valid wave model.
