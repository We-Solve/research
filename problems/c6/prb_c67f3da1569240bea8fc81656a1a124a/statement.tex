\section{Are there infinitely many Newman–Shanks–Williams primes}\label{9400154}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 9400154 · Number Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $S_0=1$, $S_1=7$ and $S_{n+2}=6S_{n+1}-S_n$ for $n\ge0$. Thus $S_n=\bigl((1+\sqrt2)^{2n+1}+(1-\sqrt2)^{2n+1}\bigr)/2$. These are the Newman--Shanks--Williams numbers; an NSW prime is a prime value $S_n$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Are there infinitely many $n\ge0$ with $S_n$ prime? \sref{9400154}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Are infinitely many Newman–Shanks–Williams numbers prime?
