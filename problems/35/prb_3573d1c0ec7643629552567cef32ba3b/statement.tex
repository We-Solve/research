\section{Norm Estimation from Asymmetric Black-Box Linear Operators}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.


For a linear map $A$ between finite-dimensional inner-product spaces, $A^*$ is its adjoint and $\lVert A\rVert=\sup_{\lVert x\rVert_2=1}\lVert Ax\rVert_2$ is the induced operator norm. A black-box query evaluates the supplied map on a chosen vector without exposing its matrix. The first access model supplies $A$ but not $A^*$. In the second, $A$ and $V$ have the same domain and codomain and only evaluations of $A$ and $V^*$ are supplied. Convergence means estimates approach the requested norm as the iteration or query budget grows.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Given black-box access to $x\mapsto Ax$ but not to $A^*$, can $\lVert A\rVert$ be estimated by a provably convergent low-memory method? Likewise, can $\lVert A-V\rVert$ be estimated using only black boxes for $A$ and $V^*$? \sref{statement}{2}
\paragraph{Scope qualification.}
Low memory is relative to the operator dimensions and the numerical representation in the cited report. No unavailable adjoint or full matrix storage is assumed.

Estimate operator norms with a convergent method despite access to only one direction of the relevant linear operators.
