\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Following the supplied research summary, let \(U,V,W\) be complex vector spaces with dimensions \(4,4,8\), and put \(T=U\otimes V\otimes W\) and \(G=\operatorname{SL}(U)\times\operatorname{SL}(V)\times\operatorname{SL}(W)\). The action is
\[
 (g_U,g_V,g_W)(u\otimes v\otimes w)
   =g_Uu\otimes g_Vv\otimes g_Ww.
\]
A polynomial \(P\in\C[T]=\operatorname{Sym}(T^*)\) is invariant if \(P(gt)=P(t)\) for every \(g\in G\) and \(t\in T\).

Cooper asks whether a nonzero invariant polynomial exists. Literally this is the condition \(\C[T]^G\ne\{0\}\), which does not exclude nonzero constants. If the intended target is a nonconstant invariant, its precise homogeneous formulation is
\[
 \exists d\geq1:\qquad
 \bigl(\operatorname{Sym}^d(T^*)\bigr)^G\ne\{0\}.
\]
The positive-degree restriction is not printed in the source and therefore remains an explicit possible intended correction rather than a silently added hypothesis. The statement itself does not name the ground field; \(\C\) is supplied by its research summary. The source relates the question to limits under conjugacy of the diagonal subgroup of \(\operatorname{SL}(8,\R)\), but provides no comparison construction in this record.
