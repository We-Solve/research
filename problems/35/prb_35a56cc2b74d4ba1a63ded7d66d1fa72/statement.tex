\section{Exact DDH leakage and sampler criteria for the CSIDH torsor}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.


The notation $\mathfrak a*E$ denotes the ideal-class action on an elliptic curve. The first distribution uses three independent ideal choices; the second replaces the third action by the product of the first two. Decisional Diffie--Hellman asks whether an algorithm can distinguish the resulting triple distributions.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Solve Decisional Diffie-Hellman (DDH) in the context of CSIDH: Let $E_0$ be a supersingular elliptic curve defined over $\mathbb F_p$ with endomorphism ring $\mathbb Z[\pi]$. Distinguish between triples $(\mathfrak a * E_0, \mathfrak b* E_0,\mathfrak c* E_0)$ and triples $(\mathfrak a * E_0, \mathfrak b* E_0,\mathfrak a\mathfrak b * E_0)$ where $\mathfrak a, \mathfrak b, \mathfrak c$ are ideals in $\mathbb Z[\pi]$ of odd norm. \sref{statement}{2}
\paragraph{Scope qualification.}
The source specifies odd-norm ideals but not their sampling distribution or finite representatives; no distributional hardness claim is assumed.

\subsection{Short English statement}
Can independent ideal-action triples be distinguished from triples whose third component uses the product of the first two secret ideals?
