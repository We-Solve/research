\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Diogo Gomes's source question asks for extensions of Aubry--Mather and Monge--Kantorovich techniques to infinite-dimensional linear programming, citing stochastic Mather measures for second-order Hamilton--Jacobi equations as an example. It specifies no single class of linear programs. The aid supplies the following restricted smooth, uniformly diffusive, quadratic model on $\mathbb T^d$.

Fix $\nu>0$, smooth $b:\mathbb T^d\to\R^d$ and $U:\mathbb T^d\to\R$, and set $L(x,v)=\tfrac12|v-b(x)|^2+U(x)$. A stochastic-holonomic occupation measure is a Borel probability $\mu$ on $\mathbb T^d\times\R^d$ with finite second velocity moment satisfying
\[
 \int(\nu\Delta\phi(x)+v\cdot D\phi(x))\,\dd\mu(x,v)=0
 \quad(\phi\in C^\infty(\mathbb T^d)).
\]
Let $J(\mu)=\int L\,\dd\mu$ and $\beta=\inf_{\mu\text{ feasible}}J(\mu)$. With these explicit sign and diffusion conventions, the aid's attained Hamilton--Jacobi dual is
\[
 \nu\Delta u+b\cdot Du-\tfrac12|Du|^2+U=\beta,
 \qquad
 \beta=\sup_{w\in C^2(\mathbb T^d)}\min_x
 \bigl(\nu\Delta w+b\cdot Dw-\tfrac12|Dw|^2+U\bigr).
\]
It reports a smooth corrector $u$, obtained from a principal positive eigenfunction, and no duality gap. The corrector is understood modulo constants.

Set $a(x)=b(x)-Du(x)$, and let $m>0$ be the normalized invariant density
\[
 \nu\Delta m-\operatorname{div}(a m)=0,\qquad\int_{\mathbb T^d}m\,\dd x=1.
\]
The reported unique primal optimizer is $\mu_*=m(x)\,\dd x\,\delta_{a(x)}(\dd v)$. For every feasible finite-cost $\mu$ the exact certificate is
\[
 J(\mu)-\beta=\tfrac12\int|v-a(x)|^2\,\dd\mu(x,v).
\]
This measures velocity deviation from the feedback graph, not an unspecified distance between probability measures. The aid also reports, for a fixed admissible H\"older exponent $0<\alpha<1$ and the corresponding elliptic Poisson estimate,
\[

where $\pi_x$ is spatial projection and the dual norm is the supremum of absolute integration against test functions of $C^\alpha$ norm at most one. The constant depends on the fixed model and exponent, not on $\mu$; the supplied summary does not state its quantitative dependence or the exact regularity range.
