\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Use the standard lattice-polytope conventions as follows. Let \(d\geq 1\), let \(M\) be a lattice of rank \(d\), let \(M_{\R}=M\otimes_{\Z}\R\), and let \(N=\operatorname{Hom}_{\Z}(M,\Z)\) be the dual lattice. A full-dimensional lattice polytope \(P\subset M_{\R}\) is the convex hull of finitely many points of \(M\) and satisfies \(\dim P=d\). Write \(V(P)\) for its vertex set. The polytope is centrally symmetric if there is \(c\in M_{\R}\) such that \(P=2c-P\).

The polytope \(P\) is reflexive if \(0\in\operatorname{int}(P)\) and its polar
\[
P^{\vee}=\{y\in N\otimes_{\Z}\R:\langle y,x\rangle\leq 1\text{ for every }x\in P\}
\]
is a lattice polytope with respect to \(N\). It is \(2\)-level if, for every facet \(F\) and a supporting equation \(\ell_F(x)=b_F\), chosen with \(P\subseteq\{x:\ell_F(x)\leq b_F\}\), there is \(a_F<b_F\) such that
\[
\{\ell_F(v):v\in V(P)\}=\{a_F,b_F\}.
\]
Thus every vertex lies on the facet hyperplane or on one other parallel hyperplane.

\textbf{Classification problem.} For arbitrary positive dimension \(d\), characterize all full-dimensional lattice polytopes \(P\subset M_{\R}\) that are simultaneously centrally symmetric, \(2\)-level, and reflexive.
