\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 700324}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(F=F(x,y)\) be the free group of rank \(2\). For \(w\in F\), define the verbal subgroup generated by \(w\) in \(F\) by
\[
V_w(F)=\bigl\langle w(a,b):a,b\in F\bigr\rangle
       =\bigl\langle \varphi(w):\varphi\in\operatorname{End}(F)\bigr\rangle,
\]
where \(w(a,b)\) denotes substitution of \(a,b\) for \(x,y\), and \(\operatorname{End}(F)\) is the set of group endomorphisms of \(F\). Thus generation by a single element here means generation as a verbal subgroup, rather than generation as an ordinary cyclic subgroup.

Does there exist a sequence \((w_n)_{n\geq 0}\) of elements of \(F\) such that
\[
V_{w_n}(F)\subsetneq V_{w_{n+1}}(F)
\qquad\text{for every integer }n\geq 0?
\]
Here an infinite ascending chain is interpreted as a strictly ascending chain, as is customary in this question. The supplied registry labels the problem open; that label is supplied source metadata, not an independently verified assessment.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can the free group on two generators contain an infinite strictly ascending chain of verbal subgroups, each generated as a verbal subgroup by one word?
