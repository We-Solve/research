\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $G$ be a Gromov-hyperbolic group realized as the fundamental group of a graph of hyperbolic groups. For each oriented edge $e$ with terminal vertex $v$, assume that its injective edge-to-vertex homomorphism $G_e\to G_v$ is a quasi-isometric embedding for word metrics: there are $A\geq1$ and $B\geq0$ such that
\[
A^{-1}d_{G_e}(a,b)-B\leq d_{G_v}(a,b)\leq A d_{G_e}(a,b)+B
\quad(a,b\in G_e),
\]
where the middle distance uses the inclusion and the constants may depend on the edge inclusion. For a vertex group $V$, let $\partial V$ and $\partial G$ be their Gromov boundaries. The source attributes to Mitra a continuous Cannon--Thurston map
\[
c_V:\partial V\longrightarrow\partial G,
\]
induced by the inclusion $V\hookrightarrow G$, meaning the boundary restriction of a continuous extension to the corresponding Gromov compactifications.

Swarup asks for a description of all point-preimages
\[
c_V^{-1}(\{\eta\})=\{\xi\in\partial V:c_V(\xi)=\eta\},\qquad\eta\in\partial G,
\]
and, in particular, asks to prove
\[
\forall\eta\in\partial G,\qquad |c_V^{-1}(\{\eta\})|<\infty.
\]
Empty fibers are permitted. Describing the nonempty fibers amounts to describing the equivalence classes of $\xi\sim\zeta$ defined by $c_V(\xi)=c_V(\zeta)$.

The supplied research summary reports a stronger uniform bound for a \emph{finite} graph of groups: for each such setting and vertex group there is an integer $N<\infty$ bounding every fiber cardinality. It attributes this to a March 2026 preprint. It further reports a fiber description using boundary points pairwise joined by contracting bi-infinite ladders flowing along a unique end of the Bass--Serre tree, with a source in Kapovich--Sardar (2024), and algebraic Cannon--Thurston lamination descriptions in free-group-extension cases. The definitions and exact qualifications for these descriptions are not reproduced in the supplied record. The original question does not explicitly say that its graph of groups is finite, so the relation between that wording and the finite-graph hypothesis of the reported general theorem requires review. The reported general resolution was not refereed at the time of the supplied summary; that summary explicitly distinguishes the free-group-extension results known earlier from the general preprint.
\subsection{Short English statement}
For each vertex group in a hyperbolic graph of hyperbolic groups with quasi-isometrically embedded edge groups, describe which boundary points the Cannon--Thurston map identifies and show every fiber is finite. The supplied summary reports a uniform bound in the finite-graph case from a March 2026 preprint, with an explicit peer-review caveat.
