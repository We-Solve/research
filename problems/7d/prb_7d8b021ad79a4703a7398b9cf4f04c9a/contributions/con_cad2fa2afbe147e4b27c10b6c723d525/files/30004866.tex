\section{Spin-Alignment Entropy Conjecture}\label{30004866}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 30004866 · Mathematical Physics}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


Let $Q$ be a density matrix on a finite-dimensional one-spin space, and fix nonnegative weights $p_I$ summing to one over subsets $I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}$. For each $I$, let $\omega_{I^c}$ be a density matrix on the complementary spins and embed $Q^{\otimes I}\otimes\omega_{I^c}$ into the common tensor product. Their mixture is $\rho=\sum_Ip_I(Q^{\otimes I}\otimes\omega_{I^c})$. Its von Neumann entropy is $S(\rho)=-\operatorname{tr}\rho\log\rho$.

\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}
Fix a mixed one-spin state $Q$. Consider $n$-spin density operators formed as convex combinations of states in which a chosen subset of spins is fixed to a product of copies of $Q$ and the remaining spins have variable joint states. Is the entropy minimized when every variable joint state is a product of copies of one pure state $|\phi\rangle$, where $|\phi\rangle$ is an eigenvector of $Q$ for its largest eigenvalue?

\paragraph{Scope and source qualification.}The primary presentation starts with spin-one-half states, so an arbitrary finite-dimensional one-spin space is the broader catalogue formulation. The weights and fixed-spin subsets are held fixed in the minimization. Degeneracy of the largest eigenvalue allows any corresponding unit eigenvector; the conjecture does not assert uniqueness.
\subsection{Short English statement}
For fixed mixing weights and fixed one-spin noise, decide whether aligning every variable spin with a largest-eigenvalue eigenvector minimizes entropy.

\paragraph{The one-qubit minimization.} Take \(n=1\), with fixed-state weight \(p\in[0,1]\). In a basis diagonalizing the qubit state write \(Q=(I+r\sigma_z)/2\), \(0\le r\le1\), and a variable state \(\omega=(I+s\cdot\sigma)/2\), \(|s|\le1\); \(\sigma\) denotes the three Pauli matrices. The mixture has Bloch vector \(b=pr e_z+(1-p)s\), and hence eigenvalues \((1\pm|b|)/2\). The entropy as a function of \(b_0=|b|\) has derivative
\[
 \frac12\log\frac{1-b_0}{1+b_0}\le0.
\]
The triangle inequality gives \(|b|\le pr+1-p\), achieved by \(s=e_z\). Thus alignment with a largest-eigenvalue eigenvector minimizes entropy in this subcase.
\paragraph{Boundary audit.} If \(r=0\), every pure direction achieves the same value, as expected from degeneracy of \(Q\). If \(p=0\), every pure state minimizes; if \(p=1\), the variable state is irrelevant. No uniqueness is asserted. The formulas include pure \(Q\) as a boundary check even though the source emphasizes mixed \(Q\).
\paragraph{Remaining target.} With several subsets and several spins, the variable complementary states may be entangled and the different mixture terms cannot all be represented by one Bloch vector. Maximizing one state's Bloch length therefore does not prove a joint majorization statement. The first gap is controlling the spectrum of the whole multi-subset mixture for all fixed weights. The broader finite-dimensional catalogue version is also not covered. This elementary one-qubit check is not claimed as new progress on the conjecture.
