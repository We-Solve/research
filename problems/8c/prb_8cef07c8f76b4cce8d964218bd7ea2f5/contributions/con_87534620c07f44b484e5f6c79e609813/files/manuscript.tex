\section*{Mutation-rate evolution}
\label{prob:169}
\noindent\textbf{Status: restricted result or obstruction; full target not established.}
\subsection{Target and scope}
\paragraph{Catalogue definitions and assumptions.}
A replication-fidelity modifier $m$ specifies deleterious mutation rate $U_d(m)$, beneficial supply $U_b(m,e)$ and direct metabolic cost $c(m)$ per generation. The feasible modifier set is compact and determined by molecular constraints. In a population of effective size $N_e$, environments follow a declared transition process. Let $\lambda(m';m)$ be rare-modifier log growth including linkage, load and adaptation, evaluated in the resident mutation--selection distribution. Define fixation probability $p_{\mathrm{fix}}(m';m)$ for one introduced copy. Assays distinguish per-base rates from the genomic mutation burden: a genome-size change modifies the latter even at constant per-base fidelity. The modifier model must also declare whether expression is constitutive or stress-dependent before comparing evolutionary equilibria. Let $n_c$ be census modifier gene copies: $n_c=N_c$ in haploids and $n_c=2N_c$ in diploids, with $N_c$ the census individual count. Under exchangeable neutral reproduction, one copy fixes with probability $1/n_c$; $N_e$ instead controls drift relative to selection.
\paragraph{Original target.}
Characterize stable rates or stationary modifier distributions using
\[
 \lambda(m';m_*)\le0\quad\forall m',
 \qquad
 |n_c p_{\mathrm{fix}}(m';m_*)-1|\le\varepsilon_{\mathrm{neut}}
 \ \text{for effectively neutral improvements}.
\]
The first condition is a deterministic invasion criterion; the second defines a drift-limited neighbourhood rather than exact optimization. Predict how its position changes with measured fidelity costs and environmental switching. Here $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{neut}}>0$ is a declared neutrality tolerance.
\paragraph{Catalogue alternative formulations.}
(i) In a constant environment with negligible beneficial mutations, isolate fidelity cost and deleterious load. (ii) In rapidly adapting asexual populations include hitchhiking of mutators; compare recombining populations where modifier--beneficial associations decay. These mechanisms can favour different rates.
\subsection{Attempted answer}
\begin{proposition}
In a lethal-mutation baseline, error-free offspring probability is $e^{-U}$ for Poisson mutation load $U$. A modifier reducing load from $U_0$ to $U_1$ at reproductive cost $c$ has relative mean multiplier $(1-c)e^{U_0-U_1}$ and invades in this scalar model exactly when $U_0-U_1>-\log(1-c)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The zero-event Poisson probability gives viable offspring fraction. Divide modifier output $(1-c)e^{-U_1}$ by resident output $e^{-U_0}$ and compare with one. This is a stipulated one-type mean-growth criterion, not a general fixation theorem.
\end{proof}
\subsection{What remains unresolved}
Beneficial mutations, weak deleterious effects, drift, linkage and environment-dependent spectra can reverse conclusions. No natural mutation-rate optimum or full modifier invasion boundary is estimated.
\subsection{Completion Estimate}
\textbf{20\%.} The displayed calculation is established under its additional assumptions. The broader theoretical and biological requirements listed above are not established.

\section{Shared mathematical bounds}\label{app:bounds}
\begin{lemma}[Independent bounded sums]\label{lem:bounded}
Let independent real variables $X_i$ satisfy $a_i\leq X_i\leq b_i$ almost surely,
and let $S=\sum_{i=1}^n(X_i-\mathbb{E} X_i)$. If
$V=\sum_i(b_i-a_i)^2>0$, then for every $t>0$,
\[
 \mathbb{P}(|S|\geq t)\leq 2\exp(-2t^2/V).
\]
In particular, independent losses in $[0,1]$ have mean-error bound
$\mathbb{P}(|\overline X-\mathbb{E}\overline X|\geq\epsilon)
\leq2e^{-2n\epsilon^2}$. The same expression for variables in $[-1,1]$
is $2e^{-n\epsilon^2/2}$. If $V=0$ then $S=0$ almost surely.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $X\in[a,b]$ with mean $\mu$, the nonnegative product
$(X-a)(b-X)$ gives
$\operatorname{Var} X\leq(\mu-a)(b-\mu)\leq(b-a)^2/4$.
Let $K(s)=\log\mathbb{E}\exp[s(X-\mathbb{E} X)]$.
Boundedness permits two differentiations. Its second derivative is the variance
under the exponentially tilted law, which has the same support, so
$K''(s)\leq(b-a)^2/4$ for every real $s$.
Because $K(0)=K'(0)=0$, integration yields
$K(s)\leq s^2(b-a)^2/8$.
Independence adds these log moment-generating bounds for $S$.
For $s>0$, Markov's inequality gives
$\mathbb{P}(S\geq t)\leq\exp(-st+s^2V/8)$.
Choosing $s=4t/V$ yields the upper-tail bound $e^{-2t^2/V}$.
Apply it also to $-S$ and add. The stated mean versions follow by substituting
$t=n\epsilon$ and the respective support widths.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Finite-family extension.}
For $M$ prespecified bounded-loss comparisons, apply the relevant inequality to
each and use a union bound. Independence between comparisons is not required;
independence of the biological units within each comparison is. Repeated cells,
reads or time points from one dependent biological unit cannot automatically be
counted as independent units.

\paragraph{No data-dependent shortcut.}
The bound does not justify choosing a model or validation loss using the same
outcomes and then reporting single-model coverage. A prespecified finite family,
a separate validation sample, or an appropriate complexity or sequential argument
is needed. Marginal predictive coverage likewise should not be read as coverage
for every subgroup or under arbitrary distribution shift.
