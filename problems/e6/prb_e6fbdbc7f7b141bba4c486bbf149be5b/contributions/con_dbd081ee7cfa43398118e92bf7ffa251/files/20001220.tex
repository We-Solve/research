\section{Research attempt: Symmetry averaging can lose even symmetry-invariant information}
Reshaping a tensor into a matrix is a bijection of entries, so a flattening
itself loses no information. Information is lost only when one retains a
coarser statistic such as rank or selected minors. For example many different
strictly positive two-by-two probability tables have rank two; their common
rank does not determine the table or its likelihood.

Consider instead the group generated by transposition, acting on two-by-two
tables, and average over that group: $R(P)=(P+P^T)/2$.
This is an idempotent linear projection, because applying transposition twice
is the identity and a symmetric matrix is fixed. But it is not an orbit
identifier. Let
\[
 P=\begin{pmatrix}3/10&1/10\\3/10&3/10\end{pmatrix},\quad
 Q=\begin{pmatrix}3/10&3/20\\1/4&3/10\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Both have positive entries summing to one and both averages equal
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}3/10&1/5\\1/5&3/10\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
Yet $Q$ is neither $P$ nor $P^T$. Moreover the group-invariant quantity
$|p_{12}-p_{21}|$ is $1/5$ for $P$ and $1/10$ for $Q$.
Thus averaging loses information even relevant to a symmetry-invariant target;
the assertion ``the target is invariant, so the trivial component suffices''
is false.

For this group the missing antisymmetric component is
$A(P)=(P-P^T)/2$, and $P=R(P)+A(P)$ reconstructs the table exactly.
Keeping both components is just an invertible coordinate decomposition.
Discarding $A$ is justified only for a task that depends on $R$ alone, a
stronger condition than invariance under transposition. Similar distinctions
must be checked before replacing flattening by group-aware collapse in a
statistical procedure.
