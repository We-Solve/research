\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 1100805}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A group $H$ is of type $F_n$ if it has an Eilenberg--Mac Lane CW complex $K(H,1)$ with finite $n$-skeleton: its fundamental group is $H$ and its universal cover is contractible. Type $F_n$ but not $F_{n+1}$ means that such a finite $n$-skeleton exists but no $K(H,1)$ has finite $(n+1)$-skeleton. These are homotopical finiteness properties, not merely the homological properties $FP_n$.

Noel Brady's question asks whether, for each integer $n\geq3$, there exists a group $H$ of type $F_n$ but not $F_{n+1}$ such that no subgroup of $H$ is isomorphic to $\Z\times\Z$:
\[
 \forall n\geq3\ \exists H,\qquad
 H\in F_n,\quad H\notin F_{n+1},\quad
 \nexists\text{ injective homomorphism }\Z^2\longrightarrow H.
\]
The source's observation that all known examples contain $\Z^{n-1}$ is historical context from its open-question presentation, not an added requirement on $H$.

The supplied literature assessment reports an affirmative answer, indeed for every $n\geq1$, with the stronger realization
\[
 1\longrightarrow H\longrightarrow G
 \mathrel{\mathop{\longrightarrow}^{\phi}}\Z\longrightarrow1,
\]
where $G$ is word-hyperbolic and $H=\ker\phi$ has the stated finiteness properties and contains no $\Z^2$. Here word-hyperbolic means that a finite-generator Cayley graph has uniformly thin geodesic triangles. This stronger existence claim is attributed to the supplied assessment, not proved in this draft.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can a group have exactly the finiteness level $F_n$ while avoiding a rank-two free abelian subgroup? The original question asks this for $n\geq3$. The supplied assessment labels it solved, attributes the general construction to Llosa Isenrich--Py, and attributes the first $n=3$ case to Llosa Isenrich--Martelli--Py. It also reports that Kropholler (2018) earlier reduced the relevant abelian-rank comparison from $n-1$ to $\lceil n/3\rceil$, without eliminating $\Z^2$. That earlier comparison is not given as a full theorem in the supplied record.
