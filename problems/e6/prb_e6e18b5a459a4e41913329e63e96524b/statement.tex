\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a lattice Laplacian $\Delta$, define
\[
\Gamma=\{A\in\operatorname{Sym}_2(\R):\exists u:\Z^2\to\Z,\ u(x)=\tfrac12x^tAx+o(1+|x|^2),\ \Delta u\le0\}.
\]
The source, attributed to Charles Smart, contrasts the standard lattice's Apollonian packing phenomenon with an apparently simpler $F$-lattice feasible set. Its extraction loses the fraction bar in $1/2$ and superscripts; the supplied verification identifies the displayed reading using Smart's 2013 handout and later work.

For an explicit $F$-lattice convention, outgoing neighbors are horizontal at $(i,j)$ with $i+j$ even and vertical at $i+j$ odd; $\Delta_Fu$ is the sum over those two neighbors minus $2u(i,j)$. Every vertex has two incoming and two outgoing edges. With
\[
M(a,b,c)=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}c+a&b\\b&c-a\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\Lambda=\{(p,q)\in\Z^2:p\equiv q\pmod2\},
\]
the attached aid attributes to Bou--Rabee (2024) the classification
\[
M(a,b,c)\in\Gamma_F\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
c\le-\operatorname{dist}_{\R^2}((a,b),\Lambda).
\]
Equivalently, using the Loewner order $A\preceq Q$ when $Q-A$ is positive semidefinite,
\[
\Gamma_F=\bigcup_{s,t\in\Z}\{A:A\preceq Q_{s,t}\},\qquad
Q_{s,t}=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}s-t&s+t\\s+t&t-s\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The convention for parity and outgoing Laplacian is explicit; applications using the opposite orientation must reconcile coordinates with the cited classification.

The aid separately supplies integer $F$-harmonic functions
\[
H(i,j)=\left\lfloor\frac{i^2-j^2}2\right\rfloor,\quad K(i,j)=ij,\quad
J(i,j)=\left\lfloor\frac{i^2+2ij-j^2}4\right\rfloor-\left\lfloor\frac j2\right\rfloor.
\]
Their leading matrices are respectively $M(2,0,0)$, $M(0,2,0)$ and $M(1,1,0)$. For a checkerboard apex $M(p,q,0)$, an integer harmonic representative is $(p/2)H+(q/2)K$ when $p,q$ are even, and $J+((p-1)/2)H+((q-1)/2)K$ when they are odd. Their error from the leading quadratic is subquadratic.

For a diagonal matrix the exact slice becomes
\[
\operatorname{diag}(r,s)\in\Gamma_F\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
r+s\le-\operatorname{dist}(r-s,2\Z).
\]
In particular $0$ and $\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$ are feasible, but their midpoint $\operatorname{diag}(1/2,-1/2)$ is not; the simple classification is nonconvex. The aid distinguishes this solved matrix-feasibility/PDE statement from general microscopic-pattern questions, which it does not resolve.
