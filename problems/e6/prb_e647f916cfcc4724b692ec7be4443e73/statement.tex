\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 30000902}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Do there exist an infinite family of finite groups $G_n$ with $|G_n|=n$ and subsets $S_n\subseteq G_n$ such that every solution of
\[
 ab^{-1}cd^{-1}ef^{-1}=1,
 \qquad a,b,c,d,e,f\in S_n,
\]
is trivial in the source's intended sense, and
\[
 |S_n|\gg n^{1/3}\ ?
\]
The product order is exact; the groups are not assumed abelian, so factors cannot be rearranged. Infinitely many groups is read as a genuine family with unbounded orders, not repeated isomorphic copies.

The term trivial solution is not defined. Assignments with $a=b$, $c=d$, and $e=f$ certainly give formal cancellation, but the source does not say whether these alone, all free-word cancellation patterns, equality of positive/negative multisets in an abelian convention, or some other class is intended. These choices give different restrictions. No trivial-solution definition is silently chosen.

There is also a size-quantifier ambiguity. With the standard Vinogradov meaning, $\gg$ would require a constant $c>0$ independent of the family index and an infinite set of orders with $|S_n|\ge c n^{1/3}$. If the intended question is growth genuinely beyond the cube-root scale, it might instead require $|S_n|/n^{1/3}\to\infty$ along the family. The assessment's phrase greater than $n^{1/3}$ does not decide this. Neither a divergent ratio nor a fixed power improvement $n^{1/3+\varepsilon}$ is silently substituted for the printed symbol.

The request is for some infinite collection of group orders, not one construction for every positive integer $n$. The source specifies no group class, prescribed construction, or further restrictions on the subsets. A precise extremal problem therefore requires both the forbidden nontrivial patterns and the intended meaning of the size comparison to be recovered.
\subsection{Short English statement}
The question seeks an infinite family of finite groups with large subsets avoiding nontrivial solutions of the exact six-factor word equation. The crucial notions of trivial solution and growth beyond or comparable to the cube-root scale are undefined.
