\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $X$ be the topological vector space intended by the source, and let $(T_j)_{j\geq1}$ be a countable family of continuous linear operators $X\to X$. A vector $x$ is hypercyclic for $T_j$ if its forward orbit is dense:
\[
\overline{\{T_j^n x:n\in\Z_{\geq0}\}}=X.
\]
Write $\operatorname{HC}(T_j)$ for this set and assume each $T_j$ is hypercyclic, meaning $\operatorname{HC}(T_j)\ne\varnothing$. A common hypercyclic vector belongs to $\bigcap_{j\geq1}\operatorname{HC}(T_j)$.

In the usual infinite-dimensional-subspace convention, the requested closed common hypercyclic subspace is a linear subspace $M\subseteq X$ such that
\[
M\text{ is closed},\qquad\dim M=\infty,\qquad
M\setminus\{0\}\subseteq\bigcap_{j\geq1}\operatorname{HC}(T_j).
\]
The zero vector is excluded from the hypercyclicity requirement. No invariance of $M$ under the operators is imposed.

The problem asks for conditions on the countable family and ambient space under which a common hypercyclic vector subspace can be chosen with these closedness and infinite-dimensionality properties. If an algebraic common hypercyclic subspace $N$ is already available, the target is existence of a suitable closed $M$; it does not assert that $\overline N$ automatically has the required property. The extract does not specify the ambient Banach or Fréchet class, scalar field, or any stronger density or invariance convention for the initial subspace.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Characterize when a countable family of hypercyclic operators has a closed infinite-dimensional linear subspace whose every nonzero vector is hypercyclic for every operator. Merely having common hypercyclic vectors or a nonclosed subspace does not state the requested closed-subspace property.
