\section{Expected-Optimum Approximation for Random MAX $R$-3SAT}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.


$n$ is the number of Boolean variables and $m$ the number of random clauses. $A(F)$ counts the clauses satisfied by the algorithm and $\operatorname{OPT}(F)$ the maximum satisfiable count; expectations are over the instance distribution and any algorithmic randomness.

Polynomial time means a number of elementary steps bounded by a polynomial in the encoded input length. A decision problem is in $\mathrm{NP}$ if yes-instances have polynomial-length certificates verifiable in polynomial time; NP-hardness is under polynomial-time reductions.

A $k$-CNF formula is a conjunction of clauses, each a disjunction of at most $k$ literals; a literal is a variable or its negation. Satisfiability asks whether a Boolean assignment makes every clause true. MAX-SAT instead maximizes the number or weight of satisfied clauses.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For fixed clause size $r$ and density $m/n$, design a polynomial-time algorithm for random MAX $R$-3SAT instances $F$ that returns an assignment with expected value $\mathbb{E}[A(F)]\geq(1-\varepsilon)\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{OPT}(F)]$ for every $\varepsilon>0$. \sref{statement}{2}
\paragraph{Scope qualification.}
The supplied name MAX $R$-3SAT and parameter $r$ are retained. The random-clause distribution and relation between these symbols must be read from the report; they are not replaced by a different random-SAT model.
