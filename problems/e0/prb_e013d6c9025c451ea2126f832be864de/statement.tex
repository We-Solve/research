\section{Is every Fermat number $2^{2^n} + 1$ composite for $n > 4$}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For $n\ge0$ the $n$th Fermat number is $F_n=2^{2^n}+1$. A composite integer is an integer greater than one that is not prime.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Is $F_n$ composite for every integer $n>4$? Equivalently, are $F_0,F_1,F_2,F_3,F_4$ the only Fermat primes? \sref{statement}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Are all Fermat numbers after the first five composite?
