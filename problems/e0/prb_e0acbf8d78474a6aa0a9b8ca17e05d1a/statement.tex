Must every finite colouring of the positive integers contain distinct integers $x,y$ of the same colour with $x+y$ a square? Does the corresponding assertion hold for every fixed positive integer exponent $k$?

The problem is known to have an affirmative answer, by Khalfalah and Szemer\'edi. This attempt gives elementary proofs for two colours in the square case and for every eventually periodic colouring in every exponent. It does not reproduce their theorem for arbitrary finite colourings. In particular, a solution with $x=y$ would not answer the question, and distinctness is checked throughout.
