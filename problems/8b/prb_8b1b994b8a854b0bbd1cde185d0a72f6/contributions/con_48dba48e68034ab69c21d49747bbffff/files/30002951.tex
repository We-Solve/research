\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


\paragraph{Definitions and conventions.}
A periodic Lorentz gas is a particle moving freely between elastic collisions with periodically arranged fixed scatterers. The Boltzmann--Grad regime shrinks scatterer radius $r$ while rescaling path length or time to keep collision statistics nontrivial. A long-time limit studies transport after many collisions. A joint limit controls these approximations as $r\to0$ and $t\to\infty$ together, rather than applying one asymptotic result after taking the other limit.

\paragraph{What a quantitative comparison would permit.} Suppose a normalized observable of the radius-$r$ gas differs from a kinetic surrogate by at most $Cr^\alpha t^p$ for $t\geq1$, while that surrogate differs from its long-time limit by at most $Ct^{-\beta}$, with $\alpha,p,\beta>0$. The triangle inequality then yields
\[
 |F_{r,t}-F_\infty|\leq C(r^\alpha t^p+t^{-\beta}).
\]
Choosing $t=r^{-\kappa}$ gives convergence whenever $0<\kappa<\alpha/p$. Balancing the two powers suggests $\kappa=\alpha/(p+\beta)$ and error order $r^{\alpha\beta/(p+\beta)}$. Every conclusion is conditional on the two explicit uniform estimates.

\paragraph{Why iterated convergence is too weak.} The bounded scalar function $G(r,t)=rt/(1+rt)$ tends to zero as $r\to0$ for each fixed $t$, but along $t=1/r$ it equals one half and along $t=r^{-2}$ it tends to one. Thus fixed-time Boltzmann--Grad convergence contains no automatic long-time window. This elementary obstruction persists even if a separate surrogate has a well-defined long-time limit.
