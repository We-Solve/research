\section{Williamson matrix existence problem}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For $n\geq1$, an $n\times n$ matrix $A$ is circulant when $A_{ij}=A_{0,(j-i)\bmod n}$ for $0\leq i,j<n$. A Williamson quadruple of order $n$ consists of symmetric circulant matrices $A,B,C,D\in\{1,-1\}^{n\times n}$ satisfying
\[A^2+B^2+C^2+D^2=4nI_n.
\]
Circulant matrices commute. Such a quadruple gives a Hadamard matrix of order $4n$, namely a matrix with entries $\pm1$ and pairwise orthogonal rows, via
\[\begin{pmatrix}A&B&C&D\\-B&A&-D&C\\-C&D&A&-B\\-D&-C&B&A\end{pmatrix}.
\]
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Determine the set of all positive integers $n$ for which a Williamson quadruple of order $n$ exists. \sref{statement}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Classify every order admitting a Williamson construction of a Hadamard matrix.
