\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a finite alphabet $A$ and integer $d\geq1$, a classical cellular automaton on $A^{\Z^d}$ is a map $F$ given by a finite neighborhood $N\subset\Z^d$ and a local rule $\varphi:A^N\to A$:
\[
 F(x)_v=\varphi((x_{v+u})_{u\in N})\qquad(v\in\Z^d).
\]
It is reversible when it is bijective and its inverse is a cellular automaton; on the full finite-alphabet shift, bijectivity yields this inverse-local-rule property. This standard reversibility notion is distinct from mere surjectivity.
