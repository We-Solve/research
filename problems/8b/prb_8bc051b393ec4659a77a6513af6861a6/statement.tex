\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 6200004}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A group $G$ is word hyperbolic if it is finitely generated and a Cayley graph for a finite generating set has uniformly thin geodesic triangles. It is torsion free if its identity is its only finite-order element. For $R=\Z$ or $\Q$, define the cohomological dimension
\[
 \operatorname{cd}_R(G)=\operatorname{pd}_{RG}R,
\]
the projective dimension of the trivial $RG$-module $R$.

Mike Davis's question is whether there exists a torsion-free word-hyperbolic group $G$ for which
\[
 \frac{\operatorname{cd}_{\Q}(G)}{\operatorname{cd}_{\Z}(G)}<\frac23.
\]
The quotient is read with a finite positive denominator, excluding the trivial-group $0/0$ case. Equivalently in this finite-dimensional setting, the desired strict inequality is
\[
 3\operatorname{cd}_{\Q}(G)<2\operatorname{cd}_{\Z}(G).
\]
No weakening to a non-strict inequality, removal of torsion freeness, or replacement of the coefficient rings is intended.
