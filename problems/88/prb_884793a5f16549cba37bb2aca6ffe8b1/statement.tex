\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(K\subset S^3\) be a hyperbolic knot, meaning that its complement admits a complete hyperbolic metric of finite volume. Let \(X_K=S^3\setminus\operatorname{int}N(K)\) be its compact exterior, where \(N(K)\) is a tubular neighborhood, and identify \(G(K)=\pi_1(S^3\setminus K)\) with \(\pi_1(X_K)\).

Use the standard meridian--preferred-longitude coordinates \((\mu,\lambda)\) on the boundary torus. For \(r=p/q\in\Q\), with \(p\in\Z\), \(q\in\N\), and \(\gcd(p,q)=1\), the slope \(r\) is represented by the unoriented primitive class \(p\mu+q\lambda\). Let \(s_r\in G(K)\) be the image of a representative of this slope under boundary inclusion, after choosing a path to the basepoint. Define
\[
N_r=\langle\!\langle s_r\rangle\!\rangle_{G(K)}
=\bigcap\{N\trianglelefteq G(K):s_r\in N\}.
\]
This normal subgroup is independent of the conjugacy and inversion choices in the representative. Write \(X_K(r)\) for the Dehn filling in which the slope \(r\) bounds a meridian disk of the attached solid torus.

Interpret the hypothesis that \(K\) has \emph{no finite surgery} in its standard nonmeridional sense:
\[
\bigl|\pi_1(X_K(r))\bigr|=\infty
\qquad\text{for every }r\in\Q.
\]
The meridional slope \(\infty=1/0\) is excluded. The supplied record does not explicitly state the slope coordinates or the exclusion implicit in the phrase ``no finite surgery''; these conventions require source confirmation. Including meridional filling in that hypothesis would make it impossible, since meridional filling gives \(S^3\).

\textbf{Question.} For every hyperbolic knot \(K\subset S^3\) satisfying this no-finite-surgery hypothesis, does there exist a subgroup \(H\leq G(K)\), isomorphic to the free group \(F_2\) on two generators, such that
\[
H\cap\left(\bigcup_{r\in\Q}N_r\right)=\{1\}?
\]
Equivalently, the required condition is \(H\cap N_r=\{1\}\) for every rational slope \(r\). The union here is a set-theoretic union; it is not asserted to be a subgroup.
\subsection{Short English statement}
For every hyperbolic knot with no finite nonmeridional Dehn surgery, the question asks for a free subgroup on two generators such that every nonidentity element survives every rational Dehn filling. The meridional filling is excluded. The supplied record labels the question open; this draft does not independently verify that status.
