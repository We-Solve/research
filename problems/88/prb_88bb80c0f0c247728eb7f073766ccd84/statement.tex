\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Under the standard complex operator-algebra interpretation, let \(H\) be an infinite-dimensional complex Hilbert space with a fixed orthonormal basis \((e_i)_{i\in I}\). Let \(B(H)\) be the unital algebra of all bounded complex-linear operators on \(H\), and define its diagonal subalgebra by
\[
D=\{T\in B(H): \text{there exists }\lambda\in\ell^\infty(I)\text{ such that }Te_i=\lambda_i e_i\text{ for every }i\in I\},
\]
where \(\ell^\infty(I)\) denotes the algebra of bounded complex-valued families indexed by \(I\).

For a unital complex \(C^*\)-algebra \(A\), a state is a complex-linear functional \(\varphi:A\to\C\) satisfying \(\varphi(a^*a)\geq 0\) for every \(a\in A\) and \(\varphi(1_A)=1\). A state is pure if it is an extreme point of the convex set of states: whenever \(\varphi=t\varphi_1+(1-t)\varphi_2\), where \(0<t<1\) and \(\varphi_1,\varphi_2\) are states, one has \(\varphi_1=\varphi_2=\varphi\). No normality assumption is imposed.

The supplied question, under this interpretation, asks whether for every such \(H\), every fixed orthonormal basis, and every pure state \(\varphi\) on \(D\), there exists exactly one pure state \(\psi\) on \(B(H)\) such that
\[
\psi|_D=\varphi.
\]
Equivalently, is
\[
\left|\{\psi:\psi\text{ is a pure state on }B(H),\ \psi|_D=\varphi\}\right|=1
\]
for every pure state \(\varphi\) on \(D\)?
