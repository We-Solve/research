\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A closed oriented \(3\)-manifold is a compact oriented \(3\)-manifold without boundary. Irreducibility means that every embedded \(2\)-sphere, in the manifold category intended by the source, bounds an embedded \(3\)-ball. A knot \(K\subset Y\) is an embedded circle. Its complement \(Y\setminus K\) carries the orientation induced from \(Y\). Two knots are equivalent here if some orientation-preserving self-homeomorphism of \(Y\) takes one onto the other; no orientation on the knots themselves is specified.

The supplied standalone formulation of the weak Gordon--Luecke conjecture is the following. For every closed oriented irreducible \(3\)-manifold \(Y\) and every pair of knots \(K_1,K_2\subset Y\), neither of which is a rational unknot in a lens space, assert that
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\exists\text{ an orientation-preserving homeomorphism }
 h:Y\setminus K_1\longrightarrow Y\setminus K_2\\
&\qquad\Longleftrightarrow
\exists\text{ an orientation-preserving homeomorphism }
 F:Y\longrightarrow Y\text{ with }F(K_1)=K_2.
\end{aligned}
\]

\subsection{Short English statement}
The supplied conjecture says that, subject to its exceptional-knot restriction, two knots in the same closed oriented irreducible \(3\)-manifold have orientation-preservingly homeomorphic complements exactly when an orientation-preserving homeomorphism of the ambient manifold carries one knot to the other.
