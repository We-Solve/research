\subsection{Attempted route: remove geometric conditioning by normalizing rows}
The report concerns progress for two-variable-per-inequality LPs, while this record repeats the unrestricted strongly polynomial question. A tempting extension of geometry-dependent pivot bounds is to normalize every constraint normal and hope that the resulting angular parameter is bounded using only the number of rows and columns.
For $B\ge1$, take two rational normals
\[
 a=(1,0),\qquad b=(1,2^{-B}).
\]
Their unit normalizations have distance-to-span parameter
\[
 \delta_B=\operatorname{dist}\left(b/\|b\|_2,\operatorname{span}(a)\right)
 =\frac{2^{-B}}{\sqrt{1+2^{-2B}}}.
\]
\begin{proposition}
Positive rescaling of individual rows cannot give a positive lower bound on this parameter depending only on the two fixed dimensions.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The span of $a$ is the horizontal axis, so the distance of normalized $b$ is the absolute value of its second coordinate, giving the displayed formula. For positive $s,t$, normalizing $sa,tb$ gives exactly the same two unit vectors. Their parameter tends to zero as $B$ increases, although there are always two rows and two columns. The unnormalized coefficients have rational encodings of length $O(B)$, so this is a legitimate family of finite inputs.
\end{proof}
Consequently a proved operation bound containing $\delta_B^{-1}$ cannot be turned into a strongly polynomial bound merely by this row normalization: its displayed bound grows at least $2^B$. This is an obstruction to that deduction, not a lower bound on the actual execution time of every geometric algorithm. Arbitrary column transformations and different pivot rules are not excluded.
\subsection{Verification and remaining gaps}
Exact arithmetic gives the unnormalized determinants $2^{-B}$ at $B=1,10,100$; normalized distances were also evaluated. No algorithm with operation count polynomial only in the row and column counts is obtained. Restricted LP advances in the original report are not promoted to a solution of general LP.
