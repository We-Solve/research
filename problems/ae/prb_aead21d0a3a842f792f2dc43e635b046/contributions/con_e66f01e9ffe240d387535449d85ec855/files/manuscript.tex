\section{Research attempt}
\subsection{Literature and scope}
The target is the full defining ideal of $\operatorname{Sec}_k(\mathbb P^{k-1}\times\mathbb P^{k-1}\times\mathbb P^{k-1})$, $k\ge4$. I tested whether ordinary matrix flattening minors could suffice. Strassen commutation equations, discussed by Landsberg and Manivel, give a direct obstruction. I rederive the needed necessary equation; this is not a claim of new equations or generators.
\begin{proposition}
Over $\mathbb C$, let a $k\times k\times k$ tensor have slices $S_0,\ldots,S_{k-1}$. Border rank at most $k$ implies
\[
S_i\operatorname{adj}(S_0)S_j-S_j\operatorname{adj}(S_0)S_i=0
\]
for every $i,j$. The tensor with $S_0=I_k$, $S_1=E_{12}$, $S_2=E_{21}$ and all other slices zero violates these equations while satisfying all ordinary flattening rank-at-most-$k$ conditions.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
On the parameter space of sums of $k$ decomposable tensors, write $S_i=B D_i C^{\mathsf T}$, where $D_i$ is diagonal. On the nonempty open subset where $S_0$ is invertible, $B,C,D_0$ are invertible. Hence
\[
S_iS_0^{-1}=B D_iD_0^{-1}B^{-1},
\]
so these matrices commute. Multiplying the resulting commutator on the right by $S_0$ and clearing the remaining inverse gives the displayed polynomial equation. The parameter space is an affine space and is irreducible; a polynomial vanishing on a nonempty Zariski open subset vanishes identically. Thus the equation holds for every sum of $k$ decomposable tensors, including degenerate parameter choices, and then on its Zariski closure, the border-rank-at-most-$k$ locus.

For the displayed tensor, the left side with $(i,j)=(1,2)$ is $E_{11}-E_{22}\ne0$. Each ordinary flattening has only $k$ rows (or its transpose only $k$ columns), so rank at most $k$ is automatic. Consequently flattening minors impose no nontrivial obstruction in this balanced format, whereas this tensor lies outside the target secant variety.
\end{proof}
\subsection{Verification and remaining gaps}
Exact integer matrix multiplication at $k=4$ gave $\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,0,0)$ for the commutator. The proof works for all $k\ge4$. It refutes the flattening-only route but does not prove that commutation equations generate the full ideal, treat scheme structure, or control the locus where every chosen slice is singular. None of those missing conclusions is inferred from an invertible-slice calculation.
