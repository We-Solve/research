\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A \(\mathrm{II}_1\) factor is an infinite-dimensional finite von Neumann algebra with center \(\C 1\), equipped with its faithful normal normalized trace. Nonamenability means failure of amenability, equivalently failure of injectivity.

A finite tracial von Neumann algebra \((N,\tau_N)\) has the Haagerup property if there is a net of normal, unital, trace-preserving completely positive maps \(\Phi_i:N\to N\) whose induced operators on \(L^2(N,\tau_N)\) are compact and such that \(\|\Phi_i(x)-x\|_{2,\tau_N}\to0\) for every \(x\in N\), where \(\|x\|_{2,\tau_N}=\tau_N(x^*x)^{1/2}\).

In the usual continuous first-order language of tracial von Neumann algebras, a unital trace-preserving inclusion \(N\subseteq M\) is existential if, for every quantifier-free real-valued formula \(\varphi(\bar x,\bar a)\) with a finite tuple of parameters \(\bar a\) from \(N\),
\[
\inf_{\bar x\in N}\varphi^N(\bar x,\bar a)
=
\inf_{\bar x\in M}\varphi^M(\bar x,\bar a),
\]
where each variable ranges over its designated operator-norm ball in the language.

For a finite tracial von Neumann algebra \((M,\tau_M)\), property \((T)\) is understood in the tracial von Neumann algebra sense: there are a finite set \(F\subset M\) and \(\delta>0\) such that any Hilbert \(M\)-\(M\) bimodule with commuting normal unital left and right actions and a unit vector \(\xi\) satisfying, for every \(x\in F\),
\[
\|x\xi-\xi x\|<\delta,\qquad
\bigl|\langle x\xi,\xi\rangle-\tau_M(x)\bigr|<\delta,\qquad
\bigl|\langle \xi x,\xi\rangle-\tau_M(x)\bigr|<\delta
\]
contains a nonzero central vector \(\eta\), meaning \(x\eta=\eta x\) for every \(x\in M\). Here the inner product is linear in its first variable.

\textbf{Problem.} Can a nonamenable \(\mathrm{II}_1\) factor \(N\) with the Haagerup property be existentially included in an algebra \(M\) having property \((T)\)? Under the interpretation that \(M\) is also a \(\mathrm{II}_1\) factor and that the inclusion is unital and trace-preserving, the question is whether there exist \(N\), \(M\), and an inclusion satisfying all four conditions
\[
N\text{ is nonamenable},\qquad
N\text{ has the Haagerup property},\qquad
N\subseteq M\text{ is existential},\qquad
M\text{ has property }(T).
\]
