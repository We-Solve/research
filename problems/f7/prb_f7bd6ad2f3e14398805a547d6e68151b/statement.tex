\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(A\) be a finite nonempty alphabet, let \(A^*\) be the set of finite words over \(A\), including the empty word \(\varepsilon\), and write \(u^{\mathrm{rev}}\) for the reversal of a finite word \(u\). A palindrome is a word \(u\) satisfying \(u=u^{\mathrm{rev}}\). For a one-sided infinite word \(w\in A^{\{0,1,2,\ldots\}}\), let \(\operatorname{Fact}(w)\) denote its finite contiguous factors. The word \(w\) is uniformly recurrent if, for every nonempty \(u\in\operatorname{Fact}(w)\), there exists an integer \(L\geq |u|\) such that every length-\(L\) factor of \(w\) contains \(u\). It contains arbitrarily long palindromes if
\[
\forall n\geq 1\ \exists u\in\operatorname{Fact}(w):\quad |u|\geq n\quad\text{and}\quad u=u^{\mathrm{rev}}.
\]
A morphism \(\sigma:A^*\to A^*\) respects concatenation. It is primitive if there exists an integer \(k\geq1\) such that, for every \(a,b\in A\), the letter \(b\) occurs in \(\sigma^k(a)\). Such a morphism is nonerasing and acts on one-sided infinite words by concatenating letter images. Under the usual common-prefix convention, \(\sigma\) belongs to class \(P\) if there exist palindromes \(p\in A^*\) and \(q_a\in A^*\), one for each \(a\in A\), such that
\[
\sigma(a)=p q_a\qquad(a\in A).
\]
Here the empty word is allowed as a palindrome.
