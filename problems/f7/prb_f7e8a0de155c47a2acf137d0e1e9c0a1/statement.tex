\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(S^1=\R/\Z\), and let \(\operatorname{Homeo}(S^1)\) denote the group of all self-homeomorphisms of \(S^1\), with composition as the group operation. A group of circle homeomorphisms is understood as a subgroup of this group, so its action on the circle is faithful. All groups below are regarded as discrete groups.

A group \(G\) has Kazhdan's property \((T)\) if every unitary representation \(\pi:G\to\mathcal U(\mathcal H)\) on a complex Hilbert space that admits almost invariant unit vectors has a nonzero invariant vector. Here admitting almost invariant unit vectors means that for every finite subset \(F\subseteq G\) and every \(\varepsilon>0\), there exists \(v\in\mathcal H\) such that
\[
\|v\|=1,\qquad \|\pi(g)v-v\|<\varepsilon\quad\text{for every }g\in F.
\]
An invariant vector is a vector \(w\) satisfying \(\pi(g)w=w\) for every \(g\in G\).

The supplied question asks whether there exists a subgroup \(G\leq\operatorname{Homeo}(S^1)\) such that
\[
|G|=\infty,\qquad G=\langle S\rangle\ \text{for some finite }S\subseteq G,\qquad G\text{ has property }(T).
\]
The source record does not specify whether all homeomorphisms must preserve orientation. The displayed formulation uses the full homeomorphism group; if orientation preservation is intended, its ambient group must instead be \(\operatorname{Homeo}^{+}(S^1)\), the subgroup of orientation-preserving homeomorphisms. This convention remains unresolved in the supplied evidence.
