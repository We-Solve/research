\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 800276}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A complex analytic space \(X\) is reduced if its structure sheaf \(\mathcal O_X\) has no nonzero nilpotent local sections. A reduced complex analytic subvariety \(A\) of a complex manifold \(M\) means a closed subset locally defined by holomorphic equations, equipped with its reduced analytic structure. A biholomorphism is an isomorphism of complex analytic spaces.

\textbf{First question.} For every compact reduced complex analytic space \(X\), do there exist a complex manifold \(M\), a reduced complex analytic subvariety \(A\subseteq M\), and a biholomorphism \(X\cong A\)?

\textbf{Second question.} If the first question has an affirmative answer, can the ambient manifold always be chosen compact? Equivalently, is every compact reduced complex analytic space biholomorphic to a reduced complex analytic subvariety of some compact complex manifold?

The source does not specify the precise convention for ``subvariety.'' The formulation above uses the reduced analytic-subspace interpretation. For an image biholomorphic to compact \(X\) in a Hausdorff complex manifold, closedness follows from compactness. No smoothness, irreducibility, or pure-dimensionality assumption on \(X\) is supplied.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every compact reduced complex analytic space occur, up to biholomorphism, as an analytic subvariety of a complex manifold? If so, can the ambient manifold always be compact? The source attributes these questions to E. L. Stout.
