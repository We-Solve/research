\section{Energy Transmission in Strongly Pinned Networks}\label{30000836}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 30000836 · Mathematical Physics}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


\paragraph{Definitions and conventions.} A finite connected graph $G=(\mathcal V,\sim)$ carries positions $q_i$ and momenta $p_i$, with Hamiltonian $H=\sum_i(p_i^2/2+V(q_i))+\sum_{i\sim j}U(q_i-q_j)$. The smooth confining potential $V$ pins individual sites; $U$ couples neighbors. The report couples specified boundary sites to heat baths. Energy transmission means enough dynamical coupling to control the network and obtain the desired invariant-measure behavior, rather than merely that an edge interaction is nonzero.

\paragraph{Source scope and qualifications.} The source distinguishes geometric obstructions from the strong-pinning potential obstruction and gives an asymmetrical-energy-transmitting condition for uniqueness. Much stronger and sufficient transmission are qualitative in the extract; a norm inequality or universal conductivity theorem is not supplied.

\paragraph{Catalogue mathematical target.}
\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}
For a heat-conduction network whose pinning potential $V$ is much stronger than its interaction potential $U$, can one prove that there is sufficient energy transmission along the network?

\paragraph{Saved literature context.} The supplied catalogue's assessment dated 2026-08-21 records: Strong pinning gives normal conductivity in important oscillator models, but the extracted network-wide energy-transmission assertion is broader than those theorems and remains an open problem without additional hypotheses. The assessment is retained as dated provenance; it is not a new comprehensive literature or open-status certification.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Prove adequate transmission of energy in a heat-bath network when on-site pinning dominates the coupling between sites.
