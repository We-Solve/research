\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


\paragraph{Definitions and conventions.} A finite connected graph $G=(\mathcal V,\sim)$ carries positions $q_i$ and momenta $p_i$, with Hamiltonian $H=\sum_i(p_i^2/2+V(q_i))+\sum_{i\sim j}U(q_i-q_j)$. The smooth confining potential $V$ pins individual sites; $U$ couples neighbors. The report couples specified boundary sites to heat baths. Energy transmission means enough dynamical coupling to control the network and obtain the desired invariant-measure behavior, rather than merely that an edge interaction is nonzero.

\paragraph{Source scope and qualifications.} The source distinguishes geometric obstructions from the strong-pinning potential obstruction and gives an asymmetrical-energy-transmitting condition for uniqueness. Much stronger and sufficient transmission are qualitative in the extract; a norm inequality or universal conductivity theorem is not supplied.

\paragraph{Catalogue mathematical target.}
\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}
For a heat-conduction network whose pinning potential $V$ is much stronger than its interaction potential $U$, can one prove that there is sufficient energy transmission along the network?

The assessment is retained as dated provenance; it is not a new comprehensive literature or open-status certification.

\paragraph{A connected network with an undamped mode.} Take a three-site chain, pinning $V(q)=\kappa q^2/2$ and interaction $U(q)=cq^2/2$, with $\kappa,c>0$, and attach a Langevin heat bath only to the middle site. Put
\[
 Q=(q_1-q_3)/\sqrt2,\qquad P=(p_1-p_3)/\sqrt2.
\]
Subtracting the two endpoint equations gives $\dot Q=P$ and $\dot P=-(\kappa+c)Q$. The middle displacement cancels, and the bath acts on neither difference. Hence
\[
 E_-=\tfrac12(P^2+(\kappa+c)Q^2)
\]
is exactly conserved, even with the random forcing and friction at site two.

\paragraph{Why stronger pinning does not cure geometry.} The ratio $\kappa/c$ can be arbitrarily large without changing this decoupling. A nonzero edge interaction and graph connectivity therefore do not ensure that every mode exchanges energy with the bath. In the deterministic antisymmetric motion $q_1=-q_3,q_2=0$, the center never moves and no bath dissipation occurs. The example is smooth and confining, and its obstruction is geometric symmetry rather than a subtle large-energy estimate.

\paragraph{Source-scope boundary.} The report already distinguishes geometric obstructions and asks about stronger transmission hypotheses. This example is not claimed to satisfy its asymmetrical-energy-transmitting condition, so it does not refute the qualified source program. It shows why that condition cannot be replaced by connectivity, even in a strongly pinned harmonic case. Proving quantitative transfer for graphs and potentials that exclude all such dark modes, especially nonlinear strongly pinned ones, is the first unresolved step.
