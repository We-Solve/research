\textbf{Composite source.} This one registry item contains Problems 5, 6 and 7 and Remark 1 from the June 2--6, 2014 workshop. They are retained as separate questions, not collapsed into a single assertion.

\textbf{Problem 5: mutual stationarity.} Put $\kappa_n=\aleph_{n+2}$. For stationary $S_n\subset\kappa_n\cap\operatorname{cof}(\omega_1)$, a sequence is mutually stationary if every algebra on $\sup_n\kappa_n$ with countably many finitary operations admits a subalgebra $N$ with $\sup(N\cap\kappa_n)\in S_n$ for all $n$; required parameters can be named by constants. This is the standard algebra formulation, not a demand that the witness be countable. A countable witness cannot have the required uncountable cofinalities. Is it consistent that every such sequence is mutually stationary? The damaged phrase ``each $S_n$'' must include \emph{stationary}; a universal assertion for arbitrary subsets would allow an empty component and be false. The aid also notes that mutual stationarity implies stationarity of each component by club coding.

The source attributes a lower bound to Koepke--Welch: an inner model with infinitely many cardinals $\kappa_n$ such that, for $m<n$, measurables $\lambda<\kappa_n$ of Mitchell order at least $\kappa_m$ form a set stationary in $V$. The exact inner-model versus ambient-model interpretations of measurability, Mitchell order and stationarity are not supplied. The aid attributes to Ben-Neria (2019) a positive relative-consistency answer to the repaired question from infinitely many supercompact cardinals. This is not a consistency proof from bare ZFC.

\textbf{Problem 6: forcing axioms and $(*)$.} Is $\mathsf{MM}(\mathfrak c^+)$ consistent with Woodin's Axiom $(*)$? Here $\mathfrak c=2^{\aleph_0}$, and the intended small-MM restriction and Woodin axiom require their standard source conventions. The aid invokes Asper\'o--Schindler's theorem $\mathsf{MM}^{++}\Rightarrow(*)$, together with $\mathsf{MM}^{++}\Rightarrow\mathsf{MM}(\mathfrak c^+)$, to give a positive answer relative to consistency of the full stronger axiom.

The older source report, attributed to Schindler--Woodin, assumes $\mathsf{MM}^{++}$, weak UBH with a proper class of Woodin cardinals and the indicated extender sequences witnessing Woodinness, and $\theta_{\mathrm{uB}}>\aleph_1$, and concludes $(*)$. Its phrase ``for arbitrary partial orders'' is preserved as an unresolved scope qualification: standard Martin's Maximum is restricted to stationary-set-preserving orders, not literally every forcing. No unrestricted forcing axiom is silently introduced.

\textbf{Problem 7: universally Baire generic absoluteness.} Let $\Gamma_{\mathrm{uB}}$ be the universally Baire sets of reals: their continuous preimages in compact Hausdorff spaces have the Baire property. Let $\theta_{\mathrm{uB}}$ be the source's associated universally Baire ordinal; its exact convention is absent. The source also uses $\Gamma_\infty$ for the universally Baire pointclass. Write $\mathrm{GI}$ for the following intended theory invariance: for every set forcing $\mathbb P$, generic extension $V[G]$, formula $\varphi$ and finitely many ground-model universally Baire parameters $A_i$,
\[
 L(\Gamma_{\mathrm{uB}})^V\models\varphi(A_1,\ldots,A_k)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 L(\Gamma_{\mathrm{uB}})^{V[G]}
             \models\varphi(A_1^G,\ldots,A_k^G),
\]
using canonical universally Baire interpretations $A_i^G$, whose existence/choice must be part of the background. Does $\mathrm{GI}$, a proper class of Woodin cardinals and $\mathsf{MM}^{++}$ imply $\operatorname{cof}(\theta_{\mathrm{uB}})>\aleph_1$?

The workshop reports, under the \emph{additional common background} $\mathsf{MM}^{++}$, weak UBH and a proper class of Woodins, the equivalence
\[
 \operatorname{cof}(\theta_{\mathrm{uB}})>\aleph_1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \mathrm{HighW},
\]
where $\mathrm{HighW}$ asserts that a semiproper forcing adds a universally Baire $A$ strictly Wadge-above every ground-model universally Baire $B$ interpreted in the extension. Wadge reducibility means continuous preimage reducibility, and strictly above means $B\le_WA$ but $A\not\le_WB$. The source conjectures both equivalent conditions hold. The aid therefore reduces Problem 7 to $\mathrm{GI}\Rightarrow\mathrm{HighW}$ \emph{under that background}; it does not remove weak UBH from the reduction or prove the implication. It reports no resolution in the literature it checked, not an independent current survey here.

\textbf{Nested Remark 1.} The source describes $(*)^+$ by: for every $A\subset\R$ there is a model $M$ containing the ambient reals, satisfying $\mathsf{AD}^+$, and an $M$-generic $g\subset\mathbb P_{\max}$ with $A\in M[g]$.
