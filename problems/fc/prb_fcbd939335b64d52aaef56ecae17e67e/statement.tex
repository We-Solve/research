\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
\textbf{Original question (Poonen).} Instead of counting bounded-height points on a variety, count points in a Diophantine subset: for a morphism $f:Y\to X$ over $\Q$, count
\[D_f=\{x\in X(\Q):Y_x(\Q)\ne\varnothing\},\qquad N_f(B)=\#\{x\in D_f:H(x)\le B\}.\]
What growth rates can occur? The source suggests that a conic bundle over an elliptic curve might have $\log\log B$ growth, unlike the frequently encountered $cB^\alpha(\log B)^\beta$ forms for variety counts. That comparison is heuristic background, not a theorem classifying every variety. The base, height and meaning of Diophantine image must be fixed for a precise asymptotic.

\textbf{Attached unreviewed explicit reduction.} The aid takes
\[E:\ y^2+y=x^3-x,\qquad E(\Q)=\Z P,\quad P=(0,0),\]
and the generic conic
\[U^2+V^2=(x+2)W^2.\]
It claims this conic has no $\Q(E)$-point, so the bundle has no rational section. For each $n\ge1$, write $x(nP)+2=A_n/D_n^2$ in lowest terms, $A_n\in\Z$, $D_n>0$. The aid asserts that the fiber at $nP$ is soluble over $\Q$ precisely when $A_n$ is a sum of two integer squares.

To state its same-constant transfer with the necessary sign convention, let $S(T)$ count \emph{positive} integers $1\le n\le T$ passing this two-square test. Let $H_x(Q)=\max\{|u|,D^2\}$ if $x(Q)=u/D^2$ is in lowest terms, and let $N(B)$ count all nonzero multiples $nP$, $n\in\Z\setminus\{0\}$, of height $H_x(nP)\le B$ whose fibers are soluble. The exceptional point at infinity is omitted; any choice of a model for its fiber changes this count by at most a constant. Since $x(-nP)=x(nP)$, soluble fibers at the two signs have the same height.

The reported canonical-height comparison has the form
\[|\log H_x(nP)-a n^2|\le C,\qquad a=2\widehat h(P)>0,\]
for a constant $C$, giving the two-sided bounds, with floor implicit in the integer count,
\[2S\left(\sqrt{\frac{\max\{0,\log B-C\}}a}\right)\le N(B)\le2S\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log B+C}a}\right)\qquad(B\ge1).\]
Under these conventions the aid claims, for every $\kappa>0$,
\[S(T)\sim\kappa\log T\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad N(B)\sim\kappa\log\log B.\]
The factor two from $\pm n$ cancels the half from the square-root height cutoff; if $S$ instead counted both signs, the constant would need adjustment. The original and summary do not specify their height/index normalization in this detail, so the convention is explicit here.

The aid invokes a Landau-type sums-of-two-squares model as a heuristic for $S(T)\sim\kappa\log T$, but does not prove it for the elliptic numerator sequence $A_n$. Thus it supplies a solubility reduction and an equivalence, not an unconditional example with the desired $\log\log B$ asymptotic.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Poonen asks which growth rates occur for rational fibers rather than all points of a variety. The aid gives a specific elliptic-base conic bundle and reduces solubility to a two-square test. Its height transfer makes logarithmic growth in the positive index equivalent to double-logarithmic growth in height; the required index asymptotic remains heuristic.
