\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Fix a closed orientable three-manifold $M$. With the usual connected-manifold convention, choose orientations and define the degree of a continuous map $f:M\to N$ by
\[
 f_*[M]=\deg(f)[N]\quad\text{in }H_3(N;\Z),
\]
where $[M]$ and $[N]$ are the fundamental classes. A degree-one map has $\deg(f)=1$.

Let $\mathcal D_1^{\mathrm{geom}}(M)$ be the set of homeomorphism classes of closed orientable geometrized three-manifolds $N$ for which such a degree-one map exists. The question is
\[
 \forall M\text{ closed orientable three-manifold}:\qquad
 \#\mathcal D_1^{\mathrm{geom}}(M)<\infty\ ?
\]
The bound may depend on $M$; no uniform bound over source manifolds is requested. The count concerns target homeomorphism types, not homotopy classes or numbers of maps.

Here ``geometrized'' retains the report's target condition: the Poincar\'e--Thurston geometrization property, allowing the appropriate geometric pieces after prime and torus decompositions. It is not replaced by the stronger condition that $N$ itself carry one geometric structure. In particular, no irreducibility, asphericity, hyperbolicity, or Haken assumption is added to $M$ or $N$.

The source says ``up to homeomorphism'' without specifying orientation-preserving homeomorphism. The displayed degree convention uses chosen orientations, while the requested equivalence is retained as ordinary homeomorphism unless the report dictates otherwise. Connectedness and the detailed geometrization convention should be checked in the source. The original historical phrase ``satisfying the Poincar\'e--Thurston geometrization conjecture'' is a condition on the target class, not a separate request to prove geometrization.
\subsection{Short English statement}
For each fixed closed orientable three-manifold, the question asks whether only finitely many geometrized target homeomorphism types admit a degree-one map from it. Geometrized targets may have several geometric pieces; the problem is not restricted to targets carrying a single geometry.
