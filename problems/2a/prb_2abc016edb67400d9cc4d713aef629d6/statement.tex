\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
The Enriques lattice is conventionally written
\[
L_{\mathrm{Enr}}=U\oplus E_8(-1),
\]
where \(U\) is the rank-two lattice with Gram matrix \(\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}\), and \(E_8(-1)\) is the negative-definite \(E_8\) root lattice. A root is an element \(r\in L_{\mathrm{Enr}}\) satisfying \((r,r)=-2\).

In the usual geometric interpretation, a \((-2)\)-curve on a smooth projective surface is a smooth rational curve \(C\) with self-intersection \(C^2=-2\). For a finite collection of distinct such curves \(C_1,\ldots,C_n\), its intersection data are
\[
C_i^2=-2,\qquad m_{ij}=C_i\cdot C_j\quad(i\ne j).
\]
A dual graph may encode these data by vertices corresponding to the curves and intersection labels \(m_{ij}\). Correspondingly, roots \(r_1,\ldots,r_n\) in \(L_{\mathrm{Enr}}\) encode these data when \((r_i,r_j)=m_{ij}\). This explains the customary intersection interpretation; the supplied record does not specify the intended graph conventions or the precise lattice data retained by an abstract graph.
