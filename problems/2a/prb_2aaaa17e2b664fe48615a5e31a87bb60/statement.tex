\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(S^2=\{x\in\R^3:\|x\|=1\}\). Under the standard smooth, strongly convex interpretation, a Finsler metric on \(S^2\) is a continuous function \(F:TS^2\to[0,\infty)\), smooth away from the zero section, such that \(F(x,v)>0\) for \(v\ne0\), \(F(x,\lambda v)=\lambda F(x,v)\) for every \(\lambda>0\), and the fundamental tensor
\[
g_{x,v}(u,w)=\left.\frac12\frac{\partial^2}{\partial s\,\partial t}F(x,v+su+tw)^2\right|_{s=t=0}
\]
is positive definite for every \(x\in S^2\) and \(v\in T_xS^2\setminus\{0\}\). A flag at \(x\) consists of a nonzero vector \(v\) and a two-dimensional subspace \(P\subseteq T_xS^2\) containing \(v\); on a surface necessarily \(P=T_xS^2\). Write \(K_F(x,v,P)\) for its Finsler flag curvature.

\textbf{Problem (Burns--Matveev, Problem 40).} Describe the Finsler metrics \(F\) on \(S^2\) having constant flag curvature \(1\), that is, satisfying
\[
K_F(x,v,T_xS^2)=1
\qquad\text{for every }x\in S^2\text{ and every }v\in T_xS^2\setminus\{0\}.
\]
The supplied item does not specify the regularity class, whether reversibility \(F(x,-v)=F(x,v)\) is required, or the equivalence relation and form of description sought. The definition above states the standard smooth, strongly convex interpretation; the intended treatment of other regularities and any reversibility restriction remain unresolved.

\textbf{Supplied background qualifications.} The source describes flag curvature as the Finsler analogue of sectional curvature and attributes the following assertions to [Akbar-Zadeh1988]: every closed Finsler surface of constant flag curvature zero is locally Minkowskian; every sufficiently smooth closed Finsler surface of constant negative flag curvature is Riemannian, with non-Riemannian examples at insufficient smoothness; and every connected closed Finsler surface of constant positive flag curvature is homeomorphic to \(S^2\) or \(\R P^2\). Here closed means compact without boundary, and locally Minkowskian means that suitable local coordinates make the Finsler norm independent of the base point. The supplied background further says that the negative-curvature result follows from [Foulon1997] and holds in all dimensions. These are attributed background assertions, not independently verified conclusions of this draft; the required smoothness threshold is not supplied.
