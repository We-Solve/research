Fix \(H\in(1/4,1)\). Let \(B=(B^1,B^2)\) be a centered Gaussian process with independent standard fractional Brownian components, so that
\[
\E[B^i_sB^j_t]=\frac{\delta_{ij}}2\bigl(s^{2H}+t^{2H}-|t-s|^{2H}\bigr),\qquad s,t\geq0,\quad i,j\in\{1,2\}.
\]
For \(T>0\), let \(B^{(m)}\) be the piecewise linear interpolation of \(B\) on the dyadic partition of \([0,T]\) into \(2^m\) intervals. Use the signed-area convention
\[
A_T=\lim_{m\to\infty}^{L^2}\frac12\left(\int_0^T B^{1,(m)}_t\,\dd B^{2,(m)}_t-\int_0^T B^{2,(m)}_t\,\dd B^{1,(m)}_t\right),
\]
where the integrals before taking the limit are ordinary integrals along piecewise linear paths. For a positive integer \(n\), define
\[
t_k=\frac{kT}{n}\quad(0\leq k\leq n),\qquad
\mathcal G_{n,T}=\sigma\bigl(B^i_{t_k}:i\in\{1,2\},\ 0\leq k\leq n\bigr),\qquad
\widehat A_{n,T}=\E[A_T\mid\mathcal G_{n,T}].
\]
Thus this convention uses \(n\) subintervals and \(n\) nonzero observation times, together with the deterministic starting value.

Under these conventions, the candidate lower-bound question is whether
