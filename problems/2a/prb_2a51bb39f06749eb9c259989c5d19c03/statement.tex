\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
In the usual closed-manifold convention, let \(X\) be a closed, connected, oriented smooth \(4\)-manifold. A smooth embedded \(3\)-sphere is an embedding \(e:S^3\hookrightarrow X\); it is nonseparating if \(X\setminus e(S^3)\) is connected.

A \((g;k_0,k_1,k_2)\)-trisection is a decomposition \(\mathcal T=(X_0,X_1,X_2)\) with \(X=X_0\cup X_1\cup X_2\), where each \(X_i\) is a \(4\)-dimensional \(1\)-handlebody diffeomorphic to the boundary connected sum of \(k_i\) copies of \(S^1\times B^3\), with \(k_i=0\) meaning \(B^4\). Each pairwise intersection \(H_{ij}=X_i\cap X_j\) is a \(3\)-dimensional handlebody of genus \(g\), and the triple intersection \(\Sigma=X_0\cap X_1\cap X_2\) is their common boundary, a closed connected surface of genus \(g\). The parameters satisfy \(g\geq 0\) and \(0\leq k_i\leq g\); equality of the three \(k_i\) is not required.

Such a trisection is reducible if there is a simple closed curve \(c\subset\Sigma\), essential in \(\Sigma\), which bounds a properly embedded disk in each of \(H_{01},H_{12},H_{20}\). Here essential means that \(c\) does not bound a disk in \(\Sigma\).
