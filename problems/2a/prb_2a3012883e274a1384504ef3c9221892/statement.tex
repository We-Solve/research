\section*{Status and modelling choices}
This is a proposed computational question, not a claim that exact-
feedback constant swap regret remains unsolved. Its oracle, memory,
precision and complexity requirements are explicit editorial choices.
No implementation or impossibility proof is supplied.

\section*{Finite-bit game and feedback model}
Fix $N,K\geq2$ and $L\geq1$. There are $N$ labelled players with
$1\leq m_i\leq K$ actions each. The fixed game's payoffs are rational
numbers in $[0,1]$, each with numerator and denominator of at most $L$
bits. Tables are not given to the learners. At round $t$, player $i$
outputs an exactly normalized rational mixed strategy
$x_i^t\in\Delta(A_i)$. Payoffs extend multilinearly.

After simultaneous play, a player may request its own expected payoff
vector at precision $b\geq1$. The oracle returns the coordinatewise
nearest dyadic approximation with denominator $2^b$ to
$v_i^t(a)=u_i(a,x_{-i}^t)$, breaking ties downward. Thus each coordinate
error is at most $2^{-b}$. A player sees only its own oracle replies,
its state and the public data $N,(m_i),L,t$. It neither sees opponents'
payoff tables nor receives a precomputed equilibrium. The oracle's
internal cost of forming expected payoffs is excluded; queries, reply
lengths, storage and subsequent bit operations are counted.

\section*{Swap-regret benchmark}
Regret is measured against the \emph{true} expected-payoff vectors, not
the rounded replies:
\[
 \SwapReg_i(T)=\max_{\varphi:A_i\to A_i}
 \sum_{t=1}^T\sum_{a\in A_i}x_{ia}^t
          (v_i^t(\varphi(a))-v_i^t(a)).
\]
The identity map shows this benchmark is nonnegative. The comparator
is a single fixed action-remapping function over the entire history.

\section*{Question R3}
Do there exist a deterministic horizon-independent uncoupled learner
family and fixed polynomials $P,Q$ such that, for every such game,
every player and every round $t$, its initialization and update use
at most $P(N,K,L,\lceil\log_2(t+1)\rceil)$ bit operations and bits of
working memory, all requested precisions and output coordinate bit
lengths obey the same bound, and simultaneously
\[
 \sup_{T\geq1}\SwapReg_i(T)\leq Q(N,K,L)?
\]
All players use the family in self-play. No adversarial guarantee is
imposed. A sharper optional goal is to preserve, up to an absolute
constant factor and additive constant, the exact-arithmetic dimensional
rate $O(\sqrt N\,K\log K\,\log^{5/2}(NK))$.

\section*{Known result and the remaining implementation issue}
Mai reports deterministic uncoupled constant swap regret at this
dimensional scale under exact expected-payoff feedback. Appendix H
explicitly excludes finite-bit complexity, inexact stationarity and
floating-point guarantees from its exact-operation counts \cite{mai}.
That separation motivates R3; it does not prove that R3 is impossible
or novel. A polynomial $Q$ is a weaker target than preservation of the
reported sharp rate.

\section*{An elementary check that is not a solution}
For any fixed sequence $x^t$, replacing true payoff vectors by vectors
with coordinate errors at most $\epsilon_t$ changes every remapping's
cumulative gain by at most $2\sum_{t\leq T}\epsilon_t$; taking the
maximum preserves that bound. Thus choosing
$\epsilon_t\leq(t+1)^{-2}$ controls this particular evaluation error
using only $O(\log(t+1))$ feedback bits. It does not control the effect
of errors on future strategies, conditioning of stationary-distribution
calculations, accumulated internal state, or exact rational
normalization. Those are part of the question, not omitted steps of a
claimed proof. No fixed-precision implementation has been tested.

\section*{Catalogue relation}
No exact we-solve target for this finite-bit swap-regret model was found.
\texttt{ECON-C73-63} concerns external regret with a fixed adversarial
budget; \texttt{ECON-C73-113} concerns Markov games. R3 does not alter or
claim to solve either. Its precise model and polynomial resource bounds
need expert assessment for open status and novelty.
