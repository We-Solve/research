\section{Reeb components — Question 5.2}\label{10300010}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 10300010 · Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A codimension-one foliation $\mathcal F$ decomposes a $3$-manifold $M$ into surface leaves, locally as the horizontal slices of a product chart. In the terminology of the source, it is taut if a closed curve transverse to $\mathcal F$ meets every leaf. A minimal foliation means that every leaf is dense; a minimal surface means a surface with vanishing mean curvature for the specified Riemannian metric. These two uses of minimal are distinct.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Formulate useful extensions of tautness for arbitrary open $3$-manifolds, addressing both topological and geometric settings rather than fixing a single candidate in advance. Source suggestions include generalized taut structures on interiors of compact manifolds; foliations transverse, possibly uniformly transverse, to the lift of a volume-preserving flow from a compact base; eventual or uniform positive transversality along sufficiently long flow segments; and lifted positive transverse loops that collectively meet every leaf. Develop and compare suitable definitions and their consequences. \sref{10300010}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Develop appropriate notions of tautness on noncompact manifolds, including topological definitions and versions controlled by transverse flows or lifted loops.
