\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 701014}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A soluble Fitting class is a nonempty class \(\mathfrak F\) of finite soluble groups, closed under isomorphism, taking normal subgroups, and taking normal products: if \(N_1,N_2\trianglelefteq H\) belong to \(\mathfrak F\), then \(N_1N_2\in\mathfrak F\). For a finite soluble group \(H\), its \(\mathfrak F\)-radical is
\[
H_{\mathfrak F}=\prod\{N\trianglelefteq H:N\in\mathfrak F\}.
\]
This is the largest normal subgroup of \(H\) belonging to \(\mathfrak F\).

A formation is a nonempty isomorphism-closed class of finite groups closed under homomorphic images and finite subdirect products. Here a subdirect product of \(H_1,\ldots,H_r\) is a subgroup of \(H_1\times\cdots\times H_r\) whose projection onto each factor is surjective.

\textbf{Existence question (A. F. Vasiliev).} Does there exist a soluble Fitting class \(\mathfrak F\) which is not a formation and satisfies
\[
\forall\,G\text{ finite soluble}\quad
\forall\,A,B\leq G,\qquad
G=AB\ \Longrightarrow\
A_{\mathfrak F}\cap B_{\mathfrak F}\leq G_{\mathfrak F}?
\]
Here \(AB=\{ab:a\in A,\ b\in B\}\), and the radicals of \(A\) and \(B\) are regarded as subgroups of \(G\). No normality assumption on \(A\) or \(B\) is imposed.

The supplied record does not define the radical notation or the class terminology; the definitions above use their conventional finite-group meanings and require confirmation against the original source. \subsection{Short English statement}
Does there exist a class \(\mathfrak F\) of finite soluble groups that is a Fitting class but not a formation, such that every factorization \(G=AB\) of a finite soluble group satisfies \(A_{\mathfrak F}\cap B_{\mathfrak F}\leq G_{\mathfrak F}\)? The subscripts are interpreted as Fitting-class radicals.
