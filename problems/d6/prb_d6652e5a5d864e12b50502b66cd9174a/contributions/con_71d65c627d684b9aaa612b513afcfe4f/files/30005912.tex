\subsection{A complete product-measure baseline}
The primary definition includes the diagonal influence and calls a product law 0-spectrally independent. Its Glauber dynamics chooses a coordinate uniformly each step. We analyze that normalization exactly. Let $\mu$ be any product law on $\{-1,1\}^n$, with both probabilities at each coordinate positive. All of its conditionals remain product laws, so off-diagonal influences vanish and each remaining diagonal entry is one.
\begin{proposition}
The uniform-coordinate heat-bath chain satisfies, from every starting state,
\[
\|\mathcal L(X_t)-\mu\|_{\rm TV}\le n(1-1/n)^t\le ne^{-t/n}.
\]
For the unbiased product law and $n\ge2$, put $a=n(1-1/n)^t$. If $a\ge4\sqrt n$, its total-variation distance from stationarity when started at all minus signs is at least $1/2$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Couple a chain from the specified state with one started from $\mu$, using the same chosen coordinate and the same fresh draw there. Each coordinate agrees permanently after it has been selected at least once. A fixed coordinate is untouched with probability $(1-1/n)^t$; the union bound proves the first inequality. The stationary copy stays at $\mu$ because resampling a coordinate from its independent marginal preserves the product law. The exponential bound follows from $1-u\le e^{-u}$, with the trivial one-coordinate boundary interpreted directly.
For the lower bound let $S_t=\sum_i X_{t,i}$. Each coordinate has expectation $-(1-1/n)^t$, so $\mathbb ES_t=-a$. For $i\ne j$, the product has expectation $(1-2/n)^t$: if either is refreshed, its last unbiased refresh has mean zero independently of the other coordinate. Since $1-2/n\le(1-1/n)^2$, pairwise covariances are nonpositive and $\operatorname{Var}(S_t)\le n$. At stationarity the sum has mean zero and variance $n$. Chebyshev gives probability at least $1-4n/a^2$ to the event $S_t\le-a/2$ for the started chain, and at most $4n/a^2$ for stationarity. The difference is at least $1-8n/a^2\ge1/2$.
\end{proof}
For example, taking $t=\lfloor n\log n/4\rfloor$ makes the lower-bound condition true for all sufficiently large $n$, because $a/\sqrt n=n^{1/4+o(1)}$. Thus the $n\log n$ scale is already necessary in this simple uniform-scan model.
A separate normalization test shows why scan probabilities matter. On the unbiased two-bit product law, refresh coordinate one with probability $0<\varepsilon<1$ and start it at $-1$. Its marginal bias at time $t$ is $-(1-\varepsilon)^t$, giving total-variation distance at least $(1-\varepsilon)^t/2$. Spectral independence remains zero while mixing can be arbitrarily slow as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. This is not a counterexample to the source's explicitly uniform scan.
