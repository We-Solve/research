\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For elements \(a,b\) of a group, adopt the commutator convention \([a,b]=a^{-1}b^{-1}ab\), and define the iterated commutator recursively by
\[
[a,{}_1b]=[a,b],\qquad [a,{}_{k+1}b]=[[a,{}_kb],b]\quad(k\geq 1).
\]
Thus \([a,{}_nb]\) is the left-normed commutator with \(n\) occurrences of \(b\). The supplied record uses iterated commutators but does not explicitly specify the commutator convention or bracketing; these conventions are an explicit interpretation requiring confirmation against the source.

For each integer \(n\geq 3\), let
\[
M_n=\left\langle x,y\ \middle|\ x=[x,{}_ny],\quad y=[y,{}_nx]\right\rangle.
\]
This is a group presentation, with no additional relations assumed. Is it true that
\[
\forall n\in\Z,\qquad n\geq 3\ \Longrightarrow\ |M_n|=\infty?
\]

\subsection{Short English statement}
For every integer \(n>2\), consider the group generated by \(x\) and \(y\), subject to each generator equalling its iterated commutator with \(n\) copies of the other generator. Must every such group be infinite? The explicit commutator conventions used here require source confirmation.
