\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(f\colon\C\to\C\) be an entire function. Define its set of critical values by
\[
\operatorname{CV}(f)=\{f(c):c\in\C,\ f'(c)=0\}.
\]
Its set of finite asymptotic values is
\[
\operatorname{AV}(f)=\left\{a\in\C:\begin{array}{l}\text{there exists a continuous curve }\gamma\colon[0,\infty)\to\C\\ |\gamma(t)|\to\infty\text{ and }f(\gamma(t))\to a\text{ as }t\to\infty\end{array}\right\}.
\]
Here a region means a nonempty connected open subset of \(\C\). For \(D\subseteq\C\), its full preimage is
\[
f^{-1}(D)=\{z\in\C:f(z)\in D\}.
\]
We use complete invariance to mean forward and backward invariance:
\[
f(D)\subseteq D\quad\text{and}\quad f^{-1}(D)\subseteq D,
\]
or, equivalently, \(f^{-1}(D)=D\). This convention does not require surjectivity of \(f|_D\).

\textbf{Question 1, with an unresolved basin qualification.} For every entire function \(f\) and every immediate basin \(D\) of \(f\), does
\[
\operatorname{CV}(f)\cup\operatorname{AV}(f)\subseteq D
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad f^{-1}(D)=D
\]
hold? The supplied record does not specify the type or period of the orbit associated with the immediate basin. For example, for an attracting fixed point \(a\), the basin is \(B(a)=\{z\in\C:f^{\circ n}(z)\to a\}\), and its immediate basin is the connected component of \(B(a)\) containing \(a\). This example defines one usual case; it does not resolve whether the source intends only this case, attracting periodic basins more generally, or also parabolic basins. Thus the quantification over immediate basins in Question 1 remains subject to source clarification.

\textbf{Question 2.} Is it true that for every entire function \(f\colon\C\to\C\) and every nonempty connected open set \(D\subseteq\C\),
\[
\operatorname{CV}(f)\cup\operatorname{AV}(f)\subseteq D
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad f^{-1}(D)\text{ is connected}?
\]
The containment hypotheses concern the values themselves; no containment of their closure, boundedness, simple connectivity, properness of \(D\), or forward invariance of \(D\) is explicitly assumed in Question 2. The record says entire, without an explicit restriction to nonconstant or transcendental entire functions.
