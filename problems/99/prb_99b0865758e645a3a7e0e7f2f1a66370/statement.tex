\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(G\) be a finitely generated group, \(\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(G)\), and \(g\in G\). Define \(g_n=\alpha^n(g)\) for integers \(n\geq0\). The source asks how \(g_n\) grows as \(n\to\infty\), and whether this sequence has a limit in a suitable sense.

For example, after choosing a finite symmetric generating set \(S\), word length is
\[
|h|_S=\min\{r:h=s_1\cdots s_r,\ s_i\in S\},
\]
with the empty product allowed. This would give the numerical sequence \(|\alpha^n(g)|_S\), but the extraction does not say that word length, rather than conjugacy length or another quantity, is its intended growth observable. Nor does it specify a normalization of lengths or a topology or compactification in which the group elements should converge. The statement therefore remains a growth and convergence question requiring these choices. No assertion of convergence for all finitely generated groups, and no restriction to free or hyperbolic groups, is inserted.
