\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
The source requests concrete operator models and spectral-measure faithfulness criteria for universal Hopf $C^*$-algebras beyond the basic $A_u(n)$, $A_o(n)$ and $A_s(n)$ examples, especially quantum automorphism algebras of finite graphs. The original refers to Theorem 2, whose precise statement is absent.

In the standard compact-matrix-quantum-group convention, $A_u(n)$ is generated by a matrix $u$ with $u$ and its entrywise conjugate unitary; $A_o(n)$ has a self-adjoint-entry orthogonal generating matrix; and $A_s(n)$ has a magic unitary, meaning projections with each row and column summing to one. Their coproduct satisfies $\Delta(u_{ij})=\sum_k u_{ik}\otimes u_{kj}$. For a finite graph $X$ with $n$ vertices and adjacency matrix $D_X$, the usual quantum automorphism algebra is
\[
 A(X)=A_s(n)/\langle uD_X-D_Xu\rangle.
\]
Graph and universal-completion conventions require source confirmation.

A concrete operator model is a unital $*$-homomorphism $\pi:A(X)\to B(\mathcal H)$, given by operators satisfying the defining relations. For this magic-unitary case the main character $\chi=\sum_i u_{ii}$ is self-adjoint. Given a specified state $\varphi$ on the model algebra, its spectral probability measure is determined by
\[
 \int t^r\,d\mu_{\pi,\varphi}(t)=\varphi(\pi(\chi)^r)
 \quad(r\geq0).
\]
The Haar state $h$ similarly gives $\mu_h$ for $\chi$. The question asks for an analogue of the source's criterion relating such spectral data to the appropriate faithfulness property, together with usable models for the target algebras. It does not supply the needed state, criterion, moment conditions or quantifier over graph classes.

Faithfulness of $\pi$ as an injective $C^*$-representation must be distinguished from inner faithfulness, which excludes a nontrivial Hopf quotient through which the model factors. Universal and reduced completions and faithfulness of the Haar state are also different issues. No equality-of-measures iff injectivity claim is asserted without Theorem 2. For non-self-adjoint characters in other quantum groups, even the spectral-data convention may need $*$-moments or another specified observable.
