\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Fix an embedding $\overline{\Q}\hookrightarrow\C$ and let $\Gamma=\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\Q}/\Q)$. For a variety $X$ over $\overline{\Q}$, let $X^\sigma$ be its conjugate by $\sigma\in\Gamma$. Compare topological invariants of the associated complex analytic spaces through the chosen embedding.

The source asks three questions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Which topological invariants of a variety over $\overline{\Q}$ are preserved by every Galois conjugation? For a proposed invariant $I$, the relevant condition is $I(X)=I(X^\sigma)$ for all varieties and conjugations in the intended class. The wording says variety generally, while the surrounding context concerns surfaces; the precise scope is not stated.
\item Is the $\Gamma$-action faithful on the union $\mathcal M$ of moduli spaces of minimal complex surfaces of general type?
\item Is it faithful on the rigid surfaces, described in the original as the zero-dimensional components?
\end{enumerate}

If $\mathcal M$ means the set of geometric moduli points represented by such surfaces over $\overline{\Q}$, the faithfulness question has the precise pattern
\[
 \forall\sigma\in\Gamma\setminus\{1\}\ \exists[S]\in\mathcal M:
                      [S^\sigma]\ne[S].
\]
The third question restricts the witness $[S]$ to the source's rigid subset. The witness may depend on $\sigma$; the question does not say that every nontrivial element moves every surface. Faithfulness on a union is also not faithfulness on each component or for every fixed set of numerical invariants.

The record does not identify whether $\mathcal M$ denotes moduli points, connected/irreducible components, a coarse space, or another object. An absolute Galois automorphism acts directly on $\overline{\Q}$-points; an action on all complex points requires further conventions and is not supplied by arbitrary extensions to $\C$. Rigid as a zero-dimensional component is not silently strengthened to vanishing of every infinitesimal deformation group. The precise smooth/projective versus canonical-model surface class and moduli equivalence must likewise be recovered. The first question supplies no fixed list or criterion of topological invariants.
\subsection{Short English statement}
The entry asks which topology survives Galois conjugation and whether the Galois action is faithful on the whole general-type-surface moduli collection or already on rigid surfaces. The exact moduli object and variety class need confirmation; the three tasks remain distinct.
