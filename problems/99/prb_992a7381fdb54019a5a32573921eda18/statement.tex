\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 701697}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(F\) be a free group of finite rank at least \(2\). An element \(w\in F\) is a proper power if \(w=u^m\) for some \(u\in F\) and integer \(m\ge 2\). For \(w\in F\setminus\{1\}\) and an integer \(n\ge 1\), define
\[
G(F,w,n)=\bigl(F*\langle t\rangle\bigr)\big/\left\langle\!\left\langle t^n w^{-1}\right\rangle\!\right\rangle,
\]
where \(\langle t\rangle\cong\Z\), the symbol \(*\) denotes the free product, and the denominator is the normal closure of \(t^n w^{-1}\) in that free product. A group is linear of degree \(2\) over a field \(K\) if it admits an injective homomorphism into \(\operatorname{GL}_2(K)\), the group of invertible \(2\times2\) matrices over \(K\).

\textbf{Question (A. Jaikin-Zapirain, Kourovka Notebook Problem 20.39).} For every such \(F\), every nonidentity element \(w\in F\) that is not a proper power, and every integer \(n\ge 1\), do there exist a field \(K\) of characteristic \(0\) and an injective homomorphism
\[
\rho:G(F,w,n)\longrightarrow\operatorname{GL}_2(K)?
\]
The field \(K\) may depend on \(F,w,n\); no single common field is requested.

\textbf{Supplied motivation.} The source relates this question to Kourovka Notebook Problem 13.39(b), which asks whether the free \(\Q\)-group \(F^{\Q}\) is linear over a field. Here a \(\Q\)-group is understood in the standard uniquely divisible sense: for every element \(g\) and every integer \(m\ge1\), there is exactly one element \(h\) with \(h^m=g\). The free \(\Q\)-group on a free basis of \(F\) is the free object on that basis among uniquely divisible groups. The motivational question asks for a faithful representation into \(\operatorname{GL}_d(L)\) for some field \(L\) and some positive integer \(d\); the supplied wording imposes neither degree \(2\) nor a characteristic condition on this separate question.
\subsection{Short English statement}
For every nonabelian finitely generated free group \(F\), nontrivial element \(w\) that is not a proper power, and integer \(n\ge1\), does adjoining an element \(t\) with \(t^n=w\) produce a group admitting a faithful two-dimensional representation over some field of characteristic \(0\)? The supplied source attributes the question to A. Jaikin-Zapirain and motivates it by the linearity question for the free \(\Q\)-group \(F^{\Q}\).
