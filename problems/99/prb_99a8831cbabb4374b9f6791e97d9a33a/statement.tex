A finite simple graph is even-hole-free if it has no induced cycle of even length at least four, and triangle-free if it has no three pairwise adjacent vertices. It is 2-degenerate if every nonempty induced subgraph has a vertex of degree at most two. Write \(\chi(G)\) for chromatic number and \(\operatorname{ch}(G)\) for list-chromatic number: the least nonnegative integer \(k\) such that every assignment of lists \(L(v)\) with \(|L(v)|\geq k\) admits a proper coloring \(c(v)\in L(v)\). For the graph with no vertices use both numbers zero.

Separately from the invalid fragment, the aid reports this motivated corollary: every finite triangle-free even-hole-free graph \(G\) is 2-degenerate and satisfies
\[
 \chi(G)=\operatorname{ch}(G)=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&V(G)=\varnothing,\\
 1,&V(G)\ne\varnothing,\ E(G)=\varnothing,\\
 2,&G\text{ is a forest with at least one edge},\\
 3,&G\text{ contains a cycle}.
 \end{cases}
\]
In particular, within this triangle-free even-hole-free class, the bipartite graphs are exactly the forests. This is a separate supplied research-aid assertion, not the missing conclusion of the canonical fragment.
