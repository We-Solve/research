\section{Source discrepancy and attempted speedup}
The primary report gives an $O(n^3)$ assignment baseline for the fixed-angle
objective $\sum_i\|A_i-B_{\pi(i)}\|^2$. Its question (4) asks for faster
optimization of that objective; the preceding questions treat the rotation
parameter. The supplied record calls the task rank-two parametric assignment.
We retain the entire record and discuss both interpretations without declaring
that the two tasks coincide. The report's geometric costs, after discarding
permutation-independent terms and a positive factor, maximize
$\sum_i A_i\cdot R(\theta)B_{\pi(i)}$.

\paragraph{A genuine sorting speedup in the collinear restriction.}
If $A_i=c+a_i e$, the contribution $c\cdot R(\theta)\sum_jB_j$ is
independent of the matching. Write $t_j=e\cdot R(\theta)B_j$.
For increasing $a_i$, an inverted assigned pair $t_{\pi(i)}>t_{\pi(j)}$
with $i<j$ can be exchanged with score gain
$(a_j-a_i)(t_{\pi(i)}-t_{\pi(j)})\ge0$.
Every finite list becomes sorted after successively removing inversions, so
the matching of increasing $a_i$ with increasing $t_j$ is globally optimal.
Both lists can be sorted in $O(n\log n)$ comparisons, and the score and
permutation then computed in linear time. This solves the fixed-angle special
case more quickly than the source baseline. Ties may be ordered consistently.
As the angle varies, every pair of nonidentical projections crosses at most
twice, since its difference is a nonzero linear combination of sine and cosine.
Thus at most $2\binom n2$ event angles suffice for a polynomial enumeration
in this special case. Simultaneous events are processed together; no assumption
of generic position is needed for the interval bound. This route was also
independently suggested by the root Codex GPT-6 agent, whose overlapping
mathematical contribution is acknowledged.

\paragraph{Pairwise exchange optimality fails already for full rank two.}
One might try to extend sorting by finding an assignment with no improving
transposition. Consider the exact planar points
\[
B_1=(1,0),\quad B_2=(0,1),\quad B_3=(-1,-1),
\qquad A_1=(7,24),\ A_2=(-24,7),\ A_3=(17,-31).
\]
They satisfy $A_i=TB_i$ for
$T=\begin{pmatrix}7&-24\\24&7\end{pmatrix}$.
For the identity matching, swapping $i,j$ decreases the dot-product score by
\[
(A_i-A_j)\cdot(B_i-B_j)
=(T(B_i-B_j))\cdot(B_i-B_j)=7\|B_i-B_j\|^2>0,
\]
since the skew-symmetric part has zero quadratic form and the three $B_i$
are distinct. Thus every two-swap is strictly worse. The identity score is
$7+7+14=28$. The cyclic assignment $(\pi(1),\pi(2),\pi(3))=(2,3,1)$
has score $24+17+17=58$, so it is strictly better. The cost matrix has
rank exactly two: its $(1,2)$-row and $(1,2)$-column submatrix is
$\begin{pmatrix}7&24\\-24&7\end{pmatrix}$ with determinant $625\ne0$,
and its factorization through planar coordinates bounds rank by two.
This is therefore a counterexample within the fixed-angle rank-two geometric
class, not an unrestricted-matrix substitute. It disproves the proposed
local-to-global exchange criterion, not the existence of another fast algorithm.

\paragraph{Why the parametric reformulation does not finish the job.}
For each permutation put
$z_\pi=(\sum_i A_i\cdot B_{\pi(i)},\sum_i A_i\cdot J B_{\pi(i)})$,
where $J$ is a quarter-turn. Expansion of $R(\theta)$ shows that the best
score in direction $(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$ is the support function
of the convex hull of these vectors. Maximizing also over angle yields
$\max_\pi\|z_\pi\|$ by Cauchy--Schwarz and equality in each vector's
direction. A prescribed direction is an ordinary rank-two assignment, but
constructing the whole polygon can still require checking up to $n!$ sums
unless a further structural bound or different optimization argument is proved.
Neither a fast support oracle nor the two-dimensional image alone supplies
that missing bound. The source expressly warns that unrestricted parametric
assignment examples do not automatically retain the geometric rank-two structure.

\section{Adversarial audit and computation}
Exact enumeration of all six assignments in the displayed example gave
scores $28,-7,14,58,-86,-7$ in lexicographic permutation order. The saved
Python program and JSON verify the strict two-swap trap independently of
any floating-point calculation. The general proof is the quadratic-form
identity above. The audit distinguished a collinear rank-one restriction,
a genuine rank-two counterexample to one method, fixed-angle optimization,
and parametric enumeration. No faster general algorithm or hardness result
