\section{A specified model within the broad source direction}
The source paragraph explicitly motivates stochastic gap analysis,
after explaining why unqualified worst-case rounding is unreliable.
The catalogue fragment is a general direction, not one formal
complexity conjecture. We analyze a fixed feasible family
$\mathcal F\subseteq\{0,1\}^n$ with linear objective and
then contrast real-valued constraints. The following complete bounds
do not purport to cover every optimization family in the question.

\paragraph{Deterministic objective stability.}
Suppose $|c_i-\widehat c_i|\le\delta$ and let $x^*$
and $\widehat x$ minimize $c\cdot x$ and
$\widehat c\cdot x$ over the same nonempty family. Every
feasible vector has at most $n$ nonzero coordinates, so
$|(c-\widehat c)\cdot x|\le n\delta$. Consequently
\[
c\cdot\widehat x
\le\widehat c\cdot\widehat x+n\delta
\le\widehat c\cdot x^*+n\delta
\le c\cdot x^*+2n\delta.
\]
If the unique optimum has a gap greater than $2n\delta$
to every other solution, the rounded objective has the same unique
optimum: any competing vector's difference loses at most $2n\delta$
and remains positive. This controls rounding accuracy, not the time
needed to find either optimum.

\paragraph{A complete stochastic isolation estimate.}
Assume the coordinates $c_i$ are independent real random variables,
each with a density bounded by $\phi$, and the finite feasible
family is fixed independently of them. For each $i$, conditional
on all other coefficients, let $a_i$ be the minimum of
$\sum_{j\ne i}c_jx_j$ over feasible $x$ with $x_i=0$,
and let $b_i$ be the analogous minimum with $x_i=1$.
If either group is empty omit that coordinate. The two best group
values are $a_i$ and $b_i+c_i$, with threshold
$t_i=a_i-b_i$ depending only on the other coefficients.

If two distinct best and second-best feasible vectors have objective
gap at most $\eta$, choose a coordinate where they differ.
The best value in each of its two groups lies between the global
best value and the second-best value: the group of the global
best attains that best value, and the other group has a candidate
with value at most the second-best one. Therefore
$|c_i-t_i|\le\eta$. Conditional independence and the density
bound give probability at most $2\phi\eta$ for this event.
The union bound over $n$ coordinates proves
\[
\Pr(\hbox{best-to-second-best gap}\le\eta)
\le2n\phi\eta.
\]
Ties are included by this argument; taking $\eta\downarrow0$
shows they have probability zero. If the family has one vector,
the gap is interpreted as infinity and the claim is immediate.

Combining this estimate with objective stability gives probability
at most $4n^2\phi\delta$ that rounding with error at most
$\delta$ changes the unique optimum. For a desired failure
probability $\beta>0$, error
$\delta\le\beta/(4n^2\phi)$ suffices. With coefficients
in a bounded fixed interval, this asks for
$O(\log(n\phi/\beta)+1)$ binary precision bits, up to
the evident nonnegative cutoff when that expression is small.
This is a concrete logarithmic-precision mechanism in the source's
direction. It does not prove polynomial optimization time, a gap
certificate computable without solving the problem, or analogous
bounds for random constraint coefficients.

\paragraph{Why exact constraints need a different promise.}
Consider minimizing $-x$ for $x\in\{0,1\}$ subject to
$\theta x\le0$. At $\theta=0$, the unique optimum is
$x=1$; at any $\theta>0$, the only feasible point is zero.
In a bounded-error real oracle model, a query of precision $b$
returns any rational within $2^{-b}$ of $\theta$. Run a
purported universally correct terminating exact algorithm on zero,
with every answer zero. It must eventually output one. Let $B$
be the largest precision queried along this finite run, or zero
if no query was made. Choose
$0<\theta<2^{-B}$. The same all-zero answers are valid at
every query, hence the adaptive transcript and output are identical,
but the output is infeasible. Thus no such algorithm can guarantee
termination and exact correctness for all real $\theta$ in this
oracle model without additional information. Interval promises, an
allowed feasibility error or a separation margin change the question
and can escape this obstruction.
