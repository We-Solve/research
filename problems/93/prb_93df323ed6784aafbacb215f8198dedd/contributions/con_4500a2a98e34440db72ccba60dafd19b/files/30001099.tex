\section{Explicit instance and complete proof}
Take vertices $s,u,v,w,t_1,t_2,t_3$ and exactly the following nine directed
arcs. The rows give cost per unit, the fractional load to be constructed,
and our capacity, respectively:
\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrrrrr}
e&st_1&st_2&su&uv&ut_3&vt_1&vw&wt_2&wt_3\\\hline
c_e&2&3&0&0&2&0&0&0&0\\
x_e&10&6&24&14&10&5&9&4&5\\
u_e&15&15&24&15&15&15&15&15&15
\end{array}
\]
The demands at $t_1,t_2,t_3$ are $(15,10,15)$, so $d_{\max}=15$.
This graph is acyclic; all arcs go forward through the displayed junction
chain or into terminal sinks. Each terminal has exactly two source paths:
\[
\begin{array}{c|l|l|c|c}
 i& P_i\text{ (paid)}&Q_i\text{ (free)}&\text{load on }P_i&\text{load on }Q_i\\\hline
1&s,t_1&s,u,v,t_1&10&5\\
2&s,t_2&s,u,v,w,t_2&6&4\\
3&s,u,t_3&s,u,v,w,t_3&10&5
\end{array}
\]
To check completeness, a path to $t_1$ must enter either from $s$ or $v$;
reaching $v$ requires $s,u,v$. A path to $t_2$ enters from $s$ or $w$;
reaching $w$ requires $s,u,v,w$. A path to $t_3$ enters from $u$ or $w$,
with those same uniquely determined prefixes. Terminal vertices have no
outgoing edges, so no other route or detour is possible.

The path amounts in each row sum to its demand. Summing the amounts through
arcs gives the stated vector $x$: in particular $x_{su}=5+4+10+5=24$,
$x_{uv}=5+4+5=14$, and $x_{vw}=4+5=9$; the remaining six arcs have their
individual route amounts. All loads are nonnegative and at most the listed
capacities. Also every capacity is at least 15, verifying the no-bottleneck
condition on every edge, including the edges carrying small fractional loads.
The cost is
\[
c(x)=2\cdot10+3\cdot6+2\cdot10=58.
\]

Any unsplittable routing chooses either $P_i$ or $Q_i$ for each $i$.
Routing a full demand on any $P_i$ costs exactly 30: the three products are
$15\cdot2$, $10\cdot3$, and $15\cdot2$. Each $Q_i$ costs zero.
Suppose a routing satisfies $y_e\le x_e+15$ on all arcs.
It cannot choose $Q_1,Q_2$ simultaneously: those two demands together
with demand 3 (whose two routes both use $su$) give
$y_{su}=15+10+15=40>24+15=39$.
It cannot choose $Q_1,Q_3$ simultaneously, because then
$y_{uv}\ge15+15=30>14+15=29$.
It cannot choose $Q_2,Q_3$ simultaneously, because then
$y_{vw}=10+15=25>9+15=24$.
Thus at most one of the three demands can use a free route, and at least
two must use paid routes. Every routing satisfying all the additive bounds
therefore has
\[
c(y)\ge60>58=c(x).
\]
This contradicts cost preservation and proves that the requested $y$ does
not exist. The proof covers non-strict congestion inequalities exactly as
printed, and would also rule out a stricter inequality or an additional
requirement $y\le u$.

\paragraph{Why the capacity adjustment preserves the counterexample.}
The AFP's displayed capacities equal its fractional loads, some below 15.
Our capacities are $u_e=\max\{x_e,15\}$. Changing them introduces no new
arc or path, leaves the feasible fractional flow and its cost unchanged,
and meets $d_{\max}\le u_e$. The required target bound still uses the
original $x_e+15$, not $u_e+15$. Consequently the same three strict
violations above apply. Replacing the target by $u_e+15$ at this step
would be an invalid weakening; no such replacement is made.

\section{Attempted rounding route and adversarial audit}
Independently choosing the two paths according to the displayed fractional
proportions preserves expected edge loads and expected cost 58, but may
violate the additive bounds. The complete inequality above proves a stronger
obstruction: no probability distribution supported entirely on bound-feasible
unsplittable routings can have expected cost 58, since every such routing
costs at least 60. Thus trying to preserve those expectations by a correlated
rounding supported only on admissible routings cannot work on this instance.
This explains exactly where an expectation-based proof of the old conjecture
must fail, rather than merely reporting an unsuccessful rounding experiment.

An exact Python checker reconstructs every source-to-terminal path directly
from the nine-arc graph, verifies that there are exactly two per terminal,
computes all fractional loads and the enlarged capacities, and enumerates
all $2^3=8$ unsplittable routings. Their minimum cost under the additive
bounds is 60. The saved program and JSON use integer arithmetic only.
