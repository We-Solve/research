\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
The supplied record asks whether the following estimate holds for every graph \(H\). The graph and Ramsey-number conventions are not explicitly defined in the record; the precise formulation below is conditional on their usual interpretation.

Let \(H\) be a finite simple undirected graph with \(n=|V(H)|\geq 1\). No connectedness or bipartiteness assumption is imposed, and isolated vertices are allowed. Define its degeneracy by
\[
 d(H)=\max_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq V(H)}\delta(H[S]),
\]
where \(H[S]\) is the subgraph induced by \(S\), and \(\delta\) denotes minimum degree. Interpret \(r(H)\) as the diagonal two-colour Ramsey number: the least positive integer \(N\) such that every red--blue colouring of the edges of the complete graph \(K_N\) contains a monochromatic copy of \(H\) as a subgraph, without requiring that copy to be induced. Fix logarithms to base \(2\).

\textbf{Question.} Do there exist absolute constants \(c,C>0\), independent of \(n\) and \(H\), such that every such graph satisfies
\[
 c\bigl(d(H)+\log_2 n\bigr)
 \leq \log_2 r(H)
 \leq C\bigl(d(H)+\log_2 n\bigr)?
\]
This expresses the supplied question
\(\log r(H)=\Theta(d(H)+\log n)\)
with uniform constants. Any other fixed logarithm base greater than \(1\) gives an equivalent formulation after changing the constants. For \(n=1\), these conventions give \(d(H)=0\) and \(r(H)=1\), so both sides of the comparison vanish. The empty graph on zero vertices is excluded because \(\log n\) is undefined; the source does not state this boundary restriction explicitly.
