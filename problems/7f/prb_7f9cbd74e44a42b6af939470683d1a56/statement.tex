\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
The question has two parts: characterize conformally compact and asymptotically hyperbolic Riemannian manifolds intrinsically, and determine whether the intended hyperbolic rigidity theorem extends to that intrinsic category.

For comparison, the usual compactification-based definition starts with $(M,g)$ as the interior of a compact manifold $\overline M$ with boundary and a defining function $\rho$ satisfying
\[
 \rho>0\text{ on }M,\qquad \rho=0\text{ on }\partial\overline M,
 \qquad d\rho\ne0\text{ on }\partial\overline M.
\]
Conformal compactness requires $\overline g=\rho^2g$ to extend to a positive-definite metric of a specified regularity on $\overline M$. In the usual curvature-$-1$ normalization, asymptotic hyperbolicity adds
\[
 |d\rho|_{\overline g}=1\quad\text{on }\partial\overline M;
\]
with sufficient regularity this corresponds to sectional curvatures tending to $-1$ near infinity. The regularity of the compactification and decay rates are material, and this definition is not simply a topological property of the bare manifold.

The intrinsic request is for conditions phrased using the complete metric and its geometry, without presupposing such a boundary and defining function, which recover the intended category and regularity. Merely observing that existence of a compactification is invariant under isometry does not supply the requested characterization. The source does not specify allowable intrinsic data, curvature decay, injectivity bounds, dimension or equivalence at infinity.

To assert that a member of the intrinsic category is isometric to hyperbolic space, one must also retain that theorem's curvature/Einstein, conformal-boundary, topology, completeness and other hypotheses; the record supplies none of their exact forms. Asymptotic hyperbolicity alone is not stated to force global hyperbolicity. Thus no unrestricted rigidity theorem or particular missing hypothesis is invented.
