\textbf{Question.} If \(L_0\) is an oriented Lagrangian torus in a quartic K3 surface and its Maslov class \(\mu_{L_0}\) vanishes, must its homology class \([L_0]\) be nonzero? In the usual projective interpretation, let \(X\subset\PP^3(\C)\) be a smooth quartic surface, equip it with the restriction \(\omega\) of the Fubini--Study form, and let \(\iota:L_0\cong\R^2/\Z^2\hookrightarrow X\) be an oriented smooth embedding with \(\iota^*\omega=0\). The conclusion is\[\mu_{L_0}=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\iota_*[L_0]\ne0\ \text{in }H_2(X;\Z).\] This specifies a conventional symplectic and integral-homology interpretation; the short source statement does not explicitly fix either convention or say whether other symplectic forms are intended.\par A K3 surface has a nowhere vanishing holomorphic two-form \(\Omega\). For an oriented Lagrangian tangent plane, the complex argument of \(\Omega\) evaluated on a positively oriented real basis defines a phase map \(\alpha:L_0\to S^1\), independent of the basis after normalization. With the squared-phase convention, its Maslov class is \(\mu_{L_0}=(\alpha^2)^*\eta\in H^1(L_0;\Z)\), where \(\eta\) generates \(H^1(S^1;\Z)\). A phase-winding convention changes the factor of two but not the vanishing condition here.
