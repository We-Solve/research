\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For each integer \(k\geq 2\), let \(R(3,k)\) be the least positive integer \(n\) such that every finite simple undirected graph on \(n\) vertices contains either a triangle or an independent set of size \(k\). A triangle consists of three pairwise adjacent vertices, and an independent set consists of pairwise nonadjacent vertices. Interpret \(\log\) as the natural logarithm, following the standard convention for this asymptotic statement; the supplied record does not explicitly specify the logarithm base.

\textbf{Conjecture (Conjecture 2 in the supplied excerpt).} As \(k\to\infty\) through the integers,
\[
R(3,k)=\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)\frac{k^2}{\log k}.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\lim_{\substack{k\to\infty\\k\in\N}}\frac{R(3,k)\log k}{k^2}=\frac12.
\]
Here \(o(1)\) denotes a real quantity tending to zero as \(k\to\infty\).
