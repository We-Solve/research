\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $\Gamma$ be either $\operatorname{Out}(F_n)$ or the extended mapping class group $\operatorname{Mod}^{\pm}(S_g)$. Question 6.2 asks, separately for these group families, whether $\Gamma$ is the colimit (called a pushout in the source) of a finite subsystem of its finite subgroups.

In a subgroup-diagram formulation, seek finitely many finite subgroups $G_i\leq\Gamma$ and compatible source-prescribed inclusion maps such that the natural map
\[
 \varinjlim_i G_i\longrightarrow\Gamma
\]
is an isomorphism. The finite subsystem refers to finitely many local groups and maps, not to a claim that $\Gamma$ is finite.

The source equivalently asks whether $\Gamma$ is the fundamental group of a developable simple complex of finite groups over a $1$-connected base. In the usual terminology, local groups are finite, face maps are injective and coherent, simple means there is no nontrivial twisting data, and developable means that the complex is realized by the local stabilizers of an action on its simply connected development. The base must be simply connected, not merely connected; finiteness of the subsystem corresponds to a finite complex/diagram. The exact complex-of-groups convention is not restated in the excerpt.

No bound on dimension or local group orders, contractibility or CAT$(0)$ geometry of the development, or a uniform construction in rank/genus is specified. The request must not be reduced to a graph of groups or a single binary amalgamated product: a higher-dimensional finite diagram is allowed. Rank, genus and surface conventions are absent.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can either free-group outer automorphism groups or extended mapping class groups be reconstructed as finite colimits of finite subgroups, in the stated developable simple-complex-of-groups form over a simply connected base?
