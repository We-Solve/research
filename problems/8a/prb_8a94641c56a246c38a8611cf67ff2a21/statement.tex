\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 11000302}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(F_n\) be the free group on \(n\) generators and \(G_n=\operatorname{Aut}(F_n)\). A finitely generated group \(G\) is automatic if it has a finite symmetric generating alphabet \(A\), a regular language \(L\subseteq A^*\) mapping onto \(G\), and a constant \(C\) with the synchronous fellow-traveler property: whenever \(u,v\in L\) and \(a\in A\cup\{1\}\) satisfy \(\overline u\,a=\overline v\), the paths labelled by \(u,v\), starting at the identity in the Cayley graph, remain within distance \(C\) at equal integer times, padding a completed path at its endpoint. Uniqueness of representatives is not required.

The question is
\[\forall n\in\Z,\quad n\geq4\Longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(F_n)\text{ admits such an automatic structure}.\]
The structure and constant may depend on \(n\); no uniform construction or fixed generating set is demanded. This is automaticity of the automorphism group itself, not of \(\operatorname{Out}(F_n)\), and not the stronger biautomaticity condition.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is \(\operatorname{Aut}(F_n)\) automatic for every rank \(n\geq4\), in the usual synchronous regular-language sense?
