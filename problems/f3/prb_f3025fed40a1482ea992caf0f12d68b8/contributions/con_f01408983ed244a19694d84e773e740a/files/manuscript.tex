\paragraph{Finite-volume normalization after pinning.} On a connected finite graph with root $o$, put spins in hyperbolic space $\mathbb H^m$, fix $s_o$, and use energy $\beta\sum_{xy}(\cosh d(s_x,s_y)-1)$ with $\beta>0$. Let $T$ be a spanning tree. Dropping nonnegative non-tree terms increases the Boltzmann weight. By isometry invariance the one-edge integral
\[
 c_{\beta,m}=\int_{\mathbb H^m}e^{-\beta(\cosh d(s_o,s)-1)}ds
\]
is independent of the parent spin and finite: in polar coordinates its tail is bounded by a constant times $e^{(m-1)r-\beta e^r/2}$. Integrating tree leaves successively gives the explicit bound $Z_{\Lambda}^{\rm pinned}\leq c_{\beta,m}^{|\Lambda|-1}$. Positivity on a compact product neighborhood also gives $Z_{\Lambda}^{\rm pinned}>0$.

\paragraph{Why this does not give a thermodynamic limit.} Without pinning, the integral over the root is an infinite target-volume factor even for a tree. With pinning, the displayed upper bound grows with volume and does not control tails of a spin far from the root. To extract a probability limit on local spin variables one needs uniform tightness: for each fixed site $x$, the probabilities of $d(s_x,s_o)>R$ must become uniformly small as $R\to\infty$. The tree calculation supplies no such bound because path lengths grow and the partition-function lower bound can deteriorate.

\paragraph{Scope.} This applies in every spatial dimension at finite volume and explains an elementary normalization mechanism. It neither proves higher-dimensional convergence nor uniqueness or independence of boundary conditions; the source explicitly allows boundary dependence even for invariant observables. Uniform control of the chosen observables after symmetry fixing is the first remaining gap.
