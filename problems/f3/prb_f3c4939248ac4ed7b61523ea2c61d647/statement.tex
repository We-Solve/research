\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A principal ideal domain is a commutative integral domain with identity in which every ideal is principal. Let \(R\) be a principal ideal domain and let \(d\geq 1\) be an integer. Write
\[
\mathrm{GL}(d,R)=\{X\in M_d(R):X\text{ is invertible over }R\},\qquad
\mathrm{SL}(d,R)=\{X\in\mathrm{GL}(d,R):\det X=1\}.
\]
Let \(X_1,\ldots,X_m\) be a finite list of matrices, all belonging to \(\mathrm{SL}(d,R)\), or, in the alternative formulation, all belonging to \(\mathrm{GL}(d,R)\). Let \(F_m\) be the free group on formal generators \(a_1,\ldots,a_m\), and let
\[
\varphi:F_m\longrightarrow\mathrm{GL}(d,R),\qquad \varphi(a_i)=X_i,
\]
be the evaluation homomorphism. The matrices freely generate a free group precisely when \(\varphi\) is injective. Equivalently, for every nonempty freely reduced word
\[
w=a_{i_1}^{\varepsilon_1}\cdots a_{i_\ell}^{\varepsilon_\ell},\qquad
\ell\geq1,\quad i_j\in\{1,\ldots,m\},\quad \varepsilon_j\in\{-1,1\},
\]
one has
\[
X_{i_1}^{\varepsilon_1}\cdots X_{i_\ell}^{\varepsilon_\ell}\ne I_d.
\]
Here freely reduced means that no adjacent pair of letters is mutually inverse.

\textbf{Question (P. M. Neumann, Kourovka Notebook 19.60).} Is it decidable, from the given matrices, whether this injectivity condition holds? If so, give an effective algorithm that terminates on every admissible input and answers correctly. The question is posed for both the special linear and general linear alternatives.

\subsection{Short English statement}
Given finitely many matrices over a principal ideal domain, decide whether every nontrivial reduced word in the matrices and their inverses differs from the identity, and provide an algorithm if this is decidable. The source asks this for both \(\mathrm{SL}(d,R)\) and \(\mathrm{GL}(d,R)\). Its computational model and intended uniformity remain unspecified.
