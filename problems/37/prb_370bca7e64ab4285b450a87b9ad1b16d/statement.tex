\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 700950}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(G\) be a group with identity \(e\). A partial order \(\preccurlyeq\) on \(G\) is called \emph{semilinear} in the supplied source if it satisfies the following conditions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item It is directed upwards: for every \(x,y\in G\), there exists \(z\in G\) such that \(x\preccurlyeq z\) and \(y\preccurlyeq z\).
\item Its positive cone \(P=\{g\in G:e\preccurlyeq g\}\) is linearly ordered by the restriction of \(\preccurlyeq\): for every \(p,q\in P\), either \(p\preccurlyeq q\) or \(q\preccurlyeq p\).
\item It is invariant under right multiplication: for every \(x,y,h\in G\), if \(x\preccurlyeq y\), then \(xh\preccurlyeq yh\).
\end{enumerate}
Here the positive cone includes the identity; excluding the identity gives the same chain condition.

A \emph{right order} on \(G\) is a total order invariant under right multiplication. The problem asks whether, for every group \(G\) and every semilinear partial order \(\preccurlyeq\) on \(G\), there exists a right order \(\leq\) on \(G\) extending \(\preccurlyeq\), that is,
\[
\forall x,y\in G,\qquad x\preccurlyeq y\ \Longrightarrow\ x\leq y.
\]
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every upward-directed, right-invariant partial order on a group whose positive cone is a chain extend to a right-invariant total order on that group? The supplied source describes this as a well-known problem and attributes it to V. M. Kopytov.
