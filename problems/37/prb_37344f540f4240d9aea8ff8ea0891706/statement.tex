Over any field $k$, consider
\[
 J=(x^2-ty,xy,y^2)\subset k[t,x,y].
\]
The quotient is free over $k[t]$ with basis $1,x,y$, so it defines a flat length-three family and an irreducible curve in $\operatorname{Hilb}^3(\A^2_k)$. Restrict weights to the positive orthant $w_x,w_y>0$ and use highest-weight initial forms. At $t=0$, the fiber ideal is $(x,y)^2$, whose restricted fan has one maximal cone. At every nonzero fiber $t=c$, the aid reports exactly two maximal cones:
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c}
 2w_x>w_y&\operatorname{in}_w(J_c)=(x,y)^2\\
 2w_x<w_y&\operatorname{in}_w(J_c)=(y,x^3).
 \end{array}
\]
Their common wall is $w_y=2w_x$. The special local algebra has embedding dimension two, whereas for $c\neq0$ it is $k[x]/(x^3)$, of embedding dimension one; the endpoint algebras are not isomorphic. Thus even the number of maximal restricted cones changes within this single irreducible family, in every characteristic. The raw restricted fan is not constant on a Hilbert-scheme component.

\subsection{Short English statement}
A flat family of three points already has a Gr\"obner fan that changes from one maximal positive-weight cone to two. This defeats pointwise constancy inside a component. The supplied generic-constancy result leaves a different question: whether suitably double-generic fans identify components.
