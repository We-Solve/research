\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
The original question asks for transport coefficients or related physical quantities for various billiard systems; it supplies no particular table or observable. The aid instead specifies an equilibrium renewal-flight surrogate. Its predictions for a billiard require a separately justified approximation.

Let \(\alpha>1\). Take independent identically distributed flight durations \(\tau_j\) with survival function
\[
 \Pr(\tau_j>t)=(1+t)^{-\alpha}\quad(t\geq0),\qquad
 \E\tau_j=\frac1{\alpha-1}.
\]
Use the stationary equilibrium renewal process on the time line, rather than starting at a renewal at time \(0\). During each flight use a fresh independent velocity with a symmetric law, speed \(v\), mean zero and covariance \(Q=\E[V V^{\mathsf T}]\). Velocities and durations are independent. Write \(V(t)\) for the stationary velocity and \(X(T)=\int_0^T V(t)\,\dd t\). This covariance convention absorbs the squared speed into \(Q\).

The aid reports the exact velocity covariance and displacement second-moment matrix
\[
 K(t)=\E[V(t)V(0)^{\mathsf T}]=Q(1+t)^{1-\alpha},\qquad
 \Sigma(T)=\E[X(T)X(T)^{\mathsf T}]
 =2Q\int_0^T(T-t)(1+t)^{1-\alpha}\,\dd t.
\]
Define the finite-time Einstein and truncated Green--Kubo matrices by
\[
 E_T=\frac{\Sigma(T)}{2T},\qquad
 G_T=\int_0^T K(t)\,\dd t.
\]
For \(\alpha>2\), the reported common finite limit is
\[
 \lim_{T\to\infty}E_T=\lim_{T\to\infty}G_T=\frac{Q}{\alpha-2}.
\]
For nonzero \(Q\), this finite diffusive transport limit occurs exactly when \(\alpha>2\); zero-variance directions are degenerate exceptions. At the threshold and below it, the aid gives
\[
 \Sigma(T)\sim2QT\log T\quad(\alpha=2),\qquad
 \Sigma(T)\sim\frac{2Q}{(2-\alpha)(3-\alpha)}T^{3-\alpha}
 \quad(1<\alpha<2),
\]
where the scalar asymptotics are interpreted on directions with positive \(Q\)-variance. In particular, for such a vector \(u\),
\[
 \frac{u^{\mathsf T}E_Tu}{u^{\mathsf T}G_Tu}\longrightarrow\frac1{3-\alpha}
 \quad(1<\alpha<2).
\]
At \(\alpha=2\), the exact expressions are
\[
 G_T=Q\log(1+T),\qquad
 E_T=Q\left[\left(1+\frac1T\right)\log(1+T)-1\right],
 \qquad G_T-E_T\longrightarrow Q.
\]
Thus the sign in the matrix difference is fixed by these definitions.

The aid also reports stable weak limits on the \(T^{1/\alpha}\) scale for \(1<\alpha<2\), a critical Gaussian limit
\[
 \frac{X(T)}{\sqrt{T\log T}}\ \Longrightarrow\ N(0,Q)\quad(\alpha=2),
\]
and the classical Gaussian limit \(X(T)/\sqrt T\Longrightarrow N(0,2Q/(\alpha-2))\) for \(\alpha>2\). The stable law depends on the full directional distribution, which is not specified merely by \(Q\). At the critical exponent,
\[
 \frac{\Sigma(T)}{T\log T}\longrightarrow2Q,
\]
so the weak Gaussian covariance \(Q\) is half the limiting normalized second-moment matrix. Weak convergence alone does not justify convergence of transport moments.

The aid's transfer diagnostic distinguishes two requirements. A coupled billiard displacement \(Y(T)\) shares a weak limit of \(X(T)/b_T\) if \((Y(T)-X(T))/b_T\to0\) in probability. Transferring second-moment transport asymptotics requires corresponding \(L^2\) control, together with control of the reference moments; for example \(\|Y(T)-X(T)\|_2=o(c_T)\) and \(\|X(T)\|_2=O(c_T)\) suffice for equality of normalized second moments on scale \(c_T^2\). No such approximation for a specified billiard is supplied.
\subsection{Short English statement}
An equilibrium Pareto renewal-flight model gives exact finite-time transport matrices and three regimes: finite diffusion for \(\alpha>2\), logarithmic enhancement at \(2\), and stronger anomalous second-moment growth below \(2\). Its critical weak Gaussian covariance differs from the normalized second-moment limit, so billiard approximations must separately justify weak limits and transport coefficients.
