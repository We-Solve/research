\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(n\geq 1\), and let \(U_n\) denote the uniform probability distribution on \(\{0,1\}^n\). For \(S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\), define the parity character
\[
\chi_S(x)=(-1)^{\sum_{i\in S}x_i}.
\]
Use the normalized-density convention: a density is a function \(\varphi:\{0,1\}^n\to[0,\infty)\) with \(\mathbb E_{U_n}[\varphi]=1\), inducing the probability distribution \(\mu_\varphi(x)=2^{-n}\varphi(x)\). It is \(\varepsilon\)-biased if
\[
\left|\mathbb E_{U_n}[\varphi\chi_S]\right|\leq\varepsilon
\qquad\text{for every nonempty }S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}.
\]
A DNF is a disjunction of terms, each term being a conjunction of literals \(x_i\) or \(\neg x_i\). In the formulation below, size at most \(s\) means at most \(s\) terms. Encode truth by \(+1\) and falsity by \(-1\), obtaining \(f:\{0,1\}^n\to\{-1,1\}\). A distribution \(\mu\) \(\delta\)-fools \(f\) if
\[
\left|\mathbb E_{U_n}[f]-\mathbb E_{y\sim\mu}[f(y)]\right|\leq\delta.
\]
\textbf{Question, under these conventions.} Is it true that
\[
\begin{gathered}
\forall\delta>0\ \exists C_\delta>0\ \forall n\geq1\ \forall s\in\Z,\ s\geq2,\\
\forall f:\{0,1\}^n\to\{-1,1\}\text{ computable by a DNF with at most }s\text{ terms},\\
\forall\varphi\text{ a normalized density},\\
\left[\ \forall\varnothing\ne S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\},\quad
\left|\mathbb E_{U_n}[\varphi\chi_S]\right|\leq s^{-C_\delta}\ \right]
\ \Longrightarrow\ 
\left|\mathbb E_{U_n}[f]-\mathbb E_{U_n}[\varphi f]\right|\leq\delta?
\end{gathered}
\]
Here \(C_\delta\) is independent of \(n,s,f,\varphi\). The restriction \(s\geq2\) makes the source's asymptotic notation \(s^{-O(1)}\) precise without forcing bias threshold \(1\) at \(s=1\); one-term DNFs are included among DNFs with at most two terms.

\subsection{Short English statement}
For each fixed error tolerance \(\delta>0\), does sufficiently small polynomial-in-\(s\) parity bias guarantee that a distribution fools every DNF with at most \(s\) terms, uniformly over the number of variables? The polynomial exponent may depend on \(\delta\). This draft uses explicit standard conventions and flags the source's domain discrepancy.
