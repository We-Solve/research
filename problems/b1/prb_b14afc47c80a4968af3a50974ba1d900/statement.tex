\section{Perfect Cuboid}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A rectangular cuboid has three mutually perpendicular edges of positive lengths $a,b,c$. A perfect cuboid requires these edge lengths, its three face diagonal lengths $d_{ab},d_{ac},d_{bc}$, and its space diagonal length $D$ all to be positive integers.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Do there exist $a,b,c,d_{ab},d_{ac},d_{bc},D\in\mathbb N$ satisfying simultaneously
\[
 a^2+b^2=d_{ab}^2,\qquad a^2+c^2=d_{ac}^2,\qquad b^2+c^2=d_{bc}^2,
 \qquad a^2+b^2+c^2=D^2?
\]
All three edges must be nonzero; degenerate planar boxes are excluded. No primitive or ordering condition is imposed. \sref{statement}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does any genuine rectangular box have integer lengths for all three edges, all three face diagonals and its space diagonal?
