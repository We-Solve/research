\section{Nonuniqueness of Distinct-Distance Minimizers}\label{statement}


\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Unless a section says otherwise, $\N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$,
$\Z$ is the ring of integers, $\Q,\R,\C$ are the rational, real, and complex
fields, $[N]=\{1,\ldots,\lfloor N\rfloor\}$, and $\log$ is the natural
logarithm. For real functions of a parameter tending to infinity,
$f\ll g$ and $f=O(g)$ mean $|f|\leq Cg$ eventually for some fixed $C$;
$f\gg g$ reverses this comparison; $f=o(g)$ means $f/g\to0$;
$f\sim g$ means $f/g\to1$; and $f\asymp g$ means both order bounds.
A subscript on $O$ or $\ll$ specifies allowed constant dependence.
The expression $n^{o(1)}$ in an upper bound means growth smaller than
$n^\epsilon$ for each fixed $\epsilon>0$. Local definitions govern any
exception, finite-field convention, or use of the same symbol for another
object. An asymptotic claim is not automatically an effective algorithm.



A similarity is a map $x\mapsto\lambda Ux+b$, with $\lambda>0$ and $U$ orthogonal. The minimization counts distinct positive distance values, not distance pairs. Optimal sets are compared modulo similarities, so translating or rescaling one optimum does not count as a new example.

Distances are Euclidean. Distinct distances mean distinct numerical values; when a distance is said to occur a certain number of times, unordered pairs of distinct points are counted. Congruence allows any Euclidean isometry, including reflection. \sref{statement}{1} \sref{statement}{2}

\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Let $n$ be a sufficently large integer. Suppose $A\subset \mathbb{R}^2$ has $\lvert A\rvert=n$ and minimises the number of distinct distances between points in $A$. Prove that there are at least two (and probably many) such $A$ which are non-similar. \sref{statement}{1}

\subsection{Short English statement}

For every sufficiently large number of planar points, must at least two configurations not related by a similarity attain the smallest possible number of distances?
