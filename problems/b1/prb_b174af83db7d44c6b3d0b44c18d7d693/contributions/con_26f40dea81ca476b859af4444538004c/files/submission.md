### Write-up

# Faithful complex representations for punctured spheres and the closed genus-two surface

**Problem:** TOP-2751

**Claim type:** Literature report

**Exact scope:** The full mapping class group of every n-punctured sphere (n≥4) is complex linear, and the closed genus-two group has a faithful 64-dimensional representation, by Bigelow–Budney’s construction.

## The proved surface regimes

Bigelow and Budney construct faithful complex representations of the full group $\operatorname{Mod}(S_{0,n})$ for each $n\ge4$, allowing arbitrary permutations of the punctures, and of $\operatorname{Mod}(\Sigma_2)$ for the closed oriented genus-two surface. Their sphere representation has dimension

$$n\binom{n-1}{2},$$

and their genus-two representation has dimension64. These give ordinary finite-dimensional linearity in precisely the sense of the prompt. The dimension is allowed to depend on the surface.

The two regimes must be distinguished from punctured or bounded genus-two surfaces: no arbitrary-puncture genus-two extension is claimed here. Likewise, the closed genus-two theorem is not a theorem about the full group in every higher genus.

## Remove the braid center correctly

The stabilizer of one puncture in $\operatorname{Mod}(S_{0,n})$ is the quotient of $B_{n-1}$ by its infinite cyclic center, generated by the full twist. A faithful representation of $B_{n-1}$ does not automatically descend to this quotient. That center must act trivially in the modified representation, while no additional kernel may appear.

The Lawrence–Krammer representation, after the parameter choice in the source, is faithful and sends the full twist to a scalar matrix. Multiply every standard generator matrix by the same nonzero scalar whose appropriate power cancels this central scalar. The braid relations are homogeneous in their generator exponents, so this still defines a representation. The full twist now acts as the identity.

If another braid becomes the identity after this rescaling, its original matrix was scalar. Since that original representation is faithful, commuting with every generator matrix forces the braid to commute with every generator. It is therefore central. Thus the kernel is exactly the center, and the quotient representation is faithful. This explains the indispensable kernel check rather than treating any quotient of a linear group as automatically linear.

## Recover the full puncture-permuting group

The point stabilizer has index $n$. Inducing its faithful representation to the full mapping class group gives a representation of dimension $n$ times the stabilizer dimension. It is faithful: an element acting trivially on the induced space fixes the block labelled by the stabilizer coset, hence lies in the stabilizer, and on that block its action is the original faithful representation. Thus it is the identity. This retains the puncture-permutation convention in the question.

## Detect the genus-two central involution

The hyperelliptic double cover of the sphere gives the exact sequence

$$1\longrightarrow\langle\iota\rangle\longrightarrow\operatorname{Mod}(\Sigma_2)
\longrightarrow\operatorname{Mod}(S_{0,6})\longrightarrow1.$$

Pull back the faithful60-dimensional sphere representation. Its only kernel is the order-two subgroup generated by $\iota$. The ordinary action on $H_1(\Sigma_2;\mathbb C)$ has dimension4 and sends the hyperelliptic involution to $-I$. Taking the direct sum kills the remaining kernel and gives a faithful64-dimensional representation. The homology representation alone has a Torelli kernel; it is used only to distinguish that last central involution.

These are the structural ingredients of the checked primary construction. The faithfulness of Lawrence–Krammer and the Birman–Hilden exact sequence are substantial credited inputs. The present report does not prove them from scratch or infer faithfulness from testing finitely many words.
