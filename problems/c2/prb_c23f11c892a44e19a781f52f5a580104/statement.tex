\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A finite set \(S\subset\mathbb R^2\) is in general position if no three of its points are collinear. A subset \(T\subseteq S\) of size \(n\ge3\) is in convex position if every point of \(T\) is a vertex of its convex hull. Thus \(\operatorname{conv}(T)\) is a convex \(n\)-gon. Define the Erd\H{o}s--Szekeres number
\[
 f(n)=\min\{N\ge n:\text{every general-position }S\subset\mathbb R^2
 \text{ with }|S|\ge N\text{ has such a subset }T\}.
\]
Determine \(f(n)\) exactly for every integer \(n\ge3\).

Call such a polygon empty relative to \(S\) if
\[
 \operatorname{int}(\operatorname{conv}(T))\cap S=\varnothing.
\]
For the natural threshold version of the second question, define
\[
 h(n)=\min\{N\ge n:\text{every general-position }S\subset\mathbb R^2
 \text{ with }|S|\ge N\text{ contains an empty convex }n\text{-gon}\},
\]
with \(h(n)=\infty\) if there is no such threshold. Determine sharp bounds for these empty-polygon thresholds where finite. The source asks more broadly for ``sharp bounds for empty convex polygons'' without naming a parameter; \(h(n)\) is an explicit threshold interpretation, not a claim that other extremal formulations were excluded.

The supplied research summary reports
\[
 2^{n-2}+1\le f(n)\le 2^{n+o(n)}\qquad(n\to\infty),
\]
with exact values known only for small \(n\). It also reports the former empty-hexagon existence question resolved, meaning that sufficiently large general-position sets always contain an empty hexagon, whereas empty heptagons are not forced by arbitrarily large cardinality. In the threshold notation these source reports say \(h(6)<\infty\) and \(h(7)=\infty\); they do not say that every small set has an empty hexagon or that no configuration contains an empty heptagon. No exact hexagon threshold or bibliographic proof of these updates is supplied. The original pages warn that they have not been systematically updated since 2001; the registry's later partial-progress assessment is therefore kept distinct from that historical source.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Find the exact number of planar points in general position needed to force \(n\) points in convex position, and sharp thresholds for forcing an empty convex polygon. The source reports asymptotic progress and the resolution of empty-hexagon existence, while exact thresholds and the scope of the broader empty-polygon question need review.
