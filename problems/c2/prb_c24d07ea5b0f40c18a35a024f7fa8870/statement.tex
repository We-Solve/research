\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 1101301}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Use the conventional connected finite CW-complex reading of a finite complex, and rational Betti numbers $b_i(Y)=\dim_{\mathbb Q}H_i(Y;\mathbb Q)$. A group $\pi$ is amenable if it admits a left-translation-invariant mean on its bounded real-valued functions. Finite covers are taken to be connected finite-sheeted covering spaces; allowing arbitrarily many disconnected copies would already make $b_0$ unbounded.

\textbf{Literal question (Kevin Whyte).} For every finite connected complex $K$ with amenable $\pi=\pi_1(K)$, does there exist $C_K<\infty$ such that for every connected finite-sheeted cover $p:K'\to K$ and every integer $i\geq0$,
\[
b_i(K')\leq C_K?
\]
Since $K$ has finite dimension, this is equivalent to asking for a separate uniform bound in each degree. The bound may depend on $K$ but not on the cover or its degree.

\textbf{Separate aspherical variant.} Ask the same question with the additional assumption that $K$ is a finite $K(\pi,1)$, namely that its universal cover is contractible. This assumption is not present in the literal statement.

The supplied research summary reports a negative answer to the literal question using $K=S^1\vee S^2$, whose connected degree-$m$ covers have $b_2=m$ and whose fundamental group is $\mathbb Z$. It describes the aspherical variant as the intended open problem, rather than providing evidence that asphericity was in the original wording. It also lists sublinear normalized homology and torsion-growth results as partial progress; their hypotheses and exact conclusions are not specified sufficiently to replace the requested uniform bound.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can the Betti numbers of all connected finite covers of one finite complex with amenable fundamental group be bounded independently of covering degree? The supplied counterexample addresses the literal wording; an additional finite-$K(\pi,1)$ condition yields a distinct variant described as open.
