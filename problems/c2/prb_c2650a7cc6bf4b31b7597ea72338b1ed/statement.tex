\section{Bartnik’s Stationary Vacuum Extension Conjecture}\label{30004802}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 30004802 · Mathematical Physics}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


\paragraph{Definitions and conventions.}
Initial data $(g,k)$ consist of a spatial Riemannian metric and second fundamental form; their constraint quantities are energy density $\mu$ and momentum density $J$. The dominant energy condition is $\mu\geq|J|_g$. On a boundary surface the outgoing expansion is $\theta=H_g+\operatorname{tr}_{S^2}k$ with a chosen outward-normal convention. Stationary vacuum data embed in a vacuum spacetime admitting the required stationary Killing field; asymptotic flatness fixes the exterior decay. Bartnik boundary data encode the source\textquotesingle s metric, mean-curvature and extrinsic-curvature boundary components. The primary report defines $B(g,k)=(g|_\Sigma,H_g,(k-(\operatorname{tr}_gk)g)(\nu,\cdot))$, where $\nu$ is the unit normal and $\Sigma$ is the matching boundary. Its exterior is $\mathbb R^3\setminus B^3$.

\paragraph{Target retained from the canonical source catalogue.}
\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}
Bartnik's stationary vacuum extension conjecture: let $(B^3,g_0,k_0)$ satisfy the dominant energy condition, with boundary $S^2$ whose expansion $\theta=H_{g_0}+\operatorname{tr}_{S^2}k_0$ is not everywhere nonpositive. Does there exist a unique asymptotically flat stationary vacuum initial-data set $(\mathbb{R}^3\setminus B^3,g,k)$ whose Bartnik boundary data $B(g,k)$ agree with $B(g_0,k_0)$ on $S^2$?
