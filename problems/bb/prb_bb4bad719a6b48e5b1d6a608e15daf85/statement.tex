\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For positive integers $m,n$, call a matrix $Y\in M_{m\times n}(\R)$ very well approximable (VWA) if for some $\varepsilon>0$ there are integer vectors $q\in\Z^n\setminus\{0\}$ of arbitrarily large norm and $p\in\Z^m$ such that
\[
 \|Yq-p\|_\infty<\|q\|_\infty^{-n/m-\varepsilon}.
\]
Equivalent exponent conventions rescale $\varepsilon$. Kleinbock and Margulis ask for reasonable checkable hypotheses on a smooth map $f:U\subset\R^k\to M_{m\times n}(\R)$, generalizing nondegeneracy of vector-valued maps, that ensure almost-everywhere non-VWA behavior. The local question does not define the measure meant on $f(U)$; a precise statement uses the pushforward of a specified parameter measure, rather than ambient almost-everywhere language that could be vacuous on a lower-dimensional image.

The aid's elementary sufficient criterion assumes $k\geq mn$, a $\sigma$-finite parameter measure $\mu\ll\operatorname{Leb}_k$, and
\[
 \operatorname{rank}Df(x)=mn\qquad\text{for }\mu\text{-almost every }x\in U.
\]
It reports that the coarea/submersion argument makes $f_*\mu$ absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure on the full matrix space. Ambient VWA nullity then gives $f(x)$ non-VWA for $\mu$-almost every $x$. Full target rank almost everywhere is the hypothesis; merely one regular point or a nonempty regular locus does not suffice. This criterion does not address the main lower-dimensional nondegenerate-manifold regime by itself.

The complementary reported obstruction assumes a fixed nonzero $q_0\in\Q^n$ and $b\in\Q^m$ with
\[
 f(x)q_0=b\qquad\text{for every }x\in U.
\]

Both rationality and the nonzero denominator vector are essential; a relation with arbitrary irrational $b$ is not silently treated the same way.
