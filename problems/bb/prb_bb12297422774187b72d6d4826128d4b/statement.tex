\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(n\ge2\), and let \(\mathbb B^n=\{z\in\C^n:\|z\|<1\}\). For the nonempty ball-map range take \(N\ge n\), and let \(\mathcal R_{n,N}\) be the proper holomorphic maps \(f:\mathbb B^n\to\mathbb B^N\) whose components are rational functions. Proper means that inverse images of compact sets are compact. All maps are compared in fixed source and target coordinates, without quotienting by automorphisms. For an interior holomorphic map set
\[
j^k_0 f=\bigl(\partial^\alpha f(0)\bigr)_{|\alpha|\le k},\qquad
k(n,N)=\min\{k\in\Z_{\ge0}:\ \forall f,g\in\mathcal R_{n,N},\ j^k_0 f=j^k_0g\Longrightarrow f=g\},
\]
with value \(+\infty\) if the set is empty. The first question, attributed to B. Lamel, is to determine this smallest uniform order.

The supplied research aid reports that \(k(n,N)\) is finite and satisfies
\[
\left\lfloor\frac{N-1}{n-1}\right\rfloor
\le k(n,N)\le
2\left\lfloor\frac{N(N-1)}{2(2n-3)}\right\rfloor,
\qquad
k(n,N)=1\quad(n\le N<2n-1).
\]
It describes an iterated Whitney family, with a target phase undetected through degree \(d-1\), as the lower-bound obstruction. The upper bound combines the cited D'Angelo--Lebl degree estimate with cross multiplication: rational maps represented by numerators and denominators of degrees at most \(D\), with denominators nonzero at zero, coincide if their \(2D\)-jets coincide. These are supplied partial-result claims, not a determination of the smallest order in all dimensions.

The second question concerns strongly pseudoconvex domains \(\Omega\subset\C^n\), \(\Omega'\subset\C^N\), and a proper holomorphic map \(f:\Omega\to\Omega'\) smooth up to \(\partial\Omega\): is \(f\) determined by a finite jet at a point? For the boundary-jet interpretation, fix \(p\in\partial\Omega\) and use the jets of the smooth boundary extension. The map-specific assertion is
\[
\exists k<\infty\ \ \forall g:\Omega\to\Omega'\text{ proper holomorphic and smooth up to }\partial\Omega,
\qquad j_p^k g=j_p^k f\Longrightarrow g=f.
\]
The source leaves unclear whether the point is prescribed, whether it is on the boundary, and whether \(k\) may depend on \(f,p\) or only on the domains or dimensions; these are distinct strengthenings. The aid treats this as a boundary question. It reports affirmative known regimes with real-analytic source boundary and strongly pseudoconvex Nash target boundary, meaning real-analytic algebraic data, under the applicable jet-determination theorem hypotheses. Those hypotheses and citations are not fully supplied. The report does not claim the full arbitrary-smooth positive-codimension case is settled.
\subsection{Short English statement}
