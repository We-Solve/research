\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
The source asks what additional nonmechanical probabilistic information or assumption is needed to obtain irreversible thermodynamic, macroscopic or kinetic behavior from time-reversal-invariant microscopic Newtonian or Schr\"odinger dynamics. It is a conceptual derivation question, not a specified universal convergence theorem.

For a classical formulation, let $\Phi_t$ be an invertible microscopic flow on phase space and let $\mathcal R$ be a time-reversal involution satisfying
\[
 \mathcal R\Phi_t\mathcal R=\Phi_{-t}.
\]
A quantum version uses unitary Schr\"odinger evolution and the relevant antiunitary reversal symmetry, when that symmetry is present. The source's hypothesis does not claim that every Hamiltonian has such a symmetry.

A probabilistic macroscopic derivation would additionally specify initial microscopic laws $\mu_N$, macroscopic observables $A_N$, a scaling/limit regime, and a convergence notion for distributions such as
\[
 (A_N\circ\Phi_t)_*\mu_N
\]
toward an effective kinetic or thermodynamic evolution. Initial-state restrictions, ensemble/typicality choices, decorrelation or molecular-chaos assumptions, coarse graining and limiting procedures are distinct possible inputs. The question asks which are justified and sufficient in the intended setting; none is asserted here to be a uniquely necessary or universally sufficient answer.

The target ``irreversible'' must also be fixed: noninvertibility of an effective semigroup, entropy production, a dissipative Lyapunov law or a time-asymmetric typicality statement are different claims. Effective coarse-grained entropy change must be distinguished from fine-grained entropy invariance under reversible measure-preserving or unitary dynamics where those quantities are defined. Changing the microscopic model itself to a stochastic irreversible law would require explaining how that addresses, rather than assumes, the requested emergence.

No microscopic interaction, initial ensemble, macroscopic variables/equation, size/time scaling, entropy notion, error estimate or time interval is supplied. Thus the record cannot identify one quantitative theorem or a unique extra assumption without further specification.
