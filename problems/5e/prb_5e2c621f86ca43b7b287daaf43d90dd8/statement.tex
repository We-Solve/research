\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(K\) denote the particular presentation of Kozen's axiomatization of the modal \(\mu\)-calculus intended by the supplied source, and let \(K^{-}\) denote that presentation with its cut rule deleted and its other axioms and inference rules retained. Write \(\vdash_{K^{-}}\varphi\) when \(\varphi\) has a finite derivation without premises in \(K^{-}\).

Under the conventional Kripke interpretation, a model has a nonempty set \(S\) of states, a binary accessibility relation \(R_a\subseteq S\times S\) for each modal index \(a\) in the intended language, and a valuation assigning subsets of \(S\) to proposition letters. Fixed-point variables also range over subsets of \(S\). The expressions \(\mu X.\psi\) and \(\nu X.\psi\) denote, respectively, the least and greatest fixed points of the operator on \(\mathcal P(S)\) induced by \(\psi\); the usual positivity restriction on occurrences of \(X\) ensures monotonicity. A formula closed with respect to fixed-point variables is valid if it holds at every state of every such model, for every valuation of its proposition letters.

With completeness understood as completeness for validity under this interpretation, the question is whether
\[
\forall\varphi\;\bigl(\models\varphi\ \Longrightarrow\ \vdash_{K^{-}}\varphi\bigr),
\]
where \(\varphi\) ranges over all well-formed formulas of the intended language that are closed with respect to fixed-point variables. Equivalently, must every valid formula have a proof in the original presentation using none of its cut rule?
