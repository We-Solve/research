\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a finite digraph $D$, let $\ell(D)$ be the maximum number of arcs in a simple directed path, let $\delta^+(D)$ be minimum outdegree, and let $g(D)$ be directed girth in arcs. In the finite-girth setting the repaired source Conjecture 6.34, attributed to Thomass\'e, asks
\[
\ell(D)\ge\delta^+(D)(g(D)-1).
\]
The detached degree notation is a product, not an exponent. The original notes that the $g=3$ case is open and that the conjecture implies Caccetta--H\"aggkvist. For acyclic graphs the source does not specify a convention for $g=\infty$ and the product with possible zero minimum outdegree.

The attached aid reports the conjecture refuted for every $g\ge4$, while retaining the $g=3$ oriented-graph case as open. In particular it claims a simple oriented family $X_d$, for every integer $d\ge3$, with
\[
g(X_d)=4,\quad\delta^+(X_d)=d,\quad\ell(X_d)=2d+2<3d.
\]
Its vertex and arc construction is not reproduced in this summary. The refutation of this stronger path conjecture does not refute Caccetta--H\"aggkvist itself.

The aid separately gives an exact positive class. Let $m\ge3$ and let $B(a_0,\ldots,a_{m-1})$ have nonempty independent layers $L_i$ of sizes $a_i$, with every arc from $L_i$ to $L_{i+1\bmod m}$ and no other arcs. Put $s=\min_i a_i$ and let $R$ be the largest cyclic consecutive run of layers with size at least $s+1$, using $R=0$ if all sizes equal $s$. Because some layer has minimum size, $0\le R\le m-1$. The aid's formula is
\[
\ell(B)=sm+R-1,\qquad g(B)=m,\qquad\delta^+(B)=s.
\]
Consequently $\ell(B)-\delta^+(B)(g(B)-1)=s+R-1\ge0$, with equality exactly when all $a_i=1$, the bare directed cycle. Thus nonuniform cyclic blow-ups satisfy the original target and are not the refuting family.
\subsection{Short English statement}
The supplied aid reports Thomass\'e's path-girth conjecture false from girth four onward while preserving the open girth-three case. It also gives an exact longest-path formula for cyclic blow-ups, a family that satisfies the target rather than providing counterexamples.
