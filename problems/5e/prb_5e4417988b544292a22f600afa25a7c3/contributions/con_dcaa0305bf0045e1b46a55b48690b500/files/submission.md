# An unconditional all-quantifier exponential fragment with algebraic shifts

Claim type: Partial result

Exact scope: Decidability of every first-order formula whose variable occurrences are confined to exp(β+sum q_i x_i), with rational q_i and real algebraic β; real algebraic polynomial coefficients and arbitrary Boolean operations and quantifier alternation are allowed.

Real algebraic input constants are encoded by integer minimal polynomials and isolating rational intervals. This is an effectively described fragment of the real exponential language (algebraic constants can equivalently be specified by quantified defining formulas). Variables may occur in rational linear exponential arguments, but not as bare polynomial variables outside exponentials and not in nested exponentials.

Choose a common positive denominator D for all q_i occurring in the finite input and set y_i=exp(x_i/D). This gives a bijection R→R_{>0} in each coordinate. Each exponential term becomes
\[
 \exp(\beta+\sum_i q_i x_i)=e^\beta\prod_i y_i^{Dq_i}.
\]
Thus every atom becomes a comparison of Laurent polynomials in the y_i with coefficients in the ordered field generated by the real algebraic numbers and finitely many e^β. Clear negative powers by a positive monomial; strict and weak inequality directions are preserved. Replace existential quantification by existence of y_i>0 and universal quantification by implication from y_i>0. Structural induction on formulas proves equivalence even with arbitrary quantifier alternation.

Here is the additional effective coefficient step, which is essential when β is allowed. Every coefficient-field element can be represented as a quotient of finite sums
\[
 \sum_j c_j e^{\gamma_j},\qquad c_j,\gamma_j\in\overline{\mathbb Q}\cap\mathbb R.
\]
Addition and multiplication preserve this form, since exponents add. Equality of algebraic exponents and arithmetic in the algebraic coefficients are decidable. Merge equal exponents, combine their coefficients and remove zeros. The Lindemann–Weierstrass theorem says that exponentials of distinct algebraic numbers are linearly independent over the algebraic numbers. Therefore a normalized sum is zero exactly when no terms remain. This is a complete zero test, also allowing verification that denominators are nonzero; the theorem is an imported classical result, not reproved here [1, Theorem 3.2].

For a nonzero real sum, compute nested rational interval enclosures using effective algebraic-number arithmetic and the convergent exponential series with rigorous remainder estimates. Since the true sum is not zero, an interval eventually excludes zero; this halts and determines its sign. Quotient signs follow. We have therefore constructed a computable ordered coefficient field with exact equality and order, rather than assuming that approximate transcendental constants can be compared exactly.

The ordinary effective quantifier-elimination procedure for real closed fields now applies to the transformed finite formula. Its algebraic operations and coefficient sign decisions are precisely the effective operations just supplied. Ordered fields embed in their real closures, and every sentence with these fixed real coefficients has the same truth in that real closure as in R, by real-closed-field quantifier elimination. This yields a terminating decision algorithm for the whole declared fragment.

For example, arbitrary Boolean combinations of exp(sqrt(2)+x/3), exp(−sqrt(3)+2y) and exp(x/3−y) lie within scope. The mixed equation exp(x)=x+2 is outside it: the substitution reintroduces log y in its bare-x term. This restriction remains decisive, and no decision algorithm for all of Th(R_exp) or any proof of Schanuel’s conjecture is claimed.

Relation to earlier contributions. The earlier report gives the homogeneous rational-exponent fragment but explicitly excludes nonzero constants in exponents because their exact relations need a separate algorithm. Here algebraic shifts and algebraic coefficients are admitted by supplying that missing unconditional Lindemann–Weierstrass zero/sign procedure. Its unrelated numerical promise and Schanuel-conditional routes are not repeated.
