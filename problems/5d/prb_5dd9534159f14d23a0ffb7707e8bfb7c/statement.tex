\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 800178}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Under the conventional weight-zero interpretation, let \(f\) be a Hecke--Maass cusp form for \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\Z)\), with normalized Hecke eigenvalues \(\lambda_f(n)\), where \(\lambda_f(1)=1\). Its standard \(L\)-function is the analytic continuation of
\[
L(s,f)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_f(n)n^{-s},\qquad \Re(s)>1.
\]
Write its Laplace eigenvalue as \(\tfrac14+t_f^2\). The formulation below uses the conventional interpretation \(t_f\in[0,\infty)\); the source does not explicitly specify this convention or address exceptional, nonreal spectral parameters.

\textbf{Requested bound.} For every \(\varepsilon>0\), prove that there exists a constant \(C_{\varepsilon}>0\), independent of \(f\) and \(t\), such that every such form and every \(t\in\R\) satisfy
\[
\left|L\!\left(\tfrac12+it,f\right)\right|
\le C_{\varepsilon}
\bigl((1+|t_f-t|)(1+|t_f+t|)\bigr)^{1/6+\varepsilon}.
\]
The quantifier over \(t\) includes \(t=t_f\) and \(t=-t_f\), as well as their neighborhoods. Uniformity in \(f\) is the interpretation indicated by the source's notation \(\ll_{\varepsilon}\).

\textbf{Supplied qualifications.} The source attributes bounds away from conductor dropping to Jutila and Motohashi. It associates conductor dropping with either
\[
t_f-t=o\!\left(\max\{t_f,|t|\}\right)
\quad\text{or}\quad
t_f+t=o\!\left(\max\{t_f,|t|\}\right).
\]
These are asymptotic descriptions, for which the source specifies neither a limiting family nor a quantitative boundary. Moreover, its wording places these conditions after ``away from the conductor-dropping regimes, namely when'', creating an ambiguity about the stated scope of the known result. This ambiguity does not change the requested bound over all real \(t\). The source also attributes a subconvex bound in the conductor-dropping regime to Michel--Venkatesh, describing its exponent as nonexplicit and not Weyl-strength. These are supplied literature assessments, not independently verified conclusions.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Prove a Weyl-strength bound for the central-line values of level-one Hecke--Maass \(L\)-functions, uniformly in the form and the real height \(t\), including the conductor-dropping regions near \(t=\pm t_f\). The spectral convention and the source's conflicting description of the previously known range require review. The supplied registry labels the problem open; that status has not been independently verified.
