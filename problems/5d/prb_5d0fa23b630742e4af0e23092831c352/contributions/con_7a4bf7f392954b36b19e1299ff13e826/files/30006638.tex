\section{Higher-Dimensional Quantum Cellular Automata beyond Translations}\label{30006638}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 30006638 · Mathematical Physics}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


A quantum cellular automaton is an automorphism of a lattice observable algebra which increases operator support by only a uniformly bounded range. Finite-depth local circuits form the source's trivial subgroup; lattice shifts supply another allowed class. The quotient asks about locality-preserving automorphisms beyond these constructions.

\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}
Classify nontrivial elements in the quotient of higher-dimensional quantum cellular automata by finite-depth circuits and shifts. In particular, determine which elements occur beyond lattice translations and the currently known three-dimensional examples.

\paragraph{The doubled-automaton circuit test.} Let \(\alpha\) be a range-\(R\) QCA on a regular lattice with finite on-site Hilbert spaces. Use two identical layers and write \(S=\prod_xS_x\) for the on-site swap between layers, understood as a depth-one automorphism. Put \(A=\alpha\otimes\operatorname{id}\). Each unitary \(V_x=A(S_x)\) is supported in a uniformly bounded neighborhood of site \(x\) across the two layers. Since \(S_x\) commute, the \(V_x\) commute.
\paragraph{Why the infinite product is a finite-depth circuit.} On a bounded-degree lattice the overlap graph of these uniformly bounded supports has bounded degree. A greedy coloring uses at most one more than that degree in colors: at each vertex avoid the finitely many colors of already colored neighbors. Gates of one color have disjoint supports, so \(V=\prod_xV_x\) is a bounded-depth local circuit. Algebraically it implements \(ASA^{-1}\). Therefore
\[
 VS=(\alpha\otimes\operatorname{id})S
 (\alpha^{-1}\otimes\operatorname{id})S
 =\alpha\otimes\alpha^{-1}.
\]
The last equality follows because swapping exchanges the tensor layers. The products define local-algebra actions because only finitely many gates affect any fixed observable.
\paragraph{What this does not classify.} This elementary cancellation construction proves that stacking an automaton with its inverse is circuit trivial. It does not show that \(\alpha\) alone is trivial, nor does it identify which higher-dimensional classes remain after shifts and the permitted stabilization. A proposed invariant must respect this cancellation as well as the source shift quotient. The first missing step is an invariant or construction distinguishing additional classes in fixed spatial dimension, with completeness or a precise realizability statement. No one-dimensional index result is substituted for that classification.
