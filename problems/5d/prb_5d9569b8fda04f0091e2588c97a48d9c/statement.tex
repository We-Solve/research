\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A quasi-order $(P,\le_P)$ is a well-quasi-order if every sequence $(p_i)_{i\in\N}$ has indices $i<j$ with $p_i\le_Pp_j$. A standard better-quasi-order convention requires that, for every infinite $A\subseteq\N$ and every continuous map $F:[A]^\omega\to P$ with $P$ discrete, some $X\in[A]^\omega$ satisfy $F(X)\le_PF(X\setminus\{\min X\})$. The domain has the topology of increasing sequences. If poset rather than quasi-order conventions are needed for poset algebras, the treatment of equivalent elements must be specified.

The source contains two questions, with a weaker alternative in the second.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Is every well-quasi-order a countable union of better-quasi-orders? Explicitly, does every such $P$ admit subsets $P_n\subseteq P$ such that
\[
 P=\bigcup_{n\in\N}P_n,\qquad
 (P_n,\le_P|_{P_n})\text{ is a better-quasi-order for every }n?
\]
The orders on the pieces are inherited; this is not a request to choose new orderings. Neither disjointness nor countability of the pieces is required. No countability restriction on $P$ itself is supplied. Such an order is often described as $\sigma$-better-quasi-ordered, matching the assessment's terminology.
\item If $P$ is a well-quasi-order, must $P\in G_{\mathrm{bqo}}$, or at least $P\in G_{\mathrm{wqo}}$? The supplied gloss defines these as the classes whose poset algebras are respectively better-quasi-order-generated and well-quasi-order-generated. Writing $\mathcal A(P)$ for the report's poset algebra, the targets are therefore
\[
 \mathcal A(P)\text{ is bqo-generated},
 \qquad\text{or, more weakly,}\qquad
 \mathcal A(P)\text{ is wqo-generated}.
\]
These retain the source's terms: neither the construction of $\mathcal A(P)$ nor the exact generation predicate is supplied. The order on a generating family, the operations allowed in generation, and any additional requirements must be recovered from the cited treatment. A naive choice of universal ordered generators could make the weaker claim merely definitional, so no such construction is silently substituted for the intended question.
\end{enumerate}

The supplied assessment reports a proved countable case of the second assertion, but does not identify whether that refers to the bqo-generated assertion, its weaker alternative, or additional hypotheses. No equivalence between the decomposition and generation questions is asserted here.
