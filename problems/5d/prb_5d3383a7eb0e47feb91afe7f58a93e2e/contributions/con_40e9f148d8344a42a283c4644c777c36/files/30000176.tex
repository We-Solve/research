\section{Multipartite Dense-Coding Capacity}\label{30000176}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 30000176 · Mathematical Physics}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


\paragraph{Definitions and conventions.} A shared multipartite state is a positive trace-one operator $\rho$ on $\bigotimes_i\mathcal H_{A_i}\otimes\bigotimes_{j=1}^r\mathcal H_{B_j}$. Sender $A_i$ encodes a classical message by a local unitary and sends its subsystem to its assigned receiver. Local operations and classical communication (LOCC) permit the receivers to exchange classical messages, but not to perform a joint quantum operation. The dense-coding capacity is the supremum asymptotic classical communication rate, in bits per use, achievable with vanishing decoding error in the chosen receiver protocol. The unassisted benchmark is $\sum_i\log_2\dim\mathcal H_{A_i}$. Bound entanglement means entanglement from which the relevant parties cannot distil singlets by LOCC.

\paragraph{Source scope and qualifications.} The capacity depends on whether receiver decoding is local, LOCC, or global and on the assignment of senders to receivers. The report distinguishes these protocols; the question must retain that distinction. It asks both for more-than-two-receiver capacities or bounds and for usefulness of multipartite bound entanglement.

\paragraph{Catalogue mathematical target.}
\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}

\paragraph{A spectral counting attempt.} Write $D_A=\prod_i\dim\mathcal H_{A_i}$, let $R$ be the rank of the joint receiver marginal $\rho_B$, and let $\lambda$ be the largest eigenvalue of $\rho$. Consider $n$ independent copies, unitary encodings on the sending systems, and $M$ equiprobable messages. Positivity implies that $\rho$ is supported on $\mathcal H_A\otimes\operatorname{supp}\rho_B$: if a receiver vector has zero marginal expectation, the corresponding positive diagonal block vanishes, and Cauchy--Schwarz for the positive quadratic form makes its off-diagonal blocks vanish too.

Every encoded state $\rho_m$ therefore obeys $\rho_m\leq\lambda^n P$, where $P$ is the common support projection of dimension $(D_AR)^n$. For any decoding POVM $E_1,\ldots,E_M$, including every LOCC decoder,
\[
 p_{\rm success}=M^{-1}\sum_m\operatorname{Tr}(E_m\rho_m)
 \leq M^{-1}\lambda^n\operatorname{Tr}P
 =M^{-1}(D_AR\lambda)^n.
\]
If success tends to one, taking logarithms and dividing by $n$ gives $\limsup n^{-1}\log_2M\leq\log_2(D_AR\lambda)$. The proof even permits joint sender unitaries and unrestricted receiver measurements, hence applies to every assignment to three or more receivers.

\paragraph{Counterchecks and obstruction.} A pure maximally entangled state with $R=D_A$ gives the familiar dimension ceiling $2\log_2D_A$. A maximally mixed shared state gives zero capacity in this fixed-state, unitary-only encoding class. That last check forbids silently importing a discard-and-reprepare operation, which would restore an unassisted rate. The estimate does not exploit separation and can be much too large. For pure $\rho$ it ignores all entanglement geometry. In particular it neither distinguishes bound entanglement from distillable entanglement nor determines any receiver-LOCC capacity. Obtaining an assignment-sensitive bound below this global counting ceiling is the first obstruction; deciding usefulness of multipartite bound entanglement remains a separate bundled question.
