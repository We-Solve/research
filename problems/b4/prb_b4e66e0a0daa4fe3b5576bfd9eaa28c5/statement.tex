\section{Are there infinitely many Wagstaff primes}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For an odd prime $p$, the integer $W_p=(2^p+1)/3$ is a Wagstaff number; a prime value is a Wagstaff prime. Equivalently, one may use all positive integer exponents $n$ for which $(2^n+1)/3$ is an integer prime: a prime value forces $n$ to be an odd prime.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Are there infinitely many odd primes $p$ for which $W_p$ is prime? \sref{statement}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Are infinitely many numbers two to an odd prime power, plus one and divided by three, prime?
