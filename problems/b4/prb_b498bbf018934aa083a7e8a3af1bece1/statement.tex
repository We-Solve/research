\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 11100062}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $X$ be a simply connected finite complex, interpreted in the usual homotopy-theoretic sense of a finite CW complex, and choose a basepoint. Write $\Omega X$ for its based loop space. For an odd prime $p$, the Steenrod reduced power is the natural operation
\[
 P^t:H^q(\Omega X;\F_p)\longrightarrow
 H^{q+2t(p-1)}(\Omega X;\F_p),\qquad t\geq0.
\]
The question attributed in the source to Wilkerson is whether, for each such $X$, there exists a bound $p_0(X)$ such that for every odd prime $p>p_0(X)$, every integer $t\geq1$, and every degree $q\geq0$, this operation is the zero map:
\[
 P^t(a)=0\quad\text{for all }a\in H^q(\Omega X;\F_p).
\]
Here a single prime bound for the chosen $X$ must work simultaneously in all degrees and for all positive reduced powers. The wording ``$p$ sufficiently large'' is taken to allow dependence on $X$, not to assert a uniform bound across all finite complexes.

The positive-index qualification is necessary to express the usual intended meaning of trivial Steenrod reduced powers: $P^0$ is the identity. The source writes $P^t$ without explicitly excluding $t=0$, so this convention must be checked against its cited problem list. No assertion about Bocksteins or the full Steenrod algebra is added.
\subsection{Short English statement}
For a fixed simply connected finite complex, must every positive Steenrod reduced power vanish on the entire mod-$p$ cohomology of its loop space once the prime is sufficiently large? The bound may depend on the complex. The supplied summary reports related results but says its verification did not identify a definitive theorem for this exact question; it recommends treating it as largely open.
