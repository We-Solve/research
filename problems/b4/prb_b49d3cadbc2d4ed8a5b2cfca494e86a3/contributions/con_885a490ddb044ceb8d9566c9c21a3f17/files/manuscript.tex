\section{Quantitative source scope}
Stehle's report, printed pages 2918--2919, compares a quantum worst-case
lattice reduction in dimension $n$ with a classical one of lattice dimension
$O(\sqrt n)$ for LWE of dimension $n$. It explicitly includes modulus,
noise and approximation promises: the quantum statement assumes prime
polynomially bounded $q$, $\alpha q\ge2\sqrt n$, and approximation on the
order of $n$ times a polylogarithmic factor divided by $\alpha$.
The dimension-only catalogue sentence omits those parameters. We do not
claim that the following elementary transformations of LWE samples implement
or remove the source's worst-case lattice reduction loss.

\section{Attempt 1: pad dimensions and randomize the secret}
Let $q$ be prime, write $\mathbb F_q$ for the field of residues, and suppose
samples of dimension $r$ have the form $(a,b)$ with uniform independent
$a\in\mathbb F_q^r$ and $b=a^Tt+e$, for a fixed secret $t$ and independent
errors of some fixed distribution modulo $q$. For $n\ge r$, append independent
uniform $c\in\mathbb F_q^{n-r}$ and choose one shared uniform vector
$w\in\mathbb F_q^n$. Output
\[
A=(a,c),\qquad B=b+A^Tw.
\]
Then $A$ is uniform, and $B=A^T((t,0)+w)+e$. The new secret is uniform on
$\mathbb F_q^n$, independently of all sample vectors, because translation
by the fixed $(t,0)$ preserves uniformity. All samples use the same new
secret and unchanged error distribution. A search oracle returning it lets
us subtract the known $w$ and recover $t$. For a decision reduction, uniform
original $b$ remains uniform after adding $A^Tw$, so uniform input also
maps to uniform output. This proves the elementary direction
$\mathrm{LWE}_r\le\mathrm{LWE}_n$ under the stated sample conventions.
It does not turn hardness of a lower-dimensional worst-case lattice problem
into hardness for a higher-dimensional worst-case lattice problem. Reversing
that reduction direction would be an unsupported inference.

\section{Attempt 2: project public vectors to fewer coordinates}
Fix a full-column-rank matrix $T\in\mathbb F_q^{n\times r}$ with $r<n$,
chosen independently of the secret. For original samples
$b=a^Ts+e$, keep only $a'=T^Ta$ and $b$.
If $s=Tt$, this is an exact $r$-dimensional LWE sample with secret $t$.
But if $s\notin\operatorname{col}(T)$, the $r+1$ linear forms in
$(T^Ta,a^Ts)$ are independent. The associated linear map has rank $r+1$:
a dependence would express $s$ as a linear combination of columns of $T$,
or contradict the rank of $T$. Gaussian elimination gives $n-r-1$ free
coordinates in each fiber, so each output has exactly $q^{n-r-1}$ preimages.
Consequently $a^Ts$ is uniform independently of $a'$.
Adding the independent error $e$ leaves it uniform and independent.
For each such fixed $s$, all projected samples are therefore independent
uniform pairs, regardless of the original noise law.

For a uniform original secret, the event $s\in\operatorname{col}(T)$ has
probability $q^{r-n}$. Conditional on it, the corresponding $t$ is uniform.
For any number $M$ of samples, the entire projected distribution is thus
\[
\mathcal P_M=q^{r-n}\mathcal L_{r,M}
 +(1-q^{r-n})\mathcal U_M,
\]
where $\mathcal L_{r,M}$ is the ordinary $r$-dimensional LWE distribution
with a shared uniform secret and $\mathcal U_M$ is uniform.
For every event $E$,
$|\mathcal P_M(E)-\mathcal U_M(E)|
=q^{r-n}|\mathcal L_{r,M}(E)-\mathcal U_M(E)|\le q^{r-n}$.
Thus the total variation distance, defined as the supremum of this difference
over events, is at most $q^{r-n}$. No distinguisher can recover more advantage
from these projected samples, even with arbitrarily many of them.
For $r$ around $\sqrt n$ this particular map discards almost all distinguishing
information. The conclusion concerns a fixed secret-independent linear
projection with the original right-hand side retained. It is not a lower
bound for general classical reductions, adaptive transformations, or quantum
algorithms.
