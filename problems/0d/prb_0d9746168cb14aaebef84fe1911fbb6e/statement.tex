\section{Accelerated Information Propagation in Bosonic Lattices}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


A bosonic lattice has unbounded local occupation numbers. For local observables $A$ and $B$, propagation can be measured by $[A(t),B]$, where $A(t)=e^{itH}Ae^{-itH}$. An effective light cone bounds the spatial range where this commutator can be appreciable. Acceleration means faster spreading than a uniform finite-velocity cone in the source's state-dependent setting.

\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}
When does accelerated information propagation occur in bosonic lattice systems with nearest-neighbor or long-range interactions?

\paragraph{Scope and source qualification.}The allowed states and interaction decay matter because local bosonic operators are unbounded. Both nearest-neighbor and long-range interactions remain within the question.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Identify when information in bosonic lattices propagates faster than a uniform finite-speed light cone.
