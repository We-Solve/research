\paragraph{A synchronized product Green kernel.}For the flat product \(\mathbb R^4_x\times\mathbb R^4_y\), the heat kernel is the product at the same time parameter,
\[H_t(x,y)=(4\pi t)^{-4}\exp[-(|x|^2+|y|^2)/(4t)].\]
Integrating from zero to infinity, with \(r^2=|x|^2+|y|^2>0\), gives
\[G_8(x,y)=\int_0^\infty H_t(x,y)dt=\frac1{2\pi^4r^6}.
\]
Indeed substitution \(s=r^2/(4t)\) reduces the integral to \((4\pi)^{-4}(4/r^2)^3\int_0^\infty s^2e^{-s}ds\), and the last integral is two by repeated integration by parts. Integrating the heat equation in time shows \(-\Delta G_8=\delta_0\) distributionally; the boundary at infinity vanishes and the small-time heat kernel converges to the delta distribution.
\paragraph{Why multiplying Green functions fails.}In four dimensions \(G_4(x)=1/(4\pi^2|x|^2)\). A product of two such Green functions satisfies
\[-\Delta_{x,y}[G_4(x)G_4(y)]=\delta_0(x)G_4(y)+G_4(x)\delta_0(y),\]
which has sources along the coordinate factors, rather than only at the product origin. Thus the product construction requires a synchronized heat integral, not the product of inverses.
