\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $\Gamma_g$ denote the mapping class group in the source and let $H\le\Gamma_g$ be a surface subgroup. In the customary closed-surface interpretation, this means an embedded abstract subgroup isomorphic to $\pi_1(\Sigma_h)$ for a closed orientable surface of genus $h\ge2$; the exact surface convention and ambient genus range are not repeated in the excerpt.

A group $\Gamma$ is $H$-separable if
\[
 \forall\gamma\in\Gamma\setminus H\quad\exists K\le\Gamma,
 \qquad [\Gamma:K]<\infty,\quad H\le K,\quad\gamma\notin K.
\]
Equivalently, $H$ is closed in the profinite topology on $\Gamma$, or each $\gamma\notin H$ has a finite quotient $q:\Gamma\to Q$ with $q(\gamma)\notin q(H)$. The subgroup $K$ need not be normal.

\textbf{Question 3.6.} For every surface subgroup $H\le\Gamma_g$, is $\Gamma_g$ $H$-separable? The question imposes no convex-cocompactness assumption on $H$. The preceding local item 11000248 asks the analogous question for convex cocompact subgroups and then introduces this surface-subgroup variant; that preceding hypothesis must not be imported into the present question. Nor does failure of separability for some other finitely generated subgroup settle separability for all surface subgroups.

\subsection{Short English statement}
Can every element outside any surface subgroup of a mapping class group be separated from that subgroup in a finite quotient? The surface subgroup is not required to be convex cocompact.
