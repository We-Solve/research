\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 701282}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(F\) be a free group with an infinite basis, with no restriction to countable rank. Write \([F,F]\) for the subgroup generated by all commutators \(x^{-1}y^{-1}xy\), where \(x,y\in F\), and let \(F_{\mathrm{ab}}=F/[F,F]\) be its abelianization. Define the group of \(IA\)-automorphisms by
\[
\operatorname{IA}(F)=\ker\!\left(\operatorname{Aut}(F)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(F_{\mathrm{ab}})\right).
\]
Thus an automorphism belongs to \(\operatorname{IA}(F)\) precisely when it induces the identity on the abelianization. For \(\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(F)\), its normal closure in \(\operatorname{Aut}(F)\) is
\[
\langle\!\langle\alpha\rangle\!\rangle_{\operatorname{Aut}(F)}
=\left\langle\beta\alpha\beta^{-1}:\beta\in\operatorname{Aut}(F)\right\rangle.
\]
Is it true that for every infinitely generated free group \(F\) there exists \(\alpha\in\operatorname{IA}(F)\) such that
\[
\langle\!\langle\alpha\rangle\!\rangle_{\operatorname{Aut}(F)}=\operatorname{IA}(F)?
\]
\subsection{Short English statement}
For every free group of infinite rank, does there exist a single automorphism acting trivially on its abelianization whose conjugates by all automorphisms generate the entire group of automorphisms acting trivially on the abelianization?
