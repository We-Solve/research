\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(F\) be a finite field, let \(n\) be a positive integer, and write
\[
H=\operatorname{SL}_n(F)=\{g\in\operatorname{Mat}_{n\times n}(F):\det g=1\}.
\]
For \(\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}(n)\), let \(P_\sigma\) be its permutation matrix, defined by \(P_\sigma e_i=e_{\sigma(i)}\). The supplied hypothesis is that \(A\subseteq H\), \(\langle A\rangle=H\), and \(P_\sigma\in A\) for every \(\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}(n)\).

There is an unresolved compatibility issue in this hypothesis: \(\det P_\sigma=\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\) in \(F\). For \(n\geq2\) in odd characteristic, odd permutation matrices do not belong to \(H\), so no such \(A\) exists. In characteristic two, all permutation matrices do belong to \(H\). The notation \(\Gamma(H,A)\) also leaves the graph convention unspecified. To expose this issue, define the undirected word diameter by
\[
D_{\pm}(H,A)=\max_{h\in H}\min\{\ell\geq0:h=a_1\cdots a_\ell,\ a_i\in A\cup A^{-1}\},
\]
and define the directed word diameter by
\[
D_+(H,A)=\max_{h\in H}\min\{\ell\geq0:h=a_1\cdots a_\ell,\ a_i\in A\}.
\]
The empty product is the identity. Since \(H\) is finite and \(A\) generates \(H\), both quantities are finite. They are the diameters of, respectively, the undirected Cayley graph with edges \(\{x,xa\}\) and the directed Cayley graph with arcs \(x\to xa\), for \(x\in H\) and \(a\in A\).
