\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 800172}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(X>0\) be real, and let
\[
\mathcal D(X)=\{d\in\Z:0<d<X,\ d\text{ is odd and square-free}\}.
\]
For \(d\in\mathcal D(X)\), the integer \(8d\) is a positive fundamental discriminant. Write
\[
\chi_{8d}(n)=\left(\frac{8d}{n}\right)
\]
for its associated primitive quadratic Dirichlet character, where the symbol is the Kronecker symbol. Define
\[
L(s,\chi_{8d})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\chi_{8d}(n)}{n^s}
\qquad (\operatorname{Re}s>1),
\]
and interpret its value at \(s=\tfrac12\) by analytic continuation. All logarithms below are natural.

Determine a polynomial
\[
P(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{10}a_jt^j\in\R[t],\qquad a_{10}\ne0,
\]
independent of \(X\), such that, as \(X\to\infty\),
\[
\sum_{d\in\mathcal D(X)}L\!\left(\tfrac12,\chi_{8d}\right)^4
=XP(\log X)+o(X).
\]
Here \(o(X)\) means that the difference between the sum and \(XP(\log X)\), divided by \(X\), tends to zero. The source states this problem without an additional conjectural hypothesis.

Can the remainder be improved to a power saving; that is, do there exist such a polynomial \(P\) and a fixed \(\delta>0\) for which
\[
\sum_{d\in\mathcal D(X)}L\!\left(\tfrac12,\chi_{8d}\right)^4
=XP(\log X)+O\!\left(X^{1-\delta}\right)
\qquad (X\to\infty)?
\]
The source also asks whether the multiple Dirichlet series method can obtain the ``first coefficients'' of \(P\), referring to [DPP]. The supplied record neither specifies which coefficients or how many are intended nor identifies [DPP] bibliographically; this methodological question therefore retains those unresolved qualifications.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Determine the degree-ten polynomial governing the fourth moment of the central values of quadratic Dirichlet \(L\)-functions with discriminants \(8d\), for positive odd square-free \(d<X\), with error \(o(X)\). Ask also for a power-saving error and whether multiple Dirichlet series yield the source's unspecified ``first coefficients.''
