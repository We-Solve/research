\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $n\geq1$, let $A\in\{0,1\}^{n\times n}$, and set
\[
 P=\{x\in\R^n:Ax\leq\mathbf1,\ x\leq\mathbf1\},
\]
with componentwise inequalities and no lower bound $x\geq0$. A facet is an actual codimension-one face, not simply a row of a possibly redundant inequality description. Let $\mathcal F(P)$ be the facets and let $c:\mathcal F(P)\to\{1,\ldots,n\}$ assign one color to each, with exactly two facets of each color.

Writing $e_i$ for the $i$th coordinate vector, the source further requires that a facet ``with extreme direction $-e_i$'' not have color $i$. The recession cone of $P$ is the negative orthant, with extreme rays $\R_{\geq0}(-e_i)$. Under the natural recession-face interpretation, the condition is
\[
 -e_i\in\operatorname{rec}(F)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad c(F)\ne i,
 \qquad F\in\mathcal F(P).
\]
Here $\operatorname{rec}(F)$ is the set of directions $d$ such that $x+td\in F$ for every $x\in F$ and $t\geq0$. The source does not explicitly define extreme direction, so this interpretation must be confirmed.

A vertex $v$ is panchromatic if
\[
 \{c(F):F\in\mathcal F(P),\ v\in F\}=\{1,\ldots,n\}.
\]
The algorithmic question is whether, for every admissible $A$ and coloring, such a vertex can be found in time polynomial in the finite input encoding. Degenerate vertices may meet more than $n$ facets; no simplicity assumption is added. Since there are at most $2n$ defining inequalities, the exact-two-per-color hypothesis also requires $2n$ actual facets; redundant/duplicate inequalities cannot be counted as separate colored facets without changing the problem.
