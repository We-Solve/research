\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(\mathbb{T}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:|\lambda|=1\}\). In the standard complex phase-retrieval interpretation of the source, the signal space is \(\mathbb{C}^{M}\), and \(x\sim y\) means that \(y=\lambda x\) for some \(\lambda\in\mathbb{T}\). The quotient includes the class of the zero vector.

For an integer \(M\geq 2\), put \(N=4M-5\). Let \(A\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times M}\) have rank \(M\), with its column space distributed according to the unitary-invariant probability measure on the Grassmannian \(\operatorname{Gr}_{\mathbb{C}}(M,N)\) of \(M\)-dimensional complex linear subspaces of \(\mathbb{C}^{N}\). Define
\[
\Phi_A:\mathbb{C}^{M}/\mathbb{T}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq0}^{N},\qquad
\Phi_A([x])=\bigl(|(Ax)_1|^2,\ldots,|(Ax)_N|^2\bigr),
\]
and
\[
p_M=\mathbb{P}\bigl(\Phi_A\text{ is injective}\bigr).
\]
Thus injectivity means
\[
\forall x,y\in\mathbb{C}^{M},\qquad
\left(\forall j\in\{1,\ldots,N\},\ |(Ax)_j|^2=|(Ay)_j|^2\right)
\Longrightarrow
\left(\exists\lambda\in\mathbb{T},\ y=\lambda x\right).
\]
This event depends only on the column space of \(A\), so the specified Grassmannian distribution determines \(p_M\). The source mentions independent identically distributed Gaussian entries as an example; independent standard circular complex Gaussian entries realize the specified distribution.

\textbf{Conjecture 1 (Bandeira et al., as attributed in the supplied record).}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] For every integer \(M\geq2\), \(p_M<1\).
\item[(b)] \(\displaystyle\lim_{M\to\infty}p_M=0\), with \(M\) ranging over integers.
\end{enumerate}
The source states part (a) for ``all \(M\)'' without specifying a lower bound. The range \(M\geq2\) above is the positive-integer range in which the stated Grassmannian exists: \(4M-5\geq M\). \subsection{Short English statement}
For a random \(M\)-dimensional complex subspace of \(\mathbb{C}^{4M-5}\), consider whether coordinatewise squared magnitudes determine every vector up to a common complex phase. The supplied conjecture says that this succeeds with probability strictly less than one for every admissible \(M\), and that this probability tends to zero as \(M\to\infty\). The unspecified small-dimension boundary and the interpretation of the signal quotient require review.
