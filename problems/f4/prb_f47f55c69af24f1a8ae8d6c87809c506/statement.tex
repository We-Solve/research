\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
All groups below are abelian and written additively. A group \(G\) is torsion-free if \(ng=0\), with \(g\in G\) and an integer \(n\geq 1\), implies \(g=0\). For groups \(C,A\), let \(\operatorname{Ext}_{\Z}^{1}(C,A)\) denote the abelian group of equivalence classes of extensions
\[
0\longrightarrow A\longrightarrow E\longrightarrow C\longrightarrow 0,
\]
with its Baer sum operation.

The question proposed by A. Mader asks whether there exist ``large, non-trivial'' classes \(\mathfrak X\) of torsion-free abelian groups such that
\[
\forall A,C\in\mathfrak X,\qquad
\operatorname{Ext}_{\Z}^{1}(C,A)\text{ is torsion-free}.
\]
Equivalently, the required extension-group condition is
\[
\forall A,C\in\mathfrak X\ \forall n\in\Z_{\geq 1}\ \forall \xi\in\operatorname{Ext}_{\Z}^{1}(C,A),
\qquad n\xi=0\ \Longrightarrow\ \xi=0.
\]
The source does not specify what ``large'' or ``non-trivial'' means for the class \(\mathfrak X\). Consequently, the displayed condition is precise, but the intended existence problem remains qualified by an unspecified requirement on admissible classes.
