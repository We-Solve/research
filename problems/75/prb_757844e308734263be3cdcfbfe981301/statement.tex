\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a smooth closed connected $n$-manifold $M$ in a category where connected sums are fixed, write $\#^k M$ for the connected sum of $k$ copies. A homotopy sphere is a manifold homotopy equivalent to $S^n$; this exclusion concerns homotopy type, not only diffeomorphism type. A Ricci-flat metric satisfies $\operatorname{Ric}_g=0$ identically, a stronger condition than zero scalar curvature.

The natural per-manifold reading of the conjecture is
\[
\forall M\not\simeq S^n\ \exists k_0=k_0(M)\in\N_{\geq1}\ \forall k\geq k_0,
\qquad \#^k M\text{ admits no Ricci-flat Riemannian metric}.
\]
The source does not specify a dimension range, orientation convention, or smooth category. The closed connected setting above is an explicit conventional interpretation of compact manifolds in this context, not a supplied hypothesis: allowing boundary would change the assertion materially. For oriented manifolds the summands and gluings should use the same stated orientation convention.

The source also does not ask for a threshold uniform over all $M$ or depending only on $n$; the display allows dependence on $M$. Nor is the conjecture merely that some connected sum fails to be Ricci flat: every sufficiently large number of copies is quantified. No quantitative value of $k_0$ is supplied.

Its broad claim that simply connected Ricci-flat manifolds necessarily have special holonomy is not supplied as an established theorem with the needed scope and is not used to justify the conjecture.
\subsection{Short English statement}
For each compact manifold not homotopy equivalent to a sphere, do sufficiently large connected sums of copies admit no Ricci-flat metric? Specify the closed/smooth/dimensional category and allow the threshold to depend on the manifold.
