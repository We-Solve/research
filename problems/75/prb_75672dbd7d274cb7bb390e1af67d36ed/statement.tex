\section{Far-From-Threshold Buckling of Indented Spherical Shells}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


\paragraph{Definitions and conventions.}
An indented shell is a thin elastic surface subjected to a localized displacement. Pressureless means no imposed internal pressure. Buckling creates a nonaxisymmetric or folded configuration after loss of stability of an earlier branch; far-from-threshold behavior concerns large departures from the onset, where linearized instability analysis is insufficient. Localization concentrates deformation or stress in a small region. A substrate supplies restoring forces whose stiffness can tend to zero.

\paragraph{Target retained from the canonical source catalogue.}
\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}
For indentation of pressureless spherical shells and related shells on substrates with vanishing properties, characterize the far-from-threshold buckled or crumpled pattern and explain how indentation-induced localization and buckling arise and evolve.

\paragraph{Source scope and qualification.}
The shell geometry, thickness, boundary loading and substrate limit are model parameters to be specified from the report. A threshold calculation alone does not characterize the requested far-from-threshold pattern.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Describe the deeply buckled or crumpled shapes of pressureless indented shells and how their localized deformation evolves.
