\section{Far-From-Threshold Buckling of Indented Spherical Shells}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


\paragraph{Definitions and conventions.}
An indented shell is a thin elastic surface subjected to a localized displacement. Pressureless means no imposed internal pressure. Buckling creates a nonaxisymmetric or folded configuration after loss of stability of an earlier branch; far-from-threshold behavior concerns large departures from the onset, where linearized instability analysis is insufficient. Localization concentrates deformation or stress in a small region. A substrate supplies restoring forces whose stiffness can tend to zero.

\paragraph{Target retained from the canonical source catalogue.}
\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}
For indentation of pressureless spherical shells and related shells on substrates with vanishing properties, characterize the far-from-threshold buckled or crumpled pattern and explain how indentation-induced localization and buckling arise and evolve.

\paragraph{Source scope and qualification.}
The shell geometry, thickness, boundary loading and substrate limit are model parameters to be specified from the report. A threshold calculation alone does not characterize the requested far-from-threshold pattern.

\paragraph{Testing a wavelength-selection shortcut.} In a local flat scalar approximation let a sinusoidal displacement have wave number \(k>0\), bending modulus \(B>0\), compressive membrane stress \(T>0\), and substrate stiffness \(K\ge0\). Its quadratic energy per squared amplitude is proportional to
\[
 q(k)=Bk^4-Tk^2+K.
\]
For \(K>0\), neutrality at fixed \(k\) occurs at \(T(k)=Bk^2+K/k^2\). Completing a square gives \(T(k)-2\sqrt{BK}=(\sqrt Bk-\sqrt K/k)^2\), so the threshold is \(2\sqrt{BK}\), attained at \(k=(K/B)^{1/4}\). For \(K=0\), the infimum threshold is zero and is approached as \(k\downarrow0\); no positive minimizing wave number remains in the infinite flat domain.
\paragraph{Obstruction and audit.} The corresponding wavelength diverges when \(K\to0\). Therefore extrapolating this local threshold calculation to a localized finite spherical indentation loses its own scale separation: eventually the wavelength is comparable to the shell radius or boundary distance. Moreover the quadratic functional becomes unbounded below along an unstable mode as its amplitude increases, so it cannot select a finite far-from-threshold amplitude or crumpled pattern. These are exact limitations of the proposed shortcut, not conclusions about actual shell minimizers. Curvature, geometric nonlinearities, indentation loading, finite thickness and boundaries must enter a controlled nonlinear analysis before one can answer the source localization and pattern-evolution questions. No such comparison is proved here, and the elementary threshold calculation earns no original-target completion.
