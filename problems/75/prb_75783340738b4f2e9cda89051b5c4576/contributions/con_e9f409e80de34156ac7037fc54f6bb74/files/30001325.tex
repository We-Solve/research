\section{Research attempt: Covariance identification and a hidden-common-input obstruction}

\paragraph{Full-state identification in a specified linear model}
Suppose zero-mean random vectors satisfy $X_{t+1}=AX_t+\xi_t$, with finite second moments and $\mathbb E[\xi_tX_t^T]=0$. Write $\Sigma=\mathbb E[X_tX_t^T]$ and $\Gamma=\mathbb E[X_{t+1}X_t^T]$. Multiplication and expectation give $\Gamma=A\Sigma$. Thus if $\Sigma$ is invertible, population observations of these two matrices determine $A=\Gamma\Sigma^{-1}$. No stationarity assumption is needed for this single-time identity, although repeated-data estimation would require further assumptions.

If $\Sigma$ is singular, choose a nonzero vector $v$ with $\Sigma v=0$ and a nonzero vector $u$, and let $D=uv^T$. Since $\mathbb E[(v^TX_t)^2]=v^T\Sigma v=0$, we have $DX_t=0$ almost surely. Replacing $A$ by $A+D$ therefore changes the connectivity coefficients while leaving the one-step observation equation unchanged on the observed state distribution. Persistently unexcited directions cannot be recovered from this experiment.

For finite matrices $Y=AX+E$, where columns are consecutive observed input states and responses, assume $X$ has full row rank. The least-squares minimizer is uniquely
\[
 \widehat A=YX^T(XX^T)^{-1},\qquad
 \widehat A-A=EX^T(XX^T)^{-1}.
\]
To verify uniqueness, the residual at this matrix is orthogonal rowwise to every row of $X$; expansion of the squared Frobenius norm for any perturbation $D$ adds $\lVert DX\rVert_F^2$, positive for $D\ne0$. Singular-value decomposition, or orthogonal diagonalization of $XX^T$, shows $\lVert X^T(XX^T)^{-1}\rVert_2=1/\sigma_{\min}(X)$. Hence
\[
 \lVert\widehat A-A\rVert_2\leq\lVert E\rVert_2/\sigma_{\min}(X).
\]
This finite bound explicitly requires full observation and excitation; it supplies neither a noise concentration theorem nor sparse recovery from incomplete observations.

\paragraph{Two different networks with exactly the same observed law}
Consider repeated independent observations of two variables. In a direct-edge model take $X\sim N(0,2)$ and $Y=X/2+\eta$, where $\eta\sim N(0,3/2)$ is independent. In a hidden-common-input model take independent $Z,\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2\sim N(0,1)$ and set $X=Z+\varepsilon_1$, $Y=Z+\varepsilon_2$. The first model has a direct coefficient $1/2$ from observed $X$ to observed $Y$; the second has no such direct edge but a latent common parent. Both observed vectors are centered Gaussian with covariance
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Their characteristic functions are therefore both $\exp(-\tfrac12 t^T\Sigma t)$, as follows by multiplying the characteristic functions of the independent Gaussian terms. The observation laws are identical for every sample size. Any estimator choosing between the two graphs has output probabilities with the same distribution under both models; its two success probabilities sum to at most one, so one is at most $1/2$. Adding independent observed coordinates embeds the ambiguity in arbitrarily large networks.

This obstruction concerns a specified static observation scheme and does not assert that these models have identical interventional or rich temporal data. It proves why more data of an unidentifiable type cannot substitute for assumptions about hidden variables and causal access.
