\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 700477}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A group identity is an equation \(u(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=v(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\), where \(n\geq 1\) and \(u,v\) are finite words in the group operations, variables, and identity element. A group satisfies the identity if the equation holds for every assignment of its variables to elements of the group. For such an identity \(e\), let \(\mathcal V(e)\) be the class of all groups satisfying \(e\). A variety of groups is a class consisting of all groups satisfying some set of group identities. A subvariety of \(\mathcal V(e)\) is a variety \(\mathcal W\) with \(\mathcal W\subseteq\mathcal V(e)\). Write
\[
\operatorname{Sub}(\mathcal V(e))=\{\mathcal W:\mathcal W\text{ is a subvariety of }\mathcal V(e)\}.
\]
Distinct subvarieties are counted as distinct classes of groups, regardless of their presentations by identities. Here ``at most countably many'' includes finitely many.

Does there exist a single algorithm which, given any group identity \(e\) in a standard finite syntactic encoding, halts and answers yes exactly when
\[
\bigl|\operatorname{Sub}(\mathcal V(e))\bigr|\leq\aleph_0,
\]
and answers no otherwise? The input is one identity, and no restriction to finite groups is imposed.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can an algorithm decide, from a single group identity, whether the variety of all groups satisfying it has only finitely or countably many subvarieties?
