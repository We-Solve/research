\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A limit group is, in the usual algebraic convention, a finitely generated fully residually free group: for every finite subset $S\subseteq G\setminus\{1\}$ there are a nonabelian free group $F$ and a homomorphism $\phi:G\to F$ such that $\phi(s)\ne1$ for every $s\in S$. A CAT(0) group means a group admitting a geometric action on a proper geodesic CAT(0) metric space, where geometric means by isometries, properly, and cocompactly. Properness of the action means that $\{g\in G:gK\cap K\ne\varnothing\}$ is finite for every compact $K\subseteq X$, and cocompactness means $G\backslash X$ is compact.

The source conjecture is
\[
\forall G\quad\bigl(G\text{ is a limit group}\Longrightarrow
\exists X\text{ a proper CAT(0) space admitting a geometric }G\text{-action}\bigr).
\]
This does not require that the action be free or that $G$ be a fundamental group of a specified CAT(0) complex.

The supplied later research summary reports that Alibegovi\'c--Bestvina proved the stronger conclusion that $X$ can have isolated flats. One standard form of this property uses a $G$-invariant family $\mathcal F$ of Euclidean flats of dimension at least two, a constant $B\ge0$ such that every such flat is contained in the $B$-neighborhood of some member of $\mathcal F$, and, for every $r\ge0$, a finite bound $D(r)$ such that
\[
\operatorname{diam}\bigl(N_r(F)\cap N_r(F')\bigr)\le D(r)
\quad\text{for distinct }F,F'\in\mathcal F.
\]
Here a flat is an isometrically embedded Euclidean space and $N_r$ denotes the closed metric neighborhood. The exact isolated-flats convention should be checked against the cited theorem; it is supplementary to the original CAT(0) question. The supplied solved classification and this strengthened theorem are attributed literature claims, not a proof given here.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every finitely generated fully residually free group act geometrically on a CAT(0) space? The supplied record reports an affirmative answer by Alibegovi\'c and Bestvina, with the additional isolated-flats property. It dates their preprint to October 2004, after the question list's July 2004 update, and the journal publication to 2006.
