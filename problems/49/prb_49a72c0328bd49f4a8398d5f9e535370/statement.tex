\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $\mathrm{SH}=\operatorname{Ho}(\mathrm{Sp})$ be the stable homotopy category of spectra, with suspension autoequivalence $\Sigma$ and sphere spectrum $S^0$. For $m\in\Z$, write $S^m=\Sigma^m S^0$, using inverse suspension when $m<0$. The principal question in the source is:
\[
 \text{For every category equivalence }G:\mathrm{SH}\longrightarrow\mathrm{SH},
 \quad\text{does there exist }m\in\Z\text{ with }G\cong\Sigma^m\text{ naturally?}
\]
The word ``self-equivalence'' in the source carries no explicit requirement of exactness, suspension compatibility, enrichment, or symmetric monoidal compatibility. If any such structure is intended, it must be stated as an additional hypothesis rather than silently imposed. The source reports the following partial observation under the additional hypothesis $G\Sigma\cong\Sigma G$: there is an integer $m$ such that $G(S^0)\cong S^m$. This is an assertion about the image of the sphere, not by itself a natural identification on every spectrum.

Namely, must every self-equivalence of either category be naturally isomorphic to an integer power of its suspension?
