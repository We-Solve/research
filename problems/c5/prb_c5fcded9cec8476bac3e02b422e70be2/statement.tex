\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $M$ be a closed smooth manifold. In the standard sectional-curvature convention, almost nonnegative curvature means that for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is a Riemannian metric $g_\varepsilon$ on $M$ such that
\[
 \sec(M,g_\varepsilon)\ge-\varepsilon,
 \qquad\operatorname{diam}(M,g_\varepsilon)\le1.
\]
This is a scale-normalized convention; the report's precise normalization should be confirmed. It does not require a single metric with nonnegative sectional curvature.

The source distinguishes this standard, ``strict $k=0$'' notion from a generalized notion whose defining collapsing sequence is allowed on
\[
 M\times\R^k\qquad\text{for some fixed integer }k\ge0.
\]
The question is whether every closed smooth $M$ satisfying that generalized definition also satisfies the standard definition on $M$ itself. In quantifier form, the existence of an allowed generalized collapsing sequence for some $k$ should imply the existence of metrics on $M$ obeying the displayed curvature and diameter bounds for every $\varepsilon>0$.

The excerpt gives only the product-manifold feature of the generalized definition. It does not give the curvature domains, completeness, basepoints, limit space, convergence notion, or fiber conditions for that sequence. Since $M\times\R^k$ is noncompact for $k>0$, the compact-manifold diameter condition above cannot simply be imposed on the entire product to define the generalized property. Nor is the generalized sequence stated to consist of product metrics. Those details must be recovered from the cited definition rather than supplied by guesswork.

Here ``strict'' labels the unstabilized $k=0$ case; it does not mean strictly positive sectional curvature. The desired implication removes the Euclidean stabilization at the level of the existence property, not necessarily by restricting each particular generalized metric to a slice.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does allowing an auxiliary Euclidean factor in the generalized collapsing definition enlarge the class of closed manifolds with almost nonnegative curvature? The target is the usual unstabilized metric property on $M$, while the full generalized definition is missing.
