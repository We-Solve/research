\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 700873}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
An \(\ell\)-group is a group \(G\), with identity \(e\), equipped with a lattice order such that \(x\leq y\) implies \(axb\leq ayb\) for all \(a,b,x,y\in G\). An \(\ell\)-subgroup is a subgroup closed under the lattice operations. Such a subgroup \(H\) is convex if \(h_1\leq x\leq h_2\), with \(h_1,h_2\in H\) and \(x\in G\), implies \(x\in H\). Let
\[
C(G)=\{H\leq G:H\text{ is a convex }\ell\text{-subgroup}\},
\]
ordered by inclusion. This is a lattice: meets are intersections, and joins are the smallest convex \(\ell\)-subgroups containing the relevant unions.

For \(g\in G\setminus\{e\}\), a value of \(g\) is a convex \(\ell\)-subgroup \(V\) maximal among those not containing \(g\). Its cover is
\[
V^+=\bigcap\{H\in C(G):V\subsetneq H\}.
\]
For a value \(V\) of \(g\), this is the smallest convex \(\ell\)-subgroup containing \(V\cup\{g\}\), and it strictly contains \(V\). Use the standard convention that \(G\) is \emph{normal valued} if every value \(V\) of every nonidentity element is normal in its cover \(V^+\).

Does there exist a normal-valued \(\ell\)-group \(G\) such that, for every abelian \(\ell\)-group \(A\),
\[
C(A)\not\cong C(G),
\]
where \(\cong\) denotes an isomorphism of lattices? No finiteness or cardinality restriction on \(G\) or \(A\) is imposed by the supplied statement.

The supplied source additionally reports that such an example exists when the requirement that \(G\) be normal valued is omitted. This contextual assertion is retained as supplied and is not independently verified here.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is there a normal-valued lattice-ordered group whose lattice of convex lattice-ordered subgroups cannot be realized by any abelian lattice-ordered group? The supplied registry labels this question open and reports an affirmative answer when the normal-valued condition is dropped.
