\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 800315}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(S^1=\R/\Z\), with its standard smooth structure and orientation. Let
\[
G=\operatorname{Diff}^{2}_{+}(S^1)
\]
be the group, under composition, of all orientation-preserving bijections \(f:S^1\to S^1\) such that both \(f\) and \(f^{-1}\) are of class \(C^2\). Equivalently, each element has a \(C^2\) lift \(F:\R\to\R\) satisfying \(F(x+1)=F(x)+1\) and \(F'(x)>0\) for every \(x\in\R\).

An abstract group is simple if it is nontrivial and its only normal subgroups are the identity subgroup and the whole group. The supplied question is: is \(G\) simple? Equivalently, does
\[
\forall N\leq G,\qquad N\trianglelefteq G\ \Longrightarrow\ \bigl(N=\{\operatorname{id}_{S^1}\}\ \text{or}\ N=G\bigr)
\]
hold? Here simplicity is interpreted in the usual abstract group sense, with no closedness requirement on \(N\). The supplied record does not explicitly distinguish abstract simplicity from topological simplicity.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does the group of all orientation-preserving \(C^2\) diffeomorphisms of the circle have no proper nontrivial normal subgroup? Simplicity is understood here in the abstract group sense.
