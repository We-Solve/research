\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
The exact extraction, labelled the equivariant Ax problem in the original, asks whether the following implication holds. Let $X$ be a normal affine $\C$-variety with effective algebraic $G$-action, let $\varphi:X\to X$ be $G$-equivariant and let $\psi:X//G\to X//G$ be induced. Does $\psi$ being an automorphism force $\varphi$ to be an automorphism, \emph{without} assuming $\varphi$ unramified?

If $A=\C[X]$ and the invariant quotient is the intended affine variety $X//G=\operatorname{Spec}(A^G)$, equivariance makes $\varphi^*$ preserve $A^G$ and $\psi^*=\varphi^*|_{A^G}$. Thus the displayed target is to deduce bijectivity of $\varphi^*:A\to A$ from bijectivity on invariants. The group class and finite-generation assumptions for the quotient are not specified; reductive groups provide a standard setting but are not silently added.

\textbf{The literal stated hypotheses are insufficient.} Take $X=\mathbb A^1_\C$, $G=\mathbb G_m$ acting by scalar multiplication, and $\varphi(z)=0$. This is a normal affine variety with an effective algebraic action, and the constant map is equivariant because zero is fixed. Its invariant ring is $\C$, so $X//G$ is a point and $\psi$ is its identity automorphism, while $\varphi$ is not an automorphism. Equivalently $z\mapsto0$ is noninjective on $\C[z]$ but the identity on the invariants. The example even has reductive $G$.
