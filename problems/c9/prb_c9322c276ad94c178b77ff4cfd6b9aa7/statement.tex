\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(K,L\subset\R^n\) be origin-symmetric convex bodies, meaning compact convex sets with nonempty interior satisfying \(K=-K\) and \(L=-L\). Write
\[
S^{n-1}=\{\theta\in\R^n:|\theta|=1\},\qquad
\theta^\perp=\{x\in\R^n:\langle x,\theta\rangle=0\},\qquad
tK=\{tx:x\in K\}.
\]
For a measure with specified density \(d\mu(x)=f(x)\,dx\), define its induced hyperplane measure by
\[
\mu_H(A)=\int_A f(x)\,d\operatorname{vol}_{n-1,H}(x),\qquad A\subset H,
\]
where \(\operatorname{vol}_{n-1,H}\) is Euclidean Lebesgue measure on the hyperplane \(H\). The section notation in the supplied statement is interpreted here as this induced measure. This interpretation requires confirmation: ordinary ambient \(\mu(A)\) is zero for every hyperplane subset \(A\) when \(\mu\) has a Lebesgue density.

A nonnegative density \(f\) is log-concave if
\[
f((1-\lambda)x+\lambda y)\geq f(x)^{1-\lambda}f(y)^\lambda
\quad(x,y\in\R^n,\ 0<\lambda<1).
\]
Rotation invariance means that the specified density has the form \(f(x)=g(|x|)\). The phrase ``strictly decreasing density'' is understood to refer to the radial profile \(g\); the supplied record does not specify whether strict decrease is required on all radii or only on its positive support.

\textbf{Question (1).} For each \(n\in\{5,6\}\), let \(\mu\) be a rotation-invariant log-concave measure with strictly decreasing radial density, and let \(K,L\subset\R^n\) be origin-symmetric convex bodies. Is the implication
\[
\left[
\forall t>0\ \forall\theta\in S^{n-1},\quad
\mu_{\theta^\perp}(tK\cap\theta^\perp)
\leq
\mu_{\theta^\perp}(tL\cap\theta^\perp)
\right]
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mu(K)\leq\mu(L)
\]
valid?

For the second question, the Minkowski functional of an origin-symmetric star body \(M\), with continuous positive radial function and the origin in its interior, is
\[
\|x\|_M=\inf\{a>0:x\in aM\}.
\]
This functional need not be a norm unless \(M\) is convex. In dimensions \(n\geq2\), a classical intersection body \(M=IB\) of an origin-symmetric star body \(B\) satisfies
\[
\rho_M(\theta)=\operatorname{vol}_{n-1,\theta^\perp}(B\cap\theta^\perp),
\qquad
\rho_M(\theta)=\sup\{r\geq0:r\theta\in M\}.
\]
The term ``intersection body'' can also denote the broader class obtained by radial limits of such bodies. The supplied record does not specify which convention is intended.
