\section{Strongly minimal edge cover}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A hypergraph is $H=(V,E)$ with $E$ a family of finite subsets of a possibly infinite set $V$. There are no isolated vertices if $\bigcup E=V$. An edge cover is a subfamily $C\subseteq E$ with $\bigcup C=V$. It is strongly minimal if for every edge cover $C'\subseteq E$ one has $|C\setminus C'|\leq|C'\setminus C|$, where the inequality compares cardinalities, including infinite ones.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Does every such hypergraph admit a strongly minimal edge cover? This recorded finite-edge question has a negative answer in the cited source: the hypergraph consisting of all finite subsets of a countably infinite vertex set is a counterexample. Mere finiteness of each edge must be distinguished from a uniform upper bound on edge sizes. \sref{statement}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Must a hypergraph with finite edges and no isolated vertices have an edge cover that cannot be improved by replacing more edges with fewer? The source gives a counterexample when edge sizes are unbounded.
