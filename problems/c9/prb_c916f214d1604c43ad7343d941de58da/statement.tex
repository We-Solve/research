\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \((M,g)\) be the Riemannian manifold intended in the source and let \(K:M\to\R\) be the prescribed curvature function. Under the usual smooth convention, its conformal class is
\[
[g]=\{e^{2u}g:u\in C^\infty(M,\R)\}.
\]
Writing \(Q_{\widetilde g}\) for the source's chosen \(Q\)-curvature, the problem is to determine whether
\[
\exists u\in C^\infty(M,\R)\quad
Q_{e^{2u}g}(x)=K(x)\quad\text{for all }x\in M.
\]
Equivalently, characterize membership of \(K\) in the conformal curvature range
\[
\mathcal Q([g])=\{Q_{e^{2u}g}:u\in C^\infty(M,\R)\}.
\]
The equality is pointwise on the fixed manifold; it is not equality after an unspecified diffeomorphism, a constant-curvature problem only, or a request for uniqueness.

The dimension, definition and normalization of \(Q\)-curvature, compactness, boundary conditions, and regularity of \(K\) are not specified in the record. Critical \(Q\)-curvature in even dimensions and lower-order or differently normalized versions require different operators and equations, so no particular Paneitz or higher-order conformal PDE is inserted silently. If a boundary is allowed, the admissible conformal factors and boundary data must also be fixed.

The supplied literature triage checked 2026-08-21 reports existence theorems under sign, operator, and geometric hypotheses, as well as explicit obstructions, but no general solution for arbitrary prescribed \(K\) in a conformal class. Thus the source is a prescribed-curvature solvability and characterization question, not an assertion that every function is realizable on every manifold.
