$\Z$ is the ring of integers, $\Q,\R,\C$ are the rational, real, and complex
fields, $[N]=\{1,\ldots,\lfloor N\rfloor\}$, and $\log$ is the natural
logarithm. For real functions of a parameter tending to infinity,
$f\ll g$ and $f=O(g)$ mean $|f|\leq Cg$ eventually for some fixed $C$;
$f\gg g$ reverses this comparison; $f=o(g)$ means $f/g\to0$;
$f\sim g$ means $f/g\to1$; and $f\asymp g$ means both order bounds.

\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Let $k\geq 4$ and $g_k(n)$ denote the largest $m$ such that there is a graph on $n$ vertices with chromatic number $k$ and girth $>m$ (i.e. contains no cycle of length $\leq m$). Does $ \lim_{n\to \infty}\frac{g_k(n)}{\log n} $ exist?
Conversely, if $h^{(m)}(n)$ is the maximal chromatic number of a graph on $n$ vertices with girth $>m$ then does $ \lim_{n\to \infty}\frac{\log h^{(m)}(n)}{\log n} $ exist, and what is its value? \sref{statement}{1}

\paragraph{Residual task reported by the archive.}

The embedded review (2026-08-17) records: Establish the limits and determine their constants. \sref{statement}{1}

\subsection{Short English statement}

Do the natural normalized extremal functions relating graph size, girth and chromatic number have limits, and what are those limits?
