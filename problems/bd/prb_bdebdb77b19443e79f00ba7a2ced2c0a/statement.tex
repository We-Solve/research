\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(d\geq1\) be an integer and let \(G\) be a finite simple \(d\)-regular target graph. In the usual unbiased Maker--Breaker interpretation of \((K_N,G)\), the players claim previously unclaimed edges of \(K_N\), one per move each, and Maker wins when his claimed edges contain a copy of \(G\). The copy need not be induced. A round convention typically counts one Maker move together with the corresponding Breaker move. The source does not reproduce its rules, bias, or first-player convention, so these standard rules must be matched before quantitative conclusions are asserted.

The question asks for infinitely many target graphs for each fixed \(d\), with a winning-time bound linear in the target order and independent of the sufficiently large board size. A natural uniform formulation is: find \(f:\Z_{\geq1}\to(0,\infty)\) and, for each \(d\), infinitely many pairwise nonisomorphic \(d\)-regular graphs \(G\), such that
\[
\forall G\text{ in that family}\quad\exists N_0=N_0(G,d)\quad
\forall N\geq N_0,
\]
Maker has a strategy winning against every legal opponent play within \(f(d)|V(G)|\) rounds. The strategies may depend on \(G,N\), and the board threshold need not be uniform over all targets. No connectedness of \(G\), exact target order, or linear relation between \(N\) and \(|V(G)|\) is required in the extract.

The requested coefficient satisfies
\[
f(d)\leq(1+o(1))d.
\]
Because the statement fixes \(d\) before choosing its graph family but leaves the little-\(o\) variable unmarked, the limiting regime needs confirmation. The natural interpretation for a function of \(d\) is \(d\to\infty\), namely \(\limsup_{d\to\infty}f(d)/d\leq1\). A regime in which the error tends to zero with \(|V(G)|\) for each fixed \(d\) would be a different quantitative statement and is not silently identified with it.

\subsection{Short English statement}
For every fixed degree, seek infinitely many regular targets that Maker can build in a number of rounds bounded by \(f(d)\) times target size on every sufficiently large complete board, with coefficient asymptotically at most \(d\). The game rules and little-\(o\) regime need confirmation.
