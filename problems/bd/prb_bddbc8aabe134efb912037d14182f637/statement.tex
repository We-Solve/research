\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A finite group \(G\) is soluble if its derived series terminates at the trivial group. A formation is a nonempty class of finite groups closed under homomorphic images and finite subdirect products. It is soluble if all its members are soluble, saturated if \(G/\Phi(G)\in\mathfrak F\) implies \(G\in\mathfrak F\), where \(\Phi(G)\) is the Frattini subgroup, and Fitting if it is closed under normal subgroups and under products of normal \(\mathfrak F\)-subgroups of a finite group.

For a finite group \(H\), write \(\ell_{\mathrm{comp}}(H)=r\) when
\[
1=H_0\triangleleft H_1\triangleleft\cdots\triangleleft H_r=H
\]
is a composition series: each inclusion is proper and each factor \(H_i/H_{i-1}\) is simple. The number \(r\) is independent of the composition series; in particular, \(\ell_{\mathrm{comp}}(1)=0\).

Let \(\mathfrak F\) be a soluble saturated Fitting formation. The supplied question asks whether, for finite soluble groups \(G\), there is a bound
\[
 l_{\mathfrak F}(G)\leq f\bigl(c_{\mathfrak F}(G)\bigr),
 \qquad c_{\mathfrak F}(G)=\ell_{\mathrm{comp}}(H),
\]
where \(H\) is some \(\mathfrak F\)-covering subgroup of \(G\), and \(l_{\mathfrak F}(G)\) denotes the \(\mathfrak F\)-length defined in Shemetkov's cited book.
