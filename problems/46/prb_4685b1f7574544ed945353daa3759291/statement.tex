\section{Hyperbolic geometry — Question 10.8}\label{10300046}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 10300046 · Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A surface lamination $\Lambda$ is a closed subset of $M$ locally homeomorphic to a product of a disk with a closed transverse set. It is essential when it has no sphere leaf or torus leaf bounding a solid torus, and its complementary regions are irreducible, have incompressible boundary, and have no compressing monogon. Such a monogon is a compressing disk with one boundary arc on a boundary leaf and the other along a transverse interval fiber. It is genuine if some complementary region is not an interval bundle. A sublamination is a closed union of leaves.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For an essential surface lamination $\Lambda$ in a hyperbolic $3$-manifold $M$, is there an ambient isotopy taking $\Lambda$ to a lamination whose leafwise intrinsic Gaussian curvature, for the metric induced from the fixed hyperbolic metric, is at least $-2$ at every point? Changing the ambient metric to a freely chosen leafwise hyperbolic metric is not the requested operation. \sref{10300046}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can an essential lamination be isotoped within the fixed hyperbolic metric so every leaf has Gaussian curvature at least minus two?
