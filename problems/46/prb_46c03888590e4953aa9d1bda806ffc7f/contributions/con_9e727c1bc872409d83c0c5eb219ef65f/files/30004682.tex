\section{An exponential point-query obstruction}
Equip $[0,1]^d$ with $\ell_\infty$ distance. For an integer $m\ge1$
put $h=1/(2m)$ and place $M=m^d$ centers at points whose
coordinates are $(2j-1)/(2m)$, $1\le j\le m$. Set
\[
 b_c(x)=\max(0,h-\|x-c\|_\infty),\qquad
 f_\sigma(x)=\sum_c\sigma_c b_c(x),\quad \sigma_c\in\{-1,1\}.
\]
The positive supports of distinct bumps are disjoint. Every
$f_\sigma$ is $1$-Lipschitz. Within the same bump this follows
from the reverse triangle inequality; if one point is outside all
bumps it follows by distance to that bump's boundary. If two
points lie in different bumps with centers $c,c'$, some coordinate
of the centers differs by at least $2h$. Writing
$r=\|x-c\|_\infty$, $r'=\|x'-c'\|_\infty$ gives
\[
 \|x-x'\|_\infty\ge2h-r-r'=b_c(x)+b_{c'}(x'),
\]
which bounds even the sum of the two absolute function values.
This proves the claim for either choice of signs.

An adaptive randomized algorithm making fewer than $M$ exact
point evaluations cannot guarantee uniform error less than $h$
with success probability greater than $1/2$ for every member of
this family. To see this, choose the signs independently and
uniformly, independently of the algorithm's random seed.
Each query reveals at most one previously unknown sign; boundary
queries reveal none. Conditional on the complete transcript and
seed, all unqueried signs remain independent fair signs, because
query choices depend only on already revealed signs. Choose an
unqueried center by a fixed ordering. Its reported prediction $z$
is independent of its sign, whereas its true value is $h$ or
$-h$. At least one of $|z-h|,|z+h|$ is at least $h$. Thus the
conditional probability that the uniform error is at least $h$
is at least $1/2$. Average first over transcripts and then over
sign vectors. At least one fixed input function has failure
probability at least $1/2$.

Consequently a worst-case point-evaluation guarantee at any
error $\epsilon<h$ and success probability exceeding $1/2$
requires at least $m^d$ evaluations. This is a concrete curse
for this oracle, norm, and function class; it is not an i.i.d.
regression lower bound under an unstated distribution. The same
input-information obstruction applies to uniform approximation
at time $T$ for the trivial PDE $\partial_tu=0$, $u(0,x)=f(x)$,
whose solution is exactly $u(t,x)=f(x)$. It says nothing against
specified parabolic PDE classes with stronger regularity or
compositional promises.

\section{A structural exception with an explicit network}
Assume instead
\[
 f(x)=\frac1d\sum_{i=1}^d g_i(x_i),
\]
where each $g_i:[0,1]\to\mathbb R$ is $1$-Lipschitz. No
smoothness or differentiability is assumed. Evaluate $f(0)$ and
$f((j/m)e_i)$ for each $i$ and $j=1,\ldots,m$, for a total of
$1+dm$ queries. They determine
\[
 q_i(j/m)=f((j/m)e_i)-f(0)
 =\frac{g_i(j/m)-g_i(0)}d.
\]
Let $\widehat q_i$ be the linear interpolant of these values,
including $q_i(0)=0$, and define
$\widehat f(x)=f(0)+\sum_i\widehat q_i(x_i)$.
For a $1$-Lipschitz function on an interval of length $1/m$,
write $x=(1-t)a+tb$. Its linear interpolation error is at most
\[
 (1-t)|g(x)-g(a)|+t|g(x)-g(b)|
 \le 2t(1-t)/m\le1/(2m).
\]
Divide by $d$ for each $q_i$ and sum to obtain
$\|\widehat f-f\|_\infty\le1/(2m)$.

This interpolant has an exact ReLU representation. If $s_{ij}$
is the slope of $\widehat q_i$ on interval $[j/m,(j+1)/m]$,
then
\[
 \widehat q_i(t)=s_{i0}t+
 \sum_{j=1}^{m-1}(s_{ij}-s_{i,j-1})\max(t-j/m,0).
\]
The derivative on each open interval telescopes to the required
slope, the value at zero is correct, and continuity proves equality
at every knot as well. On $[0,1]$, $t=\max(t,0)$, so including
one linear term per coordinate requires at most $dm$ hidden
units, with a constant output bias. Query and representation
counts are polynomial in $d$ and $1/\epsilon$. This positive
result depends on the additive promise and exact designed
samples; it does not establish statistical training efficiency for
arbitrary deep networks.

\section{Validation with a stated distribution and selection rule}
Let $H\ge1$ candidate predictors be fixed independently of
$N$ fresh validation samples drawn from a common distribution.
Suppose each validation loss lies in $[0,1]$, and write $R_j$
and $\widehat R_j$ for its population and empirical means.
Then
\[
 \Pr\{\max_{j\le H}|\widehat R_j-R_j|>\eta\}
 \le 2H e^{-2N\eta^2}.
\]
Here is a complete exponential-moment proof. For a random variable
$Z\in[0,1]$, the second derivative of
$\log\mathbb E e^{tZ}$ is the variance under its exponentially
tilted law. Any law on $[0,1]$ has variance at most $1/4$,
since its variance is no larger than
$\mathbb E(Z-1/2)^2\le1/4$. Integrating the derivative bound
from zero gives
$\mathbb E e^{t(Z-\mathbb EZ)}\le e^{t^2/8}$ for every real $t$.
Independence, Markov's inequality and $t=4\eta$ bound either
tail of a mean by $e^{-2N\eta^2}$. Union over both tails and
all candidates proves the display.

In particular, taking
$N\ge\log(2H/\delta)/(2\eta^2)$ controls all candidates with
probability at least $1-\delta$. Selecting the smallest validation
loss then has true risk at most the smallest candidate risk plus
$2\eta$, by applying the simultaneous bounds twice. Candidates
may depend on a separate training sample, since the argument can
condition on that sample. It does not cover repeatedly adapting
new candidates to the same validation observations.

Nor can validation from one distribution certify another without
assumptions: a function observed only at $x=0$ can take either
value $1$ or $-1$ at an unobserved $x=1$, with identical
observations at zero. For any prediction $z$ at one,
$\max(|z-1|,|z+1|)\ge1$. Thus a distribution concentrated at
zero cannot resolve test error concentrated at one.
