\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $M$ be a closed connected $3$-manifold admitting a fiber bundle $M\to S^1$ with connected closed orientable surface fiber $F$ of genus $g\ge2$. Equivalently, in this usual fiber convention,
\[
M\cong M_\varphi=(F\times[0,1])/((x,1)\sim(\varphi(x),0))
\]
for a homeomorphism $\varphi:F\to F$. The source does not impose orientation preservation on $\varphi$ or explicitly impose orientability on $M$; neither extra assumption is added. For a compact manifold $X$, set $b_1(X)=\dim_\Q H_1(X;\Q)$.

The question asks whether
\[
\forall M\text{ as above}\quad
\exists\ p:\widetilde M\to M\text{ a connected finite-sheeted cover}
\quad b_1(\widetilde M)>1.
\]
Connectedness of the cover is made explicit in the usual virtual-Betti-number interpretation; allowing arbitrary disconnected covers would change the question. No degree bound is requested.

The supplied research summary reports the stronger affirmative statement
\[
\forall M\text{ as above}\ \forall N\in\Z_{\ge0}\quad
\exists\ p:\widetilde M\to M\text{ a connected finite-sheeted cover}
\quad b_1(\widetilde M)>N,
\]
i.e. infinite virtual first Betti number. It sketches separate pseudo-Anosov, periodic, reducible graph-manifold, and mixed cases, invoking the cited virtual-specialness and virtual-retraction results, orbifold covers in the periodic case, and omitted elementary arguments in the reducible case. The Wang formula mentioned there, in its mapping-torus setting, is
\[
b_1(M_{\varphi^k})=1+\dim_\Q\ker\bigl((\varphi_*)^k-I:H_1(F;\Q)\to H_1(F;\Q)\bigr),\qquad k\ge1.
\]
These cyclic covers with fixed fiber are only one class of finite covers; the unboundedness assertion is about all finite covers. The supplied summary is a reported resolution, not an included complete proof.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Must a closed surface bundle over the circle, with fiber genus at least two, have a finite connected cover with more than one independent rational first-homology class? The supplied summary says yes and reports the stronger result that first Betti numbers of finite covers are unbounded. Its case-by-case proof is only sketched, with some arguments absent from the record.
