\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(\Gamma\) be a discrete group with an affine action on \(\R^5\), given by a homomorphism
\[
 \rho:\Gamma\to\operatorname{Aff}(\R^5)
 =\operatorname{GL}_5(\R)\ltimes\R^5.
\]
Interpret proper discontinuity in the usual proper-action sense: for each compact \(K\subset\R^5\), the set \(\{\gamma\in\Gamma:\rho(\gamma)K\cap K\neq\varnothing\}\) is finite. The source does not separately assume finite generation, torsion-freeness, a free action, cocompactness, or a prescribed linear part.

\emph{Question (Kassel, Mann).} Is \(\Gamma\) virtually an extension of a free group by a solvable group? In the usual extension convention, this means: does there exist a finite-index subgroup \(\Gamma_0\leq\Gamma\) and an exact sequence
\[
 1\longrightarrow S\longrightarrow\Gamma_0\longrightarrow F\longrightarrow1
\]
where \(S\) is solvable and \(F\) is free? Here solvable means its derived series reaches the trivial group in finitely many steps; the free group's rank is not restricted by the excerpt. The source gives no exact sequence to fix the extension convention explicitly. Reversing kernel and quotient would be a different question and is not silently substituted. The dimension remains exactly \(5\).

The supplied summary reports related progress in proper affine actions with semisimple linear parts, Coxeter groups, and surface groups, but no verified complete answer to this abstract \(\R^5\) structure question. It attributes properly affine examples for torsion-free virtually polycyclic groups to Milnor (1977), nonsolvable high-dimensional examples to Abels--Margulis--Soifer, and Lorentzian dimension-three surface-group results essentially to Danciger--Gu\'{e}ritaud--Kassel. These are supplied historical assessments with their stated settings, not a proof of the requested virtual extension for all \(\Gamma\).
\subsection{Short English statement}
Must every group acting properly discontinuously by affine transformations on \(\R^5\) have a finite-index subgroup with a solvable normal subgroup and free quotient, in the usual extension convention? No finite-generation, freeness, or cocompactness hypothesis is added.
