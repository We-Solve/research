\section{Optimal Fair Multiparty Coin Flipping}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Standard number systems are $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$. Specialized notation and index ranges follow the cited source and the definitions below.


In multiparty coin flipping, participants aim to produce a common random bit despite adversarial participants. Fairness restricts the adversary\textquotesingle s ability to bias output or obtain an outcome while preventing honest participants from doing so.

\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Does there exist a fair three-party coin-flipping protocol without the $O(\log^2 m)$ factor? More generally, does there exist a fair protocol for any number of parties against any number of corrupted parties? \sref{statement}{2}
