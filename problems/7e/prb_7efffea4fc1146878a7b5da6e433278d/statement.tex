\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 700625}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(G\) be a topological group: a group with a topology for which multiplication and inversion are continuous. Let \(R\) be a topological ring: an associative ring with a topology for which addition, additive inversion, and multiplication are continuous. Write \((R,\cdot)\) for its multiplicative semigroup, equipped with the topology of \(R\).

Interpret an embedding of \(G\) into \((R,\cdot)\) as an injective map \(\iota:G\to R\) satisfying
\[
\iota(gh)=\iota(g)\iota(h)\qquad(g,h\in G)
\]
that is a homeomorphism from \(G\) onto \(\iota(G)\) with its subspace topology. Denote the set of these maps by \(\operatorname{Emb}(G,(R,\cdot))\).

Under this interpretation, the question is whether
\[
\exists\text{ a topological group }G\quad
\forall\text{ topological rings }R,\qquad
\operatorname{Emb}(G,(R,\cdot))=\varnothing.
\]
The source does not specify separation axioms for either topology or whether rings must have a multiplicative identity. Those conventions must be fixed before this becomes a fully determined statement. The interpretation of embedding as a homeomorphism onto its image also requires confirmation. Since the target is described as a semigroup, no condition identifying \(\iota(e_G)\) with a ring identity is imposed here; multiplicativity only forces \(\iota(e_G)\) to be idempotent.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does there exist a topological group that admits no multiplication-preserving topological embedding into the multiplicative semigroup of any topological ring? The source leaves separation axioms, the ring identity convention, and the precise meaning of embedding unspecified.
