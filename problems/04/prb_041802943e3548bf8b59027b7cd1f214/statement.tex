\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \((X,d)\) be a compact metric space. To make the homotopy hypothesis explicit, choose a basepoint \(x_0\in X\) and suppose that
\[
\pi_k(X,x_0)\neq\{0\}
\qquad\text{for some integer }k\geq 2.
\]
Here \(\pi_k(X,x_0)\) denotes the group of based homotopy classes of continuous maps \((S^k,s_0)\to(X,x_0)\). No connectedness or local regularity assumption on \(X\) is supplied.

\textbf{Question (source Question 2).} What additional conditions on \((X,d)\) guarantee that \(X\) admits a nontrivial harmonic two-sphere, that is, a map
\[
u:S^2\longrightarrow X
\]
that is harmonic and nontrivial in the intended metric-space sense?
