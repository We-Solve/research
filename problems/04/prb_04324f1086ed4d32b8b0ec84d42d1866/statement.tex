\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(X\) be a separable Banach space. All subspaces and linear maps are understood over the scalar field of \(X\); the supplied record does not specify whether this field is \(\R\) or \(\C\). Let \((r_i)\) be independent random variables taking the values \(-1\) and \(1\), each with probability \(1/2\).

For \(1\le p\le2\), say that \(X\) has Rademacher type \(p\) if there exists \(T<\infty\) such that, for every positive integer \(n\) and every \(x_1,\ldots,x_n\in X\),
\[
\left(\E\left\|\sum_{i=1}^n r_i x_i\right\|^2\right)^{1/2}
\le T\left(\sum_{i=1}^n\|x_i\|^p\right)^{1/p}.
\]
For \(2\le q<\infty\), say that \(X\) has Rademacher cotype \(q\) if there exists \(C<\infty\) such that, for every such finite sequence,
\[
\left(\sum_{i=1}^n\|x_i\|^q\right)^{1/q}
\le C\left(\E\left\|\sum_{i=1}^n r_i x_i\right\|^2\right)^{1/2}.
\]
Two Banach spaces are isomorphic if there is a bounded linear bijection between them with bounded inverse.
