\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For \(\alpha\in\Q\), define
\[
A_\alpha=\begin{pmatrix}1&\alpha\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
B_\alpha=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\\alpha&1\end{pmatrix}
\in\operatorname{SL}_2(\Q),
\]
and let \(G_\alpha=\langle A_\alpha,B_\alpha\rangle\). Let \(F(a,b)\) be the free group on two generators, and let
\[
\varphi_\alpha:F(a,b)\longrightarrow G_\alpha,
\qquad a\longmapsto A_\alpha,\quad b\longmapsto B_\alpha
\]
be the homomorphism determined by these images.

Under the intended interpretation that the displayed matrices freely generate a group of rank two, the question is whether
\[
\exists\alpha\in\Q\quad\bigl(|\alpha|<2\ \text{ and }\ \ker\varphi_\alpha=\{1\}\bigr).
\]
Equivalently, every nonempty reduced word in \(a^{\pm1},b^{\pm1}\) must evaluate at \(A_\alpha,B_\alpha\) to a matrix different from \(I_2\).

The source says only ``generate a free group'' and does not explicitly specify rank two or exclude \(\alpha=0\). This qualification is unresolved: at \(\alpha=0\), the generated group is trivial, which is a free group of rank zero under the usual convention. The displayed injectivity formulation makes the intended nonabelian reading explicit, but that reading requires confirmation.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is there a rational parameter \(\alpha\) with \(|\alpha|<2\) for which the two displayed unipotent matrices freely generate a free group of rank two? The source does not explicitly specify the rank; allowing the trivial free group would admit \(\alpha=0\).
