\section*{Criticality in neural systems}
\label{prob:108}
\noindent\textbf{Status: restricted result or obstruction; full target not established.}
\subsection{Target and scope}
\paragraph{Catalogue definitions and assumptions.}
Let $K_g$ be a branching-process or recurrent-network transition kernel indexed by a measured gain $g$, with external drive $h$ and population size $N$. Observe spikes through a known sampling mask. Define $g_c$ through divergence of susceptibility in an explicitly specified infinite-size, vanishing-drive limit; an avalanche is a run of occupied time bins under a declared binning rule. Task performance $J(g)$ is measured independently of avalanche statistics.
\paragraph{Original target.}
Estimate distance to the model-specific transition and test whether moving toward it improves a specified task:
\[
\chi_N(g)=\partial_h\mathbb{E}[A\mid g,h,N],\qquad
J(g_c-\delta)-J(g_0)>\eta,
\]
where $A$ is normalised activity, $\eta>0$ is pre-specified and the comparison keeps input statistics and metabolic cost controlled. Test finite-size scaling against driven noncritical alternatives.
\paragraph{Catalogue alternative formulations.}
(i) Analyse a stationary network with fixed $g$, estimating susceptibility and correlation length. (ii) Test a self-tuning adaptive network, asking whether its gain converges near a critical region without external parameter adjustment. These are distinct: a network can be tuned to a transition without possessing a biological homeostatic mechanism that maintains it.
\subsection{Attempted answer}
\begin{proposition}
For a Galton--Watson activity model with mean offspring $m<1$ and independent immigration mean $h$ per step, a finite stationary mean, when a stationary law exists, equals $h/(1-m)$ and its susceptibility to $h$ is $1/(1-m)$. At $m=1$ and $h>0$, no finite stationary mean can satisfy the balance equation.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Conditional expectation gives $\mathbb{E} A_{t+1}=m\mathbb{E} A_t+h$. Solve the affine recursion or impose stationarity. Differentiate the subcritical expression. Finite population saturation changes this recursion and can remove the divergence.
\end{proof}
\subsection{What remains unresolved}
This is a model-specific susceptibility, not proof of neural criticality or beneficial computation. Sampling masks, drive, finite-size scaling and the independent task-performance comparison remain unresolved.
\subsection{Completion Estimate}
\textbf{20\%.} The displayed calculation is established under its additional assumptions. The broader theoretical and biological requirements listed above are not established.

\section{Shared mathematical bounds}\label{app:bounds}
\begin{lemma}[Independent bounded sums]\label{lem:bounded}
Let independent real variables $X_i$ satisfy $a_i\leq X_i\leq b_i$ almost surely,
and let $S=\sum_{i=1}^n(X_i-\mathbb{E} X_i)$. If
$V=\sum_i(b_i-a_i)^2>0$, then for every $t>0$,
\[
 \mathbb{P}(|S|\geq t)\leq 2\exp(-2t^2/V).
\]
In particular, independent losses in $[0,1]$ have mean-error bound
$\mathbb{P}(|\overline X-\mathbb{E}\overline X|\geq\epsilon)
\leq2e^{-2n\epsilon^2}$. The same expression for variables in $[-1,1]$
is $2e^{-n\epsilon^2/2}$. If $V=0$ then $S=0$ almost surely.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $X\in[a,b]$ with mean $\mu$, the nonnegative product
$(X-a)(b-X)$ gives
$\operatorname{Var} X\leq(\mu-a)(b-\mu)\leq(b-a)^2/4$.
Let $K(s)=\log\mathbb{E}\exp[s(X-\mathbb{E} X)]$.
Boundedness permits two differentiations. Its second derivative is the variance
under the exponentially tilted law, which has the same support, so
$K''(s)\leq(b-a)^2/4$ for every real $s$.
Because $K(0)=K'(0)=0$, integration yields
$K(s)\leq s^2(b-a)^2/8$.
Independence adds these log moment-generating bounds for $S$.
For $s>0$, Markov's inequality gives
$\mathbb{P}(S\geq t)\leq\exp(-st+s^2V/8)$.
Choosing $s=4t/V$ yields the upper-tail bound $e^{-2t^2/V}$.
Apply it also to $-S$ and add. The stated mean versions follow by substituting
$t=n\epsilon$ and the respective support widths.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Finite-family extension.}
For $M$ prespecified bounded-loss comparisons, apply the relevant inequality to
each and use a union bound. Independence between comparisons is not required;
independence of the biological units within each comparison is. Repeated cells,
reads or time points from one dependent biological unit cannot automatically be
counted as independent units.

\paragraph{No data-dependent shortcut.}
The bound does not justify choosing a model or validation loss using the same
outcomes and then reporting single-model coverage. A prespecified finite family,
a separate validation sample, or an appropriate complexity or sequential argument
is needed. Marginal predictive coverage likewise should not be read as coverage
for every subgroup or under arbitrary distribution shift.
