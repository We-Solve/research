\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(p\) be a prime. A group \(G\) is a locally finite \(p\)-group if every finitely generated subgroup of \(G\) is a finite group of order a power of \(p\). Such a group has finite exponent if there is an integer \(n\geq 1\) such that \(x^{p^n}=1\) for every \(x\in G\). It has exponent exactly \(p\) if \(x^p=1\) for every \(x\in G\) and \(G\neq\{1\}\).

For a subgroup \(A\leq G\), put \(A^g=g^{-1}Ag\) for \(g\in G\). The requirement that \(G\) be the union of the conjugates of \(A\) means
\[
G=\bigcup_{g\in G}A^g,
\]
equivalently, every element of \(G\) is conjugate to an element of \(A\).

\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] Does there exist a locally finite \(p\)-group \(G\) of finite exponent with an abelian proper subgroup \(A<G\) such that
\[
G=\bigcup_{g\in G}g^{-1}Ag?
\]
\item[(b)] Does there exist such a pair \((G,A)\) with \(G\) of exponent exactly \(p\), retaining local finiteness, the requirement that \(A\) be abelian and proper, and the displayed covering condition?
\end{enumerate}

These questions are stated with \(p\) as a prime parameter. The supplied record does not specify whether an example is sought for some prime or separately for each prime; this qualification remains unresolved.
\subsection{Short English statement}
G. Cutolo asks whether a locally finite \(p\)-group of finite exponent can be covered by the conjugates of one proper abelian subgroup, and whether this can occur already at exponent \(p\). The intended quantification over primes is not explicit in the supplied record.
