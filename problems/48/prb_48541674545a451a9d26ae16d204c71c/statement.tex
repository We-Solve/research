\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(n\geq 1\) be an integer, and let \(M=\partial D\subset\R^{2n}\) be the smooth boundary of a star-shaped domain, as in the supplied statement. A conventional interpretation of its Reeb flow uses coordinates \((x_1,y_1,\ldots,x_n,y_n)\) and the one-form
\[
\lambda_0=\frac12\sum_{j=1}^n(x_j\,\dd y_j-y_j\,\dd x_j),\qquad \alpha=\lambda_0|_M.
\]
This interpretation requires \(\alpha\) to be a contact form, meaning \(\alpha\wedge(\dd\alpha)^{n-1}\) is nowhere zero. Its Reeb vector field \(R_\alpha\) is uniquely characterized by
\[
\alpha(R_\alpha)=1,\qquad \dd\alpha(R_\alpha,v)=0\quad\text{for every }v\in TM.
\]
Write \(\varphi_\alpha^t\) for its flow. A prime closed orbit is a periodic trajectory traversed with its least positive period: it is represented by \(p\in M\) and \(T>0\) such that \(\varphi_\alpha^T(p)=p\) and \(\varphi_\alpha^t(p)\ne p\) for \(0<t<T\). Count two such representatives as the same orbit exactly when their trajectory images agree. Thus time shifts and iterated traversals do not contribute additional prime closed orbits. Let \(\mathcal P(M,\alpha)\) denote this set of geometrically distinct prime closed orbits. The supplied passage calls the flow a Reeb pseudo-rotation when \(\mathcal P(M,\alpha)\) is finite.

The proposed \(n\)-or-\(\infty\) assertion asks whether, for every dimension and every hypersurface in the intended class,
\[
\#\mathcal P(M,\alpha)<\infty
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\#\mathcal P(M,\alpha)=n.
\]
Equivalently, must the number of prime closed orbits always be either \(n\) or infinite? The general question supplies no dynamical convexity, non-degeneracy, or central symmetry assumption.

The geometric class and contact form require clarification. The record does not explicitly say that \(D\) is bounded, that star-shapedness is with respect to the origin, or that the radial vector field is everywhere transverse to \(M\). In particular, smooth star-shapedness alone does not explicitly provide the strict transversality needed for the displayed restriction of \(\lambda_0\) to be contact. The formula above records the conventional interpretation, rather than resolving these missing specifications. The original passage also qualifies the proposed scope: it expects the dichotomy for a very broad class of Reeb flows, but possibly not all. Consequently, the universal formulation in the clean statement remains a proposed target whose intended scope needs review.
\subsection{Short English statement}
The question asks whether a Reeb flow on the smooth boundary of a star-shaped domain in \(\R^{2n}\), with only finitely many geometrically distinct prime closed orbits, must have exactly \(n\). Iterates are not counted separately. The intended contact form and geometric hypotheses need clarification, and the source itself allows that the conjecture may require a restricted class of flows.
