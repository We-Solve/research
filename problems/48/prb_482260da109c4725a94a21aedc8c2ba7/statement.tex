\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(K\) be an infinite field, with no restriction on its characteristic. Choose a separable closure \(K^{\mathrm{sep}}\), and equip the absolute Galois group
\[
G_K=\operatorname{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{sep}}/K)
\]
with its Krull topology. Say that \(G_K\) is topologically finitely generated if there exist an integer \(d\geq 0\) and elements \(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_d\in G_K\) such that
\[
\overline{\langle\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_d\rangle}=G_K.
\]
A smooth geometrically integral \(K\)-curve is a smooth, separated scheme of finite type and dimension one over \(K\) that remains integral after extension to an algebraic closure of \(K\). Write \(C(K)\) for its set of \(K\)-rational points. The field \(K\) is called \emph{ample} if every such curve \(C\) satisfies
\[
C(K)\neq\varnothing\quad\Longrightarrow\quad C(K)\text{ is infinite}.
\]
No projectivity assumption is imposed on \(C\).

\textbf{Question (Bary-Soroker and Fehm, Question 4).} Is every infinite field with topologically finitely generated absolute Galois group ample? Explicitly, is it true that, for every infinite field \(K\),
\[
G_K\text{ topologically finitely generated}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\bigl(\forall\text{ smooth geometrically integral }K\text{-curves }C\bigr)
\bigl(C(K)\neq\varnothing\Longrightarrow C(K)\text{ is infinite}\bigr)?
\]
The supplied source says that this question occurs as a conjecture in the reference labelled \texttt{[JunkerKoenigsmann]}; the corresponding bibliographic entry is not supplied.

As motivation, the supplied source states that the absolute Galois groups of \(\Q_p\), for primes \(p\), and of \(\R\) and \(\C\) are topologically finitely generated and prosolvable, where prosolvable means an inverse limit of finite solvable groups. Prosolvability is contextual motivation, not an additional hypothesis of the question. The registry labels the question open; that supplied status is not an independently verified assessment.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Must every infinite field whose absolute Galois group has a finite set of topological generators be ample, meaning that every smooth geometrically integral curve over the field with one rational point has infinitely many rational points? The supplied source attributes an earlier conjectural formulation to \texttt{[JunkerKoenigsmann]}, whose bibliographic details are missing.
