\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.


For an integer matrix $A$, the fiber at $b$ is $\mathcal F_b=\{x\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^d:Ax=b\}$. A Markov basis is a finite set of integer moves $u$ with $Au=0$ such that steps $x\mapsto x\pm u$ staying nonnegative connect every nonempty fiber. The no-three-way-interaction model fixes all two-way margins of an $I\times J\times K$ table. A Markov basis is minimal if removing any move destroys its universal fiber-connectivity property. Structural zeros forbid specified cells; the accompanying questions ask how regular zero patterns and circuits affect this basis.

\section{Attempt in a fully specified subfamily}
Take the full $2$ by $J$ by $K$ model, $J,K\ge1$. Fix every two-way
margin. Writing $x_{jk}=T_{0jk}$ and $t_{jk}=T_{0jk}+T_{1jk}$, the
fiber becomes the bounded two-way tables with fixed row and column sums
and $0\le x_{jk}\le t_{jk}$. The second layer is $t-x$. All moves
below change the first layer by $u$ and the second by $-u$.

\paragraph{Cycle connectivity with coordinate bounds.}
Include every simple even cycle of the complete bipartite graph on the
$J$ row and $K$ column vertices, with alternating $+1,-1$ entries on
its edges. Let $x,y$ be two tables in the fiber and put $d=y-x$.
The signed matrix $d$ has zero row and column sums. Orient each nonzero
edge from its row to its column if $d>0$, and from column to row if
$d<0$, assigning integer flow $|d|$. Every vertex has equal incoming
and outgoing flow. Starting on any edge and repeatedly following an
outgoing edge eventually finds a directed cycle; remove repetitions to
obtain a simple directed cycle. Its signs alternate because the graph
is bipartite. Add its alternating unit move to $x$, toward $y$ on
every affected coordinate. The step remains between $x$ and $y$
coordinatewise, hence respects both zero and upper bounds. It decreases
$\sum|y-x|$ by the cycle length. Iteration terminates at $y$.
Thus the lifted simple cycles form a Markov basis for every such fiber.

\paragraph{Every cycle is indispensable, even if it has ambient chords.}
Fix one simple cycle. Set $t_{jk}=1$ on that cycle and zero off it.
Set first-layer row and column sums to one at cycle vertices and zero
elsewhere. The feasible first layers are perfect matchings of this
cycle. There are exactly two: choosing either of the two incident edges
at one vertex forces the next choice, and continuing around the even
cycle is consistent and forces every edge. Thus the full three-way
fiber has two states, differing by the lifted cycle move. Any universal
basis must contain this difference up to sign, because no other state
can occur on a connecting path. Ambient chords are unavailable since
their $t$ entries are zero. The displayed basis is therefore the unique
minimal one, up to signs, for every $2$ by $J$ by $K$ full model.
For $J=1$ or $K=1$, the margins determine the table and the empty
move set is the unique minimal basis.

\paragraph{Attempted extension and its first obstruction.}
The proof relies on one free layer and its complementary layer $t-x$.
For three or more layers, subtracting one layer leaves a multi-layer
allocation whose separate two-way margins are still coupled; a cycle
adjustment in one layer need not have a feasible compensating allocation.
The two-slice flow proof does not supply those allocations. Therefore it
does not establish uniqueness in arbitrary $I$ by $J$ by $K$ format.
Nor does an arbitrary lattice generating set imply universal Markov
connectivity at the boundary; the two-point fibers show why individual
cycle moves can be forced despite algebraic relations among them.
