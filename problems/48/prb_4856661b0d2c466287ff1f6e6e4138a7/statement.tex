\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
The exact source asks whether there is a finite graph in which every edge belongs to at least two triangles and which is ``locally a tree of triangles.'' It credits the question as solved by J. Verstraete and separately mentions Ne\v{s}et\v{r}il--R\"odl partial results, without giving the definition of local, a locality parameter, or a proof. Nontriviality is also implicit: an edgeless graph would satisfy the edge condition vacuously.

The supplied aid introduces an \(\ell\)-local formulation. An explicit working interpretation for its partial result is as follows: graphs are finite, simple and undirected; a \(2\)-tree is obtained from a triangle by repeatedly adjoining a new vertex joined to the endpoints of an existing edge; a partial \(2\)-tree is a subgraph of a \(2\)-tree. Require every induced subgraph on at most \(\ell\) vertices to be a partial \(2\)-tree, and require at least one edge and
\[
 |N_G(u)\cap N_G(v)|\ge2\qquad(uv\in E(G)).
\]
The possible general existence question is then whether such a graph exists for every prescribed \(\ell\). This is a specified interpretation of the aid's recovered terminology, not an independently source-verified expansion of the original sentence; local neighborhoods or other notions of triangle trees could lead to a different question.

Under this interpretation, the aid reports that for \(\ell\ge3\), every connected component \(H\) containing an edge has
\[
 \delta(H)\ge\ell,
 \qquad
 |V(H)|\ge1+\left\lceil
 \frac{\ell^2(\ell-1)}{2(2\ell-3)}\right\rceil.
\]
It attributes the first obstruction to cycles in vertex links and the \(K_4\)-minor obstruction for partial \(2\)-trees, and the order bound to averaging the \(2\ell-3\) edge bound for \(\ell\)-vertex partial \(2\)-trees. The asserted order bound is sharp at \(\ell=3\), witnessed by \(K_4\), and at \(\ell=4\), witnessed by the complete tripartite graph \(K_{2,2,2}\). For \(\ell=1,2\), it reports the minimum order of a nontrivial component to be four. Isolated vertices are not subject to the minimum-degree assertion.

These are necessary bounds and small-case witnesses. They do not reconstruct Verstraete's credited existence proof for arbitrary locality or resolve the ambiguity in the source's local condition. The record's solved label and the aid's partial-result label therefore have distinct scopes.
