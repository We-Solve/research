\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(\mathcal R=(R;0,1,+,-,\cdot,<,\exp)\) be an expansion of an ordered field by a unary function \(\exp:R\to R\). Equip \(R\) with its order topology. Assume that \(\exp\) is everywhere continuous and differentiable, with
\[
\exp(0)=1,\qquad \exp'(x)=\exp(x)\quad\text{for every }x\in R.
\]
Here differentiability is understood over the ordered field: for every \(x\in R\) and \(\varepsilon>0\), there exists \(\delta>0\) such that
\[
0<|h|<\delta\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\left|\frac{\exp(x+h)-\exp(x)}{h}-\exp(x)\right|<\varepsilon.
\]
No Archimedean assumption is imposed on \(R\).

All definability below is in the displayed language and allows parameters from \(R\). The structure \(\mathcal R\) is \emph{o-minimal} if every definable subset of \(R\) is a finite union of singletons and open intervals, allowing interval endpoints in \(R\cup\{-\infty,+\infty\}\). It is \emph{definably complete} if every nonempty definable subset \(A\subseteq R\) that is bounded above has a least upper bound in \(R\).

Let
\[
\mathbb R_{\exp}=(\mathbb R;0,1,+,-,\cdot,<,x\mapsto e^x)
\]
be the usual real exponential field. Elementary equivalence \(\mathcal R\equiv\mathbb R_{\exp}\) means that these structures satisfy exactly the same first-order sentences in the displayed language.

The question is whether, for every \(\mathcal R\) satisfying the hypotheses above, the following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(\mathcal R\equiv\mathbb R_{\exp}\).
\item \(\mathcal R\) is o-minimal.
\item \(\mathcal R\) is definably complete.
\end{enumerate}
