\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field, and let $S=(\operatorname{Spec}k,M_k)$ be equipped with a fine log structure. A log structure is a sheaf of monoids $M\to\mathcal O$ inducing an isomorphism on units; fine means that locally it admits charts by finitely generated integral monoids. Fix proper semistable log varieties $(X,M_X)$ and $(Y,M_Y)$ whose structural morphisms to $S$ are log smooth and integral in the logarithmic sense. Assume $(Y,M_Y)$ is of log general type, with that notion taken as in the report.

The finiteness question is
\[
 \#\{f:(X,M_X)\dashrightarrow(Y,M_Y)
       \text{ dominant separable log rational maps over }S\}<\infty.
\]
This asks for finiteness for every fixed pair satisfying the source hypotheses, not for a uniform numerical bound as $X$ and $Y$ vary. No characteristic-zero restriction is imposed: the supplied context explicitly mentions a positive-characteristic generalization, while the statement itself places no restriction on $\operatorname{char}k$.

A standard working meaning of a log rational map is a log morphism over $S$ on a dense open subspace of $X$, identified with another such representative when they agree on a common dense open subspace. For irreducible underlying varieties, dominant and separable have their usual function-field meanings: the map induces an inclusion $k(Y)\hookrightarrow k(X)$ and that extension is separably generated. Semistable objects can be reducible, however, so the report's componentwise dominance and separability conventions must be recovered. The meaning of log general type, often expressed by bigness of an appropriate logarithmic canonical object, likewise requires its precise report definition.

The adjectives smooth and integral over the log point are interpreted as properties of the logarithmic structural morphisms. They are not silently converted into ordinary smoothness or irreducibility of the underlying schemes.
