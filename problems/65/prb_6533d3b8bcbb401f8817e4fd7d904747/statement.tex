\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
In the usual meromorphic interpretation, let $f$ be a nonconstant meromorphic function on $\C$. Write $T(r,f)$ for its Nevanlinna characteristic and $N(r,a)$ for the integrated counting function of $a$-points, counted with multiplicity, with poles used when $a=\infty$. Concretely, $T(r,f)=m(r,f)+N(r,\infty)$, where
\[
m(r,f)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}\log^+|f(re^{it})|\,dt.
\]
For $a\in\widehat\C$, the Valiron deficiency is conventionally
\[
\Delta(a,f)=1-\liminf_{r\to\infty}\frac{N(r,a)}{T(r,f)},
\qquad E_V(f)=\{a:\Delta(a,f)>0\}.
\]
This uses a liminf, in contrast to the usual Nevanlinna deficiency's limsup. The supplied phrase ``set of Valiron deficiencies'' is interpreted as the set of deficient values; the source's preceding definitions are not reproduced and must be checked.

Hayman's Problem 1.2 asks how large $E_V(f)$ can be for functions in the plane, and whether the stated estimate can be sharpened. The supplied statement records the known estimate
\[
N(r,a)=T(r,f)+O\!\left(T(r,f)^{1/2+\varepsilon}\right)
\qquad(r\to\infty)
\]
