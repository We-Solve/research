\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Fix the $14$-omino
\[
 T=([2,6]\times[0,3])\ \cup\ ([0,2]\times[2,3])\subset\R^2.
\]
It is a $3\times6$ rectangle with a $2\times2$ corner removed, with its polygonal boundary retained. Thus $\operatorname{area}(T)=14$. Removing any other corner gives a congruent tile.

The question is whether there exist a positive odd integer $N$, real numbers $a,b>0$, and congruent copies $T_1,\ldots,T_N$ of $T$ such that
\[
 [0,a]\times[0,b]=\bigcup_{i=1}^N T_i,
 \qquad \operatorname{int}(T_i)\cap\operatorname{int}(T_j)=\varnothing
 \quad(i\ne j).
\]
For the literal Euclidean-congruence reading, each $T_i=g_i(T)$ for a plane isometry $g_i$; this allows rotations and reflections. The copies have the same scale as $T$. Necessarily the rectangle area is $ab=14N$; the rectangle's aspect ratio is not prescribed.

The source does not explicitly say whether the intended polyomino tilings must respect a common square lattice or whether reflections are disallowed. Those restrictions would define potentially different variants and are not silently added to the literal congruence formulation. Despite the source title's word ``rep-tiling,'' the supplied question asks to tile a rectangle, not necessarily a larger similar copy of $T$.
