\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $X/\Q$ be a smooth, absolutely irreducible projective curve with genus $g\geq2$, let $x_0\in X(\Q)$, and let $p$ be a prime of good reduction for $X$. Let $\Pi_{\mathrm{et}}$ be the pro-unipotent $\Q_p$-\'etale fundamental group of $X_{\overline{\Q}}$ based at $x_0$, with its Galois action, and let $\Pi_{\mathrm{dR}}$ be its de Rham realization, considered over $\Q_p$. In the usual lower-central-series truncation, $\Pi_{*,n}=\Pi_*/\Pi_*^{n+1}$, where $\Pi_*^1=\Pi_*$ and $\Pi_*^{j+1}=[\Pi_*,\Pi_*^j]$ with the appropriate closure. This indexing convention is not expressly given in the extract. Write $H_f^1(\Q,\Pi_{\mathrm{et},n})$ for the nonabelian Selmer variety used in the source, parameterizing the corresponding Galois torsors subject to its finite/crystalline and ramification conditions. The subscript $f$ requires those local conditions; their exact prescription, especially at bad primes, is not supplied here. Let $F^0\Pi_{\mathrm{dR},n}$ be the subgroup given by the Hodge filtration. The notation $\Pi_{\mathrm{dR},n}/F^0$ denotes the quotient variety by this subgroup, rather than a quotient of vector spaces. Kim's conjecture in the supplied record asks whether, for every such $(X,x_0,p)$, \[\exists n_0=n_0(X,x_0,p)\ \forall n\geq n_0:\qquad \dim H_f^1(\Q,\Pi_{\mathrm{et},n})<\dim\bigl(\Pi_{\mathrm{dR},n}/F^0\Pi_{\mathrm{dR},n}\bigr).\] Dimensions are algebraic-variety dimensions over $\Q_p$ in this standard realization. The quantifier is all sufficiently large levels, not just the existence of one level, and no uniform bound on $n_0$ is requested. The supplied 2026-08-21 triage labels the general conjecture open, reports important special cases and conditional results, and describes it as a conjectural input to the Chabauty--Kim method.
\subsection{Short English statement}
For every pointed projective curve over $\Q$ of genus at least two and every good prime, ask whether the global nonabelian Selmer variety eventually has strictly smaller dimension than the de Rham quotient. The source's local Selmer conditions and truncation convention need confirmation.
