\section{Resistance bounds for finite vertex-transitive graphs}\label{10000059}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 10000059 · Graph Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $G$ be a finite connected simple vertex-transitive $d$-regular graph, with $N=|V(G)|\ge2$ and diameter $\operatorname{diam}(G)$ for unit edges. Give every edge electrical resistance $1$. The effective resistance $R_G(u,v)$ is the voltage drop between $u,v$ when unit current enters at $u$ and exits at $v$ (and $R_G(u,u)=0$).
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For each degree $d$, is there a constant $C_d<\infty$ depending only on $d$ such that
\[R_G(u,v)<C_d+\frac{\operatorname{diam}(G)^2\log N}{N}\quad(u,v\in V(G))?\]
In addition, for every sequence of finite connected vertex-transitive graphs $G_n$ with $\operatorname{diam}(G_n)=o(|V(G_n)|)$, prove
\[\max_{u,v\in V(G_n)}R_{G_n}(u,v)=o(\operatorname{diam}(G_n)).\] \sref{10000059}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Bound all pairwise resistances in finite transitive graphs by degree and diameter, and show resistance is negligible relative to sublinear diameter.
