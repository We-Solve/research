\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(\operatorname{End}(\C,0)\) denote the set of germs at \(0\) of holomorphic maps \(f\) satisfying \(f(0)=0\). The multiplier of such a germ is \(\lambda_f=f'(0)\). A Cremer fixed point is an irrationally indifferent fixed point at which the germ is not holomorphically linearizable: \(\lambda_f=e^{2\pi i\theta_f}\) for some \(\theta_f\in\R\setminus\Q\), and there is no local biholomorphic change of coordinates \(\phi\), with \(\phi(0)=0\), satisfying
\[
\phi\circ f\circ\phi^{-1}(z)=\lambda_f z
\]
as germs at \(0\).

A local topological conjugacy between germs \(f\) and \(g\) is a germ of a homeomorphism \(h\) between neighborhoods of \(0\), fixing \(0\), such that \(h\circ f=g\circ h\) wherever these compositions are defined sufficiently near \(0\). The supplied excerpt does not specify whether the allowed homeomorphisms must preserve orientation; this convention must be fixed before asserting that the multiplier is a topological invariant.

\textbf{Problem OP3 (Abate, 2011).} Are the multiplier and the homeomorphism class of the stable set a complete set of topological invariants for germs in \(\operatorname{End}(\C,0)\) having a Cremer fixed point at the origin?

More explicitly, write \(\mathcal S(f)\) for the stable set intended by the source and \([\mathcal S(f)]_{\mathrm{homeo}}\) for its intended homeomorphism class. Subject to fixing those meanings and the conjugacy convention, the question asks whether, for every pair of Cremer germs \(f,g\in\operatorname{End}(\C,0)\),

f\text{ and }g\text{ are locally topologically conjugate}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\bigl(\lambda_f=\lambda_g\ \text{ and }\ [\mathcal S(f)]_{\mathrm{homeo}}=[\mathcal S(g)]_{\mathrm{homeo}}\bigr).
\]
