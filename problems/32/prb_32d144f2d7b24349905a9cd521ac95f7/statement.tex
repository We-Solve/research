\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \((X,d)\) be a compact uniformly perfect metric space. One standard uniform-perfectness convention requires a constant \(c>1\) such that, whenever \(X\setminus B_d(x,r)\ne\varnothing\), the annulus \(B_d(x,r)\setminus B_d(x,r/c)\) is nonempty. The source gives no further doubling, regularity or Poincar\'e hypothesis.

In the usual quasisymmetric convention, the conformal gauge \(\mathcal J(X,d)\) consists of metrics \(\rho\) on \(X\) for which the identity from \((X,d)\) to \((X,\rho)\) is quasisymmetric: there exists an increasing homeomorphism \(\eta:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)\) such that
\[
 \frac{\rho(x,a)}{\rho(x,b)}\leq
 \eta\left(\frac{d(x,a)}{d(x,b)}\right)
\]
for all distinct \(x,a,b\). The ordinary conformal dimension is then
\[
 \operatorname{Cdim}(X,d)=\inf_{\rho\in\mathcal J(X,d)}\dim_H(X,\rho).
\]
These standard conventions make explicit one meaning of the displayed terms; the record does not itself define the gauge. Restricting the infimum to Ahlfors-regular metrics would instead give an Ahlfors-regular conformal-dimension variant and is not silently imposed.

Retain \(p(\mathcal J(X,d))\) for the source's critical exponent of first \(\ell^p\)-cohomology over that gauge. The question is whether
\[
 \forall (X,d)\text{ compact and uniformly perfect},\qquad
 \operatorname{Cdim}(X,d)=p(\mathcal J(X,d)).
\]
The critical exponent is not defined in the record. To recover it one needs the graph, filling or other cochain model attached to the gauge, the class of \(p\), coefficient and summability conventions, and whether cohomology is reduced. A threshold for nonzero cohomology, a threshold for boundary functions separating points, and a threshold involving a reduced cohomology group are different definitions.
