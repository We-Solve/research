\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 30003857}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \((M,h)\) be a compact, connected, simply connected Riemannian symmetric space without boundary: at every point \(p\in M\), there is an isometry of \((M,h)\) fixing \(p\) whose differential at \(p\) is \(-\mathrm{id}_{T_pM}\). Its rank \(\operatorname{rank}(M,h)\) is the maximal dimension of a flat totally geodesic submanifold. For a smooth Riemannian metric \(g\) on \(M\), positive sectional curvature means
\[
K_g(\sigma)>0
\quad\text{for every }p\in M\text{ and every two-dimensional linear subspace }\sigma\subseteq T_pM.
\]
The rank condition concerns the symmetric metric \(h\); the sought metric \(g\) is arbitrary and is not required to be symmetric, homogeneous, or invariant under the isometries of \(h\).

\textbf{Question.} Does there exist such a space \((M,h)\) with \(\operatorname{rank}(M,h)>1\) whose underlying smooth manifold admits a smooth Riemannian metric of positive sectional curvature?

\textbf{Conjecture recorded in the source.} For every compact, connected, simply connected Riemannian symmetric space \((M,h)\) of rank greater than one, no such metric \(g\) exists. Equivalently, every smooth Riemannian metric \(g\) on every such \(M\) satisfies
\[
\exists p\in M\;\exists\sigma\subseteq T_pM,
\qquad \dim\sigma=2\quad\text{and}\quad K_g(\sigma)\leq 0.
\]
In particular, the question asks whether \(S^2\times S^2\), which has rank two with its product round symmetric metric, admits any smooth Riemannian metric of positive sectional curvature. The supplied original statement conjectures that it does not and calls this the Hopf case.

The supplied background mentions a requested classification but gives no separate classification target; the explicit statement above therefore records the stated existence question and its conjectured negative answer.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can the underlying manifold of any compact, simply connected symmetric space of rank greater than one carry an arbitrary metric with strictly positive sectional curvature everywhere? The supplied conjecture says none can, including \(S^2\times S^2\). The metric being sought need not preserve the symmetric-space structure.
