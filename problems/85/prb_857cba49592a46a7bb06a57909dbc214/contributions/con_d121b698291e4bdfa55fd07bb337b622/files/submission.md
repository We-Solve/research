# Finitely generated periodic linear groups in characteristic zero are finite

Claim type: Partial result

Exact scope: Every finitely presented periodic group admitting a faithful finite-dimensional representation over a characteristic-zero field is finite; in fact finite generation suffices.

This is the classical Schur positive subcase. The proof below is included in full; it does not assume bounded exponent at the outset.

Let G be generated by finitely many matrices in GL_n(k), with char(k)=0, and suppose every element has finite order. Let K be the field generated over Q by the entries of the generators and their inverses. It is finitely generated as a field. Choose a transcendence basis t_1,…,t_r so K is finite over E=Q(t_1,…,t_r), of degree D. Every eigenvalue ζ of g∈G is a root of unity and has degree at most n over K. As ζ is algebraic over Q, the algebraic independence of the t_i remains true over Q(ζ), so
\[
 [\mathbb Q(\zeta):\mathbb Q]=[E(\zeta):E]
 \le [K(\zeta):E]\le nD.
\]
If ζ has order m then this degree is φ(m). There are only finitely many such m. An elementary explicit justification is φ(m)≥sqrt(m/2): factor φ(m)^2/m over prime powers. Every odd-prime factor p^{a-2}(p−1)^2 is at least one, while the factor for 2 is at least 1/2. Hence m≤2(nD)^2. Therefore all eigenvalues of all elements belong to one finite set U. In particular, all traces belong to the finite set T of sums of n elements of U.

First assume the representation is irreducible after extending scalars to an algebraic closure. We use, and prove, the finite-dimensional density lemma: the linear span A of G is End(V). For linearly independent v_1,…,v_r, consider
\[
 W_r=\{(av_1,\ldots,av_r):a\in A\}\subset V^r.
\]
For r=1 this is V by irreducibility. Inductively its projection onto the first r−1 components is onto. The kernel, in the last component, is an A-submodule of V and is either 0 or V. If it is V, surjectivity onto V^r follows. If it is zero, W_r is the graph of an A-linear map V^{r−1}→V. Every A-endomorphism of V is scalar: over the algebraically closed field it has an eigenvalue, and the corresponding nonzero eigenspace is invariant and hence all of V. The graph map thus has the form sum c_i v_i, forcing av_r=sum c_i av_i for every a, and a=1 contradicts independence. This proves the induction. Taking a basis of V shows A=End(V).

Choose n² group elements g_i forming a basis of End(V). The function
\[
 g\longmapsto (\operatorname{tr}(gg_i))_{i=1}^{n^2}
\]
is injective: the trace pairing on full matrices is nondegenerate, as testing against matrix units recovers every entry. Its image is contained in the finite set T^{n²}, because every gg_i still belongs to G. Thus G is finite in the irreducible case.

For a general representation choose a composition series of the finite-dimensional G-module over an algebraic closure. Each diagonal irreducible quotient has a finitely generated periodic image, so the preceding argument makes its image finite. The homomorphism to the product of these images has finite image. Its kernel is upper block unitriangular, and every one of its elements is both unipotent and of finite order. Such an element is the identity in characteristic zero: its minimal polynomial divides both (X−1)^n and X^m−1, whose greatest common divisor is X−1 because the latter polynomial has simple roots. The kernel is trivial, so G is finite.

Consequently a counterexample to the prompt cannot be a characteristic-zero linear group. This excludes arbitrary linear degree and arbitrary characteristic-zero coefficient fields. Positive characteristic and non-linear groups remain outside the proof; finite presentability alone does not provide a faithful linear representation.

The present full linear-group proof applies also to nonsolvable finite images, and its new ingredient is the finite eigenvalue set plus matrix-density argument.
