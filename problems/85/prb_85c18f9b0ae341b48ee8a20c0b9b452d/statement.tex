\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Use the standard finite simplicial-complex convention and let \(k\) be a field. For a face \(\sigma\) of a complex \(K\), its link is
\[
 \operatorname{lk}_K(\sigma)=\{\tau\in K:\tau\cap\sigma=\varnothing,\ \tau\cup\sigma\in K\}.
\]
A pure \(d\)-dimensional complex is Cohen--Macaulay over \(k\) when the reduced homology of every link vanishes in degrees below the dimension of that link. It is doubly Cohen--Macaulay when it is Cohen--Macaulay and, for every vertex \(v\), the deletion \(K-v=\{\sigma\in K:v\notin\sigma\}\) is Cohen--Macaulay of the same dimension \(d\).

Let \(K\) be such a doubly Cohen--Macaulay complex, with \(d>1\). The question asks whether the decomposition supplied by the source's Theorem 7 can be chosen in the form
\[
 K=\bigcup_{i=1}^m S_i,
\]
where the \(S_i\) are subcomplexes that are homology \(d\)-spheres and satisfy the specified overlap and reordering properties. In the usual local meaning, a homology \(d\)-sphere over \(k\) is a pure complex \(S\) with
\[
 \widetilde H_j(\operatorname{lk}_S(\sigma);k)\cong
 \begin{cases}k,&j=d-|\sigma|,\\0,&j\ne d-|\sigma|,\end{cases}
\]
for every face \(\sigma\), including the empty face, using the standard reduced-homology convention in degree \(-1\). The source does not specify whether it intends this local condition or only the global homology of a sphere, or whether its sphere coefficients are the same field \(k\).

The cleaned reordering condition naturally reads: for every \(a\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\), there is a permutation \(\pi\) with \(\pi(1)=a\) such that for every \(j\geq2\), the intersection
\[
 S_{\pi(j)}\cap\bigcup_{\ell<j}S_{\pi(\ell)}
\]
contains a \(d\)-face. This permits the remaining order to depend on the selected first factor; it is not the assertion that every ordering works. However, ``intersects \dots\ in a \(d\)-face'' could instead require the entire intersection to equal the simplex on one \(d\)-face, a stronger condition. Theorem 7 is not reproduced, so the draft records this ambiguity rather than declaring these readings equivalent. It also cannot recover any additional minimal-cycle or other conditions imposed by that theorem.

\subsection{Short English statement}
Can the factors in the indicated decomposition of every doubly Cohen--Macaulay complex of dimension greater than one all be homology spheres, while preserving its overlap and choice-of-first-factor properties? The missing Theorem 7 leaves important details unresolved, especially whether an overlap contains a facet or equals a single facet.
