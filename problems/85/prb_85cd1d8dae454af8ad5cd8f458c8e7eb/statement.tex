\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 701187}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For each positive integer \(n\), let
\[
f(n)=\#\bigl\{[G]_{\cong}: G\text{ is a finite group and }|G|=n\bigr\},
\]
where \([G]_{\cong}\) denotes the isomorphism class of \(G\). Thus groups are counted up to group isomorphism.

Is the following assertion true?
\[
\forall k\in\Z_{\geq 1}\;\exists n\in\Z_{\geq 1}\quad f(n)=k.
\]
The supplied source reports that the answer is affirmative for every \(1\leq k\leq 1000\), attributing this result to G. M. Wei (1998). This reported partial result is retained as source background and has not been independently verified here.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every positive integer occur as the number of isomorphism classes of finite groups of some fixed positive order? The supplied record labels the problem open and reports an affirmative answer for \(1\leq k\leq 1000\).
