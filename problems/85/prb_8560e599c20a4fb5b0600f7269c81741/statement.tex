\section{Miscellaneous — Question 15.2}\label{10300067}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 10300067 · Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A codimension-one foliation $\mathcal F$ decomposes a $3$-manifold $M$ into surface leaves, locally as the horizontal slices of a product chart. In the terminology of the source, it is taut if a closed curve transverse to $\mathcal F$ meets every leaf. A minimal foliation means that every leaf is dense; a minimal surface means a surface with vanishing mean curvature for the specified Riemannian metric. These two uses of minimal are distinct. For a cooriented codimension-one foliation choose a nowhere-zero $1$-form $\alpha$ with $T\mathcal F=\ker\alpha$. Integrability gives $d\alpha=\alpha\wedge\omega$. The Godbillon--Vey class is $\operatorname{gv}(\mathcal F)=[\omega\wedge d\omega]\in H^3(M;\mathbb R)$, independent of these choices, and its value on an oriented closed $M$ is the integral of this $3$-form. An ordinary foliated cobordism between foliated $3$-manifolds is a compact $4$-dimensional cobordism with a codimension-one foliation transverse to its boundary and restricting to the given boundary foliations. A taut version requires an additional suitable notion of tautness for this cobordism; choosing and justifying that notion is part of the problem.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Formulate a useful notion of taut foliated cobordism and determine numerical invariants of the induced equivalence classes of taut foliations. Seek invariants finer than the Godbillon--Vey number, capable of distinguishing classes that this number alone does not separate. \sref{10300067}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Develop taut foliated cobordism and numerical invariants of its classes that distinguish more than the Godbillon--Vey invariant.
