\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a connected space \(X\) of finite CW homotopy type, let \(G=\pi_1(X)\), let \(G_{\mathrm{ab}}=G/[G,G]\cong H_1(X;\Z)\), and let \(\Z[G_{\mathrm{ab}}]\) denote its integral group ring. Regard this ring as a local coefficient system on \(X\) through the quotient map \(G\to G_{\mathrm{ab}}\) and the regular action. Under the standard integral convention, \(X\) is an \emph{abelian duality space of dimension \(n\)} if \(n\geq 0\),
\[
H^q\bigl(X;\Z[G_{\mathrm{ab}}]\bigr)=0\quad\text{for every }q\geq 0\text{ with }q\neq n,
\]
and \(H^n(X;\Z[G_{\mathrm{ab}}])\) is a nonzero torsion-free abelian group. Torsion-freeness here refers to the underlying \(\Z\)-module.

A closed manifold is compact and has empty boundary. A real \(m\)-torus is \(T^m=(S^1)^m\), with \(T^0\) a point. Using connected manifolds and interpreting ``is not a real torus'' as ``is not homeomorphic to a real torus,'' the question is:
\[
\begin{gathered}
\text{Do there exist integers }m,n\geq 0\text{ and a connected closed orientable }m\text{-manifold }M\\
\text{such that }M\text{ is an abelian duality space of dimension }n
\quad\text{and}\quad M\not\cong T^m\text{ by a homeomorphism}?
\end{gathered}
\]
The source does not explicitly specify connectedness, the manifold category, the intended equivalence with a torus, or the coefficient convention for abelian duality. Thus the displayed formulation records explicit working conventions; a requirement involving diffeomorphism or homotopy equivalence would be a different formulation and requires source confirmation.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does a connected compact orientable manifold without boundary satisfy integral abelian duality while failing to be a real torus? The displayed formulation uses homeomorphism to interpret the torus exclusion; the source leaves this equivalence and the manifold category unspecified.
