\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(B_{\pm}=\{(x,y)\in\R^2:(x\mp1)^2+(y-1)^2\leq1\}\) be the two fixed unit disks. The closed curvilinear triangular region between these disks and the \(x\)-axis is
\[
T=\left\{(x,y)\in\R^2:-1\leq x\leq1,\quad 0\leq y\leq1,\quad (x-1)^2+(y-1)^2\geq1,\quad (x+1)^2+(y-1)^2\geq1\right\}.
\]
For \(a\in\R\) and \(r>0\), write
\[
D(a,r)=\{(x,y)\in\R^2:(x-a)^2+(y-r)^2\leq r^2\}.
\]
This disk is tangent to the \(x\)-axis from above. An admissible packing is a countably infinite family \(\mathcal P=(D(a_n,r_n))_{n\in\N}\), with every \(r_n>0\), such that
\[
D(a_n,r_n)\subseteq T\quad(n\in\N),\qquad
\operatorname{int}D(a_n,r_n)\cap\operatorname{int}D(a_m,r_m)=\varnothing\quad(n\ne m).
\]
Thus boundary tangencies are permitted. Define its total area and the optimal supremum by
\[
A(\mathcal P)=\pi\sum_{n\in\N}r_n^2,
\qquad
M=\sup\{A(\mathcal P):\mathcal P\text{ is an admissible packing}\}.
\]
The fixed bounding disks \(B_{\pm}\) are not included in this area sum.

\textbf{Source question.} Does the greedy procedure described as successively inserting a disk tangent to the \(x\)-axis and to two previously placed disks produce an admissible infinite packing \(\mathcal G\) satisfying
\[
A(\mathcal G)=M?
\]
The equality asks for actual attainment of the supremum by the resulting infinite packing.
