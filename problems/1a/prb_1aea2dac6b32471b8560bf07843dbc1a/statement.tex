\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
The source asks for the Laplace transform and its ``jumps'' for a $q$-hypergeometric series referred to only as ``above'', and asks how these depend on $A,B,C$; it notes that a Mellin transform does not converge. That preceding series, the integration variable, contour, normalization and meaning of a jump are absent from this record. The accompanying aid distinguishes ordinary radial transforms from Borel--Laplace summation.

Under the standard positive-definite Nahm-sum interpretation used by the aid, take a symmetric positive-definite matrix $A$ of size $r$, vectors $B$ and scalar $C$, with real data for the positive radial statement, and set
\[
 F_{A,B,C}(q)=\sum_{n\in\Z_{\geq0}^r}
 \frac{q^{\frac12 n^TAn+B^Tn+C}}{\prod_{i=1}^r(q)_{n_i}},
 \qquad(q)_m=\prod_{j=1}^m(1-q^j),\qquad R_{A,B,C}(t)=F_{A,B,C}(e^{-t}).
\]
This makes a conventional interpretation explicit; it does not certify the omitted preceding source formula or its parameter domain. The aid attributes the cusp asymptotic to Garoufalidis--Zagier and reports
\[
 R_{A,B,C}(t)=K(A,B)e^{\Lambda(A)/t}(1+O(t)),\qquad t\downarrow0,
\]
with $K(A,B)>0$ and $\Lambda(A)>0$. The dominant action depends only on $A$, the leading amplitude depends on $A,B$, and neither depends on $C$. In this positive radial setting the ordinary Laplace integral $\int_0^\infty e^{-st}R(t)\,dt$ and the ordinary Mellin integral $\int_0^\infty t^{s-1}R(t)\,dt$ both fail local integrability at zero for every fixed transform parameter $s$. Thus changing from Mellin to a literal radial Laplace transform does not remove the cusp obstruction.

In the aid's usual Nahm-variable convention, let $z_i(A)\in(0,1)$ solve
\[
 1-z_i=\prod_{j=1}^r z_j^{A_{ij}}.
\]
The stated amplitude dependence is
\[
 \frac{K(A,B+\Delta B)}{K(A,B)}=\prod_{i=1}^r z_i(A)^{\Delta B_i}.
\]
The absolute formulas for $K$ and $\Lambda$, although described as explicit in the summary, are not included there; no further normalization formula is guessed.

Separately factor off the dominant action and amplitude and write the formal all-orders normalized expansion as $\Phi_{A,B,C}(t)$. Since the sum itself satisfies $F_{A,B,C}(e^{-t})=e^{-Ct}F_{A,B,0}(e^{-t})$, the aid reports the exact formal relation
\[
 \Phi_{A,B,C}(t)=e^{-Ct}\Phi_{A,B,0}(t).
\]
Whenever compatible lateral Borel--Laplace sums exist, their Stokes discontinuities therefore obey
\[
 \operatorname{Disc}_\theta\Phi_{A,B,C}(t)
 =e^{-Ct}\operatorname{Disc}_\theta\Phi_{A,B,0}(t).
\]
The factor is entire and nonvanishing, so the set of directions carrying nonzero jumps cannot depend on $C$ in this interpretation. The existence of lateral sums and the determination of the jumps or their directions as functions of $A,B$ are not proved by this parameter law. This summation problem must not be identified with the divergent ordinary radial integral.
\subsection{Short English statement}
For the aid's positive Nahm-sum interpretation, the cusp grows exponentially fast enough that both ordinary radial transforms diverge at zero. A different Borel--Laplace interpretation gives an exact parameter law: changing $C$ multiplies every existing Stokes jump by $e^{-Ct}$. It does not determine the jumps or prove that the required lateral sums exist.
