Suppose finitely many planar circles, with centres $c_i$ and positive radii $r_i$, cannot be divided into two nonempty groups by a line disjoint from all of them. Their closed disks are contained in a disk of radius
\[
 R=\sum_i r_i.
\]
We prove the stronger explicit statement that its centre can be chosen as
\[
 c=\frac{\sum_i r_i c_i}{R}.
\]
This reconstructs the Goodman--Goodman covering argument through its one-dimensional core, with an integral proof of that core. The result is classical; no novelty is claimed.
