\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $V$ be a finite alphabet and let $w$ be a finite word in which every letter of $V$ occurs. For distinct $x,y\in V$, let $w|_{\{x,y\}}$ be the subword obtained by deleting all letters other than $x,y$. Say $x$ and $y$ alternate in $w$ if this subword has no two consecutive equal letters. The represented finite simple graph $G_w$ has vertex set $V$ and
\[
\{x,y\}\in E(G_w)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
x\text{ and }y\text{ alternate in }w.
\]
A graph is word-representable if it equals $G_w$ for some representing word, after any specified relabeling of vertices.
