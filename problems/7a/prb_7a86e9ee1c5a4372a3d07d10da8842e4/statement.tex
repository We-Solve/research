\textbf{Attached unreviewed model separation.} A real monochromatic plane wave has spectral measure on $S^1$, covariance $J_0(|x-y|)$ in a usual unit-frequency normalization, and real-analytic Helmholtz samples satisfying $(\Delta+1)F=0$; it has ordinary nodal curves. Boundary white noise on $S^1$ is a different random distribution. The two-dimensional GFF is itself distribution valued, so canonical level lines are not its pointwise zero set. The aid describes appropriate local-set interfaces as having $\mathrm{SLE}_4/\mathrm{CLE}_4$ laws, with boundary/interface hypotheses not supplied in the summary.

For a precise diagnostic, take zero-boundary GFF $h$ on the unit disk with Green-kernel normalization $G(z,w)=(2\pi)^{-1}\log|(1-z\overline w)/(z-w)|$. Let $h_r(0)$ be its circle average. The aid states that
\[B_t=\sqrt{2\pi}\,h_{e^{-t}}(0),\qquad t\ge0,\]
is standard Brownian motion. For $N\ge2$ put $F_N=\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\mathbf1_{\{B_nB_{n+1}<0\}}$. Its reported exact expectation is
\[\E F_N=\frac1\pi\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\arcsin\frac1{\sqrt{n+1}}=\frac2\pi\sqrt N+O(1),\]
and the aid asserts infinitely many such flips almost surely. The unit-disk base point and normalization matter to this exact Brownian identification.

A separate boundary logarithmic Fourier cutoff is explicitly
\[X_N(\theta)=\sum_{n=1}^N\frac{a_n\cos(n\theta)+b_n\sin(n\theta)}{\sqrt n},\qquad0\le\theta<2\pi,\]
where $a_n,b_n$ are independent standard real Gaussians. If $Z_N$ counts its zeros over one period and $H_N=\sum_{n=1}^N1/n$, the aid gives
\[\E Z_N=\sqrt{\frac{2N(N+1)}{H_N}}\sim\frac{\sqrt2\,N}{\sqrt{\log N}}.\]
This logarithmic boundary model is not unweighted boundary white noise and is not the monochromatic plane wave.
