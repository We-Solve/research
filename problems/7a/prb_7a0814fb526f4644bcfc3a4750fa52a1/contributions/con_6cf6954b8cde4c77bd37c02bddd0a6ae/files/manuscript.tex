\section{Strong-Interaction Limits of the Hohenberg–Kohn Functional}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


\paragraph{Definitions and conventions.}
The Hohenberg--Kohn or Levy--Lieb constrained-search functional minimizes kinetic plus interaction energy among quantum states with prescribed one-particle density. In the strong-interaction limit the leading interaction problem is multimarginal optimal transport, with every marginal determined by that density. A Monge solution is concentrated on the graph of deterministic maps from one particle position to the others. The zero-point correction describes the next contribution from small quantum fluctuations about the leading optimal configurations. Bose or Fermi statistics constrain the symmetry of the underlying quantum state.

\paragraph{The proposed zero-point scale in a local normal mode.}
A natural attempted route expands the interaction cost in directions normal to a minimizing transport configuration. To see what this would require, assume one such coordinate $y$ has a fixed positive quadratic coefficient $a$ and consider the auxiliary operator
\[
 H_\lambda=-\tfrac12\frac{d^2}{dy^2}+\frac{\lambda a}{2}y^2,
 \qquad a>0,\quad\lambda>0.
\]
Write $\omega=\sqrt{\lambda a}$. For a normalized smooth rapidly decaying wave function $\psi$, integration by parts gives
\[
 \langle\psi,H_\lambda\psi\rangle
 =\tfrac12\int_{\mathbb R}|\psi'+\omega y\psi|^2dy
   +\frac\omega2.
\]
Indeed, the cross term is
$\omega\operatorname{Re}\int y\overline\psi\psi'=-\omega/2$.
Equality is attained by
$\psi(y)=(\omega/\pi)^{1/4}e^{-\omega y^2/2}$.
The ground energy is therefore $\sqrt\lambda\sqrt a/2$, and the spatial width is of order $\lambda^{-1/4}a^{-1/4}$.
For $q$ independent normal coordinates with positive coefficients $a_1,\ldots,a_q$, summing the completed squares gives the exact energy
\[
 \frac{\sqrt\lambda}{2}\sum_{j=1}^q\sqrt{a_j}.
\]
This is the local harmonic mechanism behind a zero-point correction, not yet an asymptotic expansion of the density-constrained functional.

\paragraph{Degenerate directions change the scale.}
If the quadratic coefficient vanishes and the first confining term is $\lambda y^4$, substituting $y=\lambda^{-1/6}z$ gives the exact rescaling
\[
 -\tfrac12\partial_y^2+\lambda y^4
 =\lambda^{1/3}\left(-\tfrac12\partial_z^2+z^4\right)
\]
under the corresponding unitary change of variables. Thus a positive transverse Hessian is a substantive assumption, not a harmless detail. A position-dependent Hessian, singular transport support, or tangent zero modes cannot be handled by simply summing fixed oscillators.

\paragraph{Where the constrained-search proof stops.}
The product Gaussians above generally do not have the exactly prescribed one-particle density. To turn them into an upper bound at the claimed correction order one must repair the marginals without an error of that order; a lower bound must control all admissible states near the optimal transport support and justify the harmonic approximation. Bose or Fermi symmetry imposes additional global constraints which this local oscillator ignores. Consequently the calculation proves neither the full strong-interaction asymptotics nor the one-dimensional zero-point theorem, much less its three-dimensional extension. It also gives no general three-dimensional Monge solution for more than two marginals and no effective maps or functional derivatives. All five source tasks, including the already established portions of the leading limit, remain distinct from this elementary reconstruction.
