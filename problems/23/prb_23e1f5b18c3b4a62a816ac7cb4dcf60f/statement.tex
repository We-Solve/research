\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(K\) be a field and let \(S_m=K[x_1,\ldots,x_m]\) have its standard grading, with \(\deg x_i=1\). For a nonzero finitely generated graded \(S_m\)-module \(M\), put
\[
\beta^{S_m}_{i,j}(M)=\dim_K\operatorname{Tor}^{S_m}_i(M,K)_j,
\qquad
\operatorname{reg}(M)=\max\{j-i:\beta^{S_m}_{i,j}(M)\ne0\}.
\]
A quadratic ideal means a homogeneous ideal generated by forms of degree two. If its minimal homogeneous generators are \(q_1,\ldots,q_r\), its first syzygy module is the kernel of
\[
S_m(-2)^r\longrightarrow I,\qquad e_a\longmapsto q_a.
\]
Having linear first syzygies means that this kernel is generated by homogeneous relations whose coefficients are linear forms, equivalently by elements of total degree three. In terms of the quotient resolution, this is the condition
\[
\beta^{S_m}_{2,j}(S_m/I)=0\quad\text{for all }j\ne3.
\]

The first question is whether there exists a family \((I_m)_{m\ge1}\), with \(I_m\subseteq S_m\) quadratic and having linear first syzygies, such that
\[
\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{\operatorname{reg}(S_m/I_m)}{m}>0.
\]
Here \(S_m\) makes explicit the ambient polynomial ring denoted by \(S\) in the source. The displayed condition retains an actual limit; replacing it by a positive limit superior would change the question. The source does not specify whether an infinite positive limit is allowed, or whether \(K\) is fixed arbitrarily, existentially chosen, or subject to restrictions on characteristic.

For the second question, under the usual Stanley--Reisner interpretation, let \(\Delta\) be a finite simplicial complex on \([n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}\), and set
\[
T=K[x_1,\ldots,x_n],\qquad
I_\Delta=\bigl(x_{i_1}\cdots x_{i_r}:\{i_1,\ldots,i_r\}\notin\Delta\bigr),\qquad
K[\Delta]=T/I_\Delta.
\]
The ring \(K[\Delta]\) is Cohen--Macaulay when its depth at the homogeneous maximal ideal equals its Krull dimension. In the usual graded-resolution convention used here, property \(N_2\) means
\[
\beta^T_{1,j}(K[\Delta])=0\quad(j\ne2),\qquad
\beta^T_{2,j}(K[\Delta])=0\quad(j\ne3),
\]
so its defining ideal is quadratic and has linear first syzygies.

The second question asks whether there exists a constant \(\lambda\) such that, for every such \(\Delta\),
