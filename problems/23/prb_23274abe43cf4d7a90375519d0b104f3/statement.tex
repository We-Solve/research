\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $(X,T)$ be a compact metric dynamical system, with $T$ a homeomorphism, minimal in the sense that every orbit is dense and uniquely ergodic with invariant probability $\mu$. For a continuous cocycle $A:X\to\mathrm{SL}_2(\R)$, define
\[
 A_n(x)=A(T^{n-1}x)\cdots A(x),\qquad
 L(A)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\int_X\log\|A_n(x)\|\,\dd\mu(x).
\]
Uniformity means
\[
 \sup_{x\in X}\left|\frac1n\log\|A_n(x)\|-L(A)\right|\longrightarrow0.
\]
Nonuniform means failure of this uniform limit; it is not a synonym for failure of uniform hyperbolicity.

The first question asks whether every locally constant $\mathrm{SL}_2(\R)$ cocycle over every minimal uniquely ergodic subshift is uniform. Here a subshift is a closed shift-invariant $X\subset\mathcal A^{\Z}$ for a finite alphabet, and locally constant means dependence on a fixed finite coordinate window.

The second asks whether each minimal uniquely ergodic system admits some nonuniform continuous $\mathrm{SL}_2(\R)$ cocycle. Its more specific Schr\"odinger version asks for $v\in C(X,\R)$ and $E\in\R$ such that
\[
 A_{E,v}(x)=\begin{pmatrix}E-v(x)&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}
\]
is nonuniform. This is existence of a potential and an energy, not nonuniformity at every energy. The continuity question is not restricted to locally constant maps, so it is different from the first question.

The literal universal existence assertion includes finite periodic minimal systems, which are uniquely ergodic. Over a finite periodic base, normalized cocycle growth converges uniformly, so such a base cannot support a nonuniform cocycle in the displayed sense. The intended existence question must therefore specify an aperiodic or infinite-base restriction, or another nondegeneracy convention, absent from the extract. That qualification is recorded rather than silently added. The homeomorphism/two-sided-subshift convention is likewise the usual explicit interpretation of the terse source.

The literature assessment checked on 21 August 2026 reports uniformity theorems and nonuniform examples for several important classes but does not settle the general intended questions. Its class-dependent results do not remove the missing finite-base qualification. These are dated supplied claims requiring independent verification.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Are all locally constant cocycles over strictly ergodic subshifts uniform, while suitably nonperiodic strictly ergodic systems admit a nonuniform continuous cocycle, even of Schr\"odinger form? The literal second question needs a restriction excluding finite periodic bases.
