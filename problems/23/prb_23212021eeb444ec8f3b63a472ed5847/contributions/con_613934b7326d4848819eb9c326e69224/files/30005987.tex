\paragraph{The kinematic spin-up implication.} In an axisymmetric inviscid test model on a constant Coriolis parameter \(f\), let \(r\) be radius, \(u=dr/dt\) radial velocity, and \(v\) tangential velocity. Specific absolute angular momentum is \(L=rv+fr^2/2\). If a parcel conserves \(L\), differentiation gives
\[
 \frac{dv}{dt}=-\left(\frac vr+f\right)u.
\]
For \(r>0\), \(u<0\) and \(v/r+f>0\), inward motion increases tangential speed. Equivalently \(v(r)=L/r-fr/2\) along the parcel, so the sign test is exact within these assumptions.
\paragraph{Why mean inflow alone is insufficient.} In a local incompressible axisymmetric angular-momentum balance,
\[
 \partial_t L+\frac1r\partial_r(ruL)+\partial_z(wL)=0,
\]
where \(w\) is vertical velocity. Averaging a fluctuating flow yields \(\overline{uL}=\bar u\,\bar L+\overline{u'L'}\), with primes denoting deviations from the averaging operation. Therefore a measured negative \(\bar u\) does not by itself identify the mean angular-momentum flux: the correlation term and vertical flux remain. In a compressible simulation the corresponding mass-weighted conservation law must be used instead.
