\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For an integer \(n\geq 2\), let \(J=\operatorname{diag}(I_n,-1)\) and define
\[
G_n=\operatorname{PU}(n,1)=\{A\in\operatorname{GL}_{n+1}(\C):A^*JA=J\}/\{\lambda I_{n+1}:|\lambda|=1\},
\]
where \(A^*\) denotes conjugate transpose. A subgroup \(\Gamma\leq G_n\) is a lattice if it is discrete and \(\Gamma\backslash G_n\) has finite Haar volume. Both cocompact and noncocompact lattices are allowed.

Two lattices \(\Gamma,\Lambda\leq G_n\) are commensurable up to ambient conjugacy if there exists \(g\in G_n\) such that \(\Gamma\cap g\Lambda g^{-1}\) has finite index in both \(\Gamma\) and \(g\Lambda g^{-1}\).

Use the standard algebraic-group meaning of arithmeticity: a lattice \(\Gamma\leq G_n\) is arithmetic if there exist a connected semisimple algebraic group \(\mathbf H\) over \(\Q\), a faithful rational representation \(\rho:\mathbf H\hookrightarrow\operatorname{GL}_m\), and a surjective continuous homomorphism
\[
\phi:\mathbf H(\R)^\circ\longrightarrow G_n
\]
with compact kernel, such that \(\Gamma\) is commensurable up to ambient conjugacy with \(\phi(\Delta)\), where
\[
\Delta=\{h\in\mathbf H(\Q)\cap\mathbf H(\R)^\circ:\rho(h)\in\operatorname{GL}_m(\Z)\}.
\]
Here \(\mathbf H(\R)^\circ\) is the identity component. A lattice is nonarithmetic if it is not arithmetic in this sense.
