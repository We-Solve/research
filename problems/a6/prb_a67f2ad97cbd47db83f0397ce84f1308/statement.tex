\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $G=(V,E)$ be an infinite connected undirected simple graph. It is vertex-transitive when its automorphism group acts transitively on $V$. A spanning tree is a subgraph $T=(V,E_T)$ with $E_T\subseteq E$ that is connected and has no cycles. It has no leaves when
\[
\deg_T(v)\geq2\qquad\text{for every }v\in V.
\]
For an infinite group $\Gamma$ and a symmetric generating set $S\subseteq\Gamma\setminus\{1\}$, its undirected Cayley graph has vertex set $\Gamma$ and edges $\{\gamma,\gamma s\}$ for $\gamma\in\Gamma$, $s\in S$.

\textbf{Questions.} Does every infinite Cayley graph have a spanning tree without leaves? More generally, does every infinite connected vertex-transitive graph have a spanning tree without leaves? Equivalently, for every graph in the relevant class, must there exist $E_T\subseteq E$ making $(V,E_T)$ connected and acyclic with degree at least two at every vertex? The source does not state whether local finiteness, or finiteness of the Cayley generating set, is required.
