\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
\textbf{Ramanujan assertion in the supplied real-group formulation.} For every positive integer level \(N\), let \(\Gamma(N)=\{\gamma\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\Z):\gamma\equiv I\pmod N\}\). Every irreducible unitary representation occurring in the right regular representation on\[L^2_{\mathrm{cusp}}(\Gamma(N)\backslash\mathrm{SL}_2(\R))\] should be tempered. The cuspidal subspace consists of square-integrable automorphic functions with vanishing constant term along the unipotent direction at every cusp, in the usual interpretation. Occurrence refers to the cuspidal spectral decomposition. Tempered means weakly contained in the regular representation of \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\R)\) on \(L^2(\mathrm{SL}_2(\R))\); equivalently, its compact-finite matrix coefficients have the appropriate \(L^{2+\varepsilon}\) integrability for every \(\varepsilon>0\).\par The assertion concerns the real-place representations displayed above, not every representation in the full \(L^2\) space, which has additional spectral pieces. Nor does the real-group statement by itself assert the finite-place Satake-parameter bounds of a full adelic Ramanujan conjecture. In the spherical, weight-zero cuspidal sector it entails the Selberg \(1/4\) lower bound for eigenvalues of the nonnegative hyperbolic Laplacian on \(\Gamma(N)\backslash\mathbb H\). This is the standard curvature-\(-1\) normalization, with continuous-spectrum threshold \(1/4\).\par The supplied triage checked 2026-08-21 labels the question open and describes both the Selberg \(1/4\) assertion and the corresponding general temperedness problem as unresolved. It reports the best general \(\mathrm{SL}_2\) lower bound as the Kim--Sarnak value \(975/4096\). This numerical and current-best assessment is preserved as a dated supplied claim requiring independent verification, not a newly checked literature bound. No replacement of the cuspidal hypothesis or additional finite-place conclusion is made.
\subsection{Short English statement}
At every principal congruence level, the real representations in the cuspidal spectrum should all be tempered. The weight-zero consequence is the Selberg \(1/4\) eigenvalue bound. The supplied numerical progress assessment is dated and does not assert the full finite-place Ramanujan conjecture.
