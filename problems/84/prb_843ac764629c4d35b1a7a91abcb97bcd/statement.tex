\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Let $g(n)$ be maximal such that in any set of $n$ points in $\mathbb{R}^2$ with no four points on a line there exists a subset on $g(n)$ points with no three points on a line. Estimate $g(n)$. \sref{statement}{1}

\paragraph{Residual task reported by the archive.}

The embedded review (2026-08-17) records: Sharpen either side to determine g(n). \sref{statement}{1}

\subsection{Short English statement}

If no four planar points are collinear, how large a subset with no collinear triple must exist?
