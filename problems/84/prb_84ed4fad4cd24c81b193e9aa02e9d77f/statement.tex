\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For each positive integer \(j\), let \((M_j^3,g_j)\) be a smooth three-dimensional Riemannian manifold satisfying
\[
\operatorname{Scal}_{g_j}(x)\geq 0\qquad\text{for every }x\in M_j.
\]
Let \((M_0^3,g_0)\) be a smooth three-dimensional Riemannian manifold. Here \(\operatorname{Scal}_g\) denotes the scalar curvature of the metric \(g\). Smoothness of the limit manifold does not mean that the metrics converge smoothly.

Sormani--Wenger intrinsic-flat convergence, abbreviated SWIF, is convergence of the associated integral current spaces in intrinsic-flat distance. To describe this distance, write \(X_i=(M_i,d_{g_i},T_i)\), where \(T_i\) is the integration current with the orientation and current-space structure required for this notion of convergence. Then
\[
d_{\mathcal F}(X_j,X_0)
=\inf\bigl\{\mathbf M_Z(U)+\mathbf M_Z(V):
\varphi_{j\#}T_j-\varphi_{0\#}T_0=U+\partial V\bigr\}.
\]
The infimum is over common complete metric spaces \(Z\), distance-preserving embeddings \(\varphi_j,\varphi_0\) into \(Z\), and integral currents \(U\) and \(V\) of dimensions three and four, respectively; \(\mathbf M_Z\) denotes current mass. SWIF convergence means \(d_{\mathcal F}(X_j,X_0)\to0\). This formulation presupposes appropriate integral current structures; the supplied excerpt does not specify the orientation, finiteness, completeness, compactness, or boundary assumptions needed for its intended setting.
