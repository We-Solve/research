\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Write \(u(x,t)\in\R^3\) for velocity and \(p(x,t)\in\R\) for pressure. The standard incompressible equations, allowing a prescribed force \(f\), are

\partial_t u+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p=\nu\Delta u+f,
\qquad \nabla\cdot u=0,

\textbf{Question, conditional on specifying these hypotheses.} Fix a three-dimensional spatial setting and an admissible class of smooth divergence-free initial velocities, together with any required boundary compatibility, decay, integrability, and forcing conditions. For each system separately, let \(T_{\max}\in(0,\infty]\) denote the maximal forward lifetime of a smooth solution in that setting. Is
\[
\forall\,u_0\text{ in the specified admissible class},
\qquad T_{\max}=\infty,
\]
or does there exist an admissible \(u_0\) whose smooth solution has \(T_{\max}<\infty\) and admits no smooth continuation through \(T_{\max}\) within the same prescribed setting? Here global regularity means smooth continuation for every finite forward time, and finite-time singularity means failure of such continuation. The source asks this question for both Euler and Navier--Stokes; it does not specify a particular blow-up norm or settle the missing hypotheses.
\subsection{Short English statement}
For each of the three-dimensional incompressible Euler and Navier--Stokes equations, determine whether every admissible smooth initial flow stays smooth for all forward time, or whether some such flow loses smoothness in finite time. The supplied record leaves the domain, boundary conditions, data class, and forcing unspecified, so this is a conditional formulation requiring mathematical review.
