\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Fix three volumes $v_1,v_2,v_3>0$. In the usual finite-perimeter formulation, a three-bubble cluster consists of pairwise disjoint measurable chambers $\Omega_1,\Omega_2,\Omega_3\subset\R^3$, with $|\Omega_i|=v_i$, and exterior $\Omega_0=\R^3\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^3\Omega_i$, up to null sets. Its perimeter counts each interface once:
\[
\mathcal P(\Omega)=\frac12\sum_{i=0}^3\operatorname{Per}(\Omega_i)
=\sum_{0\leq i<j\leq3}\mathcal H^2(\partial^*\Omega_i\cap\partial^*\Omega_j).
\]
Let $\mathcal T(v_1,v_2,v_3)$ denote the standard triple soap bubble pictured in the source. The requested statement is
\[
\mathcal P\bigl(\mathcal T(v_1,v_2,v_3)\bigr)\leq\mathcal P(\Omega)
\]
for every admissible cluster with those three chamber volumes, for every positive volume triple. This is global minimization, not merely stationarity or stability.

The standard configuration is normally described using spherical interfaces, with three sheets meeting at $120^\circ$ and tetrahedral junctions. These local rules alone do not uniquely specify it. The source's picture and a complete geometric construction are absent from the record, so $\mathcal T$ remains the source-designated model requiring identification rather than a new model inferred just from Plateau rules.

The statement asks least perimeter, not uniqueness. The supplied research summary additionally reports uniqueness of the standard minimizer; such uniqueness must at least be understood up to ambient Euclidean isometry, null-set changes and compatible chamber labels. No stronger uniqueness assertion is inferred without the precise reported theorem.
