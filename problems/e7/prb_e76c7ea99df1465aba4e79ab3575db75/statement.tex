\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A Coxeter system $(G,S)$ has presentation
\[
 G=\langle S\mid (st)^{m_{st}}=1\text{ whenever }m_{st}<\infty\rangle,
\]
where $m_{ss}=1$ and $m_{st}=m_{ts}\in\{2,3,\ldots\}\cup\{\infty\}$ for distinct generators. For $T\subseteq S$, the subgroup $G_T=\langle T\rangle$ is a special subgroup. In the right-angled case each off-diagonal $m_{st}$ is $2$ or $\infty$.

Let $X$ be a CAT(0) geodesic metric space, meaning its geodesic triangles satisfy the Euclidean comparison inequality. Suppose $G$ acts on $X$ properly discontinuously by isometries, as in the source. Kim Ruane's specific question is whether, for every special subgroup $H=G_T$, there exists a nonempty closed $H$-invariant subset $C\subseteq X$ such that
\[
 [x,y]\subseteq C\quad\text{for all }x,y\in C,
 \qquad H\backslash C\text{ is compact}.
\]
Thus convexity is metric convexity in $X$, and cocompactness is required for the restricted $H$-action on $C$.

The literal source does not require $G\backslash X$ to be compact, or state that $X$ is proper or complete. These hypotheses must not be silently added. The convention for proper discontinuity and whether the Coxeter system is assumed finite rank are also not made explicit. The introductory question about differences from ``the Coxeter complex'' is qualitative; the displayed invariant-subset question is its precise stated focus.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Must each special subgroup of a Coxeter group acting properly discontinuously on a CAT(0) space act cocompactly on some closed metrically convex subset? A convex subcomplex in a cube complex is a stronger, different requirement than the metric convexity asked here.

The supplied research assessment labels the question open and reports, for geometric actions, a reduction to quasiconvexity implying convex cocompactness for special subgroups. It also claims a positive word-hyperbolic case by an orbit-convex-hull argument, described there as its own folklore-level work; its needed action hypotheses are not supplied in the original statement and this is not treated as a verified general theorem here. It attributes positive cubical results to Fioravanti--Levcovitz--Sageev (2024), for right-angled Coxeter groups with no loose squares and for strongly cellular cocompact cubulations. The definitions and full hypotheses of those results are absent. It further reports that exotic cubulations and a rotated $D_\infty\times D_\infty$ example distinguish combinatorial from metric convex cocompactness, so those obstructions do not settle the literal question. These are supplied progress claims; no new status determination is made.
