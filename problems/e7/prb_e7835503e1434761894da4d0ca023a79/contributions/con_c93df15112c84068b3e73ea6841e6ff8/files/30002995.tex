\section{Model and source scope}
The original report, printed page 3056, distinguishes a special-case result
from the full KRW conjecture. The primary 2017 paper's abstract identifies
the intended subject as approximate additivity of depth under block
composition; its universal-relation result is not the full conjecture.
We use De Morgan formulas with fan-in two AND/OR gates, literal leaves,
and constants, and measure depth by the maximum number of gates on a
root-to-leaf path. Write $D(f)$ for minimum depth and $L(f)$ for minimum
number of leaves. For nonconstant $f:\{0,1\}^m\to\{0,1\}$ and
$g:\{0,1\}^n\to\{0,1\}$, put
$F(x)=f(g(x^{(1)}),\ldots,g(x^{(m)}))$ on disjoint blocks.
These conventions specify the auxiliary calculation; we do not replace
the source conjecture by an exact equality with no allowable loss.

\section{The restriction route gives a maximum, not a sum}
Every nonconstant Boolean function has a one-dimensional restriction
equal to a variable or its negation. Indeed choose two inputs with
different function values and walk between them by flipping one
coordinate at a time. Some adjacent pair has different values; fix
all other coordinates at that pair's common values.

Apply this fact to $g$ and make the same restriction in every block of
$F$. The resulting function is $f$ with each input perhaps negated.
Negating input literals does not change minimum depth. Restricting a
formula to constants cannot increase depth, so $D(F)\ge D(f)$.
Conversely choose a pivotal one-dimensional restriction of $f$.
Fix the other blocks of $F$ to inputs giving its prescribed zeros or
ones, possible since $g$ is nonconstant. The remaining function is
$g$ or its complement. De Morgan duality, obtained recursively by
interchanging AND with OR and negating every literal and constant,
preserves depth, so $D(F)\ge D(g)$.

Substitution in the opposite direction yields
\[
\max\{D(f),D(g)\}\le D(F)\le D(f)+D(g).
\]
For the upper bound replace each positive input leaf of a minimum-depth
formula for $f$ by a formula for $g$, and each negative leaf by its
De Morgan dual. Any path traverses at most $D(f)$ outer gates and
$D(g)$ inner gates. The lower-bound restrictions above are different
restrictions of the same formula. They do not exhibit a single path
which must contain both costs, so adding their inequalities is unjustified.
Identifying such simultaneous hardness is the unproved step of this route.

\section{A complete read-once special case}
Take $f=\operatorname{AND}_m$ and $g=\operatorname{OR}_n$, with
$m,n\ge1$. Every one of the $mn$ input variables of $F$ is essential:
set the other variables in its own block to zero and make each other
block's OR equal one. Flipping the selected variable flips $F$.
Any formula omitting a variable has identical evaluations when only that
variable is changed, by induction over its gates. Thus every variable
must occur in every formula for $F$, giving $L(F)\ge mn$.
The direct AND of OR trees uses exactly $mn$ leaves, proving $L(F)=mn$.

A binary tree of depth $d$ has at most $2^d$ leaves, by induction on
its two subtrees. Conversely a conjunction or disjunction of $r$ inputs
can be placed in depth $\lceil\log_2r\rceil$: pad with neutral constants
to the next power of two and use a complete binary tree.
Therefore
\[
\lceil\log_2(mn)\rceil\le D(F)
\le\lceil\log_2m\rceil+\lceil\log_2n\rceil.
\]
The endpoints differ by at most one, since
$\lceil a\rceil+\lceil b\rceil-1\le\lceil a+b\rceil$.
This gives the expected additive behavior within one gate for this easy
family, but the essential-variable argument only gives a logarithm of
arity and does not yield general superlogarithmic depth lower bounds.

There is even a harmless rounding failure of exact additivity:
$\operatorname{OR}_5\circ\operatorname{OR}_5=\operatorname{OR}_{25}$
has depth five by the same matching upper and lower bounds, whereas
$D(\operatorname{OR}_5)+D(\operatorname{OR}_5)=6$.
This refutes only an incorrectly strengthened equality. It is not a
counterexample to the actual approximate-composition conjecture.
