# The Morse-number lower bound for all standard symplectic tori

Problem: TOP-30007134

Claim type: Literature report

Exact scope: Every Hamiltonian diffeomorphism of the standard symplectic torus T^(2n) with all fixed points nondegenerate has at least2^(2n) fixed points, equal to the ordinary Morse minimum of that torus.

The substantial imported input is the nondegenerate Conley--Zehnder theorem for the standard symplectic torus: for every smooth time-periodic Hamiltonian, if its time-one fixed points are nondegenerate, their number is at least the sum of the torus's Betti numbers,2^(2n). The primary author account actually inspected is Eduard Zehnder, The beginnings of symplectic topology in Bochum in the early eighties, arXiv:1906.08618v2 (November11,2019), pp.12--14, which reproduces the original Conley--Zehnder reduction and the explicit nondegenerate Morse-polynomial argument. We use its torus theorem only; a Betti bound for arbitrary manifolds would not automatically equal their ordinary Morse number.

Here is why the bound is exactly the prompt's Morse minimum on this class. The homology of T^m=(S^1)^m has rank binom(m,j) in degree j, by the product cell complex with one zero-cell and one one-cell per circle and zero boundary maps. Thus total rank is2^m. Morse inequalities force every Morse function on T^m to have at least2^m critical points: its Morse chain complex has one generator per critical point and its homology is the manifold homology, whose total dimension cannot exceed total chain dimension.

There is an explicit matching function:
\[
 f(x_1,\ldots,x_m)=\sum_{i=1}^m\cos(2\pi x_i),\qquad x_i\in\mathbb R/\mathbb Z.
\]
Its critical points have each x_i equal to0 or1/2, giving exactly2^m points. The Hessian there is diagonal with entries -(2pi)^2 cos(2pi x_i), all nonzero, so f is Morse. Therefore Morse(T^m)=2^m. With m=2n the imported fixed-point bound equals this ordinary Morse minimum and proves the literal target for every standard torus dimension.

Nondegenerate fixed points are isolated; compactness makes their set finite, so the count is well defined. Every Hamiltonian diffeomorphism is represented by a smooth Hamiltonian path and can be generated by a smooth1-periodic-in-time Hamiltonian after reparameterizing it to be constant near the endpoints; the time-one map and its nondegeneracy do not change. Thus the periodic formulation covers the prompt's maps. The standard symplectic form is part of the advertised scope; arbitrary symplectic forms on the same smooth torus are not silently identified with it.

The source's analytical finite-dimensional reduction is credited literature and was not rebuilt in a theorem prover here. The archived unverified twelve-dimensional counterexample announcement is not needed for this positive torus class, and no conclusion is drawn about arbitrary closed symplectic manifolds whose Morse minimum can exceed their Betti sum.
