\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(n\) index the size of a random combinatorial optimization instance. To express the proposed asymptotic relation, let \(E_n\) be its finite set of purchasable edges or arcs, and let \(\varnothing\ne\mathcal F_n\subseteq 2^{E_n}\) be its family of feasible solutions. Assign independent, identically distributed random costs \((W_e)_{e\in E_n}\), with the uniform or exponential distribution intended by the source. The uniform support and exponential rate are not specified in the supplied record.

For \(F\in\mathcal F_n\), write
\[
 c_n(F)=\sum_{e\in F}W_e,
 \qquad C_n=\min_{F\in\mathcal F_n}c_n(F),
\]
and let \(F_n^*\) be a minimizing solution. For a fixed finite feasible family, the stated continuous cost laws give an almost surely unique minimizer. Under the standard procurement interpretation of Vickrey cost, define, for each selected edge or arc \(e\in F_n^*\),
\[
 C_n^{-e}=\min\{c_n(F):F\in\mathcal F_n,\ e\notin F\},
 \qquad p_{n,e}=C_n^{-e}-(C_n-W_e),
\]
where the minimum of the empty set is \(+\infty\). The total Vickrey payment is
\[
 V_n=\sum_{e\in F_n^*}p_{n,e}.
\]
Thus \(p_{n,e}\) is the minimum cost of replacing the selected solution without \(e\), less the cost of the other elements of the selected solution. This payment convention makes the term ``Vickrey cost'' explicit, but its agreement with the intended report formulation requires source review.

The named feasible families are spanning arborescences in a directed graph, perfect matchings in a graph not assumed bipartite, and Hamiltonian tours in the symmetric or asymmetric traveling-salesman problem. A perfect matching covers each vertex exactly once and therefore requires an even number of vertices. A Hamiltonian tour visits every vertex exactly once before returning to its starting vertex. A spanning arborescence is a directed spanning tree with directed paths either from a designated root to every vertex or from every vertex to a designated root; the source does not select the root convention or direction. In the symmetric traveling-salesman interpretation, each undirected edge has a single cost shared by both traversal directions. In the asymmetric interpretation, costs attach to directed arcs.
