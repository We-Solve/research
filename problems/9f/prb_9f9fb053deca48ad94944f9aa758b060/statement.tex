\section{Numerical invariants — Question 13.4}\label{10300057}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 10300057 · Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A codimension-one foliation $\mathcal F$ decomposes a $3$-manifold $M$ into surface leaves, locally as the horizontal slices of a product chart. In the terminology of the source, it is taut if a closed curve transverse to $\mathcal F$ meets every leaf. A minimal foliation means that every leaf is dense; a minimal surface means a surface with vanishing mean curvature for the specified Riemannian metric. These two uses of minimal are distinct.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Find hyperbolic $M$ with taut foliations $\mathcal F,\mathcal G$ and a finite covering $p:\widehat M\to M$ such that there are ambient isotopies upstairs whose terminal maps $h_i$ satisfy $(h_i)_*(p^*\mathcal G)\to p^*\mathcal F$ geometrically, whereas no sequence of terminal maps of ambient isotopies downstairs takes $\mathcal G$ geometrically to $\mathcal F$. The convergence is local geometric convergence of the foliated structures; the upstairs isotopies need not descend. \sref{10300057}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can geometric degeneration of one taut foliation to another become possible in a finite cover when it is impossible on the original manifold?
