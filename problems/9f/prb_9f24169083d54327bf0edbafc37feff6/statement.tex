\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Use the following standard finite-matrix interpretation of the terminology. Let \(d\geq 1\), let \(M=(M_{ij})\in\{0,1\}^{d\times d}\), and let \(B=\mathcal O_M\) be a unital Cuntz--Krieger algebra with canonical generating partial isometries \(s_1,\ldots,s_d\). Their range projections are mutually orthogonal and satisfy
\[
\sum_{i=1}^{d}s_i s_i^*=1,
\qquad
s_i^*s_i=\sum_{j=1}^{d}M_{ij}s_j s_j^*
\quad(1\leq i\leq d).
\]
Assume that \(B\) is simple and purely infinite. Here simplicity means that \(B\) has no nonzero proper closed two-sided ideals; for a simple \(C^*\)-algebra, pure infiniteness means that every nonzero hereditary \(C^*\)-subalgebra contains an infinite projection. A projection \(p\) is infinite if there are a projection \(q<p\) and a partial isometry \(v\) with \(v^*v=p\) and \(vv^*=q\).

Let \(G\) be a finite abelian group and let \(\alpha:G\to\operatorname{Aut}(B)\) be an action. Interpret ``by diagonal quasi-free automorphisms'' to mean that there are characters \(\chi_i:G\to\mathbb T\), where \(\mathbb T=\{z\in\C:|z|=1\}\), such that
\[
\alpha_g(s_i)=\chi_i(g)s_i
\quad(g\in G,\ 1\leq i\leq d).
\]
Write
\[
B^\alpha=\{b\in B:\alpha_h(b)=b\text{ for every }h\in G\}.
\]
Use the fixed-point unitary formulation of strong approximate innerness: for every \(g\in G\), there is a sequence \((u_{g,n})_{n\geq1}\) of unitaries in \(B^\alpha\) such that
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\alpha_g(b)-u_{g,n}b u_{g,n}^*\|=0
\quad\text{for every }b\in B.
\]
This condition imposes no additional representation law on the implementing unitaries.

\textbf{Question.} For every simple purely infinite Cuntz--Krieger algebra \(B\) as above, every finite abelian group \(G\), and every action \(\alpha\) by diagonal quasi-free automorphisms, is \(\alpha\) strongly approximately inner in this sense?
