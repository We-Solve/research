\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Question 13 (Cornelissen) asks to determine all $(A,B,X,Y)\in\Z^4$ satisfying
\[
(A^2+B^2)(A^2+11B^2)=3^2\cdot5^2\,(X^2-5Y^2)^2.
\]
No positivity, coprimality, or nonzero restrictions are imposed in the source. It connects the equation to defining $\Z$ in $\Q$ by a $\Sigma_3^+$-formula and cites Cornelissen--Zahidi. The exact syntactic convention for this formula class is not supplied and is not replaced here by a different definability claim.

For the aid's partial reduction, put $F(a,b)=(a^2+b^2)(a^2+11b^2)$. A primitive square point means $a,b\in\Z$, $\gcd(a,b)=1$, and $c\in\Z_{>0}$ with $F(a,b)=c^2$. The aid asserts that such a point has $a$ odd and $b$ even, and satisfies exactly one of the two systems
\[
a^2+b^2=d u^2,\qquad a^2+11b^2=d v^2,\qquad d\in\{1,5\},\quad u,v\in\Z.
\]
The uniqueness concerns $d$, not the signs of $u,v$.

Call a rational prime $p$ inert if it remains prime in $\Q(\sqrt5)$. The supplied norm assertion is that a nonzero integer $n$ has the form $X^2-5Y^2$ with $X,Y\in\Z$ exactly when $v_p(n)$ is even for every inert prime $p$, where $v_p(n)=v_p(|n|)$. Zero is represented by $(X,Y)=(0,0)$ separately. On that basis, the aid gives the scale-invariant criterion
\[
\begin{split}
&\exists g\in\Z_{>0}\ \exists X,Y\in\Z:\quad
F(ga,gb)=225(X^2-5Y^2)^2\\
&\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
v_3(c)\text{ is odd and }v_p(c)\text{ is even for every inert }p\ne3.
\end{split}
\]
When this holds, it asserts that $g=1$ suffices if $5\mid c$, and $g=5$ otherwise suffices. The norm to be represented is $\pm g^2c/15$; these scale choices clear the denominator. This criterion concerns existence at some scale and does not assert that every positive scale works.

For $(a,b)=(1,2)$ one has $c=15$, giving the Pell-type family $A=g$, $B=2g$ and $X^2-5Y^2=\pm g^2$; for example $X+Y\sqrt5=g(2+\sqrt5)^k$ with $g\geq1$, $k\geq0$ gives solutions. In contrast, $(a,b)=(35,12)$ has $c=1961=37\cdot53$ and cannot lift at any scale by the stated criterion. The zero case $A=B=0$ forces $X=Y=0$. Every other original solution has a primitive pair obtained by dividing $A,B$ by their positive gcd, but the aid does not classify all primitive square points.
