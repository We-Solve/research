\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(d\geq 3\) be an integer and let \(s\in\R\) satisfy \(1<s<d-1\). Write \(|\cdot|\) for the Euclidean norm on \(\R^d\), and define the vector-valued kernel
\[
K_s(x)=\frac{x}{|x|^{s+1}},\qquad x\in\R^d\setminus\{0\}.
\]
For a nonnegative real-valued function \(f\in C_c^\infty(\R^d)\), define
\[
T_s f(x)=(K_s*f)(x)=\int_{\R^d}\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{s+1}}f(y)\,\mathrm{d}y,
\qquad
\operatorname{supp}f=\overline{\{y\in\R^d:f(y)\neq0\}}.
\]
The integral is absolutely convergent because \(s<d\) and \(f\) is smooth with compact support; the value of the kernel at the origin does not affect it.

The question is whether, for every such pair \((d,s)\), there exists a finite constant \(C=C(d,s)>0\), independent of \(f\), such that every nonnegative \(f\in C_c^\infty(\R^d)\) satisfies
\[
\sup_{x\in\R^d}|T_s f(x)|
\leq C\sup_{x\in\operatorname{supp}f}|T_s f(x)|.
\]
For \(f\equiv0\), interpret the right-hand supremum as \(0\); equivalently, formulate the question for nonzero nonnegative \(f\). This convention resolves the empty-support case, which the source does not specify. \subsection{Short English statement}
For each fixed dimension \(d\geq3\) and exponent \(1<s<d-1\), is the largest magnitude of the Riesz convolution of any nonnegative smooth compactly supported function bounded by a constant depending only on \(d,s\) times its largest magnitude on the function's support?
