\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
The source requests an intrinsic, simple characterization of regular model sets. In the standard Euclidean formulation, a Delone set \(\Lambda\subset\R^d\) is uniformly discrete and relatively dense: distinct points have a uniform positive separation, and every sufficiently large ball meets \(\Lambda\).

A cut-and-project scheme consists of \(\R^d\), a locally compact abelian internal group \(H\), and a lattice \(\mathcal L\subset\R^d\times H\), with the physical projection \(\pi_1|_{\mathcal L}\) injective and the internal projection \(\pi_2(\mathcal L)\) dense. For a window \(W\subset H\), define
\[
 \Lambda(W)=\{\pi_1(\ell):\ell\in\mathcal L,\ \pi_2(\ell)\in W\}.
\]
The usual regular-window convention takes \(W\) compact with nonempty interior, \(W=\overline{\operatorname{int}W}\), and Haar measure \(m_H(\partial W)=0\). Translates may be included in the model-set convention. Regularity is an additional window condition: merely arising from a cut-and-project construction does not impose zero boundary measure. Singular boundary choices, generic windows, and inter-model-set conventions need specification.
