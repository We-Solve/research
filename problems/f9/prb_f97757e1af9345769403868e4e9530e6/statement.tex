\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
\textbf{Symmetric lattice-polytope volume conjecture.} For a full-dimensional origin-symmetric lattice polytope \(P\subset\R^n\), \(n\ge1\), the proposed inequality is\[\operatorname{vol}_n(P)\ge\frac{2^{n-1}}{n!}\bigl(G(\operatorname{int}P)+1\bigr),\qquad G(\operatorname{int}P)=\#(\operatorname{int}P\cap\Z^n).\] Here a lattice polytope is the convex hull of finitely many lattice points, origin symmetry means \(P=-P\), and \(\operatorname{int}\) is ordinary ambient interior. Volume is Euclidean Lebesgue measure normalized so that a fundamental cell of \(\Z^n\) has volume one; it is not normalized lattice volume multiplied by \(n!\). The plus one and coefficient \(2^{n-1}/n!\) are retained exactly.\par Full dimensionality is not stated explicitly in the source but is a necessary qualification for this ambient-volume formulation. If lower-dimensional polytopes are included, both ambient volume and the count of ambient-interior points are zero, while the right side remains positive. A relative-dimensional variant would need dimension, relative interior, and induced-lattice volume specified separately, and is not silently substituted. In the full-dimensional case the origin is an interior lattice point by symmetry, so the count is not a boundary-inclusive lattice-point enumerator. No smoothness, simplicity, or upper bound on the number of interior lattice points is imposed.\par The supplied triage checked 2026-08-21 labels the conjecture open and reports no located complete resolution, citing only the report. It supplies no exact later bound or dimensional status comparison. The universal inequality is preserved with its full-dimensional qualification and without an independently asserted present-day status.
\subsection{Short English statement}
The conjecture bounds Euclidean volume below by the stated multiple of the interior lattice-point count plus one. Ambient full dimensionality is essential; using lower-dimensional polytopes literally would make the formula false. Boundary points and normalized lattice volume are not substituted.
