\section{Convex Relaxations for Planted Graph Matching}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.


A permutation $\pi$ represents a planted vertex correspondence; its permutation matrix has one $1$ in each row and column. A doubly stochastic matrix is an entrywise nonnegative square matrix with each row and column sum equal to $1$. The Frobenius norm is $\lVert M\rVert_F^2=\sum_{i,j}|M_{ij}|^2$. The optimization relaxes a search over permutation matrices to doubly stochastic matrices. Rounding converts an optimizer back into a vertex permutation, by the source rounding rule. With high probability means probability tending to $1$ as matrix dimension grows.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Let $\pi$ be a uniformly random permutation, and let $A$ and $B$ be correlated symmetric random matrices under the planted correspondence $\pi$. For which correlations does the minimizer of
$$
\min_{X\ \mathrm{doubly\ stochastic}}\|AX-XB\|_F^2
$$
round to $\pi$ with high probability? \href{https://doi.org/10.4171/owr/2022/14}{[S2]}
\paragraph{Scope qualification.}
The correlated random-matrix family, signal normalization and rounding map must be the ones specified by the source; arbitrary joint symmetric-matrix laws do not give the same recovery threshold.

\section{Research attempt: A cyclic-vector stability route and its missing random threshold}
Relabel vertices so that the ground-truth permutation is $I$, and first put $B=A$, with real symmetric $A$. Write $\mathbf1$ for the all-ones column and define the Krylov matrix
\[
 K_A=[\mathbf1,A\mathbf1,\ldots,A^{n-1}\mathbf1].
\]
Assume $K_A$ is invertible. If $AX=XA$ and $X\mathbf1=\mathbf1$, then induction gives $XA^j\mathbf1=A^j\mathbf1$ for every $j$. Hence $XK_A=K_A$ and $X=I$. Since $I$ is doubly stochastic and has objective zero, it is the unique doubly stochastic minimizer when $B=A$.

This condition is generic in the full space of real symmetric matrices. The polynomial $\det K_A$ in the independent matrix entries is not identically zero: for $A=\operatorname{diag}(a_1,\ldots,a_n)$ with distinct $a_i$, $K_A$ is a Vandermonde matrix. Its determinant is $\prod_{i<j}(a_j-a_i)$, as follows because the determinant is alternating, vanishes whenever two $a_i$ coincide, has the product's degree, and has leading coefficient one. A nonzero real polynomial has a zero set of Lebesgue measure zero: induct on the number of variables, regard it as a polynomial in the last variable, exclude the measure-zero set where all coefficient polynomials vanish, and use the finite number of roots in the remaining one-dimensional fibers together with Fubini on bounded boxes. Thus any symmetric matrix law with a joint density on all independent entries, including diagonal entries, satisfies the condition almost surely. This statement alone does not cover Bernoulli entries or an imposed zero diagonal.

For $n\geq2$, define the real linear space
\[
 \mathcal L=\{H:H\mathbf1=0,\ \mathbf1^TH=0\},\qquad
 \kappa_A=\min_{H\in\mathcal L,\ \|H\|_F=1}\|AH-HA\|_F.
\]
The unit sphere here is nonempty and compact. If a minimizer had zero commutator, then $HA^j\mathbf1=A^jH\mathbf1=0$ for every $j$, whence $HK_A=0$ and $H=0$, a contradiction. Thus $\kappa_A>0$.

Now let $B=A+E$, and let $X$ be any doubly stochastic minimizer. Feasibility of $I$ gives $\|AX-XB\|_F\leq\|E\|_F$. Every doubly stochastic matrix satisfies $\|X\|_2\leq1$: for a real vector $v$, convexity of the square in each row gives $\sum_i(\sum_jX_{ij}v_j)^2\leq\sum_{ij}X_{ij}v_j^2=\|v\|_2^2$. Applying this bound column by column yields $\|XE\|_F\leq\|E\|_F$. Consequently
\[
 \|AX-XA\|_F\leq\|AX-XB\|_F+\|XE\|_F\leq2\|E\|_F,
 \qquad \|X-I\|_F\leq {2\|E\|_F\over\kappa_A}.       \tag{4}
\]
The last step uses $X-I\in\mathcal L$ and homogeneity; it is immediate if $X=I$.

As a declared auxiliary rounding rule, choose the permutation matrix nearest to $X$ in Frobenius norm. Every permutation $P\ne I$ moves at least two rows, so $\|P-I\|_F\geq2$. If $\delta=\|X-I\|_F<1$, the triangle inequality gives $\|X-P\|_F\geq2-\delta>\delta$, proving unique rounding to $I$. Thus $\|E\|_F<\kappa_A/2$ is sufficient.

The key obstruction is quantitative. Positivity of $\kappa_A$ at each fixed dimension does not give a useful lower bound as $n$ grows or show that constant-correlation noise satisfies (4). Also genericity cannot be replaced by perfect correlation alone: if $A\mathbf1=d\mathbf1$ and $A$ is symmetric, then $J/n$ is doubly stochastic and $A(J/n)=(J/n)A$, so for $n>1$ there is an uninformative zero-objective minimizer distinct from $I$. This regular-matrix example is not a counterexample to the source's iid random law.
\section{Verification and adversarial audit}
The perturbation estimate applies to every optimizer and uses no uniqueness assumption in the noisy case. The condition on a joint density explicitly includes the diagonal, avoiding an unjustified transfer from the full symmetric space to a lower-dimensional ensemble. At $n=1$ the doubly stochastic set is already $\{I\}$ and the sphere defining $\kappa_A$ is unnecessary. Equality $\|E\|_F=\kappa_A/2$ is not claimed sufficient because the rounding comparison would be non-strict.
