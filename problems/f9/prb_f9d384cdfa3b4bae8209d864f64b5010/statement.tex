\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A group \(\Gamma\) has the \emph{Jordan property} if there exists an integer \(J\geq 1\) such that every finite subgroup \(G\leq\Gamma\) contains an abelian normal subgroup \(A\trianglelefteq G\) with \([G:A]\leq J\).

For an integral variety \(X\) over a field \(k\), write \(\operatorname{Bir}_k(X)\) for the group of birational self-maps defined over \(k\). For a smooth projective geometrically integral \(k\)-variety, rational connectedness means that, after extension to an algebraic closure \(\overline{k}\), two general points can be joined by a rational curve.

\textbf{Qualified mathematical target.} The reference to Cremona groups suggests interpreting the requested Jordan property as that of \(\operatorname{Bir}_k(X)\). Under this interpretation, and using smooth projective geometrically integral varieties to make rational connectedness precise, the property to be proved is
\[
\begin{gathered}
\text{for every field }k\text{ with }\operatorname{char}(k)=0
\text{ and every rationally connected }k\text{-variety }X,\\
\exists J_{k,X}\in\Z_{\geq1}\quad
\forall\text{ finite }G\leq\operatorname{Bir}_k(X)\quad
\exists A\trianglelefteq G:\quad
A\text{ is abelian and }[G:A]\leq J_{k,X}.
\end{gathered}
\]
