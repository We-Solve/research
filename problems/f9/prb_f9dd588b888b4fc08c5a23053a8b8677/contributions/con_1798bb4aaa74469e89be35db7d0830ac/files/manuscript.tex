\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.


A real $n\times p$ matrix $A$ has the restricted isometry property of order $s$ with distortion $\delta\in(0,1)$ if $(1-\delta)\|x\|_2^2\leq\|Ax\|_2^2\leq(1+\delta)\|x\|_2^2$ for every $x\in\mathbb R^p$ with at most $s$ nonzero coordinates. Here $n$ counts measurements and $p$ is the ambient dimension. The asymptotic question fixes a nontrivial distortion bound independently of $n,p$. An explicit construction produces the matrix deterministically with controlled construction time.

\subsection{Known realification and the exponent quantifier}
The historical workshop discussion separates real and complex constructions. However, Bourgain, Dilworth, Ford, Konyagin and Kutzarova explicitly state realification in Section 1 of their primary paper. Their Theorem 1 supplies an effective $\varepsilon_0>0$ and explicit complex $m\times P$ matrices for sufficiently large $m$ and $m\le P\le m^{1+\varepsilon_0}$, with order $\lfloor m^{1/2+\varepsilon_0}\rfloor$ and distortion $m^{-\varepsilon_0}$. This is an attributed theorem, not reproved here. The transfer is elementary: if $\Phi=C+iD$ has complex RIP of order $s$, then
\[
B=\begin{pmatrix}C&-D\\D&C\end{pmatrix}
\]
has real RIP of that order, because a real $s$-sparse vector $(u,v)$ gives a complex vector $u+iv$ with at most $s$ nonzero coordinates and the same input and output norms. Doubling dimensions preserves a beyond-square-root exponent after any fixed slack $0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_0$, since $m^{\varepsilon_0-\varepsilon}\to\infty$. Consequently a blanket claim that no positive exponent is available over the reals is inappropriate. This known observation neither supplies every prescribed feasible $\varepsilon$ nor the separate polylogarithmic measurement regime. Realification adds constant-factor storage and arithmetic cost; we do not independently audit the full external construction's proof or precision complexity.
\subsection{Testing an explicit two-basis route}
Here is a complete obstruction to a simpler proposed construction. Let $m=2^r$ with integer $r\ge1$, set $n=m^2$, and let $H_m$ be the normalized Walsh matrix, with rows and columns indexed by $\mathbb F_2^r$ and entry $m^{-1/2}(-1)^{a\cdot b}$. Define
\[
H=H_m\otimes H_m,\qquad A=[I_n\ H].
\]
All entries of $H$ equal $\pm1/m$, so this is a rational, polynomial-time constructible real $n\times2n$ matrix. A direct parity computation writes its $O(n^2)$ entries in $O(n^2\log n)$ bit operations, with $O(\log n)$ bits per entry.
\begin{proposition}
The coherence estimate gives $\delta_s\le(s-1)/\sqrt n$ for $A$, but $A$ fails RIP with any distortion below one at order $2\sqrt n$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Summing Walsh characters gives $H_m^TH_m=I$: the sum is $m$ for the zero character and zero otherwise, by pairing terms that differ in a coordinate where the character is nonzero. Hence both blocks of $A$ are orthonormal bases, while every cross inner product has magnitude $1/m$. For any $s$-sparse $x$,
\[
\big|\|Ax\|_2^2-\|x\|_2^2\big|
\le m^{-1}\sum_{i\ne j}|x_ix_j|
\le m^{-1}(s-1)\|x\|_2^2,
\]
using Cauchy--Schwarz on its support. For a stronger obstruction, let $e_0$ be the coordinate of the zero binary vector and $\mathbf1_m$ the all-ones vector. Then $H_me_0=m^{-1/2}\mathbf1_m$ and $H_m\mathbf1_m=\sqrt m e_0$. Thus $h=e_0\otimes\mathbf1_m$ has exactly $m$ nonzero entries, and $Hh=\mathbf1_m\otimes e_0$ also has exactly $m$. The nonzero vector $z=(-Hh,h)$ has exactly $2m$ nonzero coordinates and satisfies $Az=0$. The lower RIP inequality is impossible when $\delta<1$.
\end{proof}
\subsection{Verification and remaining gaps}
Exact rational Walsh computations for $m=2,4,8$ check orthogonality, coherence and this sparse kernel. The route fails at $2\sqrt n$ for this family itself, not merely because its coherence proof is weak. It cannot achieve any fixed $n^{1/2+\varepsilon}$ eventually. The known external theorem above does cover some positive exponents; reproducing that fact is not new progress, and no construction covering the original exponent quantifier and all its unstated representation requirements is proved here.
