\section{Explicit RIP Matrices Beyond Square-Root Sparsity}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.


A real $n\times p$ matrix $A$ has the restricted isometry property of order $s$ with distortion $\delta\in(0,1)$ if $(1-\delta)\|x\|_2^2\leq\|Ax\|_2^2\leq(1+\delta)\|x\|_2^2$ for every $x\in\mathbb R^p$ with at most $s$ nonzero coordinates. Here $n$ counts measurements and $p$ is the ambient dimension. The asymptotic question fixes a nontrivial distortion bound independently of $n,p$. An explicit construction produces the matrix deterministically with controlled construction time.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For fixed $\varepsilon>0$, construct explicit real-valued restricted-isometry matrices in the polynomial regime $n=p^{O(1)}$ with sparsity $s\geq n^{1/2+\varepsilon}$. \sref{statement}{2}
\paragraph{Scope qualification.}
The source asks about the compressed regime $n<p$, with a fixed distortion below one. Sparsity must obey the feasibility restrictions of this regime; the supplied text does not state a range of exponents or a numerical distortion. Construction cost and representation precision must be stated in any answer.

\subsection{Short English statement}
Construct real restricted-isometry matrices beating square-root sparsity when the number of measurements grows polynomially with the ambient dimension.
