\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 701218}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a finite group \(G\), let
\[
\pi(G)=\{p:p\text{ is prime and }p\mid |G|\}.
\]
Its prime graph \(\Gamma(G)\) is the simple undirected graph with vertex set \(\pi(G)\), in which distinct primes \(p,q\) are adjacent if and only if \(G\) contains an element of order \(pq\). Here two prime graphs coincide when their vertex sets agree as sets of primes and their adjacency relations agree on those primes; arbitrary graph isomorphism without preservation of prime labels is not the stated relation.

A group is simple if it is nontrivial and its only normal subgroups are the identity subgroup and the whole group. The groups considered below are finite, simple, and non-abelian.

\textbf{Question (A. V. Vasil'ev, 2006).} Does there exist a positive integer \(k\) such that there is no family \(G_1,\ldots,G_k\) of pairwise non-isomorphic finite non-abelian simple groups satisfying
\[
\Gamma(G_1)=\cdots=\Gamma(G_k)?
\]
Equivalently, is there a uniform finite bound on the number of isomorphism classes of finite non-abelian simple groups sharing any fixed prime graph with its prime labels?

\textbf{Supplied conjecture.} The integer \(k=5\) has this property: there do not exist five pairwise non-isomorphic finite non-abelian simple groups whose prime graphs coincide. This asserts that each such graph belongs to at most four isomorphism classes; it does not additionally assert that five is the least possible value of \(k\).
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is the number of pairwise non-isomorphic finite non-abelian simple groups with any one fixed prime graph uniformly bounded? A. V. Vasil'ev's supplied conjecture says that no five such groups have the same graph, with vertices identified by their actual prime labels.
