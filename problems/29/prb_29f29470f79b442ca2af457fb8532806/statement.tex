\section{Oceanic Mid-Depth Stratification and Overturning Circulation}\label{30001529}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 30001529 · Mathematical Physics}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


\paragraph{Definitions and conventions.} Oceanic stratification is the vertical variation of density, often represented by $N^2=-(g/\rho_{\rm ref})\partial_z\rho$ in a stably stratified Boussinesq convention. Overturning circulation is the large-scale transport in vertical and meridional directions, measured by a corresponding streamfunction. The source's mid-depth region lies approximately 1000--3000 m below the surface, below the main thermocline and above the abyss. Its model has a semi-enclosed basin and a southern reentrant channel representing the Antarctic Circumpolar Current.

\paragraph{Source scope and qualifications.} The report argues for a mean-versus-eddy overturning mechanism in its specific basin/channel geometry. The title question is a physical mechanism program; no universal inversion or exact circulation formula for every ocean is stated.
