\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For an integer \(n\geq 1\), write \([n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}\). Let \(\Sigma\) be a real symmetric positive semidefinite \(n\times n\) matrix, and let \(X=(X_1,\ldots,X_n)\) have Gaussian distribution \(N_n(\mu,\Sigma)\), where \(\mu\in\R^n\). For \(A\subseteq[n]\), write \(X_A=(X_i)_{i\in A}\). For pairwise disjoint subsets \(A,B,C\subseteq[n]\), with \(A,B\) nonempty, the conditional independence assertion \(X_A\perp\!\!\!\perp X_B\mid X_C\) means that, for every pair of bounded Borel functions \(f,g\),
\[
\E[f(X_A)g(X_B)\mid X_C]
=
\E[f(X_A)\mid X_C]\,\E[g(X_B)\mid X_C]
\quad\text{almost surely}.
\]
When \(C=\varnothing\), this means ordinary independence.

Given a collection \(\mathcal I\) of such triples \((A,B,C)\), define its Gaussian conditional independence model in covariance coordinates by
\[
\mathcal M_{\mathcal I}^{\mathrm{psd}}
=
\left\{\Sigma\in\R^{n\times n}:
\Sigma=\Sigma^{\mathsf T},\ \Sigma\succeq0,\quad
X_A\perp\!\!\!\perp X_B\mid X_C
\text{ for every }(A,B,C)\in\mathcal I,
\ X\sim N_n(0,\Sigma)
\right\}.
\]
The nondegenerate version is
\[
\mathcal M_{\mathcal I}^{\mathrm{pd}}
=\{\Sigma\in\mathcal M_{\mathcal I}^{\mathrm{psd}}:\Sigma\succ0\}.
\]
The zero mean is a coordinate convention: conditional independence is unchanged by deterministic translations, so unrestricted means can be restored as a factor \(\R^n\). The conditions indexed by \(\mathcal I\) are required to hold; additional conditional independences are allowed in these definitions.

For positive definite \(\Sigma\), an assertion indexed by \((A,B,C)\) is equivalent to
\[
\Sigma_{A,B}-\Sigma_{A,C}\Sigma_{C,C}^{-1}\Sigma_{C,B}=0.
\]
For \(C=\varnothing\), this equation means \(\Sigma_{A,B}=0\). For nonempty \(C\), clearing the nonzero denominator \(\det(\Sigma_{C,C})\) gives polynomial equations. This formulation does not use an inverse on singular covariance matrices.
