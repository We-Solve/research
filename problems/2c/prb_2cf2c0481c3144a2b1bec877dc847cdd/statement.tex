\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
In the usual simple undirected graph convention, let $\Delta$ be a fixed graph. For a graph $\Gamma$ and vertex $v$, write
\[
 N_\Gamma(v)=\{w\in V(\Gamma):vw\in E(\Gamma)\}
\]
for its open neighborhood, and $\Gamma[N_\Gamma(v)]$ for the subgraph induced by those neighbors. The graph $\Gamma$ is locally $\Delta$ if
\[
 \Gamma[N_\Gamma(v)]\cong\Delta\quad\text{for every }v\in V(\Gamma).
\]
The local-recognition problem is to classify, up to graph isomorphism, all $\Gamma$ satisfying this condition for the given $\Delta$.

No vertex-transitivity or connectedness assumption is in the supplied target. If disconnected graphs are allowed, each connected component must itself be locally $\Delta$, so a connected classification gives the unrestricted nonempty cases by disjoint unions. \subsection{Short English statement}
For a prescribed graph $\Delta$, classify graphs whose induced neighbor graph at every vertex is $\Delta$. The source gives the general local-recognition programme, without choosing a specific local graph or finite/connected ambient class.
