\section{Second Hardy–Littlewood zeta-function conjecture}\label{9400183}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 9400183 · Number Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


The Riemann zeta function is $\zeta(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-s}$ for $\operatorname{Re}s>1$, continued meromorphically to $\mathbb C$. A zero has odd order if its multiplicity as a zero of this meromorphic function is a positive odd integer. For $T>0$, put
\[N_0(T)=\#\{t\in(0,T]:\zeta(1/2+it)=0\text{ and the zero has odd order}\}.\]
Thus this counts the distinct positive ordinates of odd-order zeros on the critical line $\operatorname{Re}s=1/2$; it includes all odd multiplicities, rather than only simple zeros.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every $\varepsilon>0$, do there exist constants $T_0(\varepsilon)>0$ and $c(\varepsilon)>0$ such that, for every $T\ge T_0(\varepsilon)$ and $H=T^{1/2+\varepsilon}$, one has $N_0(T+H)-N_0(T)\ge c(\varepsilon)H$? \sref{9400183}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every sufficiently high interval of the specified length contain at least a constant times its length many odd-order zeros of the Riemann zeta function on its critical line?
