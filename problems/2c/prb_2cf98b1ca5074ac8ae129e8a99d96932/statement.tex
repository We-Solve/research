\section{The Hadwiger Covering Conjecture}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A convex body in $\mathbb R^n$ is a compact convex set $K$ with nonempty interior. A smaller positive homothetic copy of $K$ is $t+\lambda K=\{t+\lambda x:x\in K\}$ with $t\in\mathbb R^n$ and $0<\lambda<1$. Different copies may have different translation vectors and scale factors.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For every integer $n\geq1$ and every convex body $K\subset\mathbb R^n$, do there exist $m\leq2^n$, vectors $t_1,\ldots,t_m\in\mathbb R^n$, and numbers $0<\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_m<1$ such that
\[
K\subseteq\bigcup_{j=1}^m(t_j+\lambda_jK)?
\] \sref{statement}{2}

\subsection{Short English statement}
Can every convex body be covered by at most two to the dimension smaller translated and positively scaled copies of itself?
