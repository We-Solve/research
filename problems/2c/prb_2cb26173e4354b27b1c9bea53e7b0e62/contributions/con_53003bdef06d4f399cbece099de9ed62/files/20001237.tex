\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.


For a statistical model with parameter $\theta$ and observations $D$, the likelihood is $L(\theta;D)=\Pr_\theta(D)$, or the corresponding density. Maximum likelihood seeks a global maximum over the admissible parameter space. For an integer matrix $A$, the fiber at $b$ is $\mathcal F_b=\{x\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^d:Ax=b\}$. A Markov basis is a finite set of integer moves $u$ with $Au=0$ such that steps $x\mapsto x\pm u$ staying nonnegative connect every nonempty fiber. In a log-linear model, sufficient statistics are $b=Ax$. A zero-dimensional likelihood solution space has finitely many algebraic points, counted with multiplicity; positive-dimensional fibers contain algebraic families of solutions. The task is to describe exceptional statistic vectors giving nonzero-dimensional likelihood solution sets.

\section{Research attempt}
\subsection{Literature and scope}
Question 60 asks for statistic values yielding positive-dimensional algebraic likelihood solution sets. I tested whether uniqueness of a real MLE would itself rule out such exceptional algebraic fibers. The standard convex calculation below proves real uniqueness in a carefully delimited interior region, but gives no such conclusion about complex solutions.
\begin{proposition}
Let $a_1,\ldots,a_m\in\mathbb R^r$ affinely span $\mathbb R^r$ and $p_j(\theta)=e^{a_j\cdot\theta}/\sum_ke^{a_k\cdot\theta}$. For positive total count $N$ and empirical sufficient statistic $t=b/N$ in the interior of $\operatorname{conv}\{a_j\}$, there is exactly one real finite parameter critical point; it is the unique MLE and is isolated.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Minimize $h(\theta)=\log\sum_je^{a_j\cdot\theta}-t\cdot\theta$, whose negative times $N$ is the log likelihood up to a constant. Its Hessian is the covariance of the vectors $a_j$ under the strictly positive weights $p_j(\theta)$. For any nonzero vector $v$, its quadratic form is the variance of $v\cdot a_j$. A zero variance would say these values are all equal, contradicting affine spanning. Thus the Hessian is positive definite everywhere and $h$ is strictly convex.

Since $t$ is interior, some Euclidean ball of radius $\epsilon>0$ centered at $t$ lies in the convex hull. For $\theta\ne0$, the hull contains $t+\epsilon\theta/\|\theta\|$, whence
\[
h(\theta)\ge\max_j(a_j-t)\cdot\theta\ge\epsilon\|\theta\|.
\]
Therefore $h$ is coercive. A continuous coercive function achieves a global minimum: take a closed sufficiently large ball containing a sublevel set through $\theta=0$ and minimize on that compact ball. Its minimizer is interior to parameter space and has zero gradient. Strict convexity permits at most one critical point, and positive-definite Hessian makes it isolated. This proves all assertions.
\end{proof}
\subsection{Failed inference and verification}
For features $0,1$ and $t=2/5$, the unique natural parameter is $\log(2/3)$ and its Hessian is $t(1-t)=6/25$. If instead features are duplicated as $(0,0),(1,1)$, the law depends only on $\theta_1+\theta_2$ and every fitted value has a line of parameter preimages. This is redundancy, not multiple fitted distributions.

The proof uses ordered real positivity and convexity. It does not control complex algebraic score fibers after changing to monomial coordinates, and it does not apply to boundary statistics or nonminimal coordinates. Inferring that the entire complex solution set is zero-dimensional from this real uniqueness would be the first unsupported step. The exceptional algebraic-statistic locus in the original question is therefore not determined here. The convex result is standard and not counted as new progress.
