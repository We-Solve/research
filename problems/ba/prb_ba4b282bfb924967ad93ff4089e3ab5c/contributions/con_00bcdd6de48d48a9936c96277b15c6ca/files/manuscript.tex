\paragraph{A vanishing-input instability test.} For integers \(n\geq1\), take smooth two-component inputs
\[
 x_n(t)=n^{-1/2}(\cos(nt)-1),\qquad y_n(t)=n^{-1/2}\sin(nt),\quad t\in[0,T].
\]
Both converge uniformly to zero. Drive the ordinary differential equations \(du_n=dx_n\), \(dv_n=u_n\,dy_n\), with zero initial data. Then \(u_n=x_n\) and direct integration yields
\[
 v_n(t)=\int_0^t(\cos^2(ns)-\cos(ns))ds
 =\frac t2+\frac{\sin(2nt)}{4n}-\frac{\sin(nt)}n.
\]
Thus \(v_n\to t/2\) uniformly, although the zero input produces \(v\equiv0\). Supremum control of the input alone cannot make this nonlinear solution map continuous.
\paragraph{Interpretation for unresolved scales.} The missing quantity is a signed iterated integral: the small loops retain finite area. This is an explicit reason an approximation of unresolved forcing may need more information than pointwise or uniform convergence. It does not imply that every climate closure requires rough paths; a linear additive equation would not have the same area sensitivity, and a spatially singular nonlinear PDE may require further renormalized products.
