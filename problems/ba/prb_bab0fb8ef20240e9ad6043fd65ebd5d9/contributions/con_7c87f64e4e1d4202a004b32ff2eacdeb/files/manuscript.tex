\paragraph{Shared notation.}Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.


Polynomial time means a number of elementary steps bounded by a polynomial in the encoded input length. A decision problem is in $\mathrm{NP}$ if yes-instances have polynomial-length certificates verifiable in polynomial time; NP-hardness is under polynomial-time reductions.

A real symmetric matrix is copositive if $x^TAx\geq0$ for every $x\geq0$. It is completely positive if $A=BB^T$ for an entrywise nonnegative matrix $B$; the minimum number of columns of $B$ is its cp-rank.

\subsection{Verbatim supplied catalogue question}

\section{The attempted certificate argument}
Dur's report, printed pages 2721--2723, asks both about NP membership and
cp-rank, and describes a factorization heuristic whose success is not universal.
For a standard Turing-model membership question we take the input symmetric
matrix $A$ to have rational entries in binary. Arbitrary unencoded real inputs
do not define that NP question. The proposed shortcut is to bound factor
width and then declare the factor a polynomial certificate. The first part
can be proved; the second needs additional information about the entries.

\paragraph{A complete polynomial-width proof.}
Let $D=n(n+1)/2$, the dimension of the space of real symmetric matrices.
If $A$ is completely positive, start with a finite sum
$A=\sum_{j=1}^k b_jb_j^T$, $b_j\ge0$, discarding zero terms.
If $k>D$, the symmetric rank-one matrices are linearly dependent:
$\sum_j c_jb_jb_j^T=0$ for some nonzero real vector $c$.
The coefficients have both signs: taking traces gives
$\sum_jc_j\|b_j\|^2=0$, which cannot hold with nonzero coefficients of
just one sign. Choose $t=\min_{c_j>0}1/c_j$. Then
\[
A=\sum_j(1-tc_j)b_jb_j^T,
\]
all new coefficients are nonnegative and at least one vanishes. Absorb each
positive coefficient into its vector by its nonnegative square root and
repeat. Each iteration decreases the number of terms, proving
$\operatorname{cp}(A)\le D$. The zero matrix has an empty decomposition
(or zero columns if required by a width convention).

This proof gives an existential polynomial number of real parameters, not
polynomially many bits. Even $A=(2)$ has cp-rank one with its unique
nonnegative one-column factor $(\sqrt2)$, which is not rational: if
$\sqrt2=a/b$ in lowest terms then $a^2=2b^2$ forces both $a$ and $b$ even.
This example does not disprove NP membership, because $A=(2)$ also has the
rational two-column factor $(1,1)$ and a short algebraic description of the
one-column factor. It merely rejects the inference that a minimum-width
factor can always be listed as rational numbers without further argument.
No general polynomial bound on a suitable exact certificate's bit length
or algebraic representation is proved here.

\paragraph{The exact real-feasibility reduction and its limit.}
For each integer $k\le D$, the system
\[
b_{ir}\ge0,\qquad\sum_{r=1}^k b_{ir}b_{jr}=A_{ij}
\quad(1\le i\le j\le n)
\]
is feasible over the reals if and only if $\operatorname{cp}(A)\le k$;
zero-padding proves the forward comparison of widths, and its equations
are exactly the required factorization. Thus an exact existential-real
feasibility subroutine would determine cp-rank by testing finitely many
widths. This is a reduction to another decision task, not a polynomial-time
algorithm or a supplied proof of that subroutine's complexity.

\section{A family where ordinary rank badly underestimates cp-rank}
Let $G$ be a finite simple triangle-free graph with $m$ edges and no isolated
vertices. Set
\[
A_G=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E(G)}(e_i+e_j)(e_i+e_j)^T.
\]
Its diagonal is the degree sequence, and its off-diagonal positive entries
are exactly the graph edges, all equal to one. The displayed nonnegative
factor proves $\operatorname{cp}(A_G)\le m$.
In any nonnegative factorization, each column's positive support is a clique:
if two support entries were positive at a nonedge $i,j$, their positive
product would contradict $A_{ij}=0$, since all other products are nonnegative.
Triangle-freeness therefore restricts a column to at most two positive
entries. It can account for at most one positive off-diagonal graph edge.
Every edge must be accounted for by some column, so at least $m$ columns
are necessary. Consequently $\operatorname{cp}(A_G)=m$.

For $G=K_{a,b}$ with $a,b\ge2$, this is $ab$. The ordinary rank is
$a+b-1$. Indeed, if $B$ is the displayed edge-column matrix,
$B^Tx=0$ says $x_i+x_j=0$ for every cross-part pair. These equations force
one constant on the first part and its negative on the second, giving a
one-dimensional kernel. Also $\ker(BB^T)=\ker(B^T)$ because
$x^TBB^Tx=\|B^Tx\|^2$. Hence the rank assertion follows.
Balanced parts thus give quadratic cp-rank but only linear ordinary rank.
An attempt to find a nonnegative factor merely by rotating an ordinary
rank-width factor must fail on this family: rotation preserves its width,
which is too small. Additional zero columns are necessary before such a
rotation can even be possible.
