\section*{Evolution of evolvability}
\label{prob:156}
\noindent\textbf{Status: restricted result or obstruction; full target not established.}
\subsection{Target and scope}
\paragraph{Catalogue definitions and assumptions.}
In an asexual bacterial population of size $N$, genotype $g$ has current log fitness $w(g,e)$ in environmental state $e$. A modifier allele $m$ changes a normalized mutation kernel $Q_m(g'\mid g)$ and incurs immediate cost $c_m$. Environments follow a specified finite-state Markov chain with transition matrix $P$. Define one-step adaptive opportunity $E_m(g,e)=\sum_{g'}Q_m(g'\mid g)[w(g',e)-w(g,e)]_+$, where $[a]_+=\max(a,0)$. This is not the modifier's present reproductive fitness. The modifier assay must measure the entire mutation spectrum, including nonbeneficial outcomes, before selection filters variants. Define the mutant's initial background explicitly, since its success can depend on whether a beneficial lineage was already expanding when it arose. Interpret $\lambda_m$ as the early rare-lineage growth exponent of a multitype branching approximation in a stationary infinite resident population, not the long-time exponent of an absorbed finite population. Finite-$N$ fixation is a separate stochastic quantity.
\paragraph{Original target.}
Characterize when modifiers increasing adaptive opportunity are themselves selected, and predict their fixation probability:
\[
 E_m(g,e)>E_0(g,e),\qquad
 \lambda_m(P,Q_m,c_m)>0,
 \qquad \Pr(\mathrm{fixation}\mid m\text{ introduced once})>1/N.
\]
The growth rate $\lambda_m$ must include linkage to subsequent beneficial and deleterious descendants. Seek a phase diagram that predicts outcomes of held-out environmental-switching experiments.
\paragraph{Catalogue alternative formulations.}
(i) Under rare mutation and sequential fixation, calculate path-dependent modifier success; compare with clonal interference at high mutation supply. (ii) In sexual populations vary recombination between modifier and target loci, testing whether localized mutation bias persists when hitchhiking associations are broken.
\subsection{Attempted answer}
\begin{proposition}
Increasing positive-part adaptive opportunity does not imply positive invasion. In a rank-one mutation-selection baseline that redraws offspring state independently each generation, modifier growth factor is $(1-c)\mathbb{E} e^{\Delta w}$, whereas opportunity is $\mathbb{E}[\Delta w]_+$. These can rank modifiers oppositely.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The mutation-selection matrix has rank one, with nonzero eigenvalue equal to mean reproductive multiplier. For example, increments $\log2$ with probability $0.1$ and $-\log100$ with probability $0.9$ give positive opportunity but mean multiplier $0.209<1$, even before a cost is added.
\end{proof}
\subsection{What remains unresolved}
The example does not reproduce linked finite-population evolution. The full mutation spectrum, initial background, environment, fixation probability and hitchhiking need separate modeling.
\subsection{Completion Estimate}
\textbf{20\%.} The displayed calculation is established under its additional assumptions. The broader theoretical and biological requirements listed above are not established.

\section{Shared mathematical bounds}\label{app:bounds}
\begin{lemma}[Independent bounded sums]\label{lem:bounded}
Let independent real variables $X_i$ satisfy $a_i\leq X_i\leq b_i$ almost surely,
and let $S=\sum_{i=1}^n(X_i-\mathbb{E} X_i)$. If
$V=\sum_i(b_i-a_i)^2>0$, then for every $t>0$,
\[
 \mathbb{P}(|S|\geq t)\leq 2\exp(-2t^2/V).
\]
In particular, independent losses in $[0,1]$ have mean-error bound
$\mathbb{P}(|\overline X-\mathbb{E}\overline X|\geq\epsilon)
\leq2e^{-2n\epsilon^2}$. The same expression for variables in $[-1,1]$
is $2e^{-n\epsilon^2/2}$. If $V=0$ then $S=0$ almost surely.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $X\in[a,b]$ with mean $\mu$, the nonnegative product
$(X-a)(b-X)$ gives
$\operatorname{Var} X\leq(\mu-a)(b-\mu)\leq(b-a)^2/4$.
Let $K(s)=\log\mathbb{E}\exp[s(X-\mathbb{E} X)]$.
Boundedness permits two differentiations. Its second derivative is the variance
under the exponentially tilted law, which has the same support, so
$K''(s)\leq(b-a)^2/4$ for every real $s$.
Because $K(0)=K'(0)=0$, integration yields
$K(s)\leq s^2(b-a)^2/8$.
Independence adds these log moment-generating bounds for $S$.
For $s>0$, Markov's inequality gives
$\mathbb{P}(S\geq t)\leq\exp(-st+s^2V/8)$.
Choosing $s=4t/V$ yields the upper-tail bound $e^{-2t^2/V}$.
Apply it also to $-S$ and add. The stated mean versions follow by substituting
$t=n\epsilon$ and the respective support widths.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Finite-family extension.}
For $M$ prespecified bounded-loss comparisons, apply the relevant inequality to
each and use a union bound. Independence between comparisons is not required;
independence of the biological units within each comparison is. Repeated cells,
reads or time points from one dependent biological unit cannot automatically be
counted as independent units.

\paragraph{No data-dependent shortcut.}
The bound does not justify choosing a model or validation loss using the same
outcomes and then reporting single-model coverage. A prespecified finite family,
a separate validation sample, or an appropriate complexity or sequential argument
is needed. Marginal predictive coverage likewise should not be read as coverage
for every subgroup or under arbitrary distribution shift.
