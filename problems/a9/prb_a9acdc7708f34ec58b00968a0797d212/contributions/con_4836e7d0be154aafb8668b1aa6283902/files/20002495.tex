\paragraph{Definitions and notation.}In the standard bosonic Fock representation, $a|n\rangle=\sqrt n\,|n-1\rangle$, $a^\dagger|n\rangle=\sqrt{n+1}\,|n+1\rangle$, $[a,a^\dagger]=I$ and the number operator is $N=a^\dagger a$. \paragraph{Exact diagonal parameter slice.}Set \(\beta=0\), retaining arbitrary real \(\omega,\chi,\gamma\). In the number basis, the displayed operator is diagonal:
\[H|n\rangle=d_n|n\rangle,\qquad d_n=\chi n^2+(\omega-i\gamma)n-i\gamma,\quad n\ge0.
\]
On its maximal diagonal domain \(\{u:\sum|d_nu_n|^2<\infty\}\), the spectrum is the closure of \(\{d_n\}\). Indeed outside that closure the diagonal inverse is bounded, while each \(d_n\) is an eigenvalue and every finite accumulation point prevents bounded invertibility. If \(\chi\ne0\), or if \(\chi=0\) and \(\omega-i\gamma\ne0\), then \(|d_n|\to\infty\), and the resolvent diagonal entries tend to zero, making the resolvent compact. If all three parameters vanish, \(H=0\) has infinite multiplicity and no compact resolvent on the infinite-dimensional Fock space.
\paragraph{Testing a driven perturbation.}The drive sends \(|n\rangle\) to \(\beta\sqrt{n+1}|n+1\rangle+\beta\sqrt n|n-1\rangle\). Its norm on this vector is \(|\beta|\sqrt{2n+1}\). For \(\chi\ne0\), the adjacent diagonal gap is \(d_{n+1}-d_n=\chi(2n+1)+\omega-i\gamma\), of order \(n\). The ratio of these scales is therefore \(O(n^{-1/2})\). This suggests high-level localization but is not an eigenvalue estimate: the drive is unbounded and neighboring couplings interact through the full operator.
\paragraph{Gap.}A rigorous driven distribution needs resolvent bounds on isolating contours and operator-domain control, including zero-Kerr and degenerate coefficient cases. A single basis-vector ratio does not provide those bounds. This attempt solves only the elementary undriven slice and preserves the source's full real parameter range as the remaining target; no positivity or blanket compactness assumption is added.
