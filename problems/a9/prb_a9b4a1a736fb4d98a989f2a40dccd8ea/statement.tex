\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A conventional general flat surface is a surface with a Euclidean metric away from conical singularities. The excerpt does not fix compactness, area, orientation, boundary conditions, holonomy, or the allowed singularities; in particular it is not restricted to translation surfaces.

\emph{Problem 1 (geodesics on general flat surfaces).}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Describe geodesic behavior, and prove or disprove that the geodesic flow is ergodic on a typical flat surface, with ``typical'' allowed in a reasonable sense. A precise version must choose a parameter space and measure or category notion of typicality, the phase space, invariant measure, and treatment of trajectories meeting singularities. In the usual full-flow setting one uses the unit tangent bundle over the regular locus and Liouville measure. This is different from ergodicity of a single directional flow on a translation surface; the latter is not silently substituted.
\item Does every flat surface have a closed geodesic avoiding singular points? Does almost every flat surface have one? The source's ``any (almost any)'' leaves these as distinct possible scopes, and almost-everywhere existence again needs a specified measure. A regular closed geodesic means a periodic geodesic lying entirely in the nonsingular locus.
\item If such existence holds, determine the asymptotic number of regular closed geodesics of length at most \(T\) as \(T\to\infty\), in the intended counting convention. For a fixed surface this would be a counting function \(N_F(T)\), but the source does not say whether it counts primitive trajectories, homotopy classes, cylinders, or another equivalence. Flat cylinders can give continuous families of closed geodesics, so counting individual geometric trajectories need not be finite.
\end{enumerate}
The source notes that a geodesic representative of a simple closed curve's homotopy class is typically a broken line with many segments between conical singularities. Counting such singular broken-line representatives is expressly distinguished from counting regular closed geodesics. The source does not claim every class has a regular representative or combine the two counting problems. Its following questions about arbitrary planar polygonal billiards are not supplied here.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Study full geodesic flow on general flat cone surfaces, including typical ergodicity, existence of regular closed geodesics, and their length asymptotics. Typicality and counting equivalence need fixing; regular closed trajectories must remain distinct from singular broken-line representatives.
