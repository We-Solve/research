\section*{Irrational local maxima of fractional-part autocorrelation}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Write $\{x\}=x-\lfloor x\rfloor$ for the fractional part of a real number.
For $\lambda>0$ define
\[
 A(\lambda)=\int_0^\infty\{t\}\{\lambda t\}\,\frac{dt}{t^2}.
\]
This improper integral converges.

Does there exist an irrational $\lambda_0>0$ and a number $\varepsilon>0$
such that
\[
 \lambda>0,\quad 0<|\lambda-\lambda_0|<\varepsilon
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad A(\lambda)<A(\lambda_0)?
\]
The comparison includes every nearby positive real argument.

\subsection{Short English statement}
Can this fractional-part autocorrelation function have a strict local maximum
at a positive irrational number?

\subsection{Sources}
M. Balazard, contribution to \emph{Dirichlet Series and Function Theory in
Polydiscs}, Oberwolfach Report 6/2014, p.\ 350, Question 2,
\url{https://doi.org/10.4171/owr/2014/06}.
The question is recalled there from L. B\'aez-Duarte, M. Balazard,
B. Landreau and E. Saias, \emph{\'Etude de l'autocorr\'elation multiplicative
de la fonction ``partie fractionnaire''}, Ramanujan Journal 9 (2005),
215--240. This is an independently written formulation of their question.
