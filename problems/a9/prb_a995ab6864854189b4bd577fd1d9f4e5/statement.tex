\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 800797}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(T^2=S^1\times S^1\) be the two-dimensional torus, where \(S^1=\{(x,y)\in\R^2:x^2+y^2=1\}\). A triangulation of \(T^2\) is a two-dimensional simplicial complex \(K\) whose geometric realization \(|K|\) is homeomorphic to \(T^2\).

Call \(K\) \emph{polyhedral} if there exists a topological embedding \(f:|K|\to\R^3\) that is affine on every simplex. Here a topological embedding is a continuous map that is a homeomorphism onto its image. Thus vertices map to distinct points, edges map to straight line segments, and two-dimensional simplices map to nondegenerate flat triangles contained in affine planes. The images of any two simplices must intersect exactly in the image of their common face, with empty intersection when they have no common face.

\textbf{Question (Kahle, 2019, Question 4).} Is every triangulation of \(T^2\) polyhedral? Equivalently, for every two-dimensional simplicial complex \(K\) with \(|K|\cong T^2\), does there exist a topological embedding \(f:|K|\to\R^3\) that is affine on each simplex of the given triangulation, without subdivision?
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every simplicial triangulation of the torus admit a realization in \(\R^3\) using straight edges and flat triangular faces, with no intersections except those prescribed by the triangulation?
