\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 20000553}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(0<\epsilon\leq1\). A Parseval frame \((f_i)_{i\in I}\) in a complex Hilbert space \(H\), with finite or countable \(I\), satisfies
\[
 \sum_{i\in I}|\langle h,f_i\rangle|^2=\|h\|^2\quad(h\in H).
\]
Impose \(\|f_i\|\geq\epsilon\) for every \(i\); \(\epsilon\) bounds norms, not squared norms. A subsequence indexed by \(J\subset I\) is Riesz basic if there are \(0<A_J\leq B_J<\infty\) such that
\[
 A_J\sum_{i\in J}|c_i|^2\leq\left\|\sum_{i\in J}c_if_i\right\|^2
 \leq B_J\sum_{i\in J}|c_i|^2
\]
for all finitely supported coefficient families on \(J\). The source asks for the least number of blocks in a partition of \(I\) into Riesz basic subsequences, its dependence on \(\epsilon\), and ways to exhibit or construct such partitions.

Define \(N(\epsilon)\) as the least integer \(r\) such that every frame in this finite-or-countable class admits a partition into at most \(r\) Riesz basic subsequences. The unquantified definition allows the Riesz constants to depend on the frame and blocks. Define \(N_{\mathrm{fin}}(\epsilon)\) similarly for finite frames only, without prescribing quantitative Riesz constants. The aid reports that the published Bownik--Casazza--Marcus--Speegle theorem gives
\[
 N(\epsilon)=\Theta(\epsilon^{-2})\quad(\epsilon\downarrow0).
\]
This is a supplied literature attribution, not a complete theorem citation. The separate claimed exact finite result and countable lower bound are
\[
 N_{\mathrm{fin}}(\epsilon)=\lceil\epsilon^{-2}\rceil,
 \qquad N(\epsilon)\geq\lceil\epsilon^{-2}\rceil
 \quad(0<\epsilon\leq1).
\]
The exact finite statement is weaker in its Riesz-constant requirements than a dimension-independent quantitative Riesz bound, and it does not assert equality for the countable case.

The supplied Fourier witness may be made explicit as follows. Put \(r=\lceil\epsilon^{-2}\rceil\). If \(r\geq2\), choose integers \(1\leq n<m\) with \(r-1<m/n\leq\epsilon^{-2}\). With \(\zeta=e^{2\pi i/m}\), the columns
\[
 f_j=\frac1{\sqrt m}(1,\zeta^j,\ldots,\zeta^{(n-1)j})^{\mathsf T}\in\C^n,
 \qquad0\leq j<m,
\]
form a Parseval frame, have norm \(\sqrt{n/m}\geq\epsilon\), and are full spark, meaning every \(n\) columns are linearly independent. Since every independent block contains at most \(n\) columns and \(m>(r-1)n\), at least \(r\) blocks are needed. For \(\epsilon=1\), an orthonormal basis gives the lower bound one.

For the finite upper bound, each subfamily satisfies
\[
 \epsilon^2|J|\leq\sum_{i\in J}\|f_i\|^2
 \leq\dim\operatorname{span}\{f_i:i\in J\}.
\]
The Rado--Horn criterion therefore yields a partition into \(r=\lceil\epsilon^{-2}\rceil\) linearly independent blocks; finite independent blocks are Riesz basic with their own positive constants. The aid reports an exact-arithmetic polynomial-time construction by linear-matroid partition. Its computation claim is an arithmetic-model assertion, not a supplied finite-bit complexity theorem for arbitrary complex input. No enumeration of all admissible partitions or exact countable-case optimum is supplied.
\subsection{Short English statement}
The question asks how many Riesz subsequences are needed to partition a Parseval frame whose vector norms are at least \(\epsilon\). The supplied literature report gives order \(\epsilon^{-2}\) for countable frames. The separate finite result gives exactly \(\lceil\epsilon^{-2}\rceil\) blocks when Riesz constants may depend on the finite frame, with Fourier lower examples and a matroid-partition construction.
