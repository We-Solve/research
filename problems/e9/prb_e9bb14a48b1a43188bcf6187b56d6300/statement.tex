\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(\omega\) be the first infinite ordinal and \(\omega_3\) the initial ordinal of cardinality \(\aleph_3\). For a subset \(A\subseteq\omega_3\), let \(\operatorname{otp}(A)\) denote its order type under the inherited ordinal ordering. A function is constant on a rectangle \(A\times B\) if there is an \(n\in\omega\) such that \(f(a,b)=n\) for every \(a\in A\) and \(b\in B\).

Is the following assertion consistent with the intended background set theory?
\[
\begin{gathered}
\exists f:\omega_3\times\omega_3\longrightarrow\omega\quad
\forall A,B\subseteq\omega_3,\\
\operatorname{otp}(A)=\operatorname{otp}(B)=\omega
\ \Longrightarrow\
\exists a_0,a_1\in A\;\exists b_0,b_1\in B\quad
f(a_0,b_0)\ne f(a_1,b_1).
\end{gathered}
\]
The sets \(A\) and \(B\) need not be disjoint, and no ordering relation between them is imposed. The supplied record does not specify the background axioms or any relative-consistency hypothesis; these remain unresolved qualifications of the consistency question.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can there consistently be a coloring of \(\omega_3\times\omega_3\) by natural numbers with no constant rectangle whose two coordinate sets each have order type \(\omega\)? The background theory for consistency is unspecified in the supplied record.
