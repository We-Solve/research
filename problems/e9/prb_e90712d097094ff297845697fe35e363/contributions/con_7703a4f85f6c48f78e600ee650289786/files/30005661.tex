\section{Periodic Ground States in Gross–Pitaevskii Theory}\label{30005661}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 30005661 · Mathematical Physics}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


For a pair potential $w$, $(w*|u|^2)(x)=\int w(x-y)|u(y)|^2dy$. The stationary Gross--Pitaevskii equation is the Euler equation for a nonlocal energy at chemical potential $\mu$. Superstability controls the interaction energy from below at high local density; $\widehat w$ is its Fourier transform. Periodicity means invariance under a full-rank lattice of spatial translations.

\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}
For superstable, sufficiently decaying pair interactions $w$ whose Fourier transform takes negative values, must every infinite-volume ground state of $(-\Delta+w*|u|^2)u=\mu u$ be periodic when $\mu$ exceeds the fluid–solid transition value $\mu_c$?

\paragraph{Scope and source qualification.}The quantifier is every ground state, not merely the existence of a periodic minimizer. Infinite-volume ground state and $\mu_c$ use the source definitions.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Determine whether every infinite-volume Gross--Pitaevskii ground state is crystalline above the fluid--solid transition.

\paragraph{Density-wave test on a torus.} As an auxiliary finite-volume calculation take a real even periodic integrable kernel \(w\), a nonzero reciprocal lattice vector \(k\), density \(\rho>0\), and \(|\varepsilon|<1\). Normalize convolution so its Fourier multiplier is \(\widehat w(k)\). Set \(u_\varepsilon(x)=\sqrt{\rho(1+\varepsilon\cos(k\cdot x))}\). Its mean density is exactly \(\rho\). For the energy per volume \(\langle|\nabla u|^2\rangle+\frac12\langle|u|^2(w*|u|^2)\rangle\), the interaction difference is exactly \(\rho^2\varepsilon^2\widehat w(k)/4\). Also
\[
 \langle|\nabla u_\varepsilon|^2\rangle
 =\frac{\rho\varepsilon^2|k|^2}{4}
 \left\langle\frac{\sin^2(k\cdot x)}{1+\varepsilon\cos(k\cdot x)}\right\rangle
 =\frac{\rho\varepsilon^2|k|^2}{8}+O(\varepsilon^4).
\]
The linear term inside the average vanishes; the remaining expansion is uniform for \(|\varepsilon|\leq1/2\). Thus the constant state fails this local minimum test if \(|k|^2+2\rho\widehat w(k)<0\). The chemical-potential term has zero difference because mean density is fixed.
\paragraph{Why the route stops.} A negative Fourier component can select an unstable wavelength, but this calculation selects neither a lattice nor every minimizing state. The test is periodic by construction; using it to conclude periodicity of arbitrary infinite-volume minimizers would assume the desired property. Interfaces between competing patterns and nonperiodic candidates are not excluded by a local second variation.
\paragraph{Scope.} The source requires every infinite-volume ground state above its actual \(\mu_c\), for its superstable decaying kernels. No identification of \(\mu_c\) with this finite-volume instability threshold is proved. The first missing estimate is a global energy comparison excluding all nonperiodic competitors under the source ground-state definition.
