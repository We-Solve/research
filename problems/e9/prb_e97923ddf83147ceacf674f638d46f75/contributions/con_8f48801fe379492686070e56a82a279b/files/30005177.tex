\paragraph{A scale-to-conformal diagnostic.} For distinct nonzero Euclidean points define the scalar four-point expression
\[
 F(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)=|x_1-x_2|^{-2\Delta}|x_3-x_4|^{-2\Delta}
 \left(1+\varepsilon\frac{|x_1-x_2|}{|x_3-x_4|}\right),\qquad\varepsilon\ne0.
\]
It is translation and rotation invariant and homogeneous of degree \(-4\Delta\). Under inversion \(I(x)=x/|x|^2\), direct squaring proves \(|I(x)-I(y)|=|x-y|/(|x||y|)\). The first product therefore acquires the expected factor \(\prod_j|x_j|^{2\Delta}\), but the additional ratio acquires \(|x_3||x_4|/(|x_1||x_2|)\), which is generally not one. Thus the expression fails the corresponding primary-field inversion law despite its scale covariance.
