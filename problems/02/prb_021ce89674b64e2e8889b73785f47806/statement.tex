\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $\Omega\subset\R^m$ be a bounded smooth domain and consider, for $\varepsilon>0$ tending to zero,
\[
 -\varepsilon^2\Delta u=u(u-a(x))(1-u)\quad\text{in }\Omega,
 \qquad \lim_{x\to\partial\Omega}u(x)=+\infty.
\]
The latter is an explosive boundary condition, not finite Dirichlet data; it applies for each fixed positive $\varepsilon$. The space dimension and the regularity/range of $a:\Omega\to\R$ are not specified in the restored statement.

The construction requested is a family of solutions with localized peaks \emph{in addition to} an interior-transition-layer background. Schematically, if $U_\varepsilon$ is a layer solution, one seeks a solution $u_\varepsilon$ for which $u_\varepsilon-U_\varepsilon$ has one or more localized peak profiles while the interior layer persists. This is a nonlinear gluing/asymptotic description, not the assertion that a sum of two separate solutions is automatically a solution. The supplied assessment calls the intended problem an off-layer peak construction: the relation and separation of peak centers from the transition set must be specified.

For orientation only, in the usual bistable regime $0<a(x)<1$, an interior layer separates regions approaching the two stable phases $0$ and $1$, with a thin transition near an interior set. The record does not actually supply that coefficient hypothesis, a layer geometry, the limiting phases/profile, peak number and amplitude, a scale/separation condition, or nondegeneracy conditions for the construction. None is silently imposed. A precise existence theorem must choose these data and state in what topology the peak/layer asymptotics hold while satisfying the explosive boundary condition.
