\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(X\) be a smooth projective surface over \(\C\). A reduced effective curve on \(X\) means an effective divisor
\[
C=C_1+\cdots+C_r,\qquad r\geq 1,
\]
where the \(C_i\) are distinct integral curves, each with multiplicity one. Write \(C^2=\deg(C\cdot C)\) for its self-intersection number on \(X\); explicitly,
\[
C^2=\sum_{i=1}^r C_i^2+2\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq r}C_i\cdot C_j.
\]
The question is whether
\[
\forall X\text{ smooth projective over }\C\text{ of dimension }2,\quad
\exists b_X\in\R_{\geq 0}\quad
\forall C\text{ reduced effective on }X,\qquad
C^2\geq -b_X.
\]
The constant may depend on \(X\), but must be independent of \(C\). Requiring \(b_X\geq 0\) does not change the existence question. The supplied formulation includes reducible curves and imposes no connectedness requirement on \(C\). Its precise correspondence with the cited workshop discussion requires source verification: the record supplies a report-wide page range but no specific problem location.
\subsection{Short English statement}
For each smooth projective complex surface, is there a uniform lower bound for the self-intersection numbers of all its reduced effective curves, including reducible curves? The supplied record labels this question open; that status is supplied dated curation and has not been independently verified here.
