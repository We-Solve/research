\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
The source asks whether function spaces can yield martingale decompositions and concatenations for some class of hyperbolic maps. It specifies no maps, invariant/reference measures, function spaces or concatenation rule. Let \(\mathcal F_0\supseteq\mathcal F_1\supseteq\cdots\) be sigma-algebras on one probability space, and choose block times \(0=t_0<t_1<\cdots\). Suppose \(Y_t,D_t\in L^2(\mathcal F_t)\), with
\(\E[D_t\mid\mathcal F_{t+1}]=0\), and for every block \(j\) there are \(H_t^{(j)}\in L^2(\mathcal F_t)\), \(t_j\leq t\leq t_{j+1}\), such that
\[
 Y_t=D_t+H_{t+1}^{(j)}-H_t^{(j)}\qquad(t_j\leq t<t_{j+1}).
\]
At a seam \(\tau=t_j\), \(j\geq1\), define
\[
 J_\tau=H_\tau^{(j-1)}-H_\tau^{(j)}\in L^2(\mathcal F_\tau),\qquad
 A_{\tau,t}=\E[J_\tau\mid\mathcal F_t]-\E[J_\tau\mid\mathcal F_{t+1}]\quad(t\geq\tau).
\]
The future reverse-Doob increments have conditional mean zero. Define one global reverse-martingale-difference sequence by
\[
 \widetilde D_t=D_t+\sum_{\substack{\tau\text{ a seam}\\0<\tau\leq t}}A_{\tau,t}.
\]
Each sum is finite, and \(\E[\widetilde D_t\mid\mathcal F_{t+1}]=0\).

For a horizon \(N\geq1\), let \(j_N\) be the block containing time \(N-1\). The exact finite-horizon identity reported by the aid can be written
\[
 \sum_{t=0}^{N-1}Y_t
 =\sum_{t=0}^{N-1}\widetilde D_t+R_N,
\]
\[
 R_N=H_N^{(j_N)}-H_0^{(0)}
 +\sum_{\substack{\tau\text{ a seam}\\0<\tau<N}}
 \E[J_\tau\mid\mathcal F_N].
\]
Thus each seam mismatch contributes only its still-unforgotten future conditional expectation; all its intervening projection increments are absorbed in \(\widetilde D_t\). A seam exactly at \(N\) is not included, since the horizon ends with the preceding block.

A precise summable-memory sufficient condition is: both outer correctors have \(L^2\) norm at most \(C\), uniformly in \(N\), and there is a nonnegative sequence \(k(r)\), \(r\geq1\), with \(\sum_rk(r)<\infty\) such that
\[
 \|\E[J_\tau\mid\mathcal F_N]\|_2\leq k(N-\tau)\quad(0<\tau<N).
\]
Since there is at most one seam at each integer time, this gives
\[
 \sup_N\|R_N\|_2\leq2C+\sum_{r\geq1}k(r)<\infty.
\]
In particular \(R_N/\sqrt N\to0\) in \(L^2\), even with one seam per time. This permits transfer of a separately established martingale limit theorem; it is not itself a CLT without its further variance and Lindeberg-type hypotheses. The record does not state an exact norm-kernel convention, so the displayed version makes its requirements explicit.

The aid's persistent-tail obstruction can be expressed with a single Rademacher variable \(Z\), \(\Pr(Z=1)=\Pr(Z=-1)=1/2\), and \(\mathcal F_t=\sigma(Z)\) for every \(t\). Give every time its own block, take \(Y_t=Z,D_t=0\), and choose that block's initial corrector zero and terminal corrector \(Z\). Every local corrector has norm at most one, but all \(N-1\) internal seams equal \(Z\), and the outer endpoint also equals \(Z\). Every projection increment is zero, while
\[
 R_N=(N-1)Z+Z=NZ,\qquad
 N^{-1/2}\sum_{t<N}Y_t=\sqrt N\,Z
\]
is not tight. Thus uniformly bounded local correctors alone do not control concatenation; decay of conditional seam memory is a separate requirement.
\subsection{Short English statement}
