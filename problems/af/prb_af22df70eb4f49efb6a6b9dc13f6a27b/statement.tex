\section{Local Approximation of Random-Graph Semidefinite Programs}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.


A bisection partitions the vertices into two parts of equal size (or differing by one for odd order). Its cut consists of the edges joining the parts; minimum bisection minimizes their total weight.

A semidefinite program optimizes a linear functional of a symmetric matrix subject to affine constraints and positive semidefiniteness. The condition $X\succeq0$ means $v^TXv\geq0$ for every real vector $v$.

In the LOCAL model vertices communicate synchronously along graph edges; messages and local computation are unrestricted and complexity is the number of rounds. After $t$ rounds a vertex can depend only on its radius-$t$ neighborhood and any supplied identifiers or randomness.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For a large class of random graph models, can semidefinite programs be approximately solved by local algorithms, with comparable performance for problems such as minimum bisection and community detection? \sref{statement}{2}
\paragraph{Scope qualification.}
The random graph class, objective normalization, locality radius, and meaning of comparable performance are not fully quantified.
