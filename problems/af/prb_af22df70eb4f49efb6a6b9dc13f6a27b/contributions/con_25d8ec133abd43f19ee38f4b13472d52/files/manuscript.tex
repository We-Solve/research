\subsection{A feasible one-step local filter and a checked obstruction}
The primary source uses the penalized relaxation
\[
\operatorname{SDP}(G)=\max_{X\succeq0,\,X_{ii}=1}\langle A-(d/n)J,X\rangle,
\]
not a hard equality constraint $X\mathbf1=0$. We test a radius-one Gram construction on a simple $d$-regular triangle-free graph, $d\ge1$. For real $a$, assign vertex $i$ the vector
\[
v_i=\frac{e_i+a\sum_{j\sim i}e_j}{\sqrt{1+da^2}},
\qquad X_{ij}=\langle v_i,v_j\rangle.
\]
The vector's nonzero coordinates depend only on the vertex and its neighbors; coordinate names may use vertex identifiers. This is a specific local construction, not the full class of algorithms in the source.
\begin{proposition}
The Gram matrix is feasible and its objective value is
\[
V(a)=\frac{2and-d(1+ad)^2}{1+da^2}\le n\sqrt d.
\]
On the $d$-dimensional hypercube with $d\ge3$, the SDP optimum is exactly $n(d-2)$, where $n=2^d$. Consequently even optimizing $a$ in this one-step family gives a ratio at most $\sqrt d/(d-2)$, which tends to zero.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every $v_i$ has unit norm, and every Gram matrix is positive semidefinite since $z^TXz=\|\sum_i z_iv_i\|^2\ge0$. In matrix form $X=(I+aA)^2/(1+da^2)$. For a simple regular triangle-free graph, $\operatorname{tr}A=0$, $\operatorname{tr}A^2=nd$ and $\operatorname{tr}A^3=0$. Thus $\langle A,X\rangle=2and/(1+da^2)$. Also $A\mathbf1=d\mathbf1$, so $\langle J,X\rangle=n(1+ad)^2/(1+da^2)$. This proves the formula. Its subtracted term is nonnegative and $2a\sqrt d\le1+da^2$, proving the upper bound for every real $a$.
Index hypercube vertices by $x\in\{0,1\}^d$. Its characters $\chi_S(x)=(-1)^{\sum_{j\in S}x_j}$ form an orthogonal basis: the sum of the product of two distinct characters cancels by flipping a coordinate in their symmetric difference. Flipping one coordinate shows that $A\chi_S=(d-2|S|)\chi_S$. The centering term $(d/n)J$ changes the constant character's eigenvalue from $d$ to zero and leaves the others unchanged. Hence the largest eigenvalue of $M=A-(d/n)J$ is $d-2$. For feasible $X$, positive semidefiniteness of $(d-2)I-M$ gives $\langle M,X\rangle\le(d-2)\operatorname{tr}X=n(d-2)$; the trace of a product of positive semidefinite matrices is nonnegative by expanding one matrix in its nonnegative eigenvalues and eigenvectors. Equality is attained by $X=\chi_{\{1\}}\chi_{\{1\}}^T$, a feasible rank-one matrix. This proves the exact optimum and the ratio bound.
\end{proof}
