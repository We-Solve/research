\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(A=\{0,1,2\}\). The source does not specify whether infinite words are one-sided or two-sided. Accordingly, let \(I\) denote either \(\{0,1,2,\ldots\}\) or \(\Z\), with the intended choice unresolved. A word \(w=(w_i)_{i\in I}\in A^I\) is \emph{squarefree} if it contains no contiguous factor \(uu\), where \(u\) is a nonempty finite word. Equivalently, there are no \(i\in I\) and integer \(m\geq1\), with \(i,i+1,\ldots,i+2m-1\in I\), such that
\[
 w_{i+j}=w_{i+m+j}\qquad(0\leq j<m).
\]
For a nonempty finite set \(F\subset I\) and a replacement assignment \(a\in\prod_{i\in F}(A\setminus\{w_i\})\), define the modified word
\[
 w^{F,a}_i=\begin{cases}a_i,&i\in F,\\w_i,&i\notin F.\end{cases}
\]
This interprets a symbol flip as replacing a letter by either of the other two letters; the source does not specify a ternary flip operation.

Under this interpretation, a squarefree word \(w\) is \emph{fragile} if
\[
 \forall\,\varnothing\ne F\subset I\text{ finite},\quad
 \forall\,a\in\prod_{i\in F}(A\setminus\{w_i\}),\quad
 w^{F,a}\text{ is not squarefree}.
\]
A candidate precise interpretation of \emph{robust} is that there exist nonempty finite sets \(F_1,F_2,\ldots\subset I\) and assignments \(a^{(k)}\in\prod_{i\in F_k}(A\setminus\{w_i\})\) such that
\[
 F_k\cap F_\ell=\varnothing\quad(k\ne\ell),\qquad
 w^{F_k,a^{(k)}}\text{ is squarefree for every }k\geq1.
\]
Here each modification is applied separately to the original word. Whether every replacement assignment must work, or whether combinations of these modifications must also preserve squarefreeness, is not specified.
