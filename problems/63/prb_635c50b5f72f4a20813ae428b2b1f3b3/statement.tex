\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(F\) be a finitely generated free group. For \(g\in F\), define its conjugacy class by
\[
g^F=\{xgx^{-1}:x\in F\}.
\]
For a positive integer \(k\) and elements \(g_1,\ldots,g_k\in F\), let
\[
N=\langle\!\langle g_1,\ldots,g_k\rangle\!\rangle_F
\]
be the smallest normal subgroup of \(F\) containing all the \(g_i\). Define the product of their conjugacy classes by
\[
g_1^F\cdots g_k^F
=\{x_1g_1x_1^{-1}\cdots x_kg_kx_k^{-1}:x_1,\ldots,x_k\in F\}.
\]
The profinite topology on \(F\) has the finite-index normal subgroups as a neighborhood basis at the identity. The closure of \(N\) in this topology is
\[
\overline N^{\mathrm{pf}}
=\bigcap_{\substack{K\trianglelefteq F\\[F:K]<\infty}}NK.
\]
The supplied question asks whether there exist \(k\geq 1\) and \(g_1,\ldots,g_k\in F\) such that
\[
g_1^F\cdots g_k^F\not\subseteq\overline N^{\mathrm{pf}}.
\]
\textbf{Unresolved formulation defect.} Under the stated definition of \(N\), normality gives \(g_i^F\subseteq N\) for every \(i\), and hence
\[
g_1^F\cdots g_k^F\subseteq N\subseteq\overline N^{\mathrm{pf}}.
\]
Consequently, the requested noncontainment is incompatible with the supplied normal-closure wording. This identifies a defect in the recorded formulation; it does not recover or resolve the intended conjecture.
