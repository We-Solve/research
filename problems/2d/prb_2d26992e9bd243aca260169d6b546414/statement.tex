\section{Short Monochromatic Odd Cycles in Multicoloured Complete Graphs}\label{statement}


\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Unless a section says otherwise, $\N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$,
$\Z$ is the ring of integers, $\Q,\R,\C$ are the rational, real, and complex
fields, $[N]=\{1,\ldots,\lfloor N\rfloor\}$, and $\log$ is the natural
logarithm. For real functions of a parameter tending to infinity,
$f\ll g$ and $f=O(g)$ mean $|f|\leq Cg$ eventually for some fixed $C$;
$f\gg g$ reverses this comparison; $f=o(g)$ means $f/g\to0$;
$f\sim g$ means $f/g\to1$; and $f\asymp g$ means both order bounds.
A subscript on $O$ or $\ll$ specifies allowed constant dependence.
The expression $n^{o(1)}$ in an upper bound means growth smaller than
$n^\epsilon$ for each fixed $\epsilon>0$. Local definitions govern any
exception, finite-field convention, or use of the same symbol for another
object. An asymptotic claim is not automatically an effective algorithm.



For each colouring of the edges of $K_{2^n+1}$, consider the shortest monochromatic cycle of odd length. Such a cycle must exist: if every colour graph were bipartite, assigning its two sides for each colour would give only $2^n$ possible vertex bit-vectors, and an edge between equal vectors could have no colour. The function $f(n)$ is the largest possible value of that shortest-cycle length. \sref{statement}{1} \sref{statement}{2}

\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Let $f(n)$ be the minimal $m$ such that if the edges of $K_{2^n+1}$ are coloured with $n$ colours then there must be a monochromatic odd cycle of length at most $m$. Estimate $f(n)$. \sref{statement}{1}

\paragraph{Residual task reported by the archive.}

The embedded review (2026-08-17) records: Determine the asymptotic scale of f(n). \sref{statement}{1}

\subsection{Short English statement}

How short an odd cycle must occur monochromatically when a complete graph just above a power of two is coloured with the corresponding number of colours?
