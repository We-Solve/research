\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For $N$ distinct lines in $\R^3$, a joint is a point incident to three lines with linearly independent directions, equivalently three concurrent lines not contained in a common plane.

\textbf{Problem 3.13 (T. Tao).} How many joints can $N$ lines determine? The original remark gives a lower order $N^{3/2}$ and trivial upper order $N^2$; these are interpreted as asymptotic orders, not a literal leading coefficient one in the lower bound.

\textbf{Supplied resolution.} The aid states that the extremal order is $\Theta(N^{3/2})$. Its pruning and polynomial derivative-descent argument gives the explicit bound
\[
 |J|<5N^{3/2}\qquad(N\geq1).
\]
A three-direction grid uses $3m^2$ lines and has $m^3$ joints, giving the matching order. The summary also mentions generic-plane constructions and current sharp generalized-binomial bounds, but does not print that exact sharp formula or its reference. The constant $5$ is therefore not presented as optimal.

\textbf{Reported fixed-direction inequality and stability.} Let $\mathcal L_i$ be three finite line families parallel to independent fixed directions, with sizes $N_i$ and $N=N_1+N_2+N_3$. At a common joint $p$, denote its unique line from family $i$ by $L_i(p)$. A weighted constant-one Loomis--Whitney formulation is, for arbitrary nonnegative weights $w_i$,
\[
 \sum_{p\in J}\sqrt{w_1(L_1(p))w_2(L_2(p))w_3(L_3(p))}
 \leq\left(\prod_{i=1}^3\sum_{L\in\mathcal L_i}w_i(L)\right)^{1/2}.
\]
In particular,
\[
 |J|\leq\sqrt{N_1N_2N_3}\leq(N/3)^{3/2}.
\]
This sharper constant concerns the prescribed three-direction class, not arbitrary line arrangements.

If $0\leq\varepsilon<1$ and

\]
the aid proves, for each $i$,
\[

\]
and at least $(1-\varepsilon)^2N_i$ lines of family $i$ contain a common joint. These are balance and active-line conclusions, not a full geometric closeness theorem identifying a unique near-extremal grid.
