\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a real symmetric matrix $W\in\R^{n\times n}$, the standard nonnegative-PCA problem is
\[
 \operatorname{OPT}_+(W)=\max\{x^TWx:x\in\R^n,\ x\geq0,\ \|x\|_2=1\}.
\]
A standard semidefinite relaxation is
\[
 \operatorname{OPT}_{\mathrm{SDP}}(W)
 =\max\{\operatorname{Tr}(WX):X=X^T\succeq0,\ X_{ij}\geq0\ \forall i,j,
                                      \ \operatorname{Tr}X=1\}.
\]
It is a relaxation because $X=xx^T$ is feasible for every nonnegative unit vector. Exact value tightness means $\operatorname{OPT}_{\mathrm{SDP}}(W)=\operatorname{OPT}_+(W)$, equivalently existence of a rank-one optimal matrix for this relaxation. It does not require every SDP optimizer to have rank one.

The source asks whether the positive-PCA semidefinite relaxation is tight with high probability for Wigner matrices. For an intended sequence of ensembles $W_n$, the standard exact-value reading is
\[
 \mathbb P\bigl(\operatorname{OPT}_{\mathrm{SDP}}(W_n)
                  =\operatorname{OPT}_+(W_n)\bigr)\longrightarrow1
 \qquad(n\to\infty).
\]
A real Wigner model ordinarily has independent centered upper-triangular entries, symmetry below the diagonal, and a specified variance scaling; diagonal laws and moment/tail assumptions must also be fixed.
