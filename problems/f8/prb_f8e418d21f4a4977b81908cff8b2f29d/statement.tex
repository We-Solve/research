\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(G\) be a finite abelian group of order \(n\geq 1\). Interpret its rank as its minimum number of generators,
\[
r=d(G):=\min\{\lvert S\rvert:S\subseteq G,\ \langle S\rangle=G\},
\]
with \(d(1)=0\). Let \(\Omega\) be a nonempty finite set and let \(K\leq\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega)\). A subgroup \(H\leq K\) is regular on \(\Omega\) if, for every \(\alpha,\beta\in\Omega\), there exists exactly one \(h\in H\) such that \(h(\alpha)=\beta\). Define
\[
b_G(K):=\left\lvert\{H\leq K:H\cong G,\ H\text{ is regular on }\Omega\}/\!\sim_K\right\rvert,
\]
where \(H\sim_K H'\) means \(H'=kHk^{-1}\) for some \(k\in K\).

For a precise provisional interpretation of the referenced notation, use the standard \(2\)-closure
\[
K^{(2)}:=\left\{\pi\in\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega):
(\pi(\alpha),\pi(\beta))\in K\cdot(\alpha,\beta)
\text{ for every }(\alpha,\beta)\in\Omega^2\right\},
\]
where
\[
K\cdot(\alpha,\beta)=\{(k(\alpha),k(\beta)):k\in K\}.
\]
Thus \(K^{(2)}\) consists of the permutations preserving every orbit of the diagonal action of \(K\) on ordered pairs, including pairs with equal coordinates. The source refers to Archive 19.67 for this definition, but that archive entry is not supplied; agreement with its convention requires review.

\textbf{Question.} Does there exist a function
\[
f:\mathbb Z_{\geq 0}\longrightarrow\mathbb R_{\geq 0}
\]
such that, for every finite abelian group \(G\) of order \(n\geq 1\) and rank \(r=d(G)\), every nonempty finite permutation domain \(\Omega\), and every \(K\leq\operatorname{Sym}(\Omega)\) satisfying \(K^{(2)}=K\), one has
\[
b_G(K)\leq n^{f(r)}\,?
\]
The same function must work for all these choices. The source does not specify the degree of \(K\). A regular subgroup isomorphic to \(G\) forces \(\lvert\Omega\rvert=n\); otherwise \(b_G(K)=0\). Consequently, only degree \(n\) contributes a nonzero count. The source also leaves the meaning of rank and the domain and codomain of \(f\) implicit; the conventions above make the proposed statement explicit and require source confirmation.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is the number of \(K\)-conjugacy classes of regular subgroups isomorphic to a finite abelian group \(G\) bounded by a polynomial in \(\lvert G\rvert\), whose exponent depends only on the minimum number of generators of \(G\), uniformly over all \(2\)-closed permutation groups \(K\)?
