\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A semigroup is a nonempty set \(S\) equipped with an associative binary operation. It is \emph{regular} if
\[
\forall a\in S\;\exists x\in S:\quad axa=a.
\]
It is \emph{aperiodic} if every subgroup of \(S\) is trivial. Here a subgroup may have an identity different from an identity of the whole semigroup, and trivial means having exactly one element. Let
\[
\mathcal{K}=\{S: S\text{ is a finite regular aperiodic semigroup}\}.
\]
\textbf{Problem (S.\,I. Kublanovsky).} Is the class \(\mathcal{K}\) completely solvable?
