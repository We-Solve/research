\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 700377}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(G\) range over finitely generated residually finite groups. Here residually finite means that for every \(g\in G\setminus\{1\}\) there is a homomorphism from \(G\) to a finite group whose value at \(g\) is nonidentity. Define
\[
d(G)=\min\{\lvert S\rvert:S\subseteq G,\ \langle S\rangle=G\}.
\]
The profinite completion of \(G\) is
\[
\widehat G=\varprojlim_{\substack{N\trianglelefteq G\\[G:N]<\infty}}G/N,
\]
with its inverse-limit topology, and define
\[
\delta(G)=\min\{\lvert T\rvert:T\subseteq\widehat G,\ \overline{\langle T\rangle}=\widehat G\}.
\]
Both minima are finite because \(G\) is finitely generated.

\textbf{Part (b), the remaining question in the supplied record.} Is the following assertion true?
\[
\forall k\in\Z_{\ge2}\ \exists B_k\in\Z_{\ge0}\ \forall G\quad
\bigl(G\text{ is finitely generated and residually finite and }\delta(G)=k\bigr)
\Longrightarrow d(G)\le B_k.
\]
Thus the bound may depend on the fixed value \(k\), but must be independent of the group \(G\).

\textbf{Archived part (a), retained for context.} The original question asks whether every finitely generated residually finite group satisfies \(\delta(G)=d(G)\). The supplied archive reports a negative answer, attributed to G. A. Noskov, and the supplied statement reports examples with \(d(G)>\delta(G)\). This reported resolution concerns part (a); it does not by itself answer the boundedness question in part (b). The supplied registry labels the overall record \texttt{partially\_solved}; this is supplied curation, not an independently verified literature assessment.
\subsection{Short English statement}
For each fixed integer \(k\ge2\), is there a uniform bound on the number of abstract generators needed by finitely generated residually finite groups whose profinite completions require exactly \(k\) topological generators? The supplied archive reports that equality of the two generator numbers, asked in part (a), can fail.
