\section{Asymptotic Counts of Growing-Width $k$-SAT Functions}\label{30005109}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 30005109 · Computer Science}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.


A Boolean function maps $\{0,1\}^n$ to $\{0,1\}$. A $k$-SAT function is represented by a conjunction of clauses of width at most $k$, each clause a disjunction of literals $x_i$ or $\neg x_i$; distinct formulas computing the same function count once. Uniformly choosing a function means choosing from this finite set of distinct functions. A function is unate if complementing an appropriate subset of input coordinates makes it monotone in every coordinate. As $n\to\infty$, $o(1)$ tends to zero.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
If $k=k(n)\leq(1/2-c)n$ for some constant $c>0$, what is the asymptotic number of $k$-SAT functions on $n$ Boolean variables? \href{https://doi.org/10.4171/owr/2022/22}{[S2]}
\paragraph{Scope qualification.}
The constant $c>0$ is fixed, while the integer width $k=k(n)$ may grow subject to the displayed bound. Clause-width and repeated-literal conventions follow the cited enumeration problem.

\section{Research attempt: Uniform growing-width lower bound and nonuniform-limit obstruction}

\paragraph{A formula family that can be counted without collisions.}
Use the source's equivalent DNF formulation. For $1\leq k\leq n$ and a family
$\mathcal H\subseteq\binom{[n]}k$, define
\[
 f_{\mathcal H}(x)=\bigvee_{S\in\mathcal H}\ \bigwedge_{i\in S}x_i.
\]
The empty disjunction is zero. On the input $1_T$ with exactly $k$ ones,
$f_{\mathcal H}(1_T)=1$ if and only if $T\in\mathcal H$, because a $k$-set
$S$ is contained in the $k$-set $T$ only when $S=T$.
Thus these $2^{\binom nk}$ formulas represent distinct functions. Every clause
is indispensable: $1_S$ is a private satisfying input for its own clause.
A variable $i$ is essential exactly when some $S\in\mathcal H$ contains it.
Indeed $1_S$ and $1_{S\setminus\{i\}}$ distinguish its two values; if no clause
contains $i$, independence is immediate.
With each possible clause independently included with probability $1/2$, the
probability that a specified variable is absent is
$2^{-\binom{n-1}{k-1}}$. A union bound shows that at least
\[
 \left(1-n2^{-\binom{n-1}{k-1}}\right)2^{\binom nk}
\]
functions in this family depend on all variables, whenever the displayed lower
bound is positive. Each has $2^n$ different input-coordinate sign orientations,
and the resulting functions over all such families are distinct. To prove this,
a function monotone in both directions in a coordinate must be independent of
that coordinate: with all other inputs fixed, its two values satisfy both
opposite inequalities. An essential coordinate therefore has a unique monotonic
direction. Equality of two oriented functions forces their orientations to agree,
after which the $k$-weight evaluations identify $\mathcal H$.
Consequently the total number $N(n,k)$ of source functions satisfies
\[
 N(n,k)\geq
 \left(1-n2^{-\binom{n-1}{k-1}}\right)2^{\binom nk+n}.
\]
Complementing the output gives the corresponding CNF count if that convention
is preferred. This is a lower bound for a specifically constructed subclass;
it does not count all possible prime implicants of a general unate function.

\paragraph{The lower bound remains uniform far beyond fixed width.}
For $2\leq k\leq n-1$, the binomial coefficient
$\binom{n-1}{k-1}$ is at least $n-1$: consecutive binomial coefficients increase
toward the middle because their ratio is $(n-1-j)/(j+1)$, and the two end
coefficients in the indicated range equal $n-1$. Hence the error is at most
$n2^{-(n-1)}$, independently of $k$.
In particular every sequence in the question with $k\geq2$ eventually has
\[
 \log_2 N(n,k)\geq \binom nk+n+\log_2(1-n2^{-(n-1)}).
\]
For $n$ large enough that the last error is at most $1/2$, the final logarithm
is at least $-2n2^{-(n-1)}/\log 2$: integrate $1/(1-t)\leq2$ for
$0\leq t\leq1/2$. This supplies an explicit uniform lower-side estimate.

\paragraph{Why the known fixed-width conclusion cannot be substituted.}
An estimate $E(n,k)\to0$ for each fixed $k$ gives no bound on
$E(n,k(n))$. For example the numerical array
\[
 E(n,k)=\mathbf1_{\{n<2^{2^k}\}}
\]
tends to zero for every fixed $k$, but equals one along
$k(n)=\lceil\log_2\log_2 n\rceil+1$ for all sufficiently large $n$.
This $k(n)$ is much smaller than $(1/2-c)n$ for any fixed $0<c<1/2$.
Thus even a slowly growing sequence can evade a pointwise fixed-$k$ result.
This is a counterexample to a limit exchange, not a counterexample to the
enumeration conjecture.
The elementary upper bound remains
\[
 \log_2 N(n,k)\leq\min\{2^k\binom nk,\,2^n\},
\]
since every Boolean function is one of $2^{2^n}$ truth tables. Neither term
generally matches $\binom nk$ at the requested precision. At $k=n$, outside
the requested range, every truth table has a DNF of full assignments, giving
$N(n,n)=2^{2^n}$; this boundary also prevents extrapolating the fixed-width
scale to all widths without a restriction.

Different consistent choices are distinct because their satisfying subcubes have
different forced coordinates. This endpoint is not put under the $k\geq2$
