\section*{Multiple ergodic averages — Problem 8}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
An integer sequence $a(n)>0$ is fast growing here if
\[
       \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log a(n)}{\log n}=+\infty,
\]
or equivalently $a(n)/n^k\to\infty$ for every positive
integer $k$. Construct an explicit such sequence with
the following two universal properties for every $\ell\ge1$.

For every probability space $(X,\mathcal B,\mu)$ and
commuting invertible measure-preserving transformations
$T_1,\ldots,T_\ell$, all functions $f_i\in L^\infty(\mu)$
give convergent averages
\[
       \frac1N\sum_{n=1}^N\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}
                     f_i\circ T_i^{a(n)}
                \quad\text{in }L^2(\mu).
\]
For the same class of systems, every measurable $A$ with
$\mu(A)>0$ must satisfy
\[
       \mu\bigl(A\cap T_1^{a(n)}A\cap\cdots
                        \cap T_\ell^{a(n)}A\bigr)>0
       \quad\text{for some }n\ge1.
\]
Both requirements include the powers-of-one-map versions:
take $T_i=R^{k_i}$, with arbitrary integer $k_i$ for
convergence and nonzero $k_i$ for recurrence.

The sequence must be fixed independently of the system,
the number of transformations, and the tested functions
or set. ``Explicit'' requests a specified deterministic
construction or formula together with proofs of these
properties, rather than a sequence chosen separately for
each system. No formula for the mean limit, and no
pointwise convergence assertion, is imposed. Growth alone
is insufficient; the recurrence and convergence conditions
are essential parts of the requested example.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Construct an explicit sequence growing faster than every power which still supports all finite-order commuting recurrence and mean convergence.
