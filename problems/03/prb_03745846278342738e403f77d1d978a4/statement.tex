\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(G\) be a finite group. For a positive integer \(r\), let \(F_r=\langle x_1,\ldots,x_r\rangle\) be the free group on the displayed basis. For each \(\mathbf a=(a_1,\ldots,a_r)\in G^r\), let
\[
\operatorname{ev}_{\mathbf a}:G*F_r\longrightarrow G
\]
be the homomorphism that restricts to the identity on \(G\) and sends \(x_i\) to \(a_i\). A nontrivial mixed identity for \(G\) is an element \(w\in G*F_r\) such that
\[
w\ne 1\text{ in }G*F_r,
\qquad
\operatorname{ev}_{\mathbf a}(w)=1_G
\quad\text{for every }\mathbf a\in G^r.
\]
Thus the coefficients from \(G\) remain fixed under every substitution, and nontriviality refers to the free product before substitution.

For a specified length convention \(\ell\), define
\[
\mu_{\ell,r}(G)=\min\bigl\{\ell(w):w\in G*F_r\setminus\{1\},\ \operatorname{ev}_{\mathbf a}(w)=1_G\text{ for all }\mathbf a\in G^r\bigr\}.
\]
If the number of variables is allowed to vary, the corresponding quantity is
\[
\mu_{\ell}(G)=\min_{r\geq 1}\mu_{\ell,r}(G).
\]

\subsection{Short English statement}
A mixed identity is a nonidentity word with fixed coefficients from a finite group \(G\) that evaluates to the identity for every substitution of its variables by elements of \(G\). The problem asks for its shortest possible length and sharp results across families of finite groups. The source leaves the length convention and treatment of the number of variables unspecified. Its attributed length-\(8\) symplectic-group result gives existence, not a claimed exact minimum.
