\section{Quantum Complexity of High-Precision Matrix Scaling}\label{30004868}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 30004868 · Computer Science}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Standard number systems are $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$. Specialized notation and index ranges follow the cited source and the definitions below.


Matrix scaling seeks diagonal matrices $D_1,D_2$ with positive diagonal entries such that $D_1AD_2$ has prescribed row and column marginals, up to the requested $\ell_1$ error. Here $m$ denotes the number of nonzero input entries. For the dense query question, $A\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^{n\times n}$ satisfies $\sum_{i,j}A_{ij}\leq1$, its row-sum vector is $r_i=\sum_jA_{ij}$, and the required estimate satisfies $\|\widehat r-r\|_1\leq\varepsilon$. The notation $\widetilde O$ suppresses logarithmic factors.

\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Can a quantum algorithm improve the $\widetilde O(m^{3/2})$ high-precision running time for general matrix scaling while retaining polylogarithmic dependence on $1/\varepsilon$? For a dense nonnegative $n\times n$ matrix whose entries sum to at most $1$, what is the quantum query complexity of approximating its row-sum vector to $\ell_1$ error $\varepsilon$? \sref{30004868}{2}
