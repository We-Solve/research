\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Work in $R=\C[x,y,z]$ and set
\[
 \Delta=x^2y+z^2.
\]
The source specifies an automorphism $\gamma\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\C}R$ by
\[
 \gamma(x)=x,\qquad \gamma(z)=z+x\Delta,
\]
\[
 \gamma(y)=y-2xyz-\Delta^2
       +2\bigl(3z^2\Delta+3xz\Delta^2+x^2\Delta^3\bigr).
\]
Every occurrence of $\Delta$ in these defining images is the polynomial $x^2y+z^2$ in the original variables; the images are simultaneous assignments, not successive substitutions.

In the usual polynomial-automorphism terminology, tame automorphisms form the subgroup generated by affine and triangular automorphisms. An exponential automorphism is conventionally $\exp D$ for a locally nilpotent $\C$-derivation $D$ of $R$, where for each $a\in R$ some power of $D$ kills $a$ and
\[
 (\exp D)(a)=\sum_{j\ge0}\frac{D^j(a)}{j!}
\]
is a finite sum on each polynomial. Let the generalized tame subgroup be generated by tame automorphisms together with these exponential automorphisms.

Is $\gamma$ in that subgroup? This asks for membership by a finite group expression, not merely membership in a closure or stable tameness after adjoining extra variables. The usual locally-nilpotent meaning of exponential should be matched with the report's convention.
