\paragraph{An exact complex-action rule in a restricted model.}Let \(P_h=hD+q(x)\) on the circle of length \(2\pi\), with complex analytic periodic \(q\). The first-order equation gives
\[u(x)=u(0)\exp\left(\frac{i}{h}\int_0^x(z-q(s))ds\right).
\]
Hence a nonzero periodic solution exists exactly when
\[I(z):=\int_0^{2\pi}(z-q(x))dx=2\pi hk,\qquad k\in\mathbb Z.
\]
The integral \(I\) is the action \(\int\xi\,dx\) on the complex characteristic graph \(\xi=z-q(x)\). It follows that \(z_k=hk+\bar q\). If \(q\) is real, the eigenvalues are real and the same formula is the ordinary periodic first-order action quantization; there is no turning point and therefore no half-integer correction in this model.
\paragraph{The stability obstruction.}Let \(q=iv\) with real nonconstant \(v\). As in the direct ODE, conjugation by \(e^{\phi/h}\), \(\phi'=v-\bar v\), reduces the operator to its mean, but the similarity norm product is \(e^{\operatorname{osc}\phi/h}\). An operator error of algebraic size in \(h\) can become exponentially large under that conjugation. Therefore deriving an approximate action equation is not enough: the remainder must be controlled in a norm appropriate to the nonnormal realization before zeros of a quantization expression certify eigenvalues.
