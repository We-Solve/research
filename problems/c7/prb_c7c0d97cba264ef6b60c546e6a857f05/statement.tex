\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Bangert's exact question is whether \(\R\times S^1\) admits an irreversible finite-volume Finsler metric with no nonconstant closed geodesics. A smooth strongly convex Finsler metric \(F\) is positively homogeneous on tangent vectors, with positive-definite fiber Hessian of \(F^2/2\) off the zero section; irreversible means \(F(x,v)\ne F(x,-v)\) for some \((x,v)\). The source's section heading concerns complete finite-volume metrics, although completeness and the volume convention are omitted from the sentence. Forward and backward completeness must be distinguished. The aid reports the intended complete finite-Holmes--Thompson-area question still open, and supplies a different answer for Busemann--Hausdorff area.

Use coordinates \((r,\theta)\in\R\times(\R/2\pi\Z)\), and set
\[
 a(r)=2+\pi^{-1}\arctan r,\qquad
 \alpha^2=\dd r^2+a(r)^2\dd\theta^2,
\]
\[
 f(r)=\sqrt{1+r^2}-\log(1+\sqrt{1+r^2})-1+\log2,
 \qquad F=\alpha+\dd f.
\]
This exact Randers metric has
\[
 f'(r)=\frac{r}{1+\sqrt{1+r^2}},\qquad
 b(r)=|\dd f|_\alpha<1,\qquad
 1-b(r)^2=\frac2{1+\sqrt{1+r^2}}.
\]
The aid asserts strong convexity, irreversibility, both forward and backward completeness, and no nonconstant closed geodesics. For Randers surfaces the indicated volume densities are
\[
 \dd A_{\mathrm{BH}}=(1-b^2)^{3/2}\dd A_\alpha,
 \qquad \dd A_{\mathrm{HT}}=\dd A_\alpha,
 \qquad \dd A_\alpha=a(r)\,\dd r\,\dd\theta.
\]
Thus this example has finite BH area and infinite HT area. BH uses Euclidean-normalized tangent unit-ball volume, whereas HT uses normalized symplectic cotangent co-ball volume. The exact perturbation preserves unparametrized geodesic curves, and the aid's conclusion concerns closed curves, not equality of their parametrizations.

The reported complementary obstruction is: every forward-complete exact Randers cylinder \(F=\alpha+\dd f\), with \(|\dd f|_\alpha<1\) and finite HT area, has infinitely many geometrically distinct nonconstant closed geodesics. The same holds with finite BH area if there is a uniform margin \(|\dd f|_\alpha\leq1-\eta\) for some \(\eta>0\). This restriction is substantial: the explicit example has \(b(r)\to1\) at both ends. The obstruction is for exact Randers metrics, not arbitrary irreversible Finsler metrics.

The merged record also includes the introduction to the next section, on magnetic flows on a closed surface \(M\). It describes charged-particle trajectories using the cotangent bundle projection \(\pi:T^*M\to M\), the twisted symplectic form

 \Omega=\dd p\wedge\dd x+\pi^*\omega,
\]
where \(\omega\) is a closed, not necessarily exact, two-form, and a kinetic Hamiltonian proportional to \(\sum_{i,j}g^{ij}(x)p_i p_j\). Its extraction does not fix the conventional factor \(1/2\). This is adjacent background, with no separate magnetic-flow question supplied, and is not a hypothesis of Bangert's cylinder question.
\subsection{Short English statement}
The finite-volume question depends on the Finsler volume convention. The aid gives a complete irreversible exact Randers cylinder with finite BH area and no closed geodesics, but its HT area is infinite. Finite-HT forward-complete exact Randers cylinders instead have infinitely many closed geodesics, while the general intended HT question remains open in the supplied report.
