\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 800658}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \((X,\tau)\) be a topological space, and define
\[
\mathcal D(X)=\left\{\mathcal U\subseteq\tau:\overline{\bigcup\mathcal U}^{\,X}=X\right\}.
\]
Thus a member of \(\mathcal D(X)\) is a family of open subsets whose union is dense in \(X\); it need not cover \(X\).

The game \(\mathsf G_1(\mathcal D(X),\mathcal D(X))\) has innings indexed by \(n\in\{0,1,2,\ldots\}\). In inning \(n\), Player I chooses \(\mathcal U_n\in\mathcal D(X)\), and Player II chooses one set \(U_n\in\mathcal U_n\). For a completed legal play, Player II wins exactly when
\[
\overline{\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}U_n}^{\,X}=X;
\]
otherwise Player I wins. Strategies may depend on the entire finite history of play. A winning strategy guarantees its player's victory against every legal sequence of opposing moves. The game is undetermined if neither player has a winning strategy.

The question is whether one can prove in ZFC that there exists a topological space \(X\) such that \(\mathsf G_1(\mathcal D(X),\mathcal D(X))\) is undetermined.

No separation, countability, or metrizability hypothesis is stated in the supplied record. The source also does not explicitly require \(X\ne\varnothing\). For nonempty \(X\), every family in \(\mathcal D(X)\) is nonempty, so the specified responses are available. For \(X=\varnothing\), the empty family belongs to \(\mathcal D(X)\), and a move choosing that family leaves Player II without a legal response. The record supplies no convention for this case; its treatment remains an unresolved boundary qualification.

As context, the supplied source attributes the open-point game to Berner and Juh\'asz in [BerJu] and reports, citing [coc6], that a player has a winning strategy in the open-point game on \(X\) if and only if the other player has a winning strategy in \(\mathsf G_1(\mathcal D(X),\mathcal D(X))\). This is a reported source assertion, not an independently verified result here.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does ZFC prove that there is a topological space on which neither player can force a win in the following game? Each inning, Player I supplies a family of open sets with dense union, and Player II selects one member. Player II wins when the union of all selected sets is dense. The source leaves the empty-space convention unspecified and states no separation assumptions.
