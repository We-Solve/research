\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
In the customary real singularity setting, let $f:(\R^n,0)\to(\R,0)$ be a real analytic function germ whose complexification $f_{\C}$ has an isolated critical point. Its Milnor number is
\[
 \mu(f)=\dim_{\C}
 \C\{x_1,\ldots,x_n\}/
 (\partial f_{\C}/\partial x_1,\ldots,\partial f_{\C}/\partial x_n).
\]
Let $F(x,\lambda)$ be a real versal deformation, let $B_{\R}$ be a sufficiently small real parameter neighborhood, and let $\Sigma_{\R}$ be the source's discriminant in this base. A real Morsification has nondegenerate nearby complex critical points; being totally real here means that all $\mu(f)$ of those critical points lie in the real locus.

Vassiliev's Problem 6C asks whether
\[
 \text{for every connected component }C\text{ of }B_{\R}\setminus\Sigma_{\R},
 \quad\exists\lambda\in C
 \text{ with }F_\lambda\text{ a totally real Morsification}.
\]
This uses the usual local representative and real-base interpretation. The excerpt itself does not specify real versus complex base, the exact discriminant, the permitted class of germs, or the Milnor neighborhood. In particular, the zero-level discriminant and the locus of non-Morse functions should not be identified without checking the source convention.

The supplied registry reports this component-wise existence statement established for all simple singularities by Vassiliev (2021) and all parabolic singularities by Vassiliev (2025), through complete component classifications with explicit representatives. It reports the question still open for nonsimple, nonparabolic singularities. These are supplied scope and status claims, not results verified by this draft.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every real discriminant-complement chamber of a versal deformation contain a Morsification with all Milnor-number-many critical points real? The registry reports the simple and parabolic cases established and the remaining scope open.
