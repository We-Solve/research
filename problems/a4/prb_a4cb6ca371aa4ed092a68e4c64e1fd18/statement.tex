\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(k\) be a field of characteristic zero, and let \(\mathfrak a\subseteq k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]\) be a binomial ideal containing no nonzero monomial. Here \(x^c=\prod_{j=1}^n x_j^{c_j}\) for \(c\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^n\). Let
\[
\mathfrak a=(f_1,\ldots,f_r),\qquad f_i=x^{a_i}-\gamma_i x^{b_i},\qquad a_i,b_i\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^n,\quad \gamma_i\in k^\times,
\]
be any minimal binomial generating system, meaning that no proper subcollection generates \(\mathfrak a\). Regard the exponent vectors as columns and define
\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}
a_1&\cdots&a_r&b_1&\cdots&b_r\\
&I_r&&&I_r&
\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}^{(n+r)\times 2r},
\]
where the bottom block is \((I_r\ I_r)\). For \(z=(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_r,\nu_1,\ldots,\nu_r)^{\mathsf T}\), put
\[
P=\{z\in\mathbb Q_{\geq0}^{2r}:Az\leq\mathbf1_{n+r}\},\qquad L(z)=\sum_{i=1}^r(\mu_i+\nu_i),
\]
with the inequality interpreted coordinatewise. Define the set of optimal rational solutions by
\[
\mathcal O=\{z\in P:L(z)\geq L(w)\text{ for every }w\in P\}.
\]
The question is whether, for every such field, ideal, and minimal binomial generating system,
\[
\exists z\in\mathcal O\quad\forall z'\in\mathcal O,\qquad z'\ne z\ \Longrightarrow\ Az'\ne Az.
\]
Thus the requested solution must be the only optimal rational solution having its particular image under \(A\).
