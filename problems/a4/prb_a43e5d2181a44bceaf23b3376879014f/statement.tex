\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(d\geq 1\) be an integer. Under the usual undirected nearest-neighbour interpretation of the random conductance model, let
\[
E_d=\bigl\{\{x,y\}:x,y\in\Z^d,\ \|x-y\|_1=1\bigr\}.
\]
An environment is a collection \(\omega=(\omega(e))_{e\in E_d}\) with law \(\mathbb P\). Assume that \(\mathbb P\) is stationary and ergodic under lattice translations and that, for every \(e\in E_d\),
\[
\mathbb P(0<\omega(e)<\infty)=1,
\qquad \mathbb E_{\mathbb P}[\omega(e)]<\infty.
\]
No independence assumption is supplied. Write \(\omega_{xy}=\omega(\{x,y\})\) for neighbouring vertices and \(\mu^\omega(x)=\sum_{y:\|y-x\|_1=1}\omega_{xy}\).

\subsection{Short English statement}
For a stationary ergodic random conductance environment on \(\Z^d\), assume each conductance is positive and finite and has finite expectation. Does finite expectation of every reciprocal conductance guarantee that, in almost every fixed environment, the diffusively rescaled walk converges to Brownian motion with deterministic positive-definite covariance? The intended walk and clock require clarification. Supplied dated curation labels the problem partially solved and identifies \(d\geq3\) as the remaining general range.
