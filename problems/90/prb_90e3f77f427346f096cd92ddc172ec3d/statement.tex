\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $X$ be a smooth projective toric variety over a ground field $k$ and let $L$ be ample. The source asks whether its ring $R(X,L)$ is normal and Koszul, calling this Bøgvad's conjecture, and more broadly which hypotheses on a polarized toric variety ensure either property. The notation $R(X,L)$ is not defined in the extract, and two standard rings must be distinguished. The full section ring is \[R_{\mathrm{sec}}(X,L)=\bigoplus_{m\geq0}H^0(X,L^{\otimes m}),\] with multiplication of sections. The subalgebra generated in degree one is \[R_{\mathrm{emb}}(X,L)=\operatorname{im}\bigl(\operatorname{Sym}_k H^0(X,L)\to R_{\mathrm{sec}}(X,L)\bigr).\] Equality of these rings is normal generation, equivalently surjectivity of the multiplication maps $\operatorname{Sym}^m H^0(X,L)\to H^0(X,L^{\otimes m})$ for every $m\geq0$. Normality of a domain means integral closedness in its fraction field. For a connected graded $k$-algebra $R$, Koszulness means \[\operatorname{Tor}^R_i(k,k)_j=0\qquad\text{whenever }j\ne i,\] or equivalently a linear graded free resolution of the residue field. In the usual normal-variety setting the full section ring is integrally closed; the nontrivial normality/projective-normality question normally concerns the degree-one coordinate ring and normal generation. Thus the source's requested normal-and-Koszul assertion cannot be read without distinguishing these meanings of section ring. This draft retains the literal question and exposes the convention issue rather than silently substituting one ring. The broader question also supplies no definite hypothesis class to classify. The 2026-08-21 triage labels the general Bøgvad normality/Koszulness formulation open and reports substantial special cases from diagonal splitting; this supplied status is not used to resolve the ring-definition ambiguity.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Ask for normality and Koszulness in the smooth polarized toric setting and for broader sufficient hypotheses. The full section ring and the ring generated by degree-one sections must be distinguished, since integral closedness and normal generation are different assertions.
