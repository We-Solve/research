\section*{Lab–online–field population generalizability}
\textbf{Problem ID:} \texttt{C90-639}\quad \textbf{JEL:} \texttt{C90}\par
% BEGIN ECONOMICS STATEMENT
\label{problem:OP-0070}
\label{atlas:prob:B10}

\noindent\textbf{Original identifier:} B10 (atlas item 70). \textbf{Classification:} Population parameter generalizability research program. \textbf{Original tractability label:} Easy (editorial, not a complexity result).

\subsection*{Definitions and assumptions}

Let populations $p\in\mathcal P$ represent laboratory, online, and field recruitment frames, all facing a common experimental protocol $G$. For person $i$, let $\theta_i\in\Theta$ be a latent behavioral parameter and $X_i$ observed pretreatment characteristics. The measurement model is $P(A_i\mid G,\theta_i,X_i,p)$, with common normalization and a specified noise distribution. Let $R_i=1$ denote participation and define the target population distribution independently of recruitment. Parameter comparability requires measurement invariance: identical latent types at the same $X_i$ and facing the same protocol generate identical conditional action distributions across frames, unless population-specific measurement differences are modeled. Any such differences and any dependence of the measurement law on participation must be explicit parts of the measurement model; invariance or a common normalization alone does not identify the latent distribution.

For each application, declare the population probability law indexed by each $p$, its associated recruitment frame and frame-membership rule, and its participation mechanism. Population membership, frame membership, and participation are distinct objects; no disjointness of populations or frames is presumed. Declare the source--target pairs under study and the observable-data law, including which actions, covariates, frame indicators, and sampling information are available for participants, nonparticipants, and target-population members. State whether $F_{X\mid p_t}$ is known, estimated, or only partially identified, and specify the sampling and dependence structure used for inference.

The action space and action record $A_i$, protocol $G$ including any repeated-task structure, measurable latent space $\Theta$, behavioral interpretation and normalization of $\theta_i$, and measurement kernel and noise restrictions are application inputs. Specify which measurement features and nuisance parameters are known, estimated, or unrestricted, and how the stated kernel relates to the action law among participants. These declarations specify a model to be investigated; they do not presume injectivity of its latent mixture map or identification from repeated choices.

\subsection*{Formal research question}

Identify or sharply bound $F_{\theta\mid p}$ and the extent to which between-population differences are attributable to observed composition. For each declared source--target pair $(p_s,p_t)$, conditional latent-type exchangeability means
\[
F_{\theta\mid X=x,p_t}(z)=F_{\theta\mid X=x,R=1,p_s}(z)
\]
for $F_{X\mid p_t}$-almost every $x$ and every argument $z$ of the declared distributional target, using the same distributional convention on both sides. Overlap means $F_{X\mid p_t}\ll F_{X\mid R=1,p_s}$, where these symbols denote the corresponding covariate probability laws. Conditional distributions used here must be well defined in the declared model. These assumptions link source participants to the target population, rather than only linking frames or participants within a frame. Under these assumptions, evaluate
\[
F_{\theta\mid p_t}(z)=\int F_{\theta\mid X=x,R=1,p_s}(z)\,dF_{X\mid p_t}(x),\quad z\in\Theta,
\]
interpreted componentwise for vector parameters. This is a population transport identity; it does not establish identification of the latent conditional distribution from observed actions. Unsupported target covariate regions require separate analysis rather than implicit extrapolation. Determine sensitivity to violations of sampling exchangeability and measurement invariance. Test whether parameters calibrated on one frame predict choices on held-out frames within a declared discrepancy metric and tolerance, accounting for first-stage estimation uncertainty.

The vector convention remains to be specified: a joint lower-orthant CDF and a collection of coordinate marginal CDFs are different targets. The parameter space, order, and argument domain must support the selected convention; an arbitrary measurable latent space does not itself define a CDF. Marginal CDFs do not determine latent dependence or all joint-choice predictions.

For identification and sharp bounds, declare the admissible model class and its compatibility restrictions with the observable-data law. For composition attribution, declare the distributional contrast, reference population or distribution, and standardization or decomposition convention. For sensitivity, declare the departure classes and their parameter ranges. These choices are not fixed here. Composition attribution is statistical unless supported by a separate causal argument. Compatible-model sets may be empty and bounds may be trivial; writing down such a set alone does not complete the identification task.

For held-out prediction, declare whether the validation population consists of participants or the independently defined target population, the action law to be predicted, the calibration and validation information sets, the frame holdout rule, and whether recalibration is allowed. The discrepancy and tolerance are declared inputs, as required above. State how agreement within that tolerance is assessed from data, including the statistical decision or uncertainty criterion and uncertainty in every estimated stage, including target composition. A finite-sample or uniform asymptotic claim additionally requires its sampling assumptions and law class. The validation population and assessment criterion remain unspecified here; agreement of action predictions alone does not identify latent distributions when measurement is noninjective.

\subsection*{Interpretation and scope}

This target differs from B1: it concerns transport of a latent parameter distribution, not merely transport of one treatment effect. Latent parameters may be unidentified even when an average treatment effect is experimentally identified. Subject-pool regressions should therefore account for estimation noise, rather than regress noisy individual estimates as if observed without error. Education or digital literacy can explain statistical variation without being its causal source; causal statements about these traits require a separate intervention or identification argument. Representative panels reduce some sampling discrepancies but do not eliminate participation selection or protocol differences. Common instructions, incentives, device access, and comprehension checks help support measurement invariance, although checks conducted after treatment must not create new selection bias. A benchmark infrastructure should preregister target populations and protocols, document exclusions, and evaluate both population-average and distributional predictions. Good fit in observed populations cannot establish universal generalizability to every future subject pool.

\subsection*{Common conventions: Source mathematical conventions}

Unless an entry states otherwise, agent and firm sets are finite. State and action spaces are measurable, strategies and outcome maps are measurable, probability laws are specified, and expectations exist for the targets under discussion. Infinite-horizon discount factors lie in $(0,1)$ and discounted objectives are finite. Norms, tolerances and complexity input sizes must be specified for computational comparisons. Constants or primitives that appear locally are inputs, not empirical facts asserted by this catalogue.

\textbf{Identification.} A statistical model $m\in\mathcal M$ implies an observable law $P_m$ and target $\theta(m)$. At law $P$, its identified set is
\[
\Theta(P;\mathcal M)=\{\theta(m):m\in\mathcal M,\ P_m=P\}.
\]
Point identification holds when this set is a singleton, or equivalently when $P_{m_1}=P_{m_2}$ implies $\theta(m_1)=\theta(m_2)$ for all compatible models. Sharp bounds mean bounds attained, or approached when the boundary is not attained, by compatible models. Writing this set is a definition, not a solution: the substantive task is to establish informative restrictions and derive or estimate the set. An unrestricted-confounding model may give only trivial bounds.

\textbf{Inference.} Identification, estimability and valid uncertainty quantification are separate questions. Fix $\alpha\in(0,1)$. A confidence procedure $C_n$ over a declared law class $\mathcal P$ has asymptotic uniform coverage at level $1-\alpha$ when
\[
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\inf_{P\in\mathcal P}\Pr_P\{\theta(P)\in C_n\}\geq1-\alpha.
\]
For set-identified targets the coverage event must be defined separately, for example coverage of the full identified set. If an entry uses identification-indexed models instead of uniquely indexed laws, the same coverage convention is applied over those models. Pointwise limits do not establish uniform validity, and asymptotic validity does not guarantee finite-sample precision. Sample sizes, dependence structures and weak-identification sequences are part of each application.

\textbf{Counterfactuals.} $Y_i(a)$ is a potential outcome under a specified intervention, including its timing, implementation and versions. It is not $Y_i$ conditional on voluntarily choosing $a$. A full intervention vector permits interference; shorthand scalar treatment notation does not silently impose no interference. Assignment, selection, attrition, anticipation and measurement must be specified. A claim about an instrument's local effect is not automatically a population policy effect.

\textbf{Economic equilibrium.} A counterfactual model includes best responses, constraints, feasibility, market clearing, state transitions and expectations consistent with the stated information. When several equilibria exist, either a declared selector is an input or the target is set-valued. Comparisons across policy regimes must use the stated selection convention. Reduced-form relationships are not presumed invariant after an equilibrium-changing intervention.

\textbf{Optimization and welfare.} Optimization is over the stated feasible set. If attainment is not established, $\sup$ or $\inf$ and $\varepsilon$-optimal choices replace an asserted maximizer or minimizer. For example, with value $V=\sup_{a\in\mathcal A}W(a)<\infty$, an $\varepsilon$-optimal set is $\{a\in\mathcal A:W(a)\geq V-\varepsilon\}$ for $\varepsilon>0$. Benefits, costs, risk and health or environmental outcomes can be aggregated only using declared units and conversion weights. Interpersonal utility weights, the treatment of fiscal transfers and foreign profits, discounting, and the behavioral welfare criterion are explicit inputs. A comparative-static derivative additionally requires existence and appropriate differentiability of the selected counterfactual.
