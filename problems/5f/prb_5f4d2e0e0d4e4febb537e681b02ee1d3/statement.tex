\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
\textbf{Problem 14 (A. Balog).} For fixed integer $a>1$, show that $|ap-q|$ can be small with $p,q$ prime. More generally consider real $a$. The final OCR fragment is ``$a,1$'', with its comparison symbol lost; it may intend an exclusion of $a=1$, but this is not silently restored as exact text. ``Small'' has no quantitative scale or infinitude/density requirement in the source.

\textbf{Reported quantitative short-interval transfer.} For every fixed $a>0$ and every $\eta>0$, the aid asserts that there exist $c(a,\eta)>0$, $C(a,\eta)>0$, and $P_0(a,\eta)$ such that, for every $P\geq P_0$, at least
\[
 c(a,\eta)\frac P{\log P}
\]
primes $p\in(P,3P/2)$ have a prime $q$ with
\[
 0<q-ap<C(a,\eta)p^{1/20+\eta}.
\]
This is one-sided, excludes the zero difference, holds block by block, and implies a sublinear error by choosing $\eta<19/20$. It is not a bounded-error result. The report derives it from Jia's published quantitative almost-all short-interval theorem with exceptional set $O(X(\log X)^{-B})$ and a bounded-multiplicity endpoint map. The exact theorem statement, the role/range of $B$ and the complete citation are absent.

The candidate-contribution summary also gives an ordered-prime-pair lower bound of order
\[
 \frac{P^{1+1/20+\delta}}{(\log P)^2}
\]
at error scale $p^{1/20+\delta}$, for the designated $a>0$. Its precise $\delta$ range, interval endpoints and constants are not supplied, so it is retained as a qualified report rather than an unqualified theorem for all $\delta$.

\textbf{Reported rational local optimum.} Let $a=A/B>0$ in lowest terms, $A,B\in\N$. For odd prime inputs and outputs,
\[
 B(q-ap)=Bq-Ap\in\Z,
\]
which is odd if $AB$ is even and even if $AB$ is odd. Thus the smallest arithmetically permitted strictly positive error is
\[
 \frac{d_{A,B}}B,\qquad
 d_{A,B}=\begin{cases}1,&AB\text{ even},\\2,&AB\text{ odd}.\end{cases}
\]
The aid's local calculation identifies no further obstruction and, conditional on Dickson's conjecture for the corresponding admissible pair of linear forms, predicts infinitely many prime pairs satisfying
\[
 Bq-Ap=d_{A,B}.
\]
Consequently it calls $1/B$ or $2/B$ the conjectural optimal bounded error. Exceptional pairs involving the prime $2$ do not affect this infinite-family formulation. For the absolute-value question one must also handle zero differences: if $a=1$ and $p=q$ is allowed, that reading has the trivial value zero. The strict one-sided transfer and rational optimum above avoid that degeneracy. The report does not establish Dickson's conjecture or the bounded-error prediction unconditionally.
