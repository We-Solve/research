\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Assume the Riemann hypothesis, so the nontrivial zeros of \(\zeta(s)\) have the form \(\tfrac12+i\gamma\) with \(\gamma\in\R\). Use the usual convention of counting zeros with multiplicity, and let the pair sum be over ordered pairs of zero occurrences with both ordinates in \((0,T]\), including the diagonal. Put
\[
A_T=\frac{T\log T}{2\pi},\qquad
\rho(u)=1-\left(\frac{\sin(\pi u)}{\pi u}\right)^2,
\]
where the quotient is continued as one at zero, so \(\rho(0)=0\). For an even test function \(f\), define
\[
S_T(f)=\sum_{0<\gamma,\gamma'\leq T}
f\left(\frac{(\gamma-\gamma')\log T}{2\pi}\right),
\qquad C_f=f(0)+\int_{\R}f(u)\rho(u)\,\dd u.
\]
The source asks for the sine-kernel pair-correlation limit
\[
\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{S_T(f)}{A_T}=C_f,
\quad\text{equivalently}\quad S_T(f)=A_TC_f+o_f(A_T),
\]
for every fixed suitable even \(f\). A conventional precise working class is the even Schwartz functions on \(\R\), but the source's exact meaning of suitable, including any positivity or Fourier-transform hypotheses, is not specified and must be checked. The source describes the target as a full strong pair-correlation assertion; a restricted test-function theorem must not be silently substituted for it.

The displayed source uses \(S_T(f)\sim A_TC_f\). This has the same ratio meaning when \(C_f\ne0\). For signed tests with \(C_f=0\), a ratio asymptotic to zero is undefined; the normalized limit above states the natural additive interpretation, with that textual qualification explicit. The \(f(0)\) term records the included diagonal, and no simplicity assumption on zeros is added.

The supplied literature assessment checked 2026-08-21 calls the full assertion a conjectural refinement and reports that no unconditional proof was located. The mathematical question here nevertheless retains its explicit RH assumption; the dated assessment does not establish the assertion even under that assumption.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Under RH, the scaled ordered pair statistic of zeta zeros, including its diagonal, is conjectured to converge to a diagonal mass plus the sine-kernel density. The test-function class and the meaning of the asymptotic when its leading constant vanishes need clarification.
