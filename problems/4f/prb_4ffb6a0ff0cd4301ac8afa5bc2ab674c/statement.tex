\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
The original question, attributed to Leonid Gurvits, asks for an analogue of Kadison--Singer for \(\mathrm{Zon}=\sum_{i=1}^k\alpha_i[0,z_i]\), with \(z_i\in\R^n\), \(k>n\), but states neither coefficient conditions nor a desired conclusion. The aid adopts a centered, consistently weighted repair: fix vectors \(v_1,\ldots,v_k\in\R^n\), not all zero, put \(E=\operatorname{span}\{v_i\}\), \(d=\dim E\), and
\[
 Z=\sum_{i=1}^k[-v_i,v_i],\qquad Z_J=\sum_{i\in J}[-v_i,v_i],\qquad
 h_Z(x)=\sum_{i=1}^k|\langle v_i,x\rangle|\quad(x\in E).
\]
All weights are absorbed into the \(v_i\), and the same weighted generators are used in both subzonotopes. The relation of these repaired generators to the unspecified original coefficients is not uniquely determined.

For \(0\leq\eta\leq1/2\), simultaneous approximate-half inclusions mean
\[
 (1/2-\eta)Z\subset Z_J\subset(1/2+\eta)Z,\qquad
 (1/2-\eta)Z\subset Z_{J^c}\subset(1/2+\eta)Z.
\]
Writing \(\varepsilon_i=1\) on \(J\) and \(-1\) on \(J^c\), these are equivalent to
\[
 \left|\sum_{i=1}^k\varepsilon_i|\langle v_i,x\rangle|\right|
 \leq2\eta\sum_{i=1}^k|\langle v_i,x\rangle|\qquad(x\in E).
\]
Define the actual small-segment parameter, rather than an arbitrary upper bound for it, by
\[
 \delta=\max_i\sup_{x\in E\setminus\{0\}}
 \frac{|\langle v_i,x\rangle|}{h_Z(x)}
 =\min\{a\geq0:[-v_i,v_i]\subset aZ\text{ for every }i\}.
\]
Then \(0<\delta\leq1\). The reported partial theorem provides a partition with relative support discrepancy
\[
 \sup_{x\in E\setminus\{0\}}
 \frac{|\sum_i\varepsilon_i|\langle v_i,x\rangle||}{h_Z(x)}
 \leq\min\{1,12\sqrt{3\delta d}\},
\]
hence with \(\eta\leq\min\{1/2,6\sqrt{3\delta d}\}\). The bound is also valid with any larger prescribed small-segment upper bound in place of \(\delta\).

In the opposite direction, every partition has relative discrepancy at least \(\max\{0,2\delta-1\}\), where \(\delta\) must be the actual parameter just defined. This lower bound does not follow from an arbitrarily loose inclusion parameter. The aid reports a full-dimensional volume-one box with \(\delta=1\), showing that volume normalization alone cannot force a nontrivial approximate-half partition; zero generators may be appended if necessary to meet the original \(k>n\) convention.

For the parallel-class refinement, a precise within-class ratio can be defined as follows: divide the nonzero generators into parallel classes \(C_\ell\), write \(v_i=c_iw_\ell\) in each class, and put
\[
 \rho=\max_\ell\frac{\max_{i\in C_\ell}|c_i|}{\sum_{i\in C_\ell}|c_i|}.
\]
With this explicitly stated convention, balancing each class yields the reported error form \(\eta\leq\rho/2\). The summary does not itself define its symbol \(\rho\), so this normalization is identified as part of the precise repaired formulation. None of these dimension-dependent partial bounds recovers a uniquely specified original zonotope analogue of Kadison--Singer.
\subsection{Short English statement}
