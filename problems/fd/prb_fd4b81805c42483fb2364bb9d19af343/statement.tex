\section{Many-Body Localization at Positive Density}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


\paragraph{Definitions and conventions.} A random interacting quantum many-body system at positive density has particle number $N_\Lambda$ in a finite region $\Lambda$ with $N_\Lambda/|\Lambda|\to\rho>0$ in the thermodynamic limit, or the analogous finite-energy-density spin-system description. Localization can mean spectral localization, suppressed transport, or suitable dynamical correlation bounds; these notions need to be distinguished. Stability under interactions asks whether an appropriate localized regime survives adding nonzero couplings between particles.

\paragraph{Source scope and qualifications.} The report explicitly makes precise formulation part of the challenge. Results for a fixed finite number of particles do not cover this limit; neither a universal disorder threshold nor one preferred notion of many-body localization is specified by the extract.

\paragraph{Catalogue mathematical target.}
\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}
For systems of infinitely many interacting particles at positive density, what is the precise localization regime and is it stable under interactions?

\paragraph{Saved literature context.} The supplied catalogue's assessment dated 2026-08-21 records: The report explicitly identifies localization at positive density in infinitely many-particle systems as an open problem; later literature does not supply a general resolution. The assessment is retained as dated provenance; it is not a new comprehensive literature or open-status certification.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Formulate and prove stable localization for an infinite interacting quantum system at positive density.
