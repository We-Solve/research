\section{Are there infinitely many Wilson primes}\label{9400165}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 9400165 · Number Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A Wilson prime is a prime $p$ for which $(p-1)!\equiv-1\pmod{p^2}$. Wilson's theorem makes $w_p=((p-1)!+1)/p$ an integer for every prime $p$, and the stronger condition is $p\mid w_p$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Are there infinitely many Wilson primes? \sref{9400165}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Are there infinitely many primes for which Wilson’s factorial congruence holds modulo the prime square?
