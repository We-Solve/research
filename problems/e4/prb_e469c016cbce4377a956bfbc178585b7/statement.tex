\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(K\) be a number field, fix an algebraic closure \(\overline K\), and let \(K^{\mathrm{ab}}\subseteq\overline K\) be the maximal abelian extension, the union of all finite abelian extensions of \(K\) in \(\overline K\). Let \(A/K\) be an abelian variety with \(\dim A>0\), which is the meaning of nonzero here. For any extension \(L/K\), define
\[
\operatorname{rk}A(L)=\dim_{\Q}(A(L)\otimes_{\Z}\Q).
\]
The Frey--Jarden question in the source is
\[
\forall K\text{ a number field}\quad\forall A/K\text{ with }\dim A>0,
\qquad\operatorname{rk}A(K^{\mathrm{ab}})=\infty.
\]
Equivalently, for every integer \(r\geq1\) there should exist a finite abelian extension \(L/K\) such that \(\operatorname{rk}A(L)\geq r\). The extension may depend on \(r\) and \(A\). The target is infinitely many independent points modulo torsion, not merely infinitely many points or unbounded torsion. No finite-generation assertion over the infinite field \(K^{\mathrm{ab}}\) is made.

The supplied literature assessment checked 2026-08-21 calls the Frey--Jarden question difficult and open and says that the cited Pop work supplies only a partial answer. The hypotheses or conclusion of that partial result are not reproduced, so no full-rank theorem is inferred from it.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every positive-dimensional abelian variety over every number field have infinite Mordell--Weil rank over the maximal abelian extension? Equivalently, can its ranks over finite abelian extensions be made arbitrarily large?
