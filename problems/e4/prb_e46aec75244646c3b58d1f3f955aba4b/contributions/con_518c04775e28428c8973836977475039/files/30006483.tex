\paragraph{A nonuniform-limit diagnostic.} Write a hypothetical finite-volume correlation energy density in the form
\[
 e_L(\rho)-e_{{\rm HF},L}(\rho)
 =c_1\rho\log\rho+c_2\rho+\rho r(L,\rho).
\]
The elementary remainder \(r(L,\rho)=L/(L+\rho)\) tends to zero as \(\rho\to\infty\) for every fixed \(L\), but tends to one as \(L\to\infty\) for every fixed \(\rho\). Thus
\[
 \lim_{L\to\infty}\lim_{\rho\to\infty}r(L,\rho)=0,
 \qquad
 \lim_{\rho\to\infty}\lim_{L\to\infty}r(L,\rho)=1.
\]
This is a counterexample to a proposed interchange of limits, not a candidate physical jellium energy. It shows that even a correct fixed-torus expansion can miss an entire order-\(\rho\) term after thermodynamic limiting.
\paragraph{The infrared scale behind the issue.} An auxiliary logarithmic momentum contribution \(\int_{2\pi/L}^K dk/k=\log(KL/(2\pi))\), for \(K>2\pi/L\), depends explicitly on the infrared cutoff. Replacing that cutoff by a density-dependent screening scale before controlling the sum/integral error can change the constant accompanying the logarithm. This observation is a direct integral calculation, not a derivation of a jellium ring-diagram formula or its coefficients.
