\textbf{Erd\H{o}s \#26; reconstruction, 2026-09-23.}
For an infinite set $A$ of positive integers and $k\geq1$, put
\[
 M_k=\{n\geq1:\text{some }a\in A\text{ satisfies }a+k\mid n\}.
\]
Must some $M_k$ have natural density one? We construct an $A$ for which
every $M_k$ has upper density at most $1/2$. We also give a counterexample
whose reciprocal sum diverges, so the obstruction need not be summability.
