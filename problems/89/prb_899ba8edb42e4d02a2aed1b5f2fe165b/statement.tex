Let $A(x)$ count the positive integers at most $x$ expressible as the sum of two positive squarefull numbers. A positive integer is squarefull if every prime dividing it has exponent at least two. The proposed asymptotic $A(x)\sim cx/\sqrt{\log x}$ is false. Blomer and Granville's Corollary 2 in \emph{Estimates for representation numbers of quadratic forms}, Duke Math. J. 135 (2006), gives constants $B_1,B_2$ such that
\[
 \frac{x(\log\log x)^{B_1}}{(\log x)^\alpha}\ll A(x)\ll
 \frac{x(\log\log x)^{B_2}}{(\log x)^\alpha},\qquad \alpha=1-2^{-1/3}<\frac12.
\]
Their primary text is \url{https://dms.umontreal.ca/~andrew/PDF/quadraticforms.pdf}; the source problem is \url{https://www.erdosproblems.com/1081}. The lower bound makes $A(x)/(x/\sqrt{\log x})$ tend to infinity, since a positive power of $\log x$ dominates any fixed power of $\log\log x$.
That deep distinct-value estimate is background, not a proof supplied by this attempt. Below we prove an exact parametrization and an asymptotic for the number of representations, then identify why these do not yet reconstruct the disproof.
