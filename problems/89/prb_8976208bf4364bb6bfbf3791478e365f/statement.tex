\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A group \(G\) is finitely generated if \(G=\langle S\rangle\) for some finite subset \(S\subseteq G\). It is left-orderable if it admits a strict total order \(<\) such that, for every \(a,g,h\in G\),
\[
g<h\implies ag<ah.
\]
The condition that there is no nontrivial homomorphism into \(\Z\) means that every group homomorphism \(\varphi:G\to(\Z,+)\) satisfies \(\varphi(g)=0\) for every \(g\in G\).

Interpret \(\mathrm H_b^2(G,\R)\) as ordinary bounded group cohomology with trivial real coefficients. Explicitly, let
\[
C_b^n(G,\R)=\left\{f:G^n\to\R:\sup_{\mathbf g\in G^n}|f(\mathbf g)|<\infty\right\}.
\]
For \(f\in C_b^1(G,\R)\) and \(c\in C_b^2(G,\R)\), set
\[
(\delta_1f)(g,h)=f(h)-f(gh)+f(g),
\]
\[
(\delta_2c)(g,h,k)=c(h,k)-c(gh,k)+c(g,hk)-c(g,h).
\]
Then
\[
\mathrm H_b^2(G,\R)=\ker\delta_2/\operatorname{im}\delta_1.
\]
Thus its vanishing means that every bounded real-valued \(2\)-cocycle is the coboundary of a bounded real-valued \(1\)-cochain.

The supplied question, attributed to Navas (2017), asks whether there exists a finitely generated, left-orderable group \(G\) such that
\[
\operatorname{Hom}(G,\Z)=\{0\},
\qquad
\mathrm H_b^2(G,\R)=0.
\]
The supplied formulation does not require \(G\) to be nontrivial or infinite. No such hypothesis has been added here; whether an exclusion of the trivial group was intended remains unresolved and requires checking against the source.
