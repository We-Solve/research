\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Use the customary countable-model setting: $L$ is a countable language, $T$ an $L$-theory, $L'=L\cup\{c\}$, and $T'$ an $L'$-theory whose reducts satisfy $T$. Write $\operatorname{Mod}_\omega(T)$ for its standard Borel space of models on $\omega$, and $\cong_T$ for isomorphism. For equivalence relations $E,F$, $E\leq_B F$ means that a Borel map $h$ satisfies $xEy\Longleftrightarrow h(x)Fh(y)$. In this context Borel completeness means that isomorphism of every class of countable structures reduces to the given isomorphism relation, equivalently that a standard universal isomorphism relation such as countable-graph isomorphism reduces to it. The source does not explicitly specify countability or completeness of the theories; these conventions require confirmation.

Question 23 asks whether
\[
\cong_{T'}\text{ is Borel complete}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\cong_T\text{ is Borel complete}
\]
holds for every expansion by one constant. The supplied account reports the general implication open in the literature checked, with no check date or full literature reference supplied.

To state the aid's partial reductions, let
\[
P_M=\{a\in\omega:(M,a)\models T'\},\quad
X=\{M\models T:P_M\ne\varnothing\},\quad
Y=\{(M,a):M\in X,\ a\in P_M\}.
\]
Let $E$ be reduct isomorphism on $X$, and $F$ pointed isomorphism on $Y$. The set $X$ is Borel, and the $F$-classes lying above the $E$-class of a fixed $M$ correspond to the $\operatorname{Aut}(M)$-orbits on $P_M$, so there are at most countably many. The forgetful map $r(M,a)=M$ is a Borel reduction $F\leq_B E$ exactly when $\operatorname{Aut}(M)$ acts transitively on $P_M$ for every $M\in X$. Under this additional hypothesis, the desired Borel-completeness implication follows.

For any equivalence relation $F$ on $Y$, its Friedman--Stanley jump $F^+$ on $Y^\omega$ is defined by
\[
(y_i)_{i\in\omega}\ F^+\ (z_j)_{j\in\omega}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\{[y_i]_F:i\in\omega\}=\{[z_j]_F:j\in\omega\}.
\]
Borel enumeration of every nonempty $P_M$, with legal repeated entries when it is finite, yields a reduction $E\leq_B F^+$. One can take $a_0(M)=\min P_M$ and enumerate all legal integers, padding with $a_0(M)$; the resulting sequence consists of $(M,a)$ for all $a\in P_M$. This direction of reduction does not establish $F\leq_B E$ in general.

The aid also reports a sufficient criterion using uniform orbit labels and absorption of countably many labels. One precise sufficient formulation is: there is a Borel $\ell:Y\to\omega$ such that
\[
(M,a)F(N,b)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
M E N\ \text{ and }\ \ell(M,a)=\ell(N,b),
\]
and $E\times =_\omega\leq_B\cong_T$. Then $F\leq_B\cong_T$, so Borel completeness of $F$ implies Borel completeness of $\cong_T$. The summary supplies no proof that these additional hypotheses hold in general; they must not be treated as consequences of merely naming one constant.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does Borel completeness survive forgetting a single named element? The supplied partial results identify the countable fibers of the forgetful map, prove the implication when legal pointings form one automorphism orbit, and give a reduction of reduct isomorphism to a jump of pointed isomorphism. They leave the unrestricted question open as reported.
