\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A group \(G\) is locally finite if, for every finite subset \(S\subseteq G\), the subgroup \(\langle S\rangle\) is finite. Its centre is
\[
Z(G)=\{z\in G: zg=gz\text{ for every }g\in G\}.
\]
Write \(\operatorname{Aut}(G)\) for the group of automorphisms of \(G\), and let
\[
\operatorname{Inn}(G)=\{c_g:g\in G\},\qquad c_g(x)=gxg^{-1}.
\]
The group \(G\) is complete if \(Z(G)=\{1_G\}\) and \(\operatorname{Aut}(G)=\operatorname{Inn}(G)\); the latter condition means that it has no outer automorphisms.

Does there exist a group \(G\) such that
\[
|G|=\aleph_0,\qquad
\forall S\subseteq G\;\bigl(S\text{ finite}\Longrightarrow |\langle S\rangle|<\infty\bigr),\qquad
Z(G)=\{1_G\},\qquad
\operatorname{Aut}(G)=\operatorname{Inn}(G)?
\]
The supplied source states that an uncountable locally finite complete group exists, citing K. K. Hickin. This existence assertion is retained as source background and has not been independently verified. The registry labels the countably infinite existence question as open; that label is supplied curation, not an independently verified literature assessment.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does there exist a countably infinite group in which every finitely generated subgroup is finite, the centre is trivial, and every automorphism is conjugation by a group element? The supplied source attributes the question to B. Hartley and reports that an uncountable example exists.
