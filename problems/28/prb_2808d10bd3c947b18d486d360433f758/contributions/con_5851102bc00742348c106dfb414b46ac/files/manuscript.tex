\section{A Tracy-Widom Central Limit Theorem}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


\paragraph{Definitions and notation.}Let $(a_i)_{i\ge1}$ be independent identically distributed random variables. The ordinary central limit theorem studies centered, rescaled sums. Deift instead asks for probabilistically natural operations yielding random-matrix limits. The Gaudin law describes a random-matrix spacing distribution; Tracy--Widom laws describe edge fluctuations in the corresponding symmetry classes. Finding a useful intrinsic operation includes specifying its inputs, scaling and admissible laws.

\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}
Characterize random-matrix statistics in purely probabilistic terms: identify a natural operation $X$ on i.i.d. input $(a_1,a_2,\ldots)$ producing normalized variables $(X_n)$ whose distributions tend to the Gaudin or Tracy--Widom law. Determine conditions and normalization giving a meaningful universality principle, rather than just forcing the desired law by an arbitrary transformation.
\paragraph{Scope and source qualification.}The original asks What is X rather than fixing last-passage percolation as the only operation. The Gaudin target as well as Tracy–Widom is retained. It is a characterization programme, not a statement that every i.i.d. law has such an edge limit without hypotheses.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Find a probabilistic analogue of the central limit theorem whose limit is a random-matrix spacing or edge law.

\paragraph{Testing a natural max-plus operation.}Arrange iid inputs into an \(n\times n\) array \(w_{ij}\), and define \(L_n\) as the maximum sum along up-right paths from \((1,1)\) to \((n,n)\). Every such path contains exactly \(2n-1\) vertices. This is a probabilistically intrinsic candidate operation, unlike applying an inverse target distribution function. For two arrays with \(\max_{ij}|w_{ij}-\widetilde w_{ij}|\le\delta\), every path sum changes by at most \((2n-1)\delta\). Taking maxima in both directions proves
\[|L_n(w)-L_n(\widetilde w)|\le(2n-1)\delta.
\]
Thus a uniform coupling perturbation is negligible at a candidate edge scale \(n^{1/3}\) only under \(\delta_n=o(n^{-2/3})\) by this bound. It proves no robustness for a fixed changed distribution.
\paragraph{A necessary restriction on input laws.}If \(w_{ij}=c\) almost surely, then \(L_n=(2n-1)c\) deterministically; no deterministic centering and positive scaling can produce a nondegenerate limiting law. Thus an unrestricted assertion for every iid input is impossible. An affine transformation \(w\mapsto aw+b\), \(a>0\), gives \(L_n\mapsto aL_n+b(2n-1)\) exactly, so any meaningful universality statement must absorb the resulting model-dependent center and scale.
\paragraph{Gap.}The calculation supplies neither the correct centering and scale for a general nondegenerate law nor a proof of a Tracy--Widom limit. The Gaudin spacing target is also distinct and has not been generated by this one scalar maximum. One must prove fluctuation estimates and convergence for a declared class of inputs, then explain the operation's probabilistic characterization. This elementary sensitivity calculation is a failed route to fixed-law universality, not a new central limit theorem for random-matrix statistics.
