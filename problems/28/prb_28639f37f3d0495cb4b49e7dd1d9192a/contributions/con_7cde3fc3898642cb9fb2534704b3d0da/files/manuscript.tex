\section{Research attempt: Finite mixture support does not make the continuous search disappear}
Choose a compact metric parameter space $\Theta$ and positive continuous kernels
$k_i(\theta)>0$ for $N$ fixed observations. A Borel mixing probability measure $\mu$
produces $z_i=\int k_i(\theta)\,d\mu$, with log likelihood $\sum_i\log z_i$.
Let $K=\{(k_1(\theta),\ldots,k_N(\theta)):\theta\in\Theta\}$.
Every finite convex combination of more than $N+1$ points can be shortened:
their augmented vectors $(v,1)$ are linearly dependent, so adjust weights along
a nonzero dependence until one first becomes zero, preserving both their sum
and the vector. Repeat. Therefore the convex hull is the image of compact
$\Theta^{N+1}$ times a compact weight simplex, and is compact.
Uniform continuity approximates any integral by finite sums over a finite
partition of $\Theta$ (equivalently a finite cover with a measurable partition),
so its vector lies in this closed convex hull. Conversely each convex
combination is attained by an atomic measure.

The continuous positive likelihood attains its maximum on that compact set;
positivity is bounded away from zero by compactness. At a maximizer $z_*$,
its gradient is $g_i=1/z_{*,i}$. Differentiating toward every $v\in K$ gives
$g\cdot v\le g\cdot z_*=N$. In an atomic representation of $z_*$, every
positive-weight support point must have equality, since the weighted average
equals $N$. All such points lie in one affine hyperplane of dimension at most
$N-1$. The same linear-dependence shortening argument therefore leaves at
most $N$ atoms. This is a complete elementary proof of finite support in this
declared likelihood problem, not a claim about every nonparametric model.

The attempted computational shortcut restricts atoms to a fixed finite grid.
Already for one observation $y=1/2$, Gaussian location kernel
$k(\theta)=(2\pi)^{-1/2}e^{-(y-\theta)^2/2}$ and $\Theta=[0,1]$, the
optimal atom is at $1/2$. A grid containing only zero and one gives likelihood
smaller by the factor $e^{-1/8}$. Thus finite support is not finite known support.
The global inequality $g\cdot k(\theta)\le N$ remains a continuous
optimization certificate. It is algebraic only under additional kernel and
domain assumptions that are not present in the source's nonparametric agenda.
