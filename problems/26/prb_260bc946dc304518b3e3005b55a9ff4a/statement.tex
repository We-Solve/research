\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(a,\sigma>0\) and \(b,x\geq0\). Let \(W\) be a standard real Brownian motion, and let \(X\) be the nonnegative Cox--Ingersoll--Ross process satisfying
\[
X_t=x+\int_0^t(a-bX_s)\,\dd s+\int_0^t\sigma\sqrt{X_s}\,\dd W_s.
\]
The stochastic integral is understood in the It\^o sense. The parameter range includes \(x=0\) and \(b=0\); no condition excluding attainment of the boundary \(0\) is imposed.

For an explicit interpretation of the approximation question, fix a deterministic terminal time \(T>0\). For \(N\in\N\), define the optimal absolute-mean error from equidistant Brownian observations by
\[
e_N(T;a,b,\sigma,x)
:=\inf_{\substack{\varphi:\R^N\to\R\\ \varphi\text{ Borel measurable}}}
\E\!\left[\left|X_T-\varphi\bigl(W_{T/N},W_{2T/N},\ldots,W_T\bigr)\right|\right].
\]
The approximation map may depend on the fixed parameters and on \(N\). One precise convention for its optimal algebraic convergence order is
\[
\rho(T;a,b,\sigma,x)
:=\sup\left\{r\geq0:\sup_{N\geq1}N^r e_N(T;a,b,\sigma,x)<\infty\right\}.
\]
Under these explicit conventions, the question is whether, for every fixed \(T>0\), \(a,\sigma>0\), and \(b,x\geq0\),
\[
\rho(T;a,b,\sigma,x)
=\min\left\{\frac{2a}{\sigma^2},1\right\}.
\]
Here the quantifiers concern each fixed parameter choice; constants in error estimates may depend on those parameters and on \(T\).
