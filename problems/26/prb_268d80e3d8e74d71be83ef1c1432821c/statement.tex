\section{Accessibility of the critical point in a Cremer Julia set}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For a polynomial $f$ of degree at least two, $f^n$ is its $n$-fold iterate, $K(f)=\{z\in\mathbb C:(f^n(z))_{n\geq0}\text{ is bounded}\}$ is its filled Julia set, and $J(f)=\partial K(f)$ is its Julia set. A space is locally connected if each point has a neighbourhood basis of connected sets. In Milnor's terminology here, a Cremer polynomial is $P_\alpha(z)=z^2+\lambda z$, where $\lambda=e^{2\pi i\alpha}$, $\alpha\in\mathbb R\setminus\mathbb Q$, and no holomorphic change of coordinate near $0$ conjugates $P_\alpha$ to $w\mapsto\lambda w$. Its unique critical point is $-\lambda/2$. A point $x\in J(f)$ is accessible from $\mathbb C\setminus J(f)$ if there is a continuous curve $\eta:[0,1]\to\mathbb C$ with $\eta([0,1))\subset\mathbb C\setminus J(f)$ and $\eta(1)=x$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Can the critical point $-\lambda/2$ of a Cremer polynomial $P_\alpha$ be accessible from $\mathbb C\setminus J(P_\alpha)$? \sref{statement}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can a curve outside a Cremer quadratic Julia set reach its critical point?
