\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(p\) be a prime and let \(k\) be a field of characteristic \(p\). In the symmetric-group setting indicated by the supplied workshop provenance, write \(\mathfrak S_n\) for the symmetric group on \(n\) letters. For a partition \(\lambda\vdash n\), let \(S_k^\lambda\) be the Specht \(k\mathfrak S_n\)-module: inside the permutation module on \(\lambda\)-tabloids, it is the submodule spanned by the polytabloids
\[
e_t=\sum_{\sigma\in C_t}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\,\sigma\{t\},
\]
where \(t\) ranges over tableaux of shape \(\lambda\), \(\{t\}\) is its row tabloid, and \(C_t\) is its column stabilizer.

A module is indecomposable if it is nonzero and is not a direct sum of two nonzero submodules. For a finite group \(G\) and an indecomposable finite-dimensional \(kG\)-module \(M\), a vertex is a \(p\)-subgroup \(Q\leq G\), minimal under inclusion among those for which \(M\) is isomorphic to a direct summand of
\[
\operatorname{Ind}_Q^G\operatorname{Res}_Q^G M.
\]
Vertices are conjugate in \(G\), so whether a vertex is abelian is independent of its choice. The trivial subgroup is allowed as an abelian vertex.

[Q,Q]=\{1\}.
\]
