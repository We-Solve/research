\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
In the customary boundary convention, let $\Sigma_g^b$ be a compact connected oriented genus-$g$ surface with $b\ge1$ boundary components, and let $\operatorname{Mod}_g^b$ denote orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms fixing the boundary pointwise, modulo isotopy fixing the boundary. Let $T_c$ be the right, or positive, Dehn twist about a permitted essential simple closed curve $c$. The excerpt does not explicitly define its superscript-$b$ convention or say whether boundary-parallel, separating, or only nonseparating curves are allowed; these must be checked.

For a fixed mapping class $f$ known to be a product of right twists, define its positive factorization length set by
\[
 \mathcal L^+(f)=\{N\in\Z_{\ge0}:f=T_{c_1}\cdots T_{c_N}\text{ with every }T_{c_i}\text{ an allowed right Dehn twist}\}.
\]
Repetition of curves is permitted, and there are no inverse twists in these factorizations. The set is nonempty by hypothesis.

\textbf{Problem 2.9.} Is $\mathcal L^+(f)$ bounded above for every such $f\in\operatorname{Mod}_g^b$ with $b\ge1$? Equivalently, does there exist a finite constant $C_f$, allowed to depend on $f$, such that
\[
 f=T_{c_1}\cdots T_{c_N}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad N\le C_f
\]
for every positive factorization of that same $f$? The bound is over all factorizations, not only a chosen factorization or a shortest one, and no uniform constant over all mapping classes is requested. Null-homotopic curves cannot be included without qualification, since inserting identity twists would change length trivially. The allowed essential-curve convention is therefore material to the question.
\subsection{Short English statement}
For a fixed mapping class on a surface with at least one boundary component, can lengths of all its positive Dehn-twist factorizations be bounded by a constant depending on that mapping class? The types of allowed twisting curves must be fixed.
