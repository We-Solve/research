\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Fix positive integers $d,n$. Let $P_1,\ldots,P_n\subset\R^d$ be convex $d$-polytopes, so each is full-dimensional, with $v_i=f_0(P_i)$ vertices. Their Minkowski sum is
\[
 P=P_1+\cdots+P_n
  =\{x_1+\cdots+x_n:x_i\in P_i\}.
\]
For $0\leq k\leq d-1$, let $f_k(P)$ be the number of distinct $k$-dimensional faces of $P$. The requested optimal bound can be expressed, for every feasible vertex-count tuple, as
\[
 B_{d,n,k}(v_1,\ldots,v_n)
 =\max\left\{f_k(Q_1+\cdots+Q_n):
       Q_i\subset\R^d\text{ convex }d\text{-polytope},\ f_0(Q_i)=v_i\right\}.
\]
The problem is to determine these sharp upper bounds in terms of the individual vertex numbers, together with constructions proving sharpness in the intended sense. Feasible means that full-dimensional polytopes with the specified counts exist; no assertion is made for an empty parameter class.

The range includes every face dimension below $d$ and arbitrary numbers of summands. It is not restricted to two summands, $n\leq d$, simplicial/simple polytopes or a general-position subclass. Faces are counted in the sum itself, not by potentially repeated decompositions into summand faces. A formula sharp separately for each $k$ and a single configuration simultaneously attaining all bounds are different requirements; the source does not explicitly demand the latter or clarify exact versus asymptotic sharpness, so no additional simultaneous-extremizer condition is inserted.
