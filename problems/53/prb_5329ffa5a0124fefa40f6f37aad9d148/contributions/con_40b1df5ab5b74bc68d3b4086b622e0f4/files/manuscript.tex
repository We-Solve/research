\subsection{Attempt: interpreting an authenticity flag}
I tested whether a detector with apparently favorable sensitivity establishes authorship when a submission is flagged. Let $U$ indicate unauthorized production under a separately declared rule, with prevalence $\pi$. Suppose the flag $F$ has sensitivity $s=P(F\mid U)$ and false-positive rate $f=P(F\mid U^c)$. These are hypothetical population calibration quantities, not measurements of an actual detector or student.
\begin{proposition}
Provided $P(F)>0$, the unauthorized fraction among flagged submissions is
\[
P(U\mid F)=\frac{\pi s}{\pi s+(1-\pi)f}.
\]
For any fixed $s>0$ and $f>0$, it tends to zero as $\pi\to0$, regardless of the sensitivity's size.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The flagged-and-unauthorized mass is $P(U\cap F)=\pi s$. The flagged-and-authorized mass is $(1-\pi)f$. Their sum is $P(F)$ because the two cases partition all flagged submissions. Dividing the first mass by their sum gives the formula. With fixed positive $f$, the numerator tends to zero and denominator to $f$ as $\pi\to0$, proving the limit.
\end{proof}
\subsection{Verification and remaining gaps}
At $\pi=1/100$, $s=9/10$ and $f=1/20$, the positive predictive value is $2/13$, approximately $15.38\%$. Thus most flags would concern authorized submissions in this hypothetical population. Sensitivity alone cannot support the inference that a particular flag establishes unauthorized authorship.
