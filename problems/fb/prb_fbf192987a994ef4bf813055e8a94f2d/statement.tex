\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $(V,0)$ be an isolated complex singularity germ of complex dimension $m\geq2$, embedded in some $\C^N$. Its small link $L=V\cap S^{2N-1}_\varepsilon$ is a real $(2m-1)$-manifold, with canonical contact structure $\xi=TL\cap J(TL)$. The source studies analytic lattice cohomology $\mathcal L^*_{\mathrm{an}}(V,0)$, under the restrictions required by its construction, and asks for a nonanalytic contact/Floer target determined by $(L,\xi)$ rather than by the analytic germ.

The requested package is: construct that target and a version of embedded contact homology in these dimensions; assign a graded $\Z[U]$-module $\mathcal E^*(L,\xi)$; construct a natural graded morphism
\[
\iota_{V}:\mathcal L^*_{\mathrm{an}}(V,0)\longrightarrow\mathcal E^*(L,\xi);
\]
prove $\iota_V$ injective; and, for a fixed diffeomorphism/contact type $(L,\xi)$ realizable by a singularity, characterize exactly which graded sub-$\Z[U]$-modules arise as $\operatorname{im}\iota_V$ as the analytic germ varies over those inducing it. The first clause of the source calls for a ``natural functor'' to a nonanalytic theory; it does not specify its category of objects or morphisms. The symbols for an unknown nonanalytic theory and higher-dimensional ECH denote objects to be constructed, not existing standard theories asserted here. No unspecified ``certain properties'' are presented as a characterization already known.

For complex normal surface singularities with rational homology sphere links, the background describes the established comparison setting. The topological lattice theory of a connected negative-definite plumbing graph has
\[
\mathcal L^*_{\mathrm{top}}=\bigoplus_{q\geq0}\mathcal L^q_{\mathrm{top}},
\]
with an internal $2\Z$ grading, a $\Z[U]$ action and a spin$^c$ indexing; the analytic theory has analogous gradings and a graded morphism to the topological theory. The analytically distinguished canonical spin$^c$ part extends to higher complex dimensions subject to additional assumptions, whose exact form is not supplied. The general higher-dimensional target must not silently extend all surface spin$^c$ statements to every germ.

The notes credit Némethi [Ném05, Ném08] and earlier Ozsváth--Szabó calculations [OS03b] with the topological theory, Ágoston--Némethi [ÁN21a, ÁN21b] with analytic versions, and [OSS14b] with supporting comparison evidence. They describe the lattice/Heegaard-Floer comparison as announced while giving Zemke's Duke 2025 publication; the triage explicitly reports it proved for tree plumbings. Through the matching versions of Heegaard Floer and embedded contact homology [Hut10, Hut14, CGH20], this supplies the expected surface-case Floer target, not the requested arbitrary-dimensional construction.

The background emphasizes that a higher-dimensional smooth link alone may retain too little analytic information, even when the link is a standard sphere. Contact structure is essential extra data in that setting. It reports that in the three-dimensional singularity-link case the canonical contact structure is determined by the oriented link [CNPP06], whereas higher-dimensional contact enhancement need not be redundant. Colin--Honda--Tian [CHT24] provide a higher-dimensional Heegaard-Floer/contact framework, not the full higher-dimensional ECH/module-map package sought here.

The supplied literature triage, checked 2026-08-17, labels the problem partially solved and says none of the checked sources constructs the requested general ECH module with its analytic-lattice map, proves its injectivity or characterizes all image submodules. The primary registry label is ``open''. All new objects above are explicit construction goals; no construction or comparison proof is claimed.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Find a contact/Floer cohomology target for higher-dimensional singularity links, inject analytic lattice cohomology into it naturally, and characterize the images of germs with one fixed contact link. The supplied source knows analytic theories and three-dimensional comparisons, but not this full higher-dimensional package.
