\begin{abstract}
This manuscript gives an attempted answer for each of the 330 supplied catalogue
entries. It preserves the original numbering and states the target beside the
attempt, distinguishing a theorem about a specified model from resolution of a
biological research target. The results consist of worked special cases,
identifiability obstructions, quantitative bounds, and explicitly preliminary
reductions. None is claimed to resolve its complete catalogue target. No new
biological experiments, empirical data analyses, or external proof verification
were performed for this manuscript.
\end{abstract}

A complete proof for a narrower model does not earn 100\% for the original

\section{Gene-drive population dynamics}
\label{prob:174}
\noindent\textbf{Catalogue ID: 174.}\quad \textbf{Status: restricted result or obstruction; full target not established.}
\subsection{Target and scope}
\paragraph{Catalogue definitions and assumptions.}
Let $n_{a,p}(t)$ be adults of genotype class $a$ in patch $p$ at generation $t$, where classes distinguish drive-bearing, susceptible and resistant inherited states. A finite life-cycle map $F_\theta$ specifies inheritance probabilities, genotype viability, density-dependent reproduction and patch movement. Parameters $\theta$ include uncertainty measured in contained studies and bounded seasonal demographic variation. No genotype class is assumed to retain its initial frequency. Define target patches $\mathcal P_T$, non-target patches $\mathcal P_O$, and a finite monitoring horizon $H$. Uncertainty regions use a specified initial frequency distribution and known patch capacities. Counts in an empty patch have zero rather than undefined frequency by convention. Resistance states must be observed or explicitly latent; their omission cannot be justified by initial rarity alone.
\paragraph{Original target.}
Determine parameter regions where prediction of population effects and geographic containment meets declared criteria:
\[
 \Pr_\theta\!\left(\max_{t\le H}\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_O}
 f_{\mathrm{drive},p}(t)\le\varepsilon\right)\ge1-\delta,
 \qquad
 \E_\theta\!\left[\sum_{p\in\mathcal P_T}N_p(H)\right]\le N_0.
\]
Here $f_{\mathrm{drive},p}$ is drive frequency and $N_p$ total adults. Assess whether these bounds survive resistance and demographic uncertainty rather than supplying a single nominal trajectory.
\paragraph{Catalogue alternative formulations.}
(i) For a deterministic single well-mixed patch analyze the Jacobian invasion condition as a benchmark. (ii) For spatial finite populations include stochastic extinction and recolonization, then distinguish replacement-frequency objectives from population-suppression objectives; they need not coincide.
\subsection{Attempted answer}
\begin{attemptresult}
For any finite-population frequency process satisfying the checked condition $\E[p_{t+1}\mid\mathcal F_t]\leq p_t$ and $p_t\geq0$, the probability of reaching frequency $q>p_0$ by time $T$ is at most $p_0/q$. This is a containment bound only for models with nonpositive conditional drift.
\end{attemptresult}
\begin{proof}
Stop the nonnegative supermartingale at first crossing of $q$ or at $T$. Optional stopping for this bounded stopping time gives expected stopped value at most $p_0$. On the crossing event it is at least $q$, yielding the inequality.
\end{proof}
\subsection{What remains unresolved}
The supermartingale condition is not generally true for gene-drive systems and must not be assumed from initial rarity. Resistance, spatial migration and ecological feedback require explicit risk-model validation; no deployment parameters are derived.
\subsection{Completion Estimate}
\textbf{20\%.} The displayed calculation is established under its additional assumptions. The broader theoretical and biological requirements listed above are not established.

\section{Shared mathematical bounds}\label{app:bounds}
\begin{sharedlemma}[Independent bounded sums]\label{lem:bounded}
Let independent real variables $X_i$ satisfy $a_i\leq X_i\leq b_i$ almost surely,
and let $S=\sum_{i=1}^n(X_i-\E X_i)$. If
$V=\sum_i(b_i-a_i)^2>0$, then for every $t>0$,
\[
 \Prob(|S|\geq t)\leq 2\exp(-2t^2/V).
\]
In particular, independent losses in $[0,1]$ have mean-error bound
$\Prob(|\overline X-\E\overline X|\geq\epsilon)
\leq2e^{-2n\epsilon^2}$. The same expression for variables in $[-1,1]$
is $2e^{-n\epsilon^2/2}$. If $V=0$ then $S=0$ almost surely.
\end{sharedlemma}
\begin{proof}
For $X\in[a,b]$ with mean $\mu$, the nonnegative product
$(X-a)(b-X)$ gives
$\Var X\leq(\mu-a)(b-\mu)\leq(b-a)^2/4$.
Let $K(s)=\log\E\exp[s(X-\E X)]$.
Boundedness permits two differentiations. Its second derivative is the variance
under the exponentially tilted law, which has the same support, so
$K''(s)\leq(b-a)^2/4$ for every real $s$.
Because $K(0)=K'(0)=0$, integration yields
$K(s)\leq s^2(b-a)^2/8$.
Independence adds these log moment-generating bounds for $S$.
For $s>0$, Markov's inequality gives
$\Prob(S\geq t)\leq\exp(-st+s^2V/8)$.
Choosing $s=4t/V$ yields the upper-tail bound $e^{-2t^2/V}$.
Apply it also to $-S$ and add. The stated mean versions follow by substituting
$t=n\epsilon$ and the respective support widths.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Finite-family extension.}
For $M$ prespecified bounded-loss comparisons, apply the relevant inequality to
each and use a union bound. Independence between comparisons is not required;
independence of the biological units within each comparison is. Repeated cells,
reads or time points from one dependent biological unit cannot automatically be
counted as independent units.

\paragraph{No data-dependent shortcut.}
The bound does not justify choosing a model or validation loss using the same
outcomes and then reporting single-model coverage. A prespecified finite family,
a separate validation sample, or an appropriate complexity or sequential argument
is needed. Marginal predictive coverage likewise should not be read as coverage
for every subgroup or under arbitrary distribution shift.
