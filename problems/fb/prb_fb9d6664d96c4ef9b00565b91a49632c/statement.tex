\textbf{Problem 4.2 (V. S\'os).} Let $a_1<a_2<\cdots$ be an infinite integer sequence, $A_n=\{a_1,\ldots,a_n\}$, and suppose one fixed constant $C$ satisfies
\[
 q_n:=|A_n+A_n|<Cn\qquad\text{for every }n\geq1.
\]
What can be said about the whole sequence's structure? The source does not specify a proposed classification. The trailing ``PALO ALTO PROBLEMS 11'' is a page header.

\textbf{Reported theorem below three.} More generally let $K=\limsup_{n\to\infty}q_n/n<3$. The aid proves that
\[
 d_n=\gcd(a_2-a_1,\ldots,a_n-a_1)\quad(n\geq2)
\]
stabilizes to $d=\gcd(A-A)>0$. Using the Lev--Smeliansky reduced-length theorem after stabilization, it obtains
\[
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_n-a_1}{dn}\leq K-1.
\]
Thus for $B=(A-a_1)/d\subset\Z_{\geq0}$ the lower natural density satisfies
\[
 \liminf_{X\to\infty}\frac{|B\cap[0,X]|}{\lfloor X\rfloor+1}\geq\frac1{K-1}.
\]
The elementary lower bound $q_n\geq2n-1$ ensures $K\geq2$. This is density after removing the ambient lattice spacing, not a bound independent of $d$ in the original integers.

For the uniform source hypothesis with $C<3$, the consequence is normalized lower density at least $1/(C-1)$, whenever such a sequence exists. If the uniform strict constant is $C=2$, integrality forces $q_n=2n-1$ for every prefix and the sequence is an infinite arithmetic progression. This conclusion uses the uniform bound, not merely the weaker asymptotic assertion $K=2$.

\textbf{Reported limits to stronger conclusions.} Irrational Beatty sequences show that periodicity need not follow even below three. For a concrete compatible example, $a_n=\lfloor\sqrt2\,n\rfloor$ has $q_n\leq2\lfloor\sqrt2 n\rfloor-1<2\sqrt2\,n$, while its irrational density prevents eventual periodicity. The aid also cites published Dubickas--Sarka examples showing that a positive-density conclusion fails for unrestricted constants $C$; their exact construction, constants and density convention are not printed, so no stronger general assertion is inferred.

The result is a sub-three global density theorem and structural obstruction, not a classification for all fixed doubling constants.
