# All right-angled Artin groups embed faithfully in closed surface mapping class groups

**Problem:** TOP-2777

**Claim type:** Literature report

**Exact scope:** Every Artin group with all off-diagonal labels in {2,∞} admits a faithful embedding into Mod(S_g) for some closed oriented surface, with vertex generators mapped to powers of Dehn twists.

## A whole Artin class with faithful representations

For a finite Coxeter matrix having $m_{ij}\in\{2,\infty\}$, put an edge between $i,j$ exactly when $m_{ij}=2$. Its Artin group is the right-angled Artin group

$$A(\Gamma)=\langle v_i\mid[v_i,v_j]=1\text{ for }ij\in E(\Gamma)\rangle.$$

Koberda's Proposition7.11, together with its closed-surface construction, states that every such group embeds into the mapping class group of some **closed** oriented surface, with the vertex images positive powers of Dehn twists. This is a faithful embedding of the displayed whole group, so it answers the problem on all right-angled Artin groups, not merely on their finite-index subgroups.

## The theorem that rules out hidden relations

The relevant high-powers theorem is Koberda's Theorem1.1. In its Dehn-twist case, let $c_1,\ldots,c_k$ be distinct essential isotopy classes of simple closed curves. Form their disjointness graph: an edge means the two curves can be made disjoint. For a sufficiently large common exponent $N$, the assignment

$$v_i\longmapsto T_{c_i}^N$$

identifies the corresponding RAAG with the subgroup generated by those twists. The irredundancy hypothesis is essential: repeated isotopy classes cannot represent independent vertex generators. Distinct curve twists satisfy the needed independence on commuting subcollections. The theorem asserts injectivity for **all** group words at one chosen sufficiently large exponent, rather than only checking that the defining commuting relations hold.

Proposition7.11 includes the geometric realization needed to use this theorem for an arbitrary finite graph: one can choose a sufficiently high genus closed surface and distinct curves with exactly that disjointness graph. Its proof describes building the required curve configuration and applying the high-powers theorem. We import that proposition, including this realization, rather than assume that every graph occurs on a single fixed genus surface. Genus is permitted to depend on $\Gamma$ in the literal question.

The resulting map is well-defined because twists about disjoint curves commute. Its injectivity is precisely the substantive imported part. One cannot establish it solely by sending nonedges to intersecting curves: such a check would exclude a few pairwise relations but would not exclude longer relations. The source uses its ping-pong argument and RAAG normal forms to prove that latter assertion.

## Consequences and boundaries

The construction includes all finite-rank free groups (edgeless graphs), all finite-rank free abelian groups (complete graphs), and arbitrary mixtures of commuting and noncommuting pairs. It gives explicit types of generator images within a sufficiently large mapping class group, even though the original question imposes no such restriction. It also applies to disconnected graphs without a separate finite-type Coxeter assumption.

This does not classify general Artin matrices containing labels $3,4,\ldots$. In particular, the relation $sts=tst$ is different from $[s,t]=1$; a high-power construction for a commuting graph cannot be reinterpreted as a representation of an arbitrary Artin presentation. Nor is the mere existence of a virtually special finite-index subgroup substituted for embedding the original Artin group. The exact scope here is the entire right-angled class and its faithful whole-group embeddings.
