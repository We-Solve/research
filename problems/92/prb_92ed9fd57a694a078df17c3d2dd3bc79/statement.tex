\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
In the usual noetherian normal Cohen--Macaulay local setting, let \((R,\mathfrak m_R)\) have Krull dimension \(3\). A finitely generated \(R\)-module \(M\) is reflexive if the natural map
\[
M\longrightarrow \operatorname{Hom}_R(\operatorname{Hom}_R(M,R),R)
\]
is an isomorphism. Write \(\operatorname{add}_R(M)\) for the finitely generated modules isomorphic to direct summands of finite direct sums of copies of \(M\). A nonzero reflexive module \(M\) is modifying if \(\operatorname{End}_R(M)\) is maximal Cohen--Macaulay as an \(R\)-module, meaning that its depth is \(\dim R\). It is maximal modifying if it is modifying and, for every finitely generated reflexive \(R\)-module \(N\),
\[
\operatorname{End}_R(M\oplus N)\text{ is maximal Cohen--Macaulay over }R
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
N\in\operatorname{add}_R(M).
\]
An algebra of the form \(\operatorname{End}_R(M)\) for such a maximal modifying module is called a maximal modifying algebra.
