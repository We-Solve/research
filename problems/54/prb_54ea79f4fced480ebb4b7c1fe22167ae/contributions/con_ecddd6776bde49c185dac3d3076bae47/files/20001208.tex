\section{Research attempt: Raw invariant magnitude is not an intrinsic efficacy criterion}
If a polynomial $f$ vanishes on a model, then $cf$ for any nonzero constant
$c$ and $f^2$ have the same zero set. A ranking by $|f(\widehat p)|$ alone
can therefore be changed arbitrarily by rescaling a generator. For a concrete
independence model on a binary pair, take
$f=p_{00}p_{11}-p_{01}p_{10}$ and the family
\[
 p_{00}=p_{11}=1/4+\delta,\quad
 p_{01}=p_{10}=1/4-\delta,\qquad |\delta|<1/4.
\]
Direct multiplication gives $f=\delta$ and $f^2=\delta^2$. The derivative of
$f^2$ at the independent point is zero, whereas that of $f$ along this family
is one. Thus applying the same first-order linear noise calibration to both
generators is invalid. This does not make $f^2$ intrinsically useless: a test
using a correctly calibrated squared statistic can retain the information in
$|f|$.

An exact finite-sample alternative in this particular family avoids asymptotic
claims. Encode observations as spins $Y_1,Y_2$, and set
$Z=Y_1Y_2\in\{-1,1\}$. Its expectation is $4\delta$ and variance
$1-16\delta^2$. For $N$ independent observations,
$\widehat r=N^{-1}\sum Z_i$ has mean $4\delta$ and variance
$(1-16\delta^2)/N$. Under the independence point $\delta=0$,
$\Pr(|\widehat r|\ge t)\le1/(Nt^2)$ by Markov on its square.
Under $|4\delta|>t$, failure to exceed that threshold has probability at most
$1/[N(|4\delta|-t)^2]$ by the centered variance bound.

These calculations associate usefulness with a specific alternative direction,
sample law, threshold and noise scale. Multiplying $\widehat r$ and its threshold
by the same nonzero magnitude changes neither decision nor these bounds.
They do not establish that the determinant invariant is best for every
phylogenetic alternative, and they do not attach biological meaning to a
polynomial merely because it belongs to a generating set.
