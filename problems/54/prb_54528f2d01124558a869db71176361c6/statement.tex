\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(n\geq 1\) and \(0\leq m<n\) be integers. Let \(A_1,\ldots,A_n\) be torsion-free groups, meaning that \(a^k=1\), for \(a\in A_i\) and an integer \(k\geq1\), implies \(a=1\). Set
\[
F=A_1*\cdots*A_n.
\]
Choose relators \(r_1,\ldots,r_m\in F\), let \(N=\langle\!\langle r_1,\ldots,r_m\rangle\!\rangle_F\) be their normal closure in \(F\), and put \(G=F/N\), with quotient map \(q:F\to G\). When \(m=0\), take \(N=\{1\}\).

For \(I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\), write \(F_I=*_{i\in I}A_i\), identified with its canonical subgroup of \(F\). The supplied problem asks whether, for every such choice of groups and relators, there exists \(I\) with \(|I|=n-m\) such that \(F_I\) embeds into \(G\).

The word ``embeds'' leaves a qualification unresolved. If the intended embedding is the natural map induced by the quotient, the precise question is whether
\[
\exists I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\},\qquad |I|=n-m,\qquad \ker(q|_{F_I})=F_I\cap N=\{1\}.
\]
If an arbitrary embedding is permitted, the question instead asks for such an \(I\) and an injective homomorphism \(\varphi:F_I\to G\), without requiring \(\varphi=q|_{F_I}\). The supplied wording does not explicitly distinguish these formulations.
\subsection{Short English statement}
After imposing \(m<n\) relations on a free product of \(n\) torsion-free groups, must the free product of some \(n-m\) factors embed in the resulting quotient? Whether the embedding must be induced by the quotient map needs clarification. The registry labels this problem open; that label is supplied metadata, not an independently verified assessment.
