Must \(A=\{n\geq2:\|\alpha n^2\|<1/\log n\}\), for irrational \(\alpha>0\), be an asymptotic additive basis of order two?
No. We construct an irrational \(\alpha\) for which infinitely many integers are absent from \(A+A\). The convention at \(n=1\) does not matter. The known negative resolution is due to Konieczny; see \url{https://www.erdosproblems.com/1147} and \url{https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.02410}. The elementary example below addresses the universal question, without claiming Konieczny's stronger almost-everywhere conclusions.
