\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.


A polyhedron is an intersection of finitely many closed affine half-spaces; a polytope is a bounded polyhedron. Its faces are its intersections with supporting hyperplanes.

A finite simplicial complex is a finite family of vertex subsets closed under taking subsets. Its dimension is one less than its largest simplex size. A piecewise-linear embedding is an injective map linear on simplices after finite subdivisions.

A continuous piecewise-linear function is continuous and agrees with an affine function on each member of a finite polyhedral partition. A difference-of-convex decomposition writes it as $g-h$ with both $g$ and $h$ convex.

\section{Attempt and known regimes rederived}
The target concerns embeddings after arbitrary finite subdivisions. A coordinate
search on the given vertices alone tests a stronger property. We first give
two elementary decidable regimes, then formulate exactly where this tempting
finite-coordinate route stops.

\paragraph{Explicit linear embedding into $\mathbb R^{2k+1}$.}
For a complex of dimension at most $k$ with $v$ vertices, assign distinct
integers $t=1,\ldots,v$ and place its vertices at
$p(t)=(t,t^2,\ldots,t^{2k+1})$. Every set of at most $2k+2$ such points
is affinely independent. Indeed the first $r$ columns of their augmented
coordinate matrix $(1,t,\ldots,t^{2k+1})$ have Vandermonde determinant
$\prod_{i<j}(t_j-t_i)\ne0$ for $r$ rows. This determinant formula follows
because the determinant, an alternating polynomial of total degree
$r(r-1)/2$, is divisible by every $t_j-t_i$; comparison of the coefficient
of $t_2t_3^2\cdots t_r^{r-1}$ gives multiplicative constant one.
For any two simplices $\sigma,\tau$, their union has at most $2k+2$
vertices. Uniqueness of affine coordinates on this independent set shows
that a point in both convex hulls has zero weights outside the intersection,
so the hulls intersect exactly in the common face. Each simplex is nondegenerate.
Linear extension on simplices is therefore continuous and injective.
A continuous injective map from compact $|K|$ to a Hausdorff space is an
embedding: images of closed subsets are compact and hence closed, so its
inverse on the image is continuous. This proves the claim. Append zero
coordinates for larger $d$.
For fixed $k$, the integer coordinates have $O(k\log(v+1))$ bits, giving
an explicit polynomial-size construction. This is the known general-position
regime stated in the report, not a new extension of it.

\paragraph{Complete graph-to-line criterion.}
A finite one-dimensional simplicial complex embeds in $\mathbb R$ exactly
when every connected component is a path or an isolated vertex. Necessity:
a vertex cannot have three incident edges, since three small incident arcs
on a line must place two on the same side, where continuity makes their
images overlap in an interval beyond the vertex. More explicitly, the image
of each incident interval contains every point between the vertex image
and a nearby distinct image point, so two such intervals cannot be disjoint
away from the vertex. A cycle also cannot embed: its compact connected image
would be a closed interval; remove the preimage of an interior point. The
cycle minus that point is connected, whereas the interval minus it is not,
contradicting the embedding homeomorphism. A finite graph of maximum degree
two without cycles has only path/isolated components: following successive
neighbors cannot branch or revisit a vertex and therefore exhausts a path
component. Conversely place each path in a separate interval with vertices
in path order and edges straight; place isolated vertices separately.
Degrees, cycle detection and component traversal give a finite decision method.
For zero-dimensional complexes in $\mathbb R^0$, at most one vertex is
allowed; in positive-dimensional Euclidean space all finite vertex sets embed.

\paragraph{The finite-coordinate proposal and its exact obstruction.}
For a specified finite complex $L$, use variables $p_v\in\mathbb R^d$.
For each pair of faces $\sigma,\tau$, quantify nonnegative barycentric
weights summing to one on each. Require that
\[
\sum_{v\in\sigma}\alpha_vp_v=\sum_{v\in\tau}\beta_vp_v
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\alpha_v=\beta_v\ \text{for all }v\in\sigma\cup\tau,
\]
where a weight outside its face is zero. Include pairs with $\sigma=\tau$
to enforce simplex injectivity. A coordinate assignment satisfies these
conditions exactly when its simplexwise linear map is injective, by the
uniqueness condition for the two represented domain points. Thus this is a
finite first-order formula over the reals for linear embeddability of $L$.
A decision procedure for real closed fields would decide that finite formula;
that general decision theorem is an external standard dependency, not proved
by the displayed encoding.
Putting $L=K$ cannot decide the stated PL problem: a PL map is allowed
new vertices in a subdivision, and the number of these variables is not
bounded by this formula. Even testing successively larger admissible
subdivisions would need a proven terminating bound or a separate certificate
for NO instances before becoming a decision algorithm. None is supplied.
The source itself distinguishes these notions and reports PL-versus-linear
examples, so treating the original-vertex test as equivalent would erase
an essential hypothesis rather than solve the question.
