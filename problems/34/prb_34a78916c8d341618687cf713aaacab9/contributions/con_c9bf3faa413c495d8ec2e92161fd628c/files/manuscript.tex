\subsection{Coordinate elimination for integer difference constraints}
Fix $n\ge2$, integer constants $c_{ij}$ for $i\ne j$, and
\[
 S=\{x\in\mathbb Z^n:x_i-x_j\le c_{ij}\text{ for all }i\ne j\}.
\]
This class has the usual set-level $L$-convex closure properties: translation by integer multiples of the all-one vector, and componentwise minimum and maximum. Translation preserves differences. If $x_i\le x_j+c$ and $y_i\le y_j+c$, monotonicity of minimum and maximum gives respectively $\min(x_i,y_i)\le\min(x_j,y_j)+c$ and the corresponding maximum inequality. Applying this to every constraint proves the assertion, including an empty set.
\begin{proposition}
Projection deleting coordinate $k$ consists exactly of the integer vectors on the other coordinates satisfying the old constraints and all inequalities
\[
 x_i-x_j\le c_{ik}+c_{kj}\qquad(i,j\ne k),
\]
where $i=j$ is allowed, so an inconsistent two-edge cycle is retained.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Any extension satisfies $x_i-x_k\le c_{ik}$ and $x_k-x_j\le c_{kj}$; adding proves necessity. Conversely define the integer lower and upper bounds
\[
 L=\max_{i\ne k}(x_i-c_{ik}),\qquad U=\min_{j\ne k}(x_j+c_{kj}).
\]
The displayed inequalities are exactly the pairwise comparisons ensuring $L\le U$. Choosing the integer $x_k=L$ satisfies every constraint involving $k$, and all others were assumed. Both extrema are over nonempty finite sets because $n\ge2$.
\end{proof}
\subsection{Failure of arbitrary linear images}
Even the $L$-convex set $\mathbb Z^2$ need not remain so under an arbitrary integral linear map. Its image under $(x,y)\mapsto(x,y,x+y)$ contains $(1,0,1)$ and $(0,1,1)$, but not their componentwise minimum $(0,0,1)$. Hence arbitrary image-taking does not supply an operation preserving these set axioms. Coordinate projection succeeds because its fibers are integer intervals governed by explicit compatible bounds.
