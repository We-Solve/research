\section{Ambient-Algebra Independence of Exchangeable Independence}\label{30001182}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 30001182 · Mathematical Physics}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


\paragraph{Definitions and conventions.} An algebraic probability space is a unital $*$-algebra $A$ with a positive normalized state $\phi$. Write $A^{*\,\mathbb N}$ for the unital free product of countably many copies, with canonical embeddings $j_n$. A state on it is exchangeable if invariant under every finite permutation of the copies, and extremal if not a nontrivial convex combination of exchangeable states. For $B,C\subseteq A$, the source's independence requires an extremal exchangeable state $\phi_\infty$ whose restriction to separate copies of $B$ and $C$ agrees with their original joint distribution under $\phi$.

\paragraph{Source scope and qualifications.} The comparison must retain the subalgebras, their states, and joint distribution while changing ambient embeddings. The question is not whether arbitrary embeddings preserve an unrelated probability law. The source itself identifies possible embedding dependence as unresolved.

\paragraph{Catalogue mathematical target.}
\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}
For the proposed notion of independence of subalgebras $B$ and $C$ contained in an algebra $A$ with state $\phi$, defined through an extremal exchangeable state on the infinite free product, is the dependence on the embedding algebra $A$ necessary?

\paragraph{Saved literature context.} The supplied catalogue's assessment dated 2026-08-21 records: The definition explicitly leaves dependence on the ambient embedding algebra as an open issue, and the general embedding-independence question remains unresolved. The assessment is retained as dated provenance; it is not a new comprehensive literature or open-status certification.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Determine whether the proposed exchangeability definition of independence intrinsically depends on the ambient algebra.
