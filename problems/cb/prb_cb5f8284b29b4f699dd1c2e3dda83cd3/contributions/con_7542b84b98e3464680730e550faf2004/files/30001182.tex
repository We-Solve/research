\section{Ambient-Algebra Independence of Exchangeable Independence}\label{30001182}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 30001182 · Mathematical Physics}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


\paragraph{Definitions and conventions.} An algebraic probability space is a unital $*$-algebra $A$ with a positive normalized state $\phi$. Write $A^{*\,\mathbb N}$ for the unital free product of countably many copies, with canonical embeddings $j_n$. A state on it is exchangeable if invariant under every finite permutation of the copies, and extremal if not a nontrivial convex combination of exchangeable states. For $B,C\subseteq A$, the source's independence requires an extremal exchangeable state $\phi_\infty$ whose restriction to separate copies of $B$ and $C$ agrees with their original joint distribution under $\phi$.

\paragraph{Source scope and qualifications.} The comparison must retain the subalgebras, their states, and joint distribution while changing ambient embeddings. The question is not whether arbitrary embeddings preserve an unrelated probability law. The source itself identifies possible embedding dependence as unresolved.

\paragraph{Catalogue mathematical target.}
\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}
For the proposed notion of independence of subalgebras $B$ and $C$ contained in an algebra $A$ with state $\phi$, defined through an extremal exchangeable state on the infinite free product, is the dependence on the embedding algebra $A$ necessary?

\paragraph{Why restriction of witnesses is delicate.} A natural route would restrict an extremal exchangeable state on a larger free product to a smaller one. Restriction is affine and preserves exchangeability, but affine maps need not preserve extreme points. Even ordinary state spaces show the obstruction: on $M_2(\mathbb C)$ the vector state of $v=(1,1)/\sqrt2$ is pure, while its restriction to the diagonal algebra is
\[
 \phi(\operatorname{diag}(a,b))=(a+b)/2,
\]
the nontrivial average of the two evaluation states.

\paragraph{Purity check without a classification theorem.} If $\phi=t\psi_1+(1-t)\psi_2$, $0<t<1$, positivity and $\phi(I-|v\rangle\langle v|)=0$ force each $\psi_i$ to vanish on that projection. Cauchy--Schwarz for a positive functional then kills all matrix entries touching its range. Since the complementary range is one-dimensional and each state is normalized, both $\psi_i=\phi$. This verifies extremality on the large matrix algebra, while the restricted state is visibly nonextremal.

\paragraph{What this does not show.} The example concerns extremality among all states, not extremality in the source's exchangeable-state simplex, so it is not an ambient-dependence counterexample. It invalidates only the automatic affine-restriction step. A successful proof must exploit exchangeability to construct or select an extremal extension preserving the prescribed joint distribution of $B$ and $C$. That is the first missing step; changing their joint law or replacing them with unrelated embeddings would answer a different question.
