\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(k\) be the intended field of characteristic \(p>0\), and let \(G\) be a finite group. Under the standard finite-dimensional module convention, \(kG\text{-}\mathrm{stab}\) has the same objects as the category of finite-dimensional left \(kG\)-modules, with morphisms
\[
 \underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{kG}(M,N)
 =\operatorname{Hom}_{kG}(M,N)\big/
 \{\text{maps factoring through a projective module}\}.
\]
The group algebra is self-injective, giving this stable category its usual triangulated structure, with suspension represented by inverse syzygy. This is not the ordinary abelian module category or its entire derived category.

A usual duality is \(D(M)=\operatorname{Hom}_k(M,k)\), with \((g\cdot f)(m)=f(g^{-1}m)\). A triangular Witt group requires a compatible triangulated duality, its biduality and sign conventions. In that framework \(W^i(kG\text{-}\mathrm{stab},D)\) is formed from the appropriate shifted symmetric self-dual objects modulo neutral forms admitting the relevant Lagrangian triangles. It is not simply the Grothendieck group of all modules or an unshifted Witt group of arbitrary bilinear forms.
