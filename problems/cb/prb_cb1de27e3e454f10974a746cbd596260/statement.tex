\section*{Periodic points of cellular automata}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Fix $d\ge1$ and a finite alphabet $A$. On $X=A^{\mathbb Z^d}$ with
the product topology, let $F:X\to X$ be a surjective cellular
automaton: for some finite neighborhood $D\subset\mathbb Z^d$
and rule $\varphi:A^D\to A$,
$(Fx)_v=\varphi((x_{v+u})_{u\in D})$ for every $v$.
A point is temporally periodic if $F^k x=x$ for some $k\ge1$.
It is spatially periodic if its orbit under all lattice translations
is finite, equivalently if a finite-index subgroup of $\mathbb Z^d$
fixes it. A jointly periodic point satisfies both properties.

The source asks three questions. Must the temporally periodic points
be dense in $X$? Must the jointly periodic points be dense? Must
the union of all minimal subsystems of $(X,F)$ be dense?
A minimal subsystem is a nonempty compact set $K\subset X$ with
$F(K)=K$ and no proper nonempty closed invariant subset;
equivalently, every forward orbit in $K$ is dense in $K$.

Density is tested against every nonempty finite-coordinate cylinder:
each finite pattern must extend to a point of the indicated type.
The temporal period, spatial period subgroup, or minimal set may
depend on the pattern. Surjectivity is global, on the full infinite
configuration space. It is not an assumption of injectivity or of
surjectivity on each finite periodic lattice separately.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every onto cellular automaton have dense periodic points, dense jointly periodic points, and dense minimal subsystems?
