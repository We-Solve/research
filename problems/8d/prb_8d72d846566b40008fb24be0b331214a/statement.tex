\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(F_2=\langle a,b\rangle\) be the free group on two generators. Define
\[
\operatorname{SL}_3(\C)=\{A\in M_3(\C):\det A=1\}.
\]
For a group homomorphism \(\rho:F_2\to\operatorname{SL}_3(\C)\), its character is the function \(\chi_\rho(w)=\operatorname{tr}(\rho(w))\), where \(\operatorname{tr}\) is the sum of the diagonal entries. Elements \(g,g'\in F_2\) are \(\operatorname{SL}_3(\C)\)-character equivalent if
\[
\operatorname{tr}(\rho(g))=\operatorname{tr}(\rho(g'))
\quad\text{for every group homomorphism }\rho:F_2\to\operatorname{SL}_3(\C).
\]
They are conjugate in \(F_2\) if \(g'=hgh^{-1}\) for some \(h\in F_2\).

The question is whether
\[
\exists g,g'\in F_2\quad
\left[
\begin{gathered}
\forall\rho\in\operatorname{Hom}(F_2,\operatorname{SL}_3(\C)),\quad
\operatorname{tr}(\rho(g))=\operatorname{tr}(\rho(g')),\\
\forall h\in F_2,\quad g'\ne hgh^{-1}.
\end{gathered}
\right]
\]
The quantifier ranges over all such representations, with no additional restriction. Do there exist two nonconjugate elements of the free group on two generators whose traces agree under every representation into \(\operatorname{SL}_3(\C)\)?
