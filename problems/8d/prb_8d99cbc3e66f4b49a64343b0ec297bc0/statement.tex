\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 701432}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a group \(H\), let \(Z(H)=\{z\in H:zh=hz\text{ for every }h\in H\}\) denote its centre. The group \(H\) is central-by-finite if \([H:Z(H)]<\infty\). Write \(H'=[H,H]\) and \(H''=[H',H']\); the group \(H\) is metabelian if \(H''=\{1\}\). A group is metabelian-by-finite if it has a normal metabelian subgroup of finite index. A group is soluble-by-finite if it has a normal subgroup of finite index whose derived series reaches the trivial subgroup after finitely many steps.

\textbf{Question (B. Amberg, 2014).} For every group \(G\) and subgroups \(A,B\leq G\) satisfying
\[
G=AB=\{ab:a\in A,\ b\in B\},\qquad [A:Z(A)]<\infty,\qquad [B:Z(B)]<\infty,
\]
does there exist a subgroup \(M\trianglelefteq G\) such that
\[
[G:M]<\infty\qquad\text{and}\qquad M''=\{1\}?
\]
No finiteness or finite generation assumption on \(G\), and no normality assumption on \(A\) or \(B\), is imposed.

The supplied source attributes to N. Chernikov a theorem that every such \(G\) is soluble-by-finite. This is supplied background, not an independently verified assertion here; the record does not give a separate citation for that theorem.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Must every group that is a product of two subgroups, each having centre of finite index, contain a normal metabelian subgroup of finite index? The supplied record labels this question open; that status has not been independently verified.
