\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a group \(G\), write \(\operatorname{Out}(G)=\operatorname{Aut}(G)/\operatorname{Inn}(G)\). Say it is virtually generated by Dehn twists if a subgroup generated by the intended twist classes has finite index. In a standard splitting \(G=A*_C B\), an element \(z\in C_A(C)\) gives a twist fixing \(A\) and conjugating \(B\) by \(z\), with analogous HNN twists. Infinite order must be measured in \(\operatorname{Out}(G)\), not inferred merely from the order of \(z\). The source does not specify every permitted edge-group or twist convention.

A CAT(0) group here has the usual geometric-action meaning: it acts properly and cocompactly by isometries on a proper CAT(0) geodesic metric space. No torsion-free hypothesis is imposed on the CAT(0) groups. The source attributes three related questions to Swarup:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Find a proof of Johannson's virtual Dehn-twist generation theorem for \(\operatorname{Out}(\pi_1M)\), for a Haken 3-manifold \(M\), along the lines of Rips--Sela's theorem for torsion-free one-ended hyperbolic groups. This asks for a proof approach, not whether the already named Johannson theorem is true.
\item Does the analogous virtual Dehn-twist generation assertion hold for CAT(0) groups?
\item In particular, is
\[
\forall G\text{ a CAT(0) group},\quad
|\operatorname{Out}(G)|=\infty
\Longrightarrow\exists\delta\in\operatorname{Out}(G)\text{ an intended Dehn twist of infinite order}?
\]
\end{enumerate}
The Haken/manifold boundary conventions are not reproduced; the usual orientable Haken setting includes irreducibility and a two-sided incompressible surface. No additional torsion-free or one-ended restriction is transferred from part (1) to parts (2)--(3).

\subsection{Short English statement}
Swarup asks for a Rips--Sela-style proof of Johannson's outer-automorphism theorem, its extension to CAT(0) groups, and an infinite-order twist whenever the outer automorphism group is infinite. The supplied summary reports a general CAT(0) counterexample, but its bibliographic details and the first part's truncated proof-method claim require review.
