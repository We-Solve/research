\section{Emergence of Isotropic Big-Bang Singularities}\label{30004801}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 30004801 · Mathematical Physics}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


\paragraph{Definitions and conventions.}
A Big-Bang singularity is a past boundary where the classical spacetime description ceases to be regular, often accompanied by curvature blowup or geodesic incompleteness. Homogeneity means that spatial slices have transitive symmetry; isotropy means rotational symmetry about spatial points. The Weyl tensor is the trace-free part of spacetime curvature and vanishes in a conformally flat homogeneous isotropic cosmological model. A mechanism or initial-data principle must explain why this special singular behavior arises from a wider class of data.

Within mathematical models of cosmological singularity formation, what mechanism or initial-data principle explains the emergence of a homogeneous, isotropic Big-Bang singularity with vanishing Weyl curvature from more general initial conditions?

\paragraph{Testing homogeneous damping toward the past.} In a homogeneous model with mean scale factor \(a(t)>0\), write \(H=\dot a/a\). Suppose a shear component \(\sigma\) and perfect-fluid density \(\rho>0\) satisfy the usual reduced evolution equations \(\dot\sigma+3H\sigma=0\), \(\dot\rho+3H(1+w)\rho=0\), for constant equation-of-state parameter \(w\). Integration, without a perturbative approximation, gives
\[
 \sigma=\sigma_0(a/a_0)^{-3},\qquad
 \rho=\rho_0(a/a_0)^{-3(1+w)},\qquad
 \frac{\sigma^2}{\rho}=\frac{\sigma_0^2}{\rho_0}(a/a_0)^{3(w-1)}.
\]
Thus for nonzero initial shear and \(w<1\), shear grows relative to matter as \(a\downarrow0\). For \(w=1\) the ratio is constant; for \(w>1\) it decays in this reduced calculation.
\paragraph{Mechanism audit.} Damping of shear during expansion therefore cannot simply be run backward to explain isotropic initial singularities. Exactly zero shear stays zero, but imposing it is a special-data condition rather than a dynamical explanation of emergence from general data. The equations were stated as assumptions of this homogeneous test; no existence or stability theorem for an inhomogeneous Einstein--matter solution follows.
