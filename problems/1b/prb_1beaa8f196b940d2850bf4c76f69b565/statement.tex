\section{Are there infinitely many betrothed numbers}\label{9400023}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 9400023 · Number Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For $n\in\mathbb N$, let $\sigma(n)=\sum_{d\mid n,\ d>0}d$ and $s(n)=\sigma(n)-n$. Distinct positive integers $m,n$ form a betrothed pair when $s(m)=n+1$ and $s(n)=m+1$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Are there infinitely many unordered betrothed pairs $\{m,n\}$? Equivalently, are infinitely many positive integers members of such pairs? \sref{9400023}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Are there infinitely many betrothed pairs?
