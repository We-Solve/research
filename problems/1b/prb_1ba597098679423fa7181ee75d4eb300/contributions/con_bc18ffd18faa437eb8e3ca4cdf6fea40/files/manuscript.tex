\section*{Tumour vaccinology}
\label{prob:258}
\noindent\textbf{Status: preliminary conditional reduction; full target not established.}
\subsection{Target and scope}
\paragraph{Catalogue definitions and assumptions.}
For a specified patient population let $\mathcal A$ be candidate antigens, $K$ a vaccine size limit, $p_j$ the prevalence of malignant clone $j$, and $r_{ijh}$ the probability that antigen $i$ yields effective recognition of clone $j$ in HLA stratum $h$. Let $q_i$ bound normal-tissue cross-reactivity from a defined assay panel. Antigen presentation, T-cell recognition and cytotoxicity are separate measured steps. All probabilities refer to a fixed platform and immune-adjuvant context; a predicted binding score is not itself a recognition probability.
\paragraph{Original target.}
Infer calibrated $r_{ijh}$ and select $S\subset\mathcal A$ with $|S|\leq K$ to maximize robust coverage:
\[
\min_{h\in\mathcal H}\sum_jp_j\left(1-\prod_{i\in S}(1-r_{ijh})\right),
\qquad \sum_{i\in S}q_i\leq q_{\max}.
\]
The product assumes conditionally independent recognition events; correlated responses require their joint model. Test whether increased predicted coverage reduces measured immune escape over a defined horizon.
\paragraph{Catalogue alternative formulations.}
(i) A personalized formulation optimizes each patient's antigen set using measured clonality and HLA genotype. (ii) A population vaccine seeks shared antigens under a worst-stratum criterion and manufacturing constraints. These are separate design objectives. A dynamic variant incorporates antigen loss and impaired presentation; static coverage is equivalent only when those processes are negligible.
\subsection{Attempted answer}
\noindent\emph{Preliminary step: the following worked reduction has not been shown to instantiate all assumptions of the original target. Its applicability is an outstanding proof obligation.}

\begin{proposition}
For a finite-type branching baseline with specified offspring generating functions $F_i$, extinction by generation $T$ from type $i$ equals $[F^{\circ T}(0)]_i$. Independent founder counts $n_i$ therefore give survival probability $1-\prod_i[F^{\circ T}(0)_i]^{n_i}$. A mean growth factor alone does not determine this probability.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Condition on the first offspring vector. Extinction by the horizon requires extinction of every offspring family by the preceding horizon, producing generating-function composition. Independent founder families multiply extinction probabilities. Different offspring distributions with equal means can have different generating functions and extinction probabilities.
\end{proof}
\subsection{What remains unresolved}
The offspring distributions, tissue constraints, detection process and relation between persistence and the clinical endpoint are not inferred. This is a conditional stochastic baseline, not a prediction for a patient or a therapeutic strategy.
\paragraph{First missing connection.} Specify an observation map, parameter correspondence or dynamical reduction connecting this worked model to the original formulation; prove that correspondence under the original assumptions before claiming that the proposition resolves a clause of the target.

\section{Shared mathematical bounds}\label{app:bounds}
\begin{lemma}[Independent bounded sums]\label{lem:bounded}
Let independent real variables $X_i$ satisfy $a_i\leq X_i\leq b_i$ almost surely,
and let $S=\sum_{i=1}^n(X_i-\mathbb{E} X_i)$. If
$V=\sum_i(b_i-a_i)^2>0$, then for every $t>0$,
\[
 \mathbb{P}(|S|\geq t)\leq 2\exp(-2t^2/V).
\]
In particular, independent losses in $[0,1]$ have mean-error bound
$\mathbb{P}(|\overline X-\mathbb{E}\overline X|\geq\epsilon)
\leq2e^{-2n\epsilon^2}$. The same expression for variables in $[-1,1]$
is $2e^{-n\epsilon^2/2}$. If $V=0$ then $S=0$ almost surely.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $X\in[a,b]$ with mean $\mu$, the nonnegative product
$(X-a)(b-X)$ gives
$\operatorname{Var} X\leq(\mu-a)(b-\mu)\leq(b-a)^2/4$.
Let $K(s)=\log\mathbb{E}\exp[s(X-\mathbb{E} X)]$.
Boundedness permits two differentiations. Its second derivative is the variance
under the exponentially tilted law, which has the same support, so
$K''(s)\leq(b-a)^2/4$ for every real $s$.
Because $K(0)=K'(0)=0$, integration yields
$K(s)\leq s^2(b-a)^2/8$.
Independence adds these log moment-generating bounds for $S$.
For $s>0$, Markov's inequality gives
$\mathbb{P}(S\geq t)\leq\exp(-st+s^2V/8)$.
Choosing $s=4t/V$ yields the upper-tail bound $e^{-2t^2/V}$.
Apply it also to $-S$ and add. The stated mean versions follow by substituting
$t=n\epsilon$ and the respective support widths.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Finite-family extension.}
For $M$ prespecified bounded-loss comparisons, apply the relevant inequality to
each and use a union bound. Independence between comparisons is not required;
independence of the biological units within each comparison is. Repeated cells,
reads or time points from one dependent biological unit cannot automatically be
counted as independent units.

\paragraph{No data-dependent shortcut.}
The bound does not justify choosing a model or validation loss using the same
outcomes and then reporting single-model coverage. A prespecified finite family,
a separate validation sample, or an appropriate complexity or sequential argument
is needed. Marginal predictive coverage likewise should not be read as coverage
for every subgroup or under arbitrary distribution shift.
