\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Alexeev asks whether two classes are bounded, where bounded means that every member occurs, up to isomorphism, as a fiber of a family over a finite-type base: (1) semi-log-canonical varieties of general type with fixed $K^n$; (2) $\epsilon$-log-terminal Fano varieties. The supplied verification corrects the extraction to Problem 1.4, $K^n$ and $\epsilon$, and identifies ``1.3. Positive characteristic issues'' as the next section heading, not an assumption in this problem.

A precise intended characteristic-zero version fixes dimension $n$, positive rational volume $v$ in (1), and a constant $\epsilon>0$ in (2). In (1), take projective slc canonical models with $K_X$ ample and $K_X^n=v$. Semi-log-canonical means the usual $S_2$, nodal-in-codimension-one and $\Q$-Cartier canonical conditions, with the normalization plus conductor log canonical. In (2), take normal projective Fano $n$-folds with $-K_X$ ample and $\Q$-Cartier, and all log discrepancies exceeding $\epsilon$ under the $\epsilon$-log-terminal convention; the related $\epsilon$-log-canonical convention uses at least $\epsilon$. The dimension, field, positive fixed epsilon, canonical-model and precise discrepancy conventions are not spelled out in the damaged original.

The attached unreviewed aid reports affirmative boundedness for the canonical-model version by Hacon--McKernan--Xu and for fixed-dimensional $\epsilon$-log-terminal Fano varieties by Birkar's BAB theorem. It distinguishes a literal reading of (1) that only assumes $K_X$ big. For every integer $m\ge5$, it takes a smooth degree-$m$ surface $S_m\subset\PP^3$ and blows up $m(m-4)^2-1$ distinct points. The resulting smooth general-type surface $X_m$ is reported to satisfy
\[
K_{X_m}^2=1,\qquad
\chi(\mathcal O_{X_m})=1+\binom{m-1}{3}.
\]
The aid uses this unbounded Euler characteristic to refute boundedness of the literal big-$K$ class. These surfaces have non-nef canonical divisor, so their numerical self-intersection $K^2=1$ is not a fixed canonical volume and does not contradict boundedness of canonical models.
