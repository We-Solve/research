\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
The supplied assessment identifies the short question ``Does strong disorder imply superdiffusivity?'' as concerning directed polymers in a random environment. It does not specify the model or the precise transverse-fluctuation criterion.

In the standard discrete example, let $(S_j)$ be simple random walk on $\Z^d$, $d\geq1$, starting at zero, let $(\omega_{j,x})$ be independent identically distributed environmental variables, and assume $\lambda(\beta)=\log\mathbb E[e^{\beta\omega_{1,0}}]<\infty$ for the chosen $\beta>0$. With $E_0$ denoting random-walk expectation and $\mathbb E$ environmental expectation, define
\[
 Z_n^\omega=E_0\exp\!\left(\beta\sum_{j=1}^n\omega_{j,S_j}\right),\qquad
 W_n^\omega=e^{-n\lambda(\beta)}Z_n^\omega,
\]
\[
 \frac{dQ_{n,\beta}^\omega}{dP_0}
 =\frac{\exp(\beta\sum_{j=1}^n\omega_{j,S_j})}{Z_n^\omega}.
\]
The normalized partition functions form a nonnegative mean-one martingale in the time environment. In this convention strong disorder is the assertion $W_\infty=0$ almost surely. It should not be silently replaced by the stronger strict quenched/annealed free-energy-gap condition often called very strong disorder.

The desired implication is from this strong-disorder condition to transverse spreading faster than the underlying diffusive scale $n^{1/2}$, under the intended polymer law. For example, one possible power-law second-moment criterion would be
\[
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}
 \frac{\log Q_{n,\beta}^\omega[|S_n|^2]}{2\log n}>\frac12
 \quad\text{almost surely in the environment}.
\]
This is not the unique definition of superdiffusivity: divergence of the second moment divided by $n$, escape from every fixed diffusive ball, maximal path displacement, and an annealed rather than quenched exponent are different formulations. Centered thermal variance also differs from uncentered displacement in a random environment. The nearest-neighbor, independent-environment and finite-exponential-moment assumptions likewise describe this example, not an extracted universal model class.
