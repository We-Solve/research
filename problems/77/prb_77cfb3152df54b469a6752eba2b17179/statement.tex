This is Irene Gamba's 2005 participant research agenda, not a single posed conjecture. It concerns reactive kinetic--Poisson systems for electron and biological transport, granular flows and other dissipative phenomena. Its motivation is that stationary or self-similar nonequilibrium distributions may be far from Gaussian, so closures based on Maxwellian statistics need correction in those regimes. The stated interests include existence, uniqueness, self-similarity, stability, higher-moment hydrodynamic corrections, boundary problems and numerical methods for kinetic, quantum and macroscopic models.

\item Quantum trajectory and quantum hydrodynamic models from a semiclassical picture, thermalization and Bose--Einstein condensates: existence and nonexistence for dispersion/diffusion models, strong-field Chapman--Enskog expansion of Wigner transport, high-velocity finite-time behavior of QHD, and numerical quantum-state calculations. The aid adds a moment-closure obstruction in three-velocity space. For a smooth strictly positive probability density $f$ on $\R^3$, define mass, momentum, full second-moment tensor and energy-flux convention by
\[
 \int f=1,\quad m_i(f)=\int v_i f(v)\,\dd v,\quad
 P_{ij}(f)=\int v_iv_j f(v)\,\dd v,\quad
 Q_i(f)=\int v_i|v|^2f(v)\,\dd v.
\]
At zero momentum, $P$ is also the central pressure tensor. A convention with a factor $1/2$ in $Q$ has the same nonuniqueness. The reported first family has one fixed positive-definite tensor $P_*$ and, for every $q\in\R$, a smooth positive Gaussian-mixture density $f_q$ with finite moments such that
\[
 m(f_q)=0,\qquad P(f_q)=P_*,\qquad Q_1(f_q)=q.
\]
Thus even the entire pressure tensor, in addition to mass and momentum, neither determines nor bounds the next flux component. The reported second smooth family preserves these lower data, fixed longitudinal moments $\int v_1^j f$ for $0\le j\le3$, and zero energy-flux vector, while
\[

\]
A power-law variant has the same lower-moment finiteness but infinite longitudinal fourth moment. The summary does not supply the exact mixture weights or tail formula. These are supplied realizability claims, not a proof that every closure is impossible: an additional constitutive assumption, equilibrium principle or tail condition may select a closure.
