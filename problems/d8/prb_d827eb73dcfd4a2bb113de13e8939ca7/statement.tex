\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(M\) be a compact smooth manifold with boundary \(N=\partial M\), and let \(d_g^\partial=d_g|_{N\times N}\) be intrinsic boundary distance. Question 5.1.3, attributed by the source to [Michel1981], asks which hypotheses on \(M,N,g\) imply boundary rigidity. In the usual fixed-manifold formulation this means that every admissible \(g'\) with \(d_{g'}^\partial=d_g^\partial\) is related to \(g\) by a boundary-fixing diffeomorphism. The source permits variants imposing additional assumptions on \(g'\), and also asks about Finsler metrics.

The aid supplies a pointwise-ordered special case. Let \(g,h\) be smooth Riemannian metrics on the same \(M\) with
\[
 h_x(v,v)\leq g_x(v,v)\quad(x\in M,v\in T_xM),\qquad
 d_h^\partial=d_g^\partial.
\]
Assume a family of \(g\)-minimizing boundary-to-boundary geodesics whose velocity squares quadratically span at every interior point: for each \(x\), the tensors \(v\otimes v\) from velocities of these geodesics through \(x\) span \(\operatorname{Sym}^2(T_xM)\). The reported conclusion is literal equality
\[
 h=g.
\]
Along each such geodesic, metric order and equality of endpoint distances force equality of its two lengths; the spanning hypothesis is what turns directional equality into equality of tensors. It is not a hypothesis only about spanning ordinary tangent vectors.

In particular, if \(g\) is simple, meaning strictly convex boundary and the standard unique minimizing/no-conjugate-point geodesic structure, the aid reports equality against every pointwise smaller competitor \(h\) with the same boundary distances. The competitor need not itself be simple. This order-restricted result does not solve boundary rigidity for arbitrary competitors, nor remove the usual boundary-fixing-diffeomorphism gauge from the full question.

For the Finsler variant, the aid records an additional exact-one-form freedom. Let \(F\) be a smooth Finsler norm and \(u\) smooth with \(u|_N=0\). Whenever
\[
 F'(x,v)=F(x,v)+\dd u_x(v)
\]
is still a positive strongly convex Finsler norm, every oriented curve from \(p\) to \(q\) has length changed by \(u(q)-u(p)\). Thus \(d_{F'}^\partial=d_F^\partial\). Nonzero \(\dd u\) applied to a reversible norm generally produces a nonreversible norm, so an unrestricted nonreversible formulation must account for this extra gauge or impose additional conditions.

The record then crosses into Section5.2, which separately defines \(C^k\) geodesic-flow conjugacy by an invertible \(C^k\) map \(H:SM_1\to SM_2\), with \(C^k\) inverse and \(H\phi_1^t=\phi_2^tH\), before an unfinished sentence about a longstanding conjecture. That adjacent conjecture is not supplied and must not be invented or treated as part of the ordered boundary-rigidity lemma.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Michel's broad boundary-rigidity question is only partly addressed. The aid proves equality for pointwise ordered metrics when minimizing boundary geodesics quadratically span, in particular when the larger metric is simple. Nonreversible Finsler metrics also have an exact-one-form gauge. The following flow-conjugacy fragment belongs to a separate section.
