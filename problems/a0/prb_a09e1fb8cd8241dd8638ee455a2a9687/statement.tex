\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 800217}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(\mathbb{B}^{2}=\{(z_1,z_2)\in\C^2:|z_1|^2+|z_2|^2<1\}\) be the unit ball and let \(\Delta=\{z\in\C:|z|<1\}\) be the unit disc. Interpret ``equivalent'' as biholomorphically equivalent.

Suppose that \(M\) is a complex manifold of complex dimension \(2\) such that, for every compact subset \(K\subseteq M\), there exists an open subset \(U\subseteq M\) containing \(K\) and a biholomorphism \(U\to\mathbb{B}^{2}\). Must \(M\) be biholomorphic to one of
\[
\mathbb{B}^{2},\qquad \Delta\times\C,\qquad \C^{2}?
\]
No relative compactness condition on \(U\) is stipulated in the supplied statement.

The source appends the remark ``This is false in \(\C^3\).'' The precise three-dimensional analogue intended by that remark, including its proposed list of model manifolds, is not specified in the supplied record.
\subsection{Short English statement}
If every compact subset of a complex surface lies in an open subset biholomorphic to the two-dimensional unit ball, must the surface itself be biholomorphic to that ball, the product of the unit disc with \(\C\), or \(\C^2\)?
