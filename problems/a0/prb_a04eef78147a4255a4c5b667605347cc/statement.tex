\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(k\) be a field and let \(S=k[x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3]\) have its standard grading. Under the usual interpretation of a line configuration as a nonempty finite reduced union of distinct projective lines, write
\[
L=\ell_1\cup\cdots\cup\ell_r\subseteq\mathbb P^3_k,\qquad r\geq1,
\]
and let \(\mathfrak p_i\subset S\) be the homogeneous prime ideal of \(\ell_i\). Its saturated homogeneous ideal is
\[
I=I(L)=\bigcap_{i=1}^r\mathfrak p_i.
\]
For a nonzero homogeneous ideal \(J\subseteq S\), define its initial degree by
\[
\alpha(J)=\min\{d\in\mathbb Z_{\geq0}:J_d\neq0\}.
\]
The second symbolic power of this reduced line ideal is
\[
I^{(2)}=\bigcap_{i=1}^r\bigl(I^2S_{\mathfrak p_i}\cap S\bigr)
=\bigcap_{i=1}^r\mathfrak p_i^2.
\]
The configuration is arithmetically Cohen--Macaulay (ACM) when \(S/I\) is Cohen--Macaulay; here \(\dim(S/I)=2\), so this means \(\operatorname{depth}_{(x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3)}(S/I)=2\).

The supplied question is whether there exists such a configuration \(L\) for which \(S/I\) is not Cohen--Macaulay and
\[
\alpha\bigl(I^{(2)}\bigr)-\alpha(I)=1.
\]
