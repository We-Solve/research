\paragraph{Shared notation.}Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.


Capacitated facility location opens facilities at a cost and assigns clients subject to facility capacities, minimizing opening plus connection cost. A partial assignment fixes some client-facility relationships and strengthens the residual relaxation.

A relaxation contains the integral feasible solutions. For a nonnegative minimization objective, its integrality gap is the supremum of the ratio between the integral optimum and the relaxation optimum over the specified instances.

A linear program optimizes $c^Tx$ over a polyhedron $\{x:Ax\leq b\}$. In the simplex method a pivot moves between adjacent basic feasible solutions; a pivot rule chooses the next such move.

\section{Recovering the precise relaxation}
The report refers to An--Singh--Svensson for the full definition. We inspected
its Definition 2.1. Let $x_{ij},y_i\in[0,1]$ be assignment and opening
variables, with integer capacities $U_i$. For every partial assignment $g\ge0$
satisfying $\sum_i g_{ij}\le1$, $\sum_jg_{ij}\le U_i$, set
$d_j=1-\sum_i g_{ij}$. The network has arcs
\[
j^s\longrightarrow i\ (x_{ij}),\quad i\longrightarrow j^s\ (g_{ij}),\quad
i\longrightarrow i'\ \left(y_i\left(U_i-\sum_jg_{ij}\right)\right),\quad
i'\longrightarrow j^t\ (y_i d_j),
\]
where parentheses indicate shared arc capacity. Commodity $j$ must send $d_j$
from $j^s$ to $j^t$. Requiring feasibility for every valid $g$ is the
MFN relaxation named by the source question. In particular, a bad point for
the simpler assignment LP is not automatically a feasible lower-bound example
for MFN. We test exactly that tempting transfer.

\section{A family with exact values for both relaxations}
Fix an integer $U\ge1$ and put $N=U+1$. There are $N$ unit clients and two
facilities $A,B$, with capacities $U_A=U,U_B=N$, opening costs $0,F$,
where $F>0$. Put all distinct client and facility points at distance
$\varepsilon>0$ from each other. This is a metric: each nonzero distance
$\varepsilon$ is at most the sum $2\varepsilon$ of two others.
Every integral solution must open $B$, since $A$ alone has capacity $N-1$.
Opening only $B$ and assigning all clients to it is feasible. Therefore
\[
\mathrm{OPT}_{\rm int}=F+N\varepsilon.
\]

The standard LP has
$\sum_i x_{ij}=1$, $0\le x_{ij}\le y_i\le1$ and
$\sum_jx_{ij}\le U_i y_i$.
Summing capacities forces $U y_A+N y_B\ge N$, hence $y_B\ge1/N$.
The connection cost is always $N\varepsilon$. The point
\[
y_A=1,\quad y_B=1/N,\quad x_{Aj}=U/N,\quad x_{Bj}=1/N
\quad(j=1,\ldots,N)
\]
is feasible: the two loads are $U,1$, and the assignment/opening inequalities
hold. It attains the lower bound, so
\[
\mathrm{OPT}_{\rm std}=F/N+N\varepsilon.
\]
For fixed $N,F$ the ratio tends to $N$ as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$;
letting $N$ grow gives unbounded standard-LP gap.

\paragraph{Why the same instance has MFN gap exactly one.}
Choose $g$ assigning any $U$ clients to $A$ and leaving client $j_0$ unassigned.
Facility $A$ has residual capacity zero. The only possible incoming arc to
$j_0^t$ carrying positive flow is then from $B'$, and its capacity is $y_B$.
Since $d_{j_0}=1$, every MFN-feasible point must have $y_B\ge1$, hence $y_B=1$.
At $g=0$, a client source has outgoing arcs of total capacity $\sum_i x_{ij}$
and no incoming arcs. Its demand is one, so $\sum_i x_{ij}\ge1$.
Consequently every MFN point has objective at least $F+N\varepsilon$.

For completeness, the upper bound does not rely on an unproved assertion
that the MFN system is a relaxation. Take $y_B=1,y_A=0,x_{Bj}=1,x_{Aj}=0$.
For an arbitrary valid fractional $g$, send each demand $d_j$ directly along
$j^s,B,B',j^t$. Its first arc has capacity one and its last capacity $d_j$.
The total load on $B\to B'$ is
\[
\sum_jd_j=N-\sum_jg_{Aj}-\sum_jg_{Bj}
\le N-\sum_jg_{Bj},
\]
which is precisely the available $B$ capacity. All demands are nonnegative,
so this constructs a feasible flow for every $g$. The resulting objective is
$F+N\varepsilon$. Thus $\mathrm{OPT}_{\rm MFN}=\mathrm{OPT}_{\rm int}$
on the entire family, while the weaker relaxation has arbitrarily large gap.

\section{Attempt audit and remaining scope}
