\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Fix an algebraically closed field \(k\) of characteristic zero and define
\[
 d_{\min}=\min\{d\ge1:\operatorname{Hilb}^d(\A_k^3)\text{ is reducible}\}.
\]
Reducibility refers to the underlying topological space, not to failure of reducedness. The source asks to determine \(d_{\min}\). Its original remark gives the historical bounds \(10<d_{\min}\le78\), explicitly requiring characteristic zero for the lower bound and attributing it to a then-recent paper of Sivic. The supplied later aid reports \(12\le d_{\min}\le78\); this is its dated literature assessment, not an independently checked current bound.

Call a length-\(d\) quotient \(A=k[x,y,z]/I\) smoothable if its corresponding point belongs to the closure of the locus of \(d\) distinct reduced points. Call it local if \(\operatorname{Spec}A\) is supported at one point. The aid reports
\[
 d_{\min}=\min\{\dim_k A:A\text{ is a nonsmoothable local quotient of }k[x,y,z]\}.
\]
Its input is componentwise smoothability: a finite algebra, decomposed as a product of its local factors, is smoothable if and only if every local factor is smoothable, using lifting of idempotents in deformation families. It also reports that reducibility propagates upward: once \(\operatorname{Hilb}^d(\A^3)\) is reducible, so is \(\operatorname{Hilb}^{d+1}(\A^3)\). In particular a minimal-length nonsmoothable witness must be local; this does not mean that disconnected nonsmoothable algebras never exist.

The reported exclusion sieve concerns very-compressed ternary local algebras. With support moved to the origin and \(\mathfrak m=(x,y,z)\), the relevant Hilbert function consists of the maximal values
\(\binom{i+2}{2}\) in degrees \(0\le i<s\), followed by a final value \(a\) in degree \(s\), and zero thereafter, where
\[
 1\le a\le\binom{s+2}{2},\qquad
 d=\binom{s+2}{3}+a.
\]
Equivalently this graded family has \(\mathfrak m^{s+1}\subseteq I\subseteq\mathfrak m^s\), with the final degree-\(s\) quotient dimension \(a\). Its fixed-support parameter space is a Grassmannian of dimension
\(a(\binom{s+2}{2}-a)\); allowing the support to vary adds \(3\). The aid reports that this locus has dimension less than \(3d\) for \(2\le d\le95\), first exceeding \(3d\) at \(d=96\). This parameter-count comparison alone is not a smoothability proof. Separately, the aid says very-compressed ternary algebras through length \(95\) are known to be smoothable.

Accordingly, any local witness improving the reported upper bound \(78\) must have length at most \(77\) and a Hilbert function outside that very-compressed family. This reduction does not determine \(d_{\min}\), construct a smaller witness, or exclude arbitrary non-very-compressed local algebras.
\subsection{Short English statement}
What is the first length for which the Hilbert scheme of points in affine three-space is reducible? The supplied aid reduces the search to nonsmoothable local algebras, reports bounds from twelve to seventy-eight, and excludes very-compressed algebras from any smaller minimal witness.
