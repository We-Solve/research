\section{Research attempt: Deterministic snapshots and grid-consistent regularization}

\paragraph{A deterministic calculation inside a stochastic update.}
Let $F(\theta)=N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^N f_i(\theta)$ on a fixed Euclidean parameter space. Suppose every $f_i$ has $L$-Lipschitz gradient. At a snapshot $w$, compute the full deterministic gradient $\nabla F(w)$, and for a uniform random index $I$ define
\[
 g_I(\theta;w)=\nabla f_I(\theta)-\nabla f_I(w)+\nabla F(w).
\]
Then $\mathbb Eg_I=\nabla F(\theta)$. Writing $D_I=\nabla f_I(\theta)-\nabla f_I(w)$ gives
\[
 \mathbb E\|g_I-\nabla F(\theta)\|^2
 =\mathbb E\|D_I\|^2-\|\mathbb ED_I\|^2
 \le L^2\|\theta-w\|^2.
\]
The snapshot costs $N$ component gradients; a later sample evaluates two component gradients unless one is cached. The bound quantifies both the benefit and the cost of deterministic information, rather than treating the snapshot as free.

For a concrete exact case, take $f_i(\theta)=a\theta^2/2-b_i\theta$ with common $a>0$. Then $g_I=a\theta-\overline b$ is exactly the full gradient for every $I,w$, even if the raw stochastic gradients have variance $\operatorname{Var}(b_I)>0$. With nonidentical curvatures $a_i$, the variance becomes $\operatorname{Var}(a_I)(\theta-w)^2$, so stale snapshots again matter.

\paragraph{A conditional descent estimate.}
Assume in addition that $F$ is $\mu$-strongly convex and $L$-smooth, and let $R$ be a proper closed convex regularizer with a minimizer $\theta_*$ of $F+\lambda R$, $\lambda\ge0$. For the proximal update
\[
 \theta^+=\operatorname{prox}_{\eta\lambda R}(\theta-\eta g_I),
\]
nonexpansiveness of the proximal map gives
\[
 \mathbb E\|\theta^+-\theta_*\|^2
 \le(1-2\eta\mu+\eta^2L^2)\|\theta-\theta_*\|^2
       +\eta^2L^2\|\theta-w\|^2.
\]
Here $\theta_*$ is a fixed point of the full-gradient proximal map by its first-order optimum condition. To justify nonexpansiveness, if $u=\operatorname{prox}_{\eta\lambda R}(a)$ and $v=\operatorname{prox}_{\eta\lambda R}(b)$, the optimality conditions and the two convex subgradient inequalities give $\langle (a-u)-(b-v),u-v\rangle\ge0$. Hence $\|u-v\|^2\le\langle a-b,u-v\rangle\le\|a-b\|\|u-v\|$. Expanding the squared distance before the proximal map, using the zero-mean gradient error, strong monotonicity $\langle\nabla F(\theta)-\nabla F(\theta_*),\theta-\theta_*\rangle\ge\mu\|\theta-\theta_*\|^2$, and the Lipschitz bound proves the estimate. For $\lambda=0$ the proximal map is simply the identity.

The estimate supplies a contraction when snapshots are sufficiently close and steps are small; it does not establish a whole adaptive refresh policy without controlling $\|\theta-w\|$. In the source's nonconvex networks, strong convexity is absent, and when layers are inserted the parameter spaces themselves change. A transport rule for snapshots and a new stability analysis are needed before applying this calculation.

\paragraph{A regularizer that survives time-grid refinement.}
Let $0=t_0<\cdots<t_m=T$, and linearly interpolate parameters $\theta_j$ between these times. Its exact squared time-derivative energy is
\[
 \int_0^T\|\dot\theta(t)\|^2\,dt
 =\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\frac{\|\theta_{j+1}-\theta_j\|^2}{t_{j+1}-t_j}.
\]
This follows because the derivative is constant on each interval. Subdividing an interval while preserving that linear interpolant leaves the energy unchanged. In contrast, for $\theta(t)=tv$ on a uniform grid of spacing $\Delta t$, the unscaled penalty $\sum_j\|\theta_{j+1}-\theta_j\|^2=T\Delta t\|v\|^2$ tends to zero as layers are refined. Thus omitting the inverse time-step factor silently weakens the same nominal regularization during shallow-to-deep refinement.

Even a grid-consistent penalty requires problem-specific bias control. In the scalar noiseless loss $(\theta-b)^2/2$ with penalty $\lambda\theta^2/2$, the minimizer is $b/(1+\lambda)$ and its squared error relative to $b$ is $\lambda^2b^2/(1+\lambda)^2$. No fixed positive $\lambda$ can be uniformly appropriate for arbitrarily large unknown $b$. These calculations delimit, rather than solve, the source's request for a useful regularizer.
