\section{Density and Geometry Recovery from Nearest-Neighbor Graphs}\label{30002105}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 30002105 · Computer Science}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.


A directed $k$-nearest-neighbor graph links each sample to its $k$ closest distinct samples. An unweighted adjacency matrix records only adjacency, without distances. Similarities consist of translation, orthogonal transformation, and common rescaling.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Let $X_1,\ldots,X_n$ be i.i.d. samples from a density $p$ on $\mathbb R^d$, and let $A$ be the adjacency matrix of their unweighted $k$-nearest-neighbor graph. Can $p(X_i)$ be estimated up to constant factors from $A$ alone, and can the sample locations be approximately reconstructed up to translation, rotation, and rescaling when $n$ is large? \sref{30002105}{2}
\paragraph{Scope qualification.}
The supplied question does not specify directed versus symmetrized adjacency, density regularity, scaling of $k$, or reconstruction-error norm.
