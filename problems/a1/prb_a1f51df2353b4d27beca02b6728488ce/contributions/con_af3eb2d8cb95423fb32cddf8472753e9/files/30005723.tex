\section{Modular Generators for Massive Double Cones}\label{30005723}
{\small UnsolvedMath ID 30005723 · Mathematical Physics}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


For a von Neumann algebra with cyclic separating vacuum vector, modular theory supplies a one-parameter group of automorphisms and its generator. A double cone is the intersection of the causal future of one point with the causal past of another. $M_-$ denotes the particular modular-generator component used in the report; multiplication means pointwise action in the specified representation.

\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}
For a massive free scalar field localized in a double cone, determine whether the modular-generator component $M_-$ is independent of the mass and whether $M_-$ is a multiplication operator.

\paragraph{Scope and source qualification.}The decomposition defining $M_-$ is source-specific. Mass independence of this component would not automatically imply mass independence of the entire modular flow.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Determine whether the double-cone modular-generator component for a massive scalar field is mass independent and acts by multiplication.

\paragraph{Testing algebraic cancellation in the source formula.} The cached [S2] report gives \(M_-=2A^{-1/4}\operatorname{arcoth}(B)A^{-1/4}\), with \(A=-\Delta+m^2\) and \(B=A^{1/4}\chi A^{-1/4}+A^{-1/4}\chi A^{1/4}-I\). Here \(\chi\) is spatial localization. Its numerical discussion explicitly lacks rigorous discretization error estimates. To test whether mass independence follows from this algebraic form alone, replace \(A\) by \(\operatorname{diag}(m^2+1,m^2+4)\) and \(\chi\) by the rank-one projection with every entry \(1/2\).
\paragraph{Exact two-dimensional calculation.} Put \(a=m^2+1\), \(b=m^2+4\), \(r=(a/b)^{1/4}\), and \(c=(r+r^{-1})/2>1\). Direct multiplication gives
\[
 B=\begin{pmatrix}0&c\\c&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 M_-=2(ab)^{-1/4}\operatorname{arcoth}(c)
 \begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The odd real branch \(\operatorname{arcoth}(x)=\frac12\log((x+1)/(x-1))\) for \(|x|>1\) justifies the functional calculus. At \(m=0\) the coefficient is positive. As \(m\to\infty\), \(c-1\) has order \(m^{-4}\), so the coefficient is of order \(m^{-1}\log m\) and tends to zero. It is therefore mass dependent.
\paragraph{What is and is not falsified.} This disproves a proposed purely algebraic cancellation valid for arbitrary positive \(A\) and projections \(\chi\). It is not a counterexample for the continuum double cone: this matrix pair does not satisfy all localization/geometric relations of the differential operator. Furthermore the matrix \(M_-\) commutes with \(\chi\), so the same test does not even obstruct multiplication in its two-point position basis. Both source questions require continuum estimates, either a nonzero mass derivative of a smeared matrix element or a nonvanishing commutator with a spatial multiplier, with domains and limits controlled.
