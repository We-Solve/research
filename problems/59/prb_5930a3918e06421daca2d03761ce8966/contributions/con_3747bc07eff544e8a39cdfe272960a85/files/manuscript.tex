\section{Algorithms for Amoeba Dimensions from Tropical Fans}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.


A rational polyhedral cone is generated by finitely many rational vectors with nonnegative real coefficients. A rational polyhedral fan is a finite collection of such cones closed under taking faces, with intersections common faces; its support $F$ is their union. Its dimension is the maximum cone dimension. A rational subspace $U\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ is the real span of rational vectors. The Minkowski sum is $U+F=\{u+f:u\in U,f\in F\}$, whose dimension is the maximum dimension of its polyhedral pieces. The input is a finite rational description of the fan; the output is the minimum displayed in the source question.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Given a $d$-dimensional rational polyhedral fan with support $F\subseteq\mathbb R^n$, compute $\min_U\{2\dim_{\mathbb R}(U+F)-\dim_{\mathbb R}U\}$ over subspaces $U$ spanned by rational vectors. \href{https://doi.org/10.4171/owr/2023/15}{[S2]}
\paragraph{Scope qualification.}
The minimization allows every rational subspace, including zero and the whole ambient space. The question asks for an algorithm; no polynomial runtime or restriction to coordinate subspaces is added.

\section{Research attempt: Rank reduction, a three-cone obstruction, and an uncertified limit search}

\paragraph{Replacing cone geometry by rational linear algebra.}
Let the nonempty finite rational fan have cones $\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_s$,
and put $L_i=\operatorname{span}\sigma_i$. For a rational subspace $U$,
\[
 \dim(U+F)=\max_i\dim(U+L_i).
\]
Indeed $U+F$ is the finite union of $U+\sigma_i$. Each such polyhedral set
has linear span $U+L_i$ and nonempty relative interior there: a relative
interior point of the cone, added to $U$, has the expected quotient-relative
neighborhood. Equivalently projecting the cone to $(U+L_i)/U$ preserves a
full-dimensional relative interior in that quotient. Its dimension is therefore
$\dim(U+L_i)$, and the dimension of a finite union is the maximum.
All these dimensions are exact rational matrix ranks.
If $d=\max_i\dim L_i$, the objective is at least $d$. To see this, write
$k=\dim U$ and choose a cone of dimension $d$; then
$2\dim(U+L_i)-k\geq2\max(k,d)-k\geq d$.
Let $L=\sum_iL_i$ and $r=\dim L$. Restricting to $U\subseteq L$ loses
nothing: put $U_0=U\cap L$ and $e=\dim U-\dim U_0$. Since
$U\cap L_i=U_0\cap L_i$,
$\dim(U+L_i)=e+\dim(U_0+L_i)$; the objective decreases by exactly $e$
when $U$ is replaced by $U_0$.
Taking $U=L$ gives the upper bound $r$, and taking $U=0$ gives $2d$.
For a pure fan with every maximal cone span of dimension $d$, taking their
common intersection $C$ gives $2d-\dim C$.

\paragraph{An exact case defeating two natural candidate choices.}
In $\mathbb R^6$, take the three coordinate cones
\[
 \sigma_1=\operatorname{cone}(e_1,e_2,e_4),\quad
 \sigma_2=\operatorname{cone}(e_2,e_3,e_5),\quad
 \sigma_3=\operatorname{cone}(e_1,e_3,e_6),
\]
with all their faces. Their pairwise intersections are coordinate rays,
which are faces of each cone, so this really is a rational polyhedral fan.
Its maximal spans have dimension three, common intersection zero, and total
span $\mathbb R^6$. Both $U=0$ and $U=\mathbb R^6$, and also the common
intersection choice, give value six.
But $U=\operatorname{span}(e_1,e_2,e_3)$ meets each maximal span in
dimension two, hence each sum has dimension four and the objective is five.
This is optimal. For an arbitrary $k$-dimensional $U$, write
$t=\min_i\dim(U\cap L_i)$. The value is $k+6-2t$.
For $k=0$ it is six; for $k=1$ it is at least five; for $k\geq5$ it is
at least $k\geq5$. For $k=2$, a value below five would require $t=2$,
forcing $U$ into the zero common intersection, impossible.
For $k=3$, a value below five would require $t=3$, forcing all three
$L_i$ to equal $U$, impossible. For $k=4$, a value below five would again
require $t=3$, forcing their six-dimensional sum into $U$, impossible.
Thus the minimum is exactly five. Only testing common intersections and the
whole span is not a correct algorithm for the original optimization.

\paragraph{A convergent enumeration is not a terminating algorithm.}
Enumerate all rational matrices with at most $r$ columns, for example by
increasing bounds on numerator and positive denominator sizes, and take their
column spans as candidates $U\subseteq L$. Maintain the smallest exact rank
objective seen, starting from $r$. Its values are integers between $d$ and $r$.
The original minimum is attained by some rational subspace, since the set of
attained integer values is a nonempty finite subset of $\{d,\ldots,r\}$.
A rational basis for an optimal subspace eventually appears in the enumeration,
so the maintained bound eventually equals the answer and stays there.
However, finitely many changes in an integer sequence do not give an effective
way to recognize its last change. Hitting the lower bound $d$ certifies
termination in that special case; in the displayed example the answer is
$5>d=3$, so that stopping rule cannot apply. A proof of a finite candidate
family or an effective exclusion test for smaller values is still missing.
