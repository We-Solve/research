\section{Attempt and explicit example}
The source explicitly reports that its conjecture was refuted in the
workshop. We do not claim a new discovery. Its word ``all'' is preserved:
failure upstairs is claimed equivalent to failure for every binary
collapse. The stronger diagnostic idea that some collapse must detect
every failure is a different statement. Structural-zero and network-flow
questions in the same extract also remain separate.

\paragraph{A $2$ by $2$ by $3$ counterexample to the literal universal wording.}
Index $i,j\in\{0,1\}$ and $k\in\{0,1,2\}$. Give count zero to
$(0,0,0),(0,1,0)$ and count one to each other cell. The original
no-three-way-interaction model has no strictly positive MLE.
Indeed, the model permits a parameter multiplying the probabilities in
the pair-configuration $i=0,k=0$ by $e^{-t}$ before renormalizing; this
is a two-factor potential. None of the observed counts lies in those
cells. Decreasing their positive fitted probability strictly increases
every observed cell probability after normalization, and hence strictly
increases the likelihood for every finite $t$. There is no interior maximum.

Now collapse $k$ into $\{0,1\}$ and $\{2\}$. For either value of $j$,
the two rows indexed by $i$ in the resulting $i$-by-collapsed-$k$ slice
are $(1,1)$ and $(2,1)$. All eight counts are positive and the slices
for $j=0,1$ agree. Their empirical distribution factors as a positive
joint law on $(i,k')$ times the uniform law on $j$, and therefore lies
in the no-three-way-interaction model.
It is a global MLE: if empirical probabilities are $q_l>0$ and candidate
probabilities $p_l>0$, then
\[
\sum_lq_l\log(p_l/q_l)\le\sum_lq_l(p_l/q_l-1)=0,
\]
using $\log u\le u-1$, whose proof follows by differentiating
$u-1-\log u$ and locating its minimum at one. Equality holds exactly
when $p=q$. Thus the collapsed table has an ordinary MLE even though
the original does not, disproving the displayed ``all collapsings''
implication. The proof accounts for every count and uses no numerical fit.

\paragraph{Why this does not settle the stronger detection interpretation.}
For the different collapse $\{0\}$ versus $\{1,2\}$, the empty
pair-configuration remains empty. The same two-factor likelihood argument
then rules out a positive MLE. Thus our example has both a detecting
and a nondetecting collapse. It does not prove that all binary collapses
can miss a likelihood face. The source reports a $3$ by $3$ by $3$
counterexample to its historical conjecture, but no explicit nine-cell
construction is imported from the unsupported expanded title.
