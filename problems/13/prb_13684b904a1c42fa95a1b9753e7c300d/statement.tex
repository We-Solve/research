\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(k\) be a finite field and let \(F/k\) be a finitely generated field extension of transcendence degree one, so that \(F\) is the function field of an integral curve over \(k\). Fix such a field \(F\).

For this draft, use the customary admissibility convention that an \(F\)-division algebra \(D\) is a finite-dimensional central division algebra: \(D\) is an associative division algebra with center \(F\) and \(\dim_F D<\infty\). A subfield \(L\subseteq D\) containing \(F\) is maximal if no strictly larger commutative subfield of \(D\) contains it. A finite group \(G\) is \emph{admissible over \(F\)} if there exist such an algebra \(D\) and a maximal subfield \(L\subseteq D\) for which \(L/F\) is Galois and
\[
\operatorname{Gal}(L/F)\cong G.
\]

\textbf{Problem.} For each fixed function field \(F\) as above, characterize the finite groups \(G\) admissible over \(F\); that is, determine a necessary-and-sufficient condition for the existence of \(D\) and \(L\) satisfying these requirements.

\textbf{Qualification.} The supplied statement does not explicitly specify finite dimensionality or centrality of the division algebra. The convention used above requires confirmation against the cited report. The supplied record labels the problem open; this draft does not independently verify that status.
