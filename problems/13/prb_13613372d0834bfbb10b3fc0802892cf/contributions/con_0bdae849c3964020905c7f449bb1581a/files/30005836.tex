\paragraph{Separating state energy from kinetic loss in a test model.} On a periodic interval let \(\phi\) be a smooth scalar phase field and \(W\) a differentiable double-well potential. For \(\epsilon>0\) define
\[
 E_\epsilon(\phi)=\int\left(\frac\epsilon2|\phi_x|^2+\frac1\epsilon W(\phi)\right)dx,
 \quad \mu_\phi=-\epsilon\phi_{xx}+\epsilon^{-1}W'(\phi),
 \quad \phi_t=-M\mu_\phi,
\]
with constant mobility \(M>0\). Integration by parts yields
\[
 \frac{dE_\epsilon}{dt}=\int\mu_\phi\phi_tdx
 =-M\int\mu_\phi^2dx=-M^{-1}\int\phi_t^2dx.
\]
Stationary interfaces are independent of \(M\), but their time-dependent relaxation is not. Equal stored energy therefore cannot determine rates of dissipation.
\paragraph{Misorientation and degeneracy checks.} A scalar phase field alone contains no lattice orientation. Even a planar orientation angle \(\theta\) is defined modulo the crystal symmetry: for an \(n\)-fold rotational symmetry, the mismatch magnitude must compare \(\theta_1-\theta_2\) modulo \(2\pi/n\). The function \(\min_{k\in\mathbb Z}|\theta_1-\theta_2-2\pi k/n|\) passes that invariance test, while the raw angle difference fails it. This distance is nonsmooth at equivalent choices and is not by itself a calibrated interface energy.
