\section*{Scaling genetic circuits}
\label{prob:269}
\noindent\textbf{Status: preliminary conditional reduction; full target not established.}
\subsection{Target and scope}
\paragraph{Catalogue definitions and assumptions.}
Define a sequence of circuit families $\mathcal C_n$ computing specified Boolean functions with $n$ molecular gates in one bacterial cell. $P_{\rm err}(d,c)$ is the probability that a calibrated output threshold disagrees with the truth table after a fixed settling time. Context $c\in C$ includes growth and copy number; $B(d,c)$ is burden and $\tau(d,c)$ response delay. Input states have a stated distribution. Architectural constraints bound resource pools and available orthogonal regulators. Count only gates with a declared minimum signal throughput; adding silent components does not increase the functional gate count.
\paragraph{Original target.}
Determine the largest feasible circuit size
\[
 n_{\max}=\sup\{n:\exists d\in\mathcal C_n,
 \ \sup_{c\in C}P_{\rm err}(d,c)\leq\varepsilon,
 \ B(d,c)\leq B_{\max},\ \tau(d,c)\leq\tau_{\max}\ \text{for all }c\in C\}.
\]
Develop measured error-growth laws and show when predicted reliability survives composition of gates characterized separately.
\paragraph{Catalogue alternative formulations.}
(i) A layered acyclic circuit studies propagation of gain loss and timing error. (ii) A recurrent memory circuit studies attractor stability and switching noise rather than a truth table alone. These are different scaling questions. Population-distributed architectures trade single-cell burden for communication noise and address \href{https://we-solve.github.io/problems/distributed-computation-by-cell-populations-e20ba48e/}{Problem~271}; they should not be treated as equivalent resources.
\subsection{Attempted answer}
\noindent\emph{Preliminary step: the following worked reduction has not been shown to instantiate all assumptions of the original target. Its applicability is an outstanding proof obligation.}

\begin{proposition}
For a stipulated integral controller $\dot z=r-y$, every equilibrium with finite controller state necessarily has output $y=r$. This algebraic set-point condition is not a stability guarantee. In the linear plant $\dot y=-ay+kz$, $a,k>0$, the closed-loop matrix is Hurwitz and the set point is attracting.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At equilibrium the controller derivative is zero. For constant reference, subtract equilibrium values: the characteristic polynomial of the plant-controller pair is $s^2+as+k$, whose positive coefficients give negative-real-part roots.
\end{proof}

Molecular conservation, actuator saturation, controller degradation, stochastic moments and attainable reaction mechanisms can invalidate the reduction. No implementable biological circuit or experimentally demonstrated adaptation is claimed.

\section{Shared mathematical bounds}\label{app:bounds}
\begin{lemma}[Independent bounded sums]\label{lem:bounded}
Let independent real variables $X_i$ satisfy $a_i\leq X_i\leq b_i$ almost surely,
and let $S=\sum_{i=1}^n(X_i-\mathbb{E} X_i)$. If
$V=\sum_i(b_i-a_i)^2>0$, then for every $t>0$,
\[
 \mathbb{P}(|S|\geq t)\leq 2\exp(-2t^2/V).
\]
In particular, independent losses in $[0,1]$ have mean-error bound
$\mathbb{P}(|\overline X-\mathbb{E}\overline X|\geq\epsilon)
\leq2e^{-2n\epsilon^2}$. The same expression for variables in $[-1,1]$
is $2e^{-n\epsilon^2/2}$. If $V=0$ then $S=0$ almost surely.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $X\in[a,b]$ with mean $\mu$, the nonnegative product
$(X-a)(b-X)$ gives
$\operatorname{Var} X\leq(\mu-a)(b-\mu)\leq(b-a)^2/4$.
Let $K(s)=\log\mathbb{E}\exp[s(X-\mathbb{E} X)]$.
Boundedness permits two differentiations. Its second derivative is the variance
under the exponentially tilted law, which has the same support, so
$K''(s)\leq(b-a)^2/4$ for every real $s$.
Because $K(0)=K'(0)=0$, integration yields
$K(s)\leq s^2(b-a)^2/8$.
Independence adds these log moment-generating bounds for $S$.
For $s>0$, Markov's inequality gives
$\mathbb{P}(S\geq t)\leq\exp(-st+s^2V/8)$.
Choosing $s=4t/V$ yields the upper-tail bound $e^{-2t^2/V}$.
Apply it also to $-S$ and add. The stated mean versions follow by substituting
$t=n\epsilon$ and the respective support widths.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Finite-family extension.}
For $M$ prespecified bounded-loss comparisons, apply the relevant inequality to
each and use a union bound. Independence between comparisons is not required;
independence of the biological units within each comparison is. Repeated cells,
reads or time points from one dependent biological unit cannot automatically be
counted as independent units.

\paragraph{No data-dependent shortcut.}
The bound does not justify choosing a model or validation loss using the same
outcomes and then reporting single-model coverage. A prespecified finite family,
a separate validation sample, or an appropriate complexity or sequential argument
is needed. Marginal predictive coverage likewise should not be read as coverage
for every subgroup or under arbitrary distribution shift.
