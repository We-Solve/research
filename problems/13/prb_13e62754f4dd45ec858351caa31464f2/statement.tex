\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
\textbf{Extracted interpolation question.} For the backward shift \(B\) on a Bergman space \(A^p\), if \(M_1\subsetneq M_2\), does there exist a \(B\)-invariant subspace \(M\) with \(M_1\subsetneq M\subsetneq M_2\)? In the usual invariant-subspace interpretation, all three subspaces are closed linear subspaces of \(A^p\), and \(B(M_j)\subseteq M_j\). The source does not state these endpoint hypotheses or any restriction on \(M_2/M_1\).\par A conventional model is \(A^p(D)=\{f\text{ holomorphic on }D:\int_D|f|^p\,dA<\infty\}\), where \(D\) is the unit disc and \(dA\) area measure. The source does not specify the range of \(p\) or weights. One common backward shift is \(Bf(z)=(f(z)-f(0))/z\), with the removable value \(Bf(0)=f'(0)\). On the Hilbert Bergman space, the adjoint of multiplication by \(z\) is a different weighted backward shift: for \(f(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}a_nz^n\), it is \(\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{n+1}{n+2}a_{n+1}z^n\). These operators must not be identified without the source's convention.\par \textbf{Missing essential qualification.} A literal assertion for every strict nested pair cannot hold if \(\dim(M_2/M_1)=1\), because then no intermediate linear subspace exists. In either of the conventional shifts above, \(M_1=\operatorname{span}\{1\}\) and \(M_2=\operatorname{span}\{1,z\}\) are closed invariant subspaces with one-dimensional quotient. Thus an omitted restriction excluding such pairs is essential to the intended open interpolation problem. A condition such as an infinite-dimensional quotient would define a different, qualified question, but it is not inserted as if supplied. Once the endpoint and closedness conventions are fixed, the intermediate-space problem corresponds to asking for a nonzero proper closed invariant subspace of the induced operator on \(M_2/M_1\); this restates the need for the quotient hypotheses rather than settling that qualified problem.\par The supplied triage checked 2026-08-21 labels the Bergman backward-shift interpolation problem open, reporting structural partial results and no located general intermediate-subspace theorem.
