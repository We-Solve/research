\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
N. Shah's Question 19 asks for the limiting distribution of \(\mu_T\) as \(T\to\infty\), but the extracted sentence omits the definition of \(\mu_T\). The source says special cases are known [53,73] and motivates distribution of \(\Gamma\)-orbits on \(G/H\) when \(\Gamma\cap H\) is a lattice in \(H\), or when \(\Gamma H\) is dense in \(G\). These are context, not a complete universal equidistribution hypothesis.

The supplied partial criterion uses a locally compact second-countable group \(G\) with left Haar measure \(m\), a lattice \(\Gamma\), quotient map \(\pi:G\to G/\Gamma\), and measurable sets \(R_T\subset G\) with \(0<m(R_T)<\infty\). Define
\[
 \lambda_T=\frac{1_{R_T}\,m}{m(R_T)},\qquad\mu_T=\pi_*\lambda_T.
\]
For every \(h\in G\), the aid's sharp group-level estimate and quotient contraction give
\[
 \|h_*\mu_T-\mu_T\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
 \leq\frac{m(hR_T\mathbin{\triangle}R_T)}{2m(R_T)}.
\]
Here \(\|\mu-\nu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}=\sup_E|\mu(E)-\nu(E)|\) for probability measures; the factor \(1/2\) depends on this convention.

Define the asymptotic left-symmetry set
\[
 A(R)=\left\{h\in G:\frac{m(hR_T\mathbin{\triangle}R_T)}{m(R_T)}\longrightarrow0\right\}.
\]
The reported result is that \(A(R)\) is a subgroup and every weak probability cluster measure of \(\mu_T\) is invariant under \(\overline{A(R)}\). If \(A(R)\) contains a subset generating a dense subgroup of \(G\), and the family \((\mu_T)\) is tight, then
\[
 \mu_T\Longrightarrow m_{G/\Gamma},
\]
where the quotient Haar probability is normalized to mass one. Tightness prevents escape of probability mass; dense asymptotic symmetries alone are not asserted sufficient. The theorem does not require \(A(R)=G\). In fact, the aid notes that full-\(G\) asymptotic invariance of these normalized Haar sets is a F\o lner condition and is impossible for a connected noncompact semisimple Lie group, by nonamenability.

For the representation-ball setup alluded to in the aid, it also reports that for a finite-dimensional continuous linear action the orbit map
\[
 G\longrightarrow V,\qquad g\longmapsto gp
\]
is proper exactly when the stabilizer of \(p\) is compact and the orbit is closed, in the intended Lie-action setting. Compact stabilizer alone is not sufficient. Its example is the binary cubic \(p=x^2y\) under \(\operatorname{SL}_2(\R)\): the stabilizer is trivial, but a diagonal family drives the cubic toward zero along an unbounded group sequence. The orbit is nonclosed and norm sublevels in \(G\) are unbounded. Unboundedness alone is not here identified with infinite Haar volume. The exact representation-ball definition and the properness, volume-growth, balance, mixing, and nonescape assumptions required by broader positive results are not supplied in the extracted question.
