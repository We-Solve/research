\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(K\) be a number field, that is, a finite extension of \(\Q\), and let \(\overline K\) be an algebraic closure. Let \(B\geq 1\) and let \(d\geq 2\) be an integer. Write \(\mathcal H_d(K)\) for the class of generalized H\'{e}non maps of degree \(d\) over \(K\) intended in the supplied source. These are polynomial automorphisms of the affine plane. The supplied record does not define this class precisely: in particular, it does not specify whether it includes compositions of generalized H\'{e}non maps. This qualification remains unresolved, and no convention for that class is imposed here.

For \(f\in\mathcal H_d(K)\), interpret the periodic points relevant to the stated field-degree condition as
\[
\operatorname{Per}(f)=\{P\in\A^2(\overline K): f^m(P)=P\text{ for some integer }m\geq 1\}.
\]
For \(P=(x,y)\), put \(K(P)=K(x,y)\). Thus individual algebraic periodic points are counted, without identifying points in the same orbit or points conjugate over \(K\).

\textbf{Conjecture 47 (Berger and Bedford, as attributed in the supplied record).} There exists a finite bound depending only on \([K:\Q]\), \(d\), and \(B\) for
\[
\#\{P\in\operatorname{Per}(f):[K(P):K]\leq B\}.
\]
More explicitly, for every integer \(n\geq 1\), every integer \(d\geq 2\), and every \(B\geq 1\), there exists a finite constant \(C(n,d,B)\) such that for every number field \(K\) with \([K:\Q]=n\) and every \(f\in\mathcal H_d(K)\),
\[
\#\{P\in\operatorname{Per}(f):[K(P):K]\leq B\}\leq C(n,d,B).
\]
There is no restriction on the periods of the counted points. This formulation remains conditional on clarifying the source's intended class \(\mathcal H_d(K)\).
\subsection{Short English statement}
The conjecture predicts a uniform bound on the number of algebraic periodic points of bounded field degree for generalized H\'{e}non maps. The bound depends only on the degree of the number field, the degree of the map, and the field-degree cutoff \(B\). The precise convention for generalized H\'{e}non maps requires clarification. The supplied registry labels the problem open; that label is not an independently verified status assessment.
