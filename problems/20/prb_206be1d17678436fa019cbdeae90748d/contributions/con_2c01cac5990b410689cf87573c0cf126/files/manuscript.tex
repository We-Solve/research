\paragraph{When uniform concentration already forces an atom.} Suppose $t\mapsto F_t$ is a narrowly continuous family of finite positive measures on $[0,\infty)$ for $0\leq t\leq t_0$, with $F_0(\{0\})=0$. Assume the source's weaker quantity is positive. Then some $c>0$, sequence $\epsilon_j\downarrow0$ and times $t_j\in[0,t_0]$ satisfy $F_{t_j}([0,\epsilon_j])\geq c$. Extract a subsequence with $t_j\to t_*$. For any $\delta>0$, choose a continuous compactly supported cutoff $\chi_\delta$ equal to one on $[0,\delta/2]$, between zero and one, and supported in $[0,\delta]$. For large $j$,
\[
 \int\chi_\delta\,dF_{t_j}\geq c,
 \quad\text{hence}\quad F_{t_*}([0,\delta])\geq c.
\]
The limit uses time continuity. Taking $\delta\downarrow0$ and continuity from above of the finite measure gives $F_{t_*}(\{0\})\geq c$. Since $F_0$ has no atom, $t_*>0$.

\paragraph{Strength of the extra hypothesis.} Only continuity against these cutoffs is needed; smooth $C_b^2$ cutoffs can be used. But it must hold on the whole compact interval, including any candidate condensation time. Without time compactness or continuity, narrow spikes may concentrate along times that do not yield an admissible limiting state. The proof does not establish the positive lower bound $c$ from the collision equation.
