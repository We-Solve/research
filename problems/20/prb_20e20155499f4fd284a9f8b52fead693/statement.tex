\[
T_S=\bigcup_{h\in G}h(S\cup S^{-1})h^{-1}.
\]
For \(g\in G\), define
\[
\ell_S(g)=\min\bigl\{n\in\Z_{\geq 0}:g=t_1\cdots t_n,\quad t_i\in T_S\bigr\},
\]
where the empty product is the identity, and define
\[
d_S(x,y)=\ell_S(x^{-1}y)\qquad(x,y\in G).
\]
This is the conjugation-invariant word metric associated with \(S\); adjoining inverses makes explicit the usual symmetric word-metric convention.

The question is whether, for every such \(H\), every lattice \(G\leq H\), and every finite generating set \(S\) of \(G\), there exists \(N=N(G,S)\in\Z_{\geq 0}\) such that

\forall x,y\in G,\qquad d_S(x,y)\leq N.
\]
Equivalently, must every element of \(G\) be a product of at most \(N\) conjugates in \(G\) of elements of \(S\cup S^{-1}\)? No bound uniform over lattices or generating sets is requested.
