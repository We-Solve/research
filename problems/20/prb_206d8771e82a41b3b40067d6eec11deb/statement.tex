\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 700520}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A group is finitely presented if it admits a presentation \(\langle x_1,\ldots,x_n\mid r_1,\ldots,r_m\rangle\) with finitely many generators and defining relators. A free group means a group with a free basis, of arbitrary cardinality; the empty basis is allowed, so the trivial group is free. For a prime \(p\), let \(C_p\) denote the cyclic group of order \(p\).

Does there exist a finitely presented group \(G\) such that \(G\) is not free, \(G\not\cong C_p\) for every prime \(p\), and
\[
\text{for every subgroup }H\leq G,\qquad H\neq G\ \Longrightarrow\ H\text{ is free}?
\]
The subgroup condition applies to all proper subgroups, with no finite generation or finite index restriction.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does there exist a finitely presented group that is neither free nor cyclic of prime order, but whose every proper subgroup is free?
