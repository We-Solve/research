Using that stronger sequential stability, the aid proposes the following nonconstructive uniform-gap corollary. For every $a\geq0$ and $\nu>0$, there is $\delta(a,\nu)>0$ such that any two nondiffeomorphic members of

          \ \operatorname{diam}_gM\leq1,

 d_{\mathrm{GH}}((M,g),(N,h))\geq\delta(a,\nu).
\]
Here $d_{\mathrm{GH}}$ is Gromov--Hausdorff distance between compact metric spaces. If the class is empty the assertion is vacuous. The dependence is on the fixed bounds, not on the individual metrics, and no explicit computable formula for $\delta$ is supplied.

The stated reasoning uses Gromov--Hausdorff precompactness and noncollapse: failure of a gap gives nondiffeomorphic pairs at distance tending to zero, a subsequence of pairs converging to the same four-dimensional limit, and hence an interleaved convergent sequence. The quoted sequential stability would make its late terms diffeomorphic, a contradiction. Mere finiteness of the number of smooth types is not by itself enough for this gap; the stronger stability input is essential.

Rescaling by the common diameter bound, $g'=D^{-2}g$, gives the dimensionless parameters
\[
 a=\Lambda D,\qquad\nu=V/D^4.
\]
Thus the unnormalized gap is $D\,\delta(\Lambda D,V/D^4)$. A finite Gromov--Hausdorff covering of $\mathcal M_{a,\nu}$ by balls of radius $\delta/3$ has at most one diffeomorphism type in each ball, since two of its members have distance less than $\delta$. This bounds the number of types by a finite covering number, without giving a numerical bound.
