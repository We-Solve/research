\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Fix an ordinary propositional Frege system $F$: a finite collection of sound axiom schemata and inference-rule schemata, over a fixed finite propositional basis, complete for tautologies. A proof is a finite sequence of formulas obtained from those schemata. Under the total-symbol convention let $|\pi|$ be the number of symbols in the whole proof and define
\[
 s_F(\tau)=\min\{|\pi|:\pi\text{ is an }F\text{-proof of the tautology }\tau\}.
\]
Write $|\tau|$ for formula length. The superpolynomial lower-bound task is to prove that no polynomial bounds all these minimum proof sizes:
\[
 \forall\text{ polynomials }p\quad\exists\tau\text{ a tautology},
 \qquad s_F(\tau)>p(|\tau|).
\]
Equivalently one seeks a sequence of growing tautologies whose required proof sizes escape every fixed polynomial bound. No explicitness or efficient-construction requirement on that sequence is supplied by the source.

The stronger question asks for exponential lower bounds. Under an exponential-in-formula-length interpretation this would mean a constant $c>0$ and tautologies $\tau_j$ with $|\tau_j|\to\infty$ such that
\[
 s_F(\tau_j)\ge2^{c|\tau_j|}.
\]
The source does not fix the size convention or state whether exponential is measured in formula length or a separate indexing parameter; those must be matched in any claimed result. The question concerns unrestricted ordinary Frege proofs, not an unannounced restriction to a bounded-depth subsystem or an expansion to extended Frege.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Find tautologies requiring more than polynomial-size ordinary Frege proofs, and ask whether an exponential lower bound is possible. Proof-size and parameter conventions matter for the stronger claim.

The supplied summary says no superpolynomial lower bound for general Frege is known and contrasts results for resolution, polynomial calculus, and constant-depth Frege. It places the question in NP-versus-coNP proof-complexity context; a lower bound for one specified proof system is not by itself a separation for all propositional proof systems.
