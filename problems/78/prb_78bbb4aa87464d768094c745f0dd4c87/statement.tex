\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
In the usual shadow-problem setting, let $f:M\to\mathbb R^3$ be a sufficiently smooth immersion of a compact connected oriented surface, and let $\nu:M\to S^2$ be its Gauss map. For a direction $u\in S^2$, the open shadow is
\[
S_u=\{p\in M:\langle\nu(p),u\rangle<0\}.
\]
Using the opposite sign gives the same all-directions condition. This is a normal-direction shadow on the parameter surface, not an added cast-shadow/occlusion model.

Does
\[
S_u\text{ is connected for every }u\in S^2
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad f(M)\text{ is a convex surface}
\]
hold in the intended class? A convex surface here means the boundary of a convex body in $\mathbb R^3$, rather than a two-dimensional image being a solid convex subset.

The short source statement supplies neither the immersion assumptions nor the definition of shadows. The definitions above explain the standard Gauss-map interpretation used by its research summary, but the exact regularity, boundary/topology restrictions on $M$, and open-versus-closed shadow convention require checking. Connectedness is retained and is not strengthened to simple connectedness. The supplied summary attributes an affirmative resolution to Ghomi's \emph{Shadows and convexity of surfaces}, without supplying the theorem's full hypotheses.
\subsection{Short English statement}
If every directional shadow of an immersed surface is connected, must its image bound a convex body? The supplied summary reports a Ghomi resolution, but the extract omits the full surface and shadow hypotheses.
