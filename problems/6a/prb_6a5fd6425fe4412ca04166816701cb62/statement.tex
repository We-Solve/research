\section{Classical 3-manifold theory — Question 9.4}\label{10300037}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 10300037 · Topology}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A surface lamination $\Lambda$ is a closed subset of $M$ locally homeomorphic to a product of a disk with a closed transverse set. It is essential when it has no sphere leaf or torus leaf bounding a solid torus, and its complementary regions are irreducible, have incompressible boundary, and have no compressing monogon. Such a monogon is a compressing disk with one boundary arc on a boundary leaf and the other along a transverse interval fiber. It is genuine if some complementary region is not an interval bundle. A sublamination is a closed union of leaves. An alternating knot has a projection with alternating over- and undercrossings along it; a torus knot lies on an unknotted torus. A polygon-bundle complementary region is a bundle over $S^1$ whose fiber is an ideal polygon. A monkey-saddle filling uses saddle leaves asymptotic to consecutive boundary leaves in alternating transverse directions; an even number of sides permits this alternation.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For each non-torus alternating knot $K$, can essential laminations realizing the nontrivial boundary slopes be arranged with an even-sided polygon-bundle complementary region containing $K$, so that every nontrivial surgery on $K$ admits the monkey-saddle filling? The requirement is the extra complementary-region structure and its filling, beyond merely realizing boundary slopes. \sref{10300037}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Strengthen the lamination constructions for non-torus alternating knots so every nontrivial surgery can be filled using an even-sided region and monkey saddles.
