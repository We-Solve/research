\section{Homology-only escape and a strict zero-winding choreography gap}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


\paragraph{Definitions and notation.}In the Newtonian $n$-body problem, positions $q_i\in\mathbb R^d$ and masses $m_i>0$ obey $m_i\ddot q_i=\nabla_{q_i}U$, where $U(q)=\sum_{i<j}m_im_j/|q_i-q_j|$ and the gravitational constant is normalized to one. Here $T=\tfrac12\sum_i m_i|\dot q_i|^2$, $E=T-U$, and a collision has $q_i=q_j$. The center of mass can be fixed at zero; moment of inertia is $I=\sum_i m_i|q_i|^2$. For a fixed period $P$, the action is $\mathcal A(q)=\int_0^P(T+U)\,dt$. A collision-free minimizer in an admissible loop class satisfies the Newtonian equations when variations are permitted. The period, constraints and closure of the class must be specified: a minimizing sequence can escape or approach collisions. For a planar collision-free periodic loop, identify the plane with $\mathbb C$ and define $k_{ij}=\deg(t\mapsto(q_i(t)-q_j(t))/|q_i(t)-q_j(t)|)\in\mathbb Z$. These pairwise winding numbers describe homology information; they do not determine the full braid homotopy class. The figure-eight is the named equal-mass planar three-body choreography. Its pairwise winding triple is $(0,0,0)$. A claim of minimization requires comparison with every admissible loop in the stated class, rather than only symmetric perturbations.

\paragraph{Testing the literal full homology class.}Fix the positive period \(P\) and equal positive masses. Choose any distinct planar points \(a_i\) with center of mass zero. For \(R>0\), the constant loops \(q_i^{(R)}(t)=Ra_i\) are collision free and have each pairwise winding equal to zero. Their kinetic action is zero, and homogeneity of the Newtonian potential gives
\[\mathcal A(q^{(R)})=P\,U(a)/R\longrightarrow0.
\]
Every finite collision-free loop has strictly positive action, since \(U(q(t))>0\) and \(T\ge0\). Consequently the infimum on the entire zero-winding class is zero and is not attained. Any fixed figure-eight orbit has positive action and is beaten by these loops for sufficiently large \(R\).
\paragraph{Boundary and constraint audit.}The competitors are constant loops, not Newtonian solutions. They are nevertheless admissible in action minimization over loops; requiring competitors themselves to solve the equations would change the variational question. If nonconstant loops were demanded, take \(q_i(t)=Ra_i+R^{-1}v_i\sin(2\pi t/P)\), with constants \(v_i\) of zero mass-weighted sum and at least one nonzero. The full loop has least period \(P\). For large \(R\) every pair remains in a disk avoiding zero, so its degree stays zero; kinetic action is \(O(R^{-2})\) and potential action is \(O(R^{-1})\). Thus even a nonconstant least-period requirement does not prevent escape.
