\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.


Let $z\in\mathbb{C}^n$ satisfy $|z_i|=1$ and let $C=zz^*+\sigma W$, where $\sigma\ge0$, $W=W^*$ has independent centered complex Gaussian entries above the diagonal, and the lower triangle is determined by conjugation. The diagonal convention changes the objective only by a constant. The Gaussian variance convention is held fixed. Angular synchronization estimates $z$ from $C$, up to multiplication by a common complex phase. Its maximum-likelihood optimization and semidefinite relaxation are
\[
 \max_{|x_i|=1} x^*Cx,\qquad
 \max\{\operatorname{Tr}(CX):X=X^*,\ X\succeq0,\ X_{ii}=1\}.
\]
The relaxation is tight when it has a rank-one optimum $X=xx^*$, which yields a global optimum of the first problem. Correlation with ground truth means the normalized overlap $|z^*x|/n$; multiplying $x$ by a common phase does not change it. ``With high probability'' refers to the Gaussian draw and means probability tending to one as $n$ grows along a stated noise scaling.

\section{Research attempt: Testing a local optimality certificate}
For a feasible phase vector $x$, suppose $d_i=(Cx)_i\overline{x_i}$ are real and
$S=\operatorname{diag}(d)-C\succeq0$. Then $Sx=0$ and every SDP-feasible $X$ obeys
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(CX)=\sum_i d_i-\operatorname{Tr}(SX)\le\sum_i d_i=x^*Cx.
\]
Indeed $\operatorname{Tr}(SX)=\operatorname{Tr}(S^{1/2}XS^{1/2})\ge0$ by
diagonalizing a positive semidefinite matrix. Thus this is a sufficient certificate.
If $\ker S=\mathbb Cx$, equality forces the range of $X$ into that kernel:
$\operatorname{Tr}(S^{1/2}XS^{1/2})=0$ forces this positive matrix to vanish,
and hence $S^{1/2}X^{1/2}=0$. Its diagonal then forces $X=xx^*$.

The attempted shortcut was to infer positivity of $S$ from a second-order local
maximum with high overlap. Here is an explicit obstruction. Put $n=3$,
\[
 P=I-\tfrac13\mathbf1\mathbf1^*,\quad
 A=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&-1\\-1&0&1\\1&-1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
 S=P+iA,\quad C=\tfrac23 I-S.
\]
Then $C=C^*$, $C_{ii}=0$, and $C\mathbf1=\tfrac23\mathbf1$, so $S$ is precisely
the slack associated to $x=\mathbf1$. For real $h\perp\mathbf1$ and
$x_j(t)=e^{ith_j}$, differentiation gives
\[
 \left.\frac{d^2}{dt^2}x(t)^*Cx(t)\right|_{t=0}
 =-2h^T\operatorname{Re}(S)h=-2\|h\|^2.
\]
The first derivative is zero. Smooth Taylor expansion on the two-dimensional
phase quotient therefore makes $x$ a strict local maximum modulo global phase.
But $A^2=-3P$, $A\mathbf1=0$, and the two eigenvalues of $iA$ on
$\mathbf1^\perp$ are $\sqrt3,-\sqrt3$. Hence $S$ has eigenvalues
$0,1+\sqrt3,1-\sqrt3$ and is not positive semidefinite.

More explicitly, for $\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}$, the vector
$v=(1,\omega,\omega^2)$ has $Av=i\sqrt3v$, so
$Sv=(1-\sqrt3)v$. Thus
\[
 v^*Cv=2-3(1-\sqrt3)>2=\mathbf1^*C\mathbf1.
\]
This even falsifies the intermediate assertion that every strict local maximum
is globally optimal. Taking the putative truth $z=\mathbf1$ gives the local
maximum overlap one. This deterministic example is not a Gaussian-probability
counterexample and $x$ is not the MLE; those distinctions are essential.

Consequently a route from local optimality and overlap to the SDP certificate
needs additional probabilistic control of the imaginary and complex directions
of the slack. Second-order phase tests see only its restriction to real tangent
vectors in the rotated coordinates. They do not provide that control.
