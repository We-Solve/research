\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a prime \(p\), a finite \(p\)-group is a group of order \(p^n\) for some integer \(n\geq 0\). Interpret the source's term ``multiplicator'' in its standard sense as the Schur multiplier \(M(H)=H_2(H,\Z)\). An embedding is an injective group homomorphism.

Find a finite \(p\)-group \(G\) such that, for every finite \(p\)-group \(H\),
\[
M(H)=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{there is no injective homomorphism }G\longrightarrow H.
\]
The same prime \(p\) is used for \(G\) and every candidate ambient group \(H\). The source does not specify whether finding an example for some prime suffices or whether examples are requested for every prime; this quantifier qualification remains unresolved.

The source additionally states that every finite group can be embedded in a group with trivial multiplicator. This is retained as a supplied background assertion; its ambient group is not explicitly required to be finite or a \(p\)-group.
