\section*{Kirby Problem 1.11}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a smooth knot $K\subset S^3$, use the meridian--preferred-longitude basis
$(\mu,\lambda)$ of its exterior boundary. If $r=a/b\in\mathbb Q$ is in lowest
terms, $S^3_r(K)$ denotes Dehn filling along $a\mu+b\lambda$.
A closed oriented $3$-manifold is prime if any expression as a connected sum has
an $S^3$ summand. Thus nonprime means a connected sum of two manifolds different
from $S^3$.

A cable of a knot $J$ is a knot lying on $\partial\nu J$ as a primitive curve
whose absolute longitudinal winding is at least two, pushed slightly into the
solid torus. The parallel copy on that torus gives the cabling annulus in the
exterior of the cable. Its boundary curves specify the cabling slope on
$\partial\nu K$.

The Cabling Conjecture asserts: if $S^3_r(K)$ is nonprime, then $K$ is a nontrivial
cable of some knot $J$, and $r$ is exactly its cabling slope. Describing the slope
by the annulus makes the assertion independent of competing conventions for
ordering the two integer cable parameters.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every nonprime surgery on a knot come from a cable knot filled along its cabling annulus?
