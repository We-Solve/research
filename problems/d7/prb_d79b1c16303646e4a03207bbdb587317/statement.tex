\section{Piltz divisor problem}\label{9400076}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 9400076 · Number Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For $k\ge2$ and $n\in\mathbb N$, let $d_k(n)$ count ordered positive integer tuples $(n_1,\ldots,n_k)$ with $n_1\cdots n_k=n$. Put $D_k(x)=\sum_{n\le x}d_k(n)$ for real $x\ge1$. Define the polynomial $P_k$ of degree $k-1$ by
\[
xP_k(\log x)=\operatorname{Res}_{s=1}\left(\zeta(s)^k\frac{x^s}{s}\right),\qquad
\Delta_k(x)=D_k(x)-xP_k(\log x).
\]
Here $\zeta(s)=\sum_{n\ge1}n^{-s}$ for $\operatorname{Re}s>1$ and then by analytic continuation, and $\operatorname{Res}$ is the Laurent coefficient of $(s-1)^{-1}$. Set
\[
\alpha_k=\inf\{a\in\mathbb R:\forall\varepsilon>0,\ \Delta_k(x)=O_{k,\varepsilon}(x^{a+\varepsilon})\text{ as }x\to\infty\}.
\]
Big-$O$ means an absolute-value bound by a constant, depending on the indicated parameters, for all sufficiently large $x$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Determine $\alpha_k$ for every integer $k\ge2$, equivalently the optimal exponents in the Piltz divisor-error bounds. In particular, is the conjectural value $\alpha_k=(k-1)/(2k)$ correct for every $k\ge2$? \sref{9400076}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Find the sharp growth exponents for the errors in all higher divisor-counting sums, including the predicted exponent for each number of factors.
