\section{Zero Submatrices in Sparse Low-Rank Matrices}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.


Rank is column-space dimension. An all-zero square submatrix chooses equally many rows and columns with every selected entry zero. The hidden constant in the displayed exponent is intended to be absolute or as specified in the source.

The rank of a matrix is the dimension of its column space. Matrix completion reconstructs a low-rank matrix from a subset of its entries; matrix sensing reconstructs it from specified linear measurements. Tensor decomposition represents a multiway array as a sum of simple outer products.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Let $M$ be an $n\times n$ real matrix of rank $r$ with at most $\varepsilon n^2$ nonzero entries, where $0<\varepsilon<1/2$. Must $M$ contain an all-zero square submatrix of size at least $n\exp\!\left(-O(\sqrt{\varepsilon r})\right)$? \sref{statement}{2}
\paragraph{Scope qualification.}
The integer rounding and uniform constant conventions of the lower bound are not specified in the short source statement.

Find a large completely zero square inside every sparse real matrix of the stated rank.
