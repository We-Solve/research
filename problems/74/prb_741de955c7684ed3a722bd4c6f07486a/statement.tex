\section{Numerical invariants — Question 13.1}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A codimension-one foliation $\mathcal F$ decomposes a $3$-manifold $M$ into surface leaves, locally as the horizontal slices of a product chart. In the terminology of the source, it is taut if a closed curve transverse to $\mathcal F$ meets every leaf. A minimal foliation means that every leaf is dense; a minimal surface means a surface with vanishing mean curvature for the specified Riemannian metric. These two uses of minimal are distinct. A manifold is atoroidal if it has no incompressible embedded torus other than a boundary-parallel one. Write $\widetilde M$ for its universal cover, and put a tilde on a foliation or lamination for its lift. For a cooriented codimension-one foliation choose a nowhere-zero $1$-form $\alpha$ with $T\mathcal F=\ker\alpha$. Integrability gives $d\alpha=\alpha\wedge\omega$. The Godbillon--Vey class is $\operatorname{gv}(\mathcal F)=[\omega\wedge d\omega]\in H^3(M;\mathbb R)$, independent of these choices, and its value on an oriented closed $M$ is the integral of this $3$-form.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Let $\mathcal F$ be a minimal taut $C^2$ foliation of an atoroidal oriented closed $3$-manifold $M$, with $\operatorname{gv}(\mathcal F)[M]\ne0$. Can its defining form $\alpha$ and a corresponding $\omega$ satisfying $d\alpha=\alpha\wedge\omega$ be chosen so that the $3$-form $\omega\wedge d\omega$ is everywhere nonnegative or everywhere nonpositive relative to the orientation? This property is called monotone wobble. Nonnegative permits zero loci. \sref{statement}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can a minimal taut foliation with nonzero Godbillon--Vey number be described by a Godbillon--Vey density of one sign everywhere?
