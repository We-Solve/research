\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a positive integer \(n\), equip \(\{-1,1\}^n\) with the uniform probability measure \(\mu_n\). For \(0<\rho<1\), define the usual Boolean noise operator by
\[
(\mathbb T_\rho f)(x)=\mathbb E[f(Y)\mid X=x],
\]
where, conditional on \(X=x\), the coordinates of \(Y\) are independent and satisfy
\[
\Pr(Y_i=x_i)=\frac{1+\rho}{2},\qquad
\Pr(Y_i=-x_i)=\frac{1-\rho}{2}.
\]
These are standard conventions used to make the statement precise; the supplied record does not explicitly define its measure or noise operator.

\textbf{Talagrand's ``Convolution with a Biased Coin'' conjecture, under the dimension-uniform interpretation.} For every fixed \(\rho\in(0,1)\) and every \(\varepsilon>0\), there exists \(T=T(\rho,\varepsilon)>1\) such that, for every positive integer \(n\), every function \(f:\{-1,1\}^n\to[0,\infty)\) satisfying
\[
\mathbb E_{\mu_n}[f]=1,
\]
and every real \(t\ge T\),
\[
\mu_n\bigl(\{x:(\mathbb T_\rho f)(x)\ge t\}\bigr)<\frac{\varepsilon}{t}.
\]
