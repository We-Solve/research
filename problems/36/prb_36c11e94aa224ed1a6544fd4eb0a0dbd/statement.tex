\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 30004388}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A closed smooth manifold means a compact smooth manifold without boundary. A Riemannian metric \(g\) on a smooth manifold \(X\) has positive scalar curvature if its scalar curvature satisfies \(\operatorname{Scal}_g(x)>0\) for every \(x\in X\).

For every integer \(n\geq 5\), let \(M\) be a closed connected smooth \(n\)-manifold, choose a base point \(m_0\in M\), and suppose that \(\pi_1(M,m_0)\) is finite with odd order. Let \(p\colon\widetilde M\to M\) be its universal covering, with the induced smooth structure. Is the following implication always true?
\[
\left(\exists\text{ a smooth Riemannian metric }\widetilde g\text{ on }\widetilde M\quad\forall x\in\widetilde M,\quad\operatorname{Scal}_{\widetilde g}(x)>0\right)
\Longrightarrow
\left(\exists\text{ a smooth Riemannian metric }g\text{ on }M\quad\forall y\in M,\quad\operatorname{Scal}_{g}(y)>0\right).
\]
The metric \(\widetilde g\) in the hypothesis is not assumed invariant under deck transformations, and the conclusion does not require \(p^*g=\widetilde g\).

The restriction \(n\geq5\) follows the supplied corrected statement. The original transcription omitted a dimension restriction; the supplied verification note says that this hypothesis was restored after checking the official report and that the analogous assertion fails in dimension four. \subsection{Short English statement}
For every closed connected smooth manifold of dimension at least five with finite fundamental group of odd order, does the existence of a positive scalar curvature metric on its universal cover imply the existence of such a metric on the manifold itself?
