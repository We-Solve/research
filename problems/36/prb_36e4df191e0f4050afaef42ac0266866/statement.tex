\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Ollivier coarse Ricci curvature requires a metric space $(X,d)$ and a Markov kernel $x\mapsto P_x$ with finite first moments:
\[
\kappa(x,y)=1-\frac{W_1(P_x,P_y)}{d(x,y)}\qquad(x\ne y).
\]
Here $W_p(\mu,\nu)$ is the $p$-Wasserstein distance, defined by minimizing the expected $p$th power of distance over couplings and taking the $p$th root.

Sturm--Lott--Villani curvature instead concerns a metric-measure space $(X,d,m)$ and displacement convexity along $W_2$ geodesics. For example, in the infinite-dimensional convention, $CD(K,\infty)$ requires appropriate endpoint measures to admit a constant-speed $W_2$ geodesic $(\mu_t)_{0\leq t\leq1}$ satisfying
\[
\operatorname{Ent}_m(\mu_t)\leq(1-t)\operatorname{Ent}_m(\mu_0)
+t\operatorname{Ent}_m(\mu_1)-\frac K2t(1-t)W_2(\mu_0,\mu_1)^2,
\]
where $\operatorname{Ent}_m(\rho m)=\int\rho\log\rho\,\mathrm{d}m$, with the usual extended-value convention. Finite-dimensional $CD(K,N)$ conditions additionally use dimension-dependent entropy/distortion inequalities; they are not merely this same inequality with an extra parameter.

The problem asks for relationships, including possible implications and necessary hypotheses, between coarse curvature bounds and displacement-convexity curvature-dimension bounds, particularly for discrete spaces. No specific universal implication or numerical conversion between the bounds is proposed. The metric-measure structure, the transition kernel and its compatibility with $m$, time scaling, and the precise $CD$ convention must be specified before such an implication is meaningful.

The discrete qualification is material. Ordinary finite discrete spaces with their graph metric do not have the nonconstant geodesics required by the classical geodesic displacement-convexity framework. Modified transport metrics or discrete interpolation notions can address different questions. The source does not specify such a modification, so its discrete comparison cannot silently be read as a statement about classical $CD(K,N)$ on the same unmodified graph metric.

How are Markov-kernel contraction curvature and metric-measure displacement-convexity curvature related? Clarify especially which discrete transport geometry and curvature-dimension condition permit a meaningful comparison.
