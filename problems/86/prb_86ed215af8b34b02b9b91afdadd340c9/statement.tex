\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 701285}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(D\) be an arbitrary division ring and let \(V\) be an infinite-dimensional vector space over \(D\). Write \(GL(V)=\operatorname{Aut}_D(V)\) for the group of all invertible \(D\)-linear maps from \(V\) to itself. No restriction on the infinite dimension or on the division ring is stated in the source.

For a group \(H\), define
\[
[x,y]=xyx^{-1}y^{-1},\qquad C(H)=\{[x,y]:x,y\in H\},\qquad [H,H]=\langle C(H)\rangle.
\]
The group \(H\) is perfect if \(H=[H,H]\). For a perfect group, its commutator width is
\[
\operatorname{cw}(H)=\sup_{h\in H}\min\left\{m\in\Z_{\geq 0}:h=\prod_{i=1}^{m}[x_i,y_i]\text{ for some }x_i,y_i\in H\right\},
\]
where the empty product is the identity and an unbounded supremum is \(+\infty\).

The supplied source states as known background that \(GL(V)\) has finite commutator width; under its definition, this includes the assertion that \(GL(V)\) is perfect. This background assertion is reproduced from the source, not independently verified here.

\textbf{Problem (V. Tolstykh, Kourovka Notebook 16.94).} Is it true, for every division ring \(D\) and every infinite-dimensional vector space \(V\) over \(D\), that
\[
\operatorname{cw}(GL(V))=1?
\]
Equivalently, must every \(g\in GL(V)\) admit a representation
\[
g=aba^{-1}b^{-1}\qquad\text{with }a,b\in GL(V)?
\]
\subsection{Short English statement}
For every infinite-dimensional vector space over any division ring, is every invertible linear map a single commutator of invertible linear maps? The source states that a uniform finite bound on the required number of commutators is already known.
