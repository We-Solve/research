\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a topological group \(G\), give each subgroup \(H\leq G\) the subspace topology. Say that \(H\) is \emph{compactly covered} if
\[
H=\bigcup\{K\leq H:K\text{ is compact}\}.
\]
Equivalently, every element of \(H\) belongs to some compact subgroup of \(H\). Such a subgroup \(H\) is \emph{maximal compactly covered} in \(G\) if, for every compactly covered subgroup \(L\leq G\),
\[
H\leq L\quad\Longrightarrow\quad L=H.
\]
Maximality here is with respect to inclusion among all compactly covered subgroups, without assuming that they are closed.

\textbf{Question (Yu. N. Mukhin).} Let \(G\) be a null-dimensional locally compact group. Must every maximal compactly covered subgroup \(H\leq G\) satisfy
\[
\overline{H}^{\,G}=H?
\]
The source does not define ``null-dimensional'' or explicitly state separation conventions. With the conventional reading that \(G\) is Hausdorff and that null-dimensional means having a base of sets that are both open and closed, this asks whether the displayed assertion holds for every such \(G\) and every such \(H\). These conventions require confirmation against the source; no countability or metrizability hypothesis is supplied.
