\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
The source, attributed to Conlon--Fox--Sudakov, asks whether $r_f(H,H)\leq c_k(d)n$ for all $n$-vertex $d$-degenerate $k$-uniform hypergraphs, ``in the above sense''. Neither the notation $r_f$ nor that preceding definition is included. The aid interprets the intended question using ordinary Ramsey numbers and skeletal degeneracy; this is made explicit rather than silently identifying undefined notation.

For a $k$-uniform hypergraph $H$, $k\geq2$, its skeleton is the graph on $V(H)$ joining two distinct vertices when they belong to a common hyperedge. Its skeletal degeneracy is the graph degeneracy
\[
 d_{\mathrm{sk}}(H)=\max_{\varnothing\neq U\subseteq V(H)}
      \delta(\operatorname{sk}(H)[U]).
\]
Let $r(H;q)$ be the least $N$ such that every $q$-coloring of the edges of the complete $k$-uniform hypergraph on $N$ vertices contains a monochromatic copy of $H$. The supplied summary attributes to Fox--Sankar--Simkin--Tidor--Zhou the affirmative theorem: for fixed $k,d,q$ there exists $C(k,d,q)$ such that
\[
 d_{\mathrm{sk}}(H)\leq d\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 r(H;q)\leq C(k,d,q)|V(H)|.
\]
This is the stated resolution under the aid's intended reading; it does not define an arbitrary separate $f$-dependent Ramsey parameter.

The aid reports exact preservation under disjoint union:
\[
 d_{\mathrm{sk}}(H_1\sqcup\cdots\sqcup H_q)
 =\max_i d_{\mathrm{sk}}(H_i).
\]
Consequently, for fixed $k,d,q$ and color-specific targets of skeletal degeneracy at most $d$,
\[
 r(H_1,\ldots,H_q)\leq A(k,d,q)\sum_i|V(H_i)|,
\]
where the asymmetric Ramsey number requires an $H_i$ in color $i$ for some $i$. A monochromatic copy of the disjoint union in color $i$ contains that target, so a diagonal theorem for the union gives the transfer.

Likewise, for every integer $t\geq1$,
\[
 r(tH;q)\leq B(k,d,q)t|V(H)|,
\]
where $tH$ is a disjoint union of $t$ copies. Thus the packing is vertex disjoint and all copies have the same color; the constant is independent of $t$. This is not a bounded-$t$ assertion with a hidden multiplicity-dependent constant.

Finally, the aid works out that a loose $k$-uniform forest with at least one edge has skeletal degeneracy exactly $k-1$, using the incidence-acyclic convention for a loose forest. Isolated vertices do not change that value; an entirely edgeless hypergraph instead has degeneracy zero. This example illustrates the fixed-parameter theorem and does not replace skeletal degeneracy by an unspecified weaker notion of hypergraph degeneracy.
