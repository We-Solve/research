\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 8800039}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Fix an elliptic curve \(E/\F_q\), and for \(n\ge1\) put
\[
N_n=\#E(\F_{q^n}),\qquad Q_n=N_n/N_1.
\]
The inclusion \(E(\F_q)\subseteq E(\F_{q^n})\) makes \(Q_n\) a positive integer. Prime means an ordinary positive rational prime; \(Q_1=1\) is not prime. The proposed conclusion is
\[
\forall B\ge1\ \exists n>B,\qquad Q_n\text{ is prime}.
\]
The source does not state whether the claim is for every fixed curve or only fixed curves satisfying an unstated admissibility condition.

For \(t=q+1-N_1\), let \(\alpha,\bar\alpha\) be the roots of \(z^2-tz+q\). The usual order formula gives
\[
Q_n=\frac{(1-\alpha^n)(1-\bar\alpha^n)}{(1-\alpha)(1-\bar\alpha)}.
\]
Exceptional Frobenius behavior matters. If \(\alpha=\bar\alpha=a\in\Z\), then
\[
Q_n=(1+a+\cdots+a^{n-1})^2,
\]
so none of these integers is prime. The scalar-Frobenius/supersingular case therefore cannot simply be included in a universal assertion. No condition excluding this or other systematic factorization obstructions is supplied, and an ordinary-curve restriction alone is not invented as a sufficient replacement.

The supplied research summary calls the intended problem a Koblitz/Mersenne-type prime-value conjecture and reports no proof. That assessment does not repair the absent curve hypotheses or certify a version for every \(E\). The draft preserves the infinitude target while marking the admissible fixed-curve class for review.
\subsection{Short English statement}
For the intended fixed elliptic curve, are the extension-order quotients prime infinitely often? The curve class needs exclusions: scalar Frobenius makes every quotient a square, so the unrestricted every-curve reading cannot stand.
