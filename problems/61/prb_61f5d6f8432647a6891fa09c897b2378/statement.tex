\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A CAT(0) space is a geodesic metric space satisfying the Euclidean comparison inequality for geodesic triangles. A triplane is the metric space obtained by identifying the boundary lines of three Euclidean half-planes, with its induced length metric. In the wording of this source, $X$ has \emph{isolated flats} if it contains no isometrically embedded triplane; this source-specific definition is retained.

Suppose $G$ acts by isometries, properly discontinuously and cocompactly on such a CAT(0) space $X$, and $H\leq G$ is finitely generated. An orbit $Hx$, with $x\in X$, is quasiconvex if there is $K\geq0$ such that every geodesic segment with endpoints in $Hx$ is contained in
\[
N_K(Hx)=\{y\in X:d_X(y,Hx)\leq K\}.
\]
With word metrics $d_H,d_G$ from finite generating sets, the inclusion is a quasi-isometric embedding when there exist $A\geq1$ and $B\geq0$ such that, for all $h,k\in H$,
\[
A^{-1}d_H(h,k)-B\leq d_G(h,k)\leq A d_H(h,k)+B.
\]
\textbf{Conjecture (Dani Wise).} For every $G,X,H$ as above, $H\hookrightarrow G$ is a quasi-isometric embedding if and only if there exist $x\in X$ and $K\geq0$ for which $Hx$ is quasiconvex in the displayed sense. The source does not explicitly state the properness assumptions on $X$ normally accompanying geometric actions; the exact scope of the reported theorem and the source's no-triplane definition must be checked together.

The supplied research summary reports this conjecture proved by Hruska--Kleiner, building on Hruska, with the two-dimensional case established earlier. It identifies the implication from undistortion to orbit quasiconvexity as the substantive direction and states that it fails without the isolated-flats hypothesis, citing Wise's $F_2\times\mathbb Z$ example.
\subsection{Short English statement}
For a cocompact properly discontinuous CAT(0) action with the source's isolated-flats condition, a finitely generated subgroup should be undistorted exactly when one of its orbits is quasiconvex. The record reports an affirmative theorem.
