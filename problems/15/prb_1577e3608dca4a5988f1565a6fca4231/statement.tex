\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 701160}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Fix a prime number \(p\). For a finite group \(G\), define its number of conjugacy classes by
\[
k(G)=\left|\left\{\{gxg^{-1}:g\in G\}:x\in G\right\}\right|.
\]
Use \(\log\) for the natural logarithm. The source does not specify a logarithm base; the requested zero limit is independent of the choice of any fixed base greater than \(1\).

Does there exist a sequence \((P_n)_{n\geq 1}\) of finite groups such that
\[
|P_n|=p^n\quad\text{for every integer }n\geq 1,
\qquad
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log k(P_n)}{\sqrt n}=0?
\]
The prime \(p\) remains fixed throughout the sequence. No inclusions or homomorphisms between the groups \(P_n\) are required.

The supplied source adds that J. M. Riedl constructed \(p\)-groups for which the corresponding limit equals \(2\log p\). This comparison is retained as a source-reported result, not independently verified here.
\subsection{Short English statement}
For a fixed prime \(p\), can one choose a group of order \(p^n\) for every positive integer \(n\) so that the logarithm of its number of conjugacy classes, divided by \(\sqrt n\), tends to zero? The supplied registry labels the problem open; this draft does not independently verify that status.
