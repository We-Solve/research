\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A smooth Riemannian metric \(g\) on a manifold \(M\) is a shrinking Ricci soliton if there exist a smooth vector field \(X\) and a constant \(\lambda>0\) such that
\[
\operatorname{Ric}_g+\frac12\mathcal L_Xg=\lambda g,
\]
where \(\operatorname{Ric}_g\) is the Ricci tensor and \(\mathcal L_Xg\) is the Lie derivative of the metric. In the gradient case, \(X=\nabla_g f\) for a smooth function \(f:M\to\R\), and the equation becomes
\[
\operatorname{Ric}_g+\nabla_g^2 f=\lambda g.
\]
The supplied record does not specify whether only gradient solitons are intended.

A metric \(g\) is K\"ahler if there is a complex structure \(J\) on \(M\) compatible with \(g\) and parallel for its Levi--Civita connection:
\[
J^2=-\operatorname{Id},\qquad g(Ju,Jv)=g(u,v),\qquad \nabla^gJ=0.
\]
Under this metric interpretation, non-K\"ahler means that no such \(J\) exists. The source does not specify whether a complex structure is instead part of the initial data.
