\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 1100806}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Use integral cohomological dimension: \(\operatorname{cd}(K)=\operatorname{pd}_{\Z K}\Z\), the projective dimension of the trivial module, with value \(\infty\) if there is no finite projective resolution. A group is torsion-free if its identity is its only finite-order element. Talelli's existence question is
\[
\exists G\ \exists n_0\in\Z_{\ge0}:\quad
G\text{ torsion-free},\quad \operatorname{cd}(G)=\infty,\quad
\forall H\le G,\quad
\operatorname{cd}(H)<\infty\ \Longrightarrow\ \operatorname{cd}(H)\le n_0.
\]
There is no finite-generation hypothesis in the question, and the subgroup quantifier includes \(H=G\), whose antecedent is false.

The supplied research summary reports an affirmative answer with the optimal value \(n_0=1\). It attributes to Fournier-Facio--Sun, Theorem 5.7, continuum many pairwise nonisomorphic finitely generated simple torsion-free groups of infinite cohomological dimension whose every proper nontrivial subgroup is infinite cyclic. For these reported examples, finite-dimensional subgroups are therefore trivial or infinite cyclic, of integral cohomological dimension \(0\) or \(1\), respectively. The summary describes the construction as small cancellation over acylindrically hyperbolic groups with Cohen--Lyndon Dehn filling for homological control. It additionally reports consequences for Petrosyan's 2007 no-jump conjecture over all coefficient rings and fixed points for actions on finite-dimensional contractible CW-complexes. These collateral claims are retained as supplied literature descriptions; they do not change the integral-coefficient existence statement above.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is there a torsion-free group of infinite cohomological dimension for which the finite cohomological dimensions of all subgroups have a common finite upper bound? The supplied research summary reports such examples with bound \(1\).
