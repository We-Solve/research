\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a group \(G\), write \(Z(G)=\{z\in G:zg=gz\text{ for all }g\in G\}\) for its centre. An \(FC\)-group is a group in which every conjugacy class \(\{xgx^{-1}:x\in G\}\), for \(g\in G\), is finite. For a group \(H\), its derived subgroup is \(H'=\langle a^{-1}b^{-1}ab:a,b\in H\rangle\). Say that \(G\) is residually in the class of groups with finite derived subgroups if, for every \(g\in G\setminus\{1\}\), there exists a normal subgroup \(N\trianglelefteq G\) such that \(g\notin N\) and \((G/N)'\) is finite. The quotient \(G/N\) itself need not be finite.

\textbf{Question.} For every \(FC\)-group \(G\) that is residually in the class of groups with finite derived subgroups, do there exist an index set \(I\), finite groups \(F_i\) for \(i\in I\), and an injective group homomorphism
\[
G/Z(G)\longrightarrow\prod_{i\in I}F_i?
\]
Here the product is the full direct product; no finiteness or countability restriction on \(I\), and no finite generation or countability assumption on \(G\), is stated in the supplied record. The record contains conflicting proposer attributions, detailed below; this leaves the mathematical question unchanged.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Must the quotient by the centre of every \(FC\)-group that is residually a group with finite derived subgroup embed in a direct product of finite groups? Each target factor must be finite, although the groups witnessing the residual hypothesis may be infinite.
