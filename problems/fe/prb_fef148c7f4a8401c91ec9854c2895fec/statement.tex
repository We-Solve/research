\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A lattice polytope in \(\R^n\) is the convex hull of a finite nonempty subset of \(\Z^n\). For such a polytope \(P\), write \(d=\dim P\), and let \(kP=\{kp:p\in P\}\). Use the ambient lattice \(\Z^n\), including when \(P\) has smaller affine dimension. The polytope \(P\) is \emph{normal} if, for every integer \(k\geq 1\),
\[
 kP\cap\Z^n
 =\{u_1+\cdots+u_k:u_1,\ldots,u_k\in P\cap\Z^n\}.
\]
It is \emph{nonnormal} if this equality fails for some \(k\geq 1\).

For a vertex \(v\) of \(P\), define its tangent cone and its monoid of lattice-point differences by
\[
 C_v(P)=\{t(p-v):t\geq 0,\ p\in P\},\qquad
 M_v(P)=\left\{\sum_{u\in P\cap\Z^n}m_u(u-v):m_u\in\Z_{\geq 0}\right\}.
\]
Use the convention that \(P\) is \emph{very ample} if
\[
 M_v(P)=C_v(P)\cap\Z^n
 \quad\text{for every vertex }v\text{ of }P.
\]
A \(d\)-dimensional polytope is \emph{simple} if exactly \(d\) edges meet at each vertex. A lattice \emph{simplex} is the convex hull of \(d+1\) affinely independent lattice points. These definitions make explicit the lattice convention used in this draft; the supplied record does not itself define normality or very ampleness.

\item For every dimension \(n\geq 1\) and every Euclidean ball \(B\subset\R^n\) for which \(B\cap\Z^n\neq\varnothing\), is

\end{enumerate}
