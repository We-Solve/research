\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(M\) be a compact manifold and \(\mathbb T=\R/\Z\). In the usual Tonelli interpretation, \(L:TM\times\mathbb T\to\R\) is time-periodic, fiberwise strictly convex and superlinear, with the required complete Euler--Lagrange flow. For \(c\in H^1(M;\R)\), choose a closed form \(\eta_c\) representing it and let \(\mathcal M_c\) be the invariant probability measures minimizing \(\int(L-\eta_c(v))\,\mathrm d\mu\). Its minimum is \(-\alpha(c)\). The Mather set is the union of supports of these minimizing measures. Ergodic minimizing measures are the extreme invariant components. Write \(b_1(M)=\dim H^1(M;\R)\).

\textbf{Problem 21.} The source attributes generic uniqueness of the minimizing measure, for generic \(L\) and generic \(c\), to Ma\~n\'e. Must this unique measure generically be supported on a periodic orbit? It separately records Jeff Xia's conjecture: for generic \(L\), the number of ergodic minimizing measures is finite for \emph{every} \(c\in H^1(M;\R)\). Genericity requires a topology and perturbation category; generic \(c\), generic pairs and a residual set of \(L\) with residual sets of classes are not automatically the same formulation.

\textbf{Reported rational-locking obstruction.} For a fixed time-periodic \(L\), a periodic orbit of period an integer \(q>0\) has rotation vector \(h\in H_1(M;\Q)\), viewed in real homology. Its measure being \(c\)-minimizing implies \(h\in\partial\alpha(c)\). Define the closed rational subgradient fibers
\[
 F_h=\{c:h\in\partial\alpha(c)\},\qquad h\in H_1(M;\Q).
\]
If periodic minimizing measures exist for a residual set of \(c\) in a nonempty open region \(U\subseteq H^1(M;\R)\), the aid reports that
\[
 \bigcup_{h\in H_1(M;\Q)}\operatorname{int}_U(F_h\cap U)
 \quad\text{is dense in }U.
\]
If \(U\) is convex and \(\alpha\) is strictly convex there, each \(F_h\cap U\) contains at most one point. Hence periodic minimizing classes in \(U\) are at most countable, and are meagre when \(b_1(M)>0\). The positive-dimensional qualification is essential: a singleton zero-dimensional cohomology space is not meagre in itself. This is a necessary obstruction for a fixed \(L\), not a proof that the Lagrangian-generic periodicity question is false.

\textbf{Reported flat-torus calibration.} For the time-periodic extension of \(L(x,v)=|v|^2/2\) on \(M=\mathbb T^n\), \(n\geq1\), the exact model has \(\alpha(c)=|c|^2/2\), and minimizing measures are supported on \(v=c\). When
\[
 k\cdot c+m\ne0\quad\text{for every }(k,m)\in\Z^n\times\Z\setminus\{(0,0)\},
\]
the translation with frequency \((c,1)\) on the extended \((x,t)\)-torus is uniquely ergodic; the unique minimizing measure is Haar measure on that torus at \(v=c\), and is nonperiodic. Such classes form a residual full-Lebesgue-measure set. This explicitly distinguishes uniqueness from periodic support for a fixed integrable example; it is not a generic perturbation theorem. All supplementary results require independent verification.
