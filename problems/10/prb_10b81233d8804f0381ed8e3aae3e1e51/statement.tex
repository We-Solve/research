A complex \(C^*\)-algebra \(A\) is separable if it has a countable norm-dense subset. Interpret amenability in its standard \(C^*\)-algebra sense, equivalently nuclearity: for every finite subset \(F\subseteq A\) and every \(\varepsilon>0\), there are an integer \(n\geq 1\) and completely positive contractive linear maps
\[
A\xrightarrow{\phi}M_n(\C)\xrightarrow{\psi}A
\]
such that \(\|\psi(\phi(a))-a\|<\varepsilon\) for all \(a\in F\). A linear map is completely positive if all its matrix amplifications preserve positivity, and contractive if its norm is at most one.

The primitive spectrum of \(A\) is
\[
\operatorname{Prim}(A)=\{\ker\pi:\pi\text{ is a nonzero irreducible }*\text{-representation of }A\}.
\]
Here irreducibility means that the representation has no proper nonzero closed invariant subspace. Equip \(\operatorname{Prim}(A)\) with the hull--kernel topology, whose closed sets are
\[
\operatorname{hull}(I)=\{P\in\operatorname{Prim}(A):I\subseteq P\},
\]
where \(I\) ranges over norm-closed two-sided ideals of \(A\).
