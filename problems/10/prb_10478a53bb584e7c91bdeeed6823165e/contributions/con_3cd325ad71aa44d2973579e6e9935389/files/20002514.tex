\paragraph{A Mellin representation under explicit growth hypotheses.}Let \(f(\tau)=\sum_{n\ge1}a_ne^{2\pi in\tau}\), with \(|a_n|\le Cn^A\), \(A\ge0\). For \(\operatorname{Re}s>A+1\), absolute convergence permits termwise integration and the substitution \(u=2\pi nt\):
\[\int_0^\infty f(it)t^{s-1}dt=(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)\sum_{n\ge1}a_nn^{-s}.
\]
Indeed the sum of the absolute integrals is bounded by a constant times \(\sum n^{A-\operatorname{Re}s}\). This gives an analytic starting half-plane for an L-series, whether or not the function is modular.
\paragraph{A functional-equation obstruction.}Suppose a completion \(\widehat f=f+R\) has a prescribed transformation under \(t\mapsto1/t\). Splitting its Mellin integral at one and changing variables in the lower interval gives a relation involving both \(f\) and \(R\). Dropping \(R\) discards the Mellin transform of the nonholomorphic correction. Without proving that correction vanishes or has a controlled continuation, a functional equation for the holomorphic-coefficient Dirichlet series does not follow. Mixed products can also alter growth at cusps, so the polynomial bound above must be checked rather than assumed universally.
\paragraph{Arithmetic check and gap.}An Euler product with normalized coefficient \(a_1=1\) forces multiplicativity \(a_{mn}=a_ma_n\) for coprime \(m,n\), by multiplying its prime-power factors. Neither the Mellin identity nor the existence of a modular completion proves that condition. The first missing step for a concrete mixed mock form is to calculate its completion/cusp corrections and coefficient arithmetic. This elementary framework supplies no universal Euler product or functional equation and does not pretend the source asked for one fixed theorem.
