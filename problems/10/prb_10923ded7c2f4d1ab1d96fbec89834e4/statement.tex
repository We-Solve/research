\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 10900056}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a group $G$, its profinite completion is
\[
 \widehat G=\varprojlim_{N\triangleleft G,\,[G:N]<\infty}G/N,
\]
with its profinite topology. The completion of a manifold in this question is understood to mean that of its fundamental group, without additional marked peripheral subgroups or slopes unless specified.

Let $M$ be a compact $3$-manifold with torus boundary whose interior is hyperbolic. In the usual $S^3$-knot interpretation, being a knot complement means that $M$ is homeomorphic to an exterior $S^3\setminus\operatorname{int}\nu K$ of a knot $K\subset S^3$; the corresponding open complement is its interior.

Dunfield's item 8.2 asks whether this property is determined by $\widehat{\pi_1(M)}$. Within the stated class, the detection assertion reads: for every such pair $M,N$,
\[
 \widehat{\pi_1(M)}\cong\widehat{\pi_1(N)}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \bigl(M\text{ is an }S^3\text{-knot exterior}\iff
 N\text{ is an }S^3\text{-knot exterior}\bigr),
\]
where the isomorphism of completions is continuous. This is detection of a property, not necessarily full homeomorphism classification of knot exteriors.

The excerpt does not explicitly state the ambient $S^3$ convention, whether there is exactly one boundary torus, completeness/finite volume of the interior, orientability, or the comparison class for $N$. Those choices require source confirmation. The registry's reported detection of some boundary and knot invariants is not, by itself, detection of the $S^3$-knot-exterior property.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can the profinite completion of a hyperbolic manifold group detect whether the manifold is a knot exterior in $S^3$? The ambient and comparison-class conventions need confirmation.
