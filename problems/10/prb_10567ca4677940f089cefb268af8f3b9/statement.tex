\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(k\) be a field and \(A\) a unital associative \(k\)-algebra. Its center is
\[
Z(A)=\{z\in A:za=az\text{ for every }a\in A\}.
\]
Under the interpretation that all algebra automorphisms are allowed, define the ozone group by
\[
\operatorname{Oz}(A)=\operatorname{Aut}_{Z(A)}(A)
=\{\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}_{k\text{-alg}}(A):\sigma(z)=z\text{ for every }z\in Z(A)\}.
\]
This gives an explicit interpretation of the supplied notation \(\operatorname{Gal}(A/Z(A))\). Triviality means
\[
\operatorname{Oz}(A)=\{\operatorname{id}_A\}.
\]
The record does not specify whether automorphisms must preserve a grading.

For clarity, one standard convention calls \(A\) \(d\)-Calabi--Yau, for an integer \(d\geq 0\), if it is homologically smooth and
\[
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{A^e}(A,A^e)\cong
\begin{cases}
0,&i\ne d,\\
A,&i=d,
\end{cases}
\]
as \(A\)-bimodules, using the usual identification of the resulting module structures, where \(A^e=A\otimes_k A^{\mathrm{op}}\). Homological smoothness means that \(A\), as an \(A^e\)-module, admits a bounded resolution by finitely generated projective modules. This convention is supplied to explain the terminology; the record does not specify whether it intends this ungraded definition or a graded variant, nor whether additional finiteness or domain hypotheses apply.

An algebra is PI if there are an integer \(m\geq1\) and a nonzero polynomial \(f\in k\langle x_1,\ldots,x_m\rangle\) such that \(f(a_1,\ldots,a_m)=0\) for every \((a_1,\ldots,a_m)\in A^m\). In a standard connected graded convention, an Artin--Schelter regular algebra is a locally finite \(\N\)-graded algebra \(A\) with \(A_0=k\), finite global dimension \(n\), finite Gelfand--Kirillov dimension, and the Artin--Schelter Gorenstein condition

\begin{cases}0,&i\ne n,\\k(\ell_L),&i=n,\end{cases}
\qquad
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{A^{\mathrm{op}}}(k,A)\cong
\begin{cases}0,&i\ne n,\\k(\ell_R),&i=n,\end{cases}
