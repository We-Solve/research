\subsection{A second-moment route for a precisely defined spread model}
The workshop discusses several sunflower variants and applications. Rather than silently repair the direction of containment in an extracted robustness definition, we specify our own auxiliary random-containment experiment. Let $\mathcal F$ be a nonempty family of $k$-element subsets of a finite universe, $k\ge1$. Suppose a uniform $B\in\mathcal F$ satisfies
\[
\Pr(S\subseteq B)\le r^{-|S|}\quad\hbox{for every subset }S,
\]
where $r>0$. Let $R$ include each universe element independently with probability $0<p\le1$, and let $Z$ count members of $\mathcal F$ contained in $R$.
\begin{proposition}
The containment probability obeys
\[
\Pr(Z>0)\ge\left(1+\frac{1-p}{pr}\right)^{-k}
\ge 1-\frac{k(1-p)}{pr}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The mean is $|\mathcal F|p^k$, and independence gives
\[
\frac{\mathbb EZ^2}{(\mathbb EZ)^2}
=\mathbb E_{A,B\in\mathcal F}p^{-|A\cap B|}.
\]
Fix $A$. Expand the product over its elements:
\[
\mathbb E_B p^{-|A\cap B|}
=\sum_{S\subseteq A}(p^{-1}-1)^{|S|}\Pr(S\subseteq B)
\le\sum_{S\subseteq A}\left(\frac{1-p}{pr}\right)^{|S|}
=\left(1+\frac{1-p}{pr}\right)^k.
\]
All terms are nonnegative. Cauchy--Schwarz applied to $Z\mathbf1_{Z>0}$ gives $(\mathbb EZ)^2\le\mathbb EZ^2\Pr(Z>0)$, proving the first bound. For $u\ge0$, the derivative of $(1+u)^{-k}-(1-ku)$ is $k[1-(1+u)^{-k-1}]\ge0$, proving the second.
\end{proof}
For $0<\varepsilon<1$, the elementary sufficient condition $pr\ge k/\varepsilon$ therefore gives failure probability at most $\varepsilon$. This is a weak spread-to-containment bound, not an improvement of modern sunflower thresholds. For example, all two-element subsets of a six-element universe satisfy the spread condition with $r=3$. At $p=1/2$, the first bound is $9/16$, while the exact containment probability is $57/64$.
The attempted route toward sharper applications stops at the product bound: it uses only the separate upper bounds for each intersection event, discarding dependencies that stronger sunflower arguments exploit. Moreover, obtaining a suitably spread subfamily or core from an arbitrary family has not been proved here. The displayed estimate alone does not supply that extraction step.
