\subsection{The direct random-coherence route loses a logarithm}
The primary report already discusses deterministic constructions close to the square-root scale and asks for a stronger polylogarithmic-regime result. We test a direct sign-matrix and exhaustive-search route. Let $n\ge1$, $p\ge2$ be integers and let $A$ have independent entries $\pm1/\sqrt n$ with equal probabilities. Its columns have norm one.
\begin{proposition}
With probability at least $1/2$, all distinct column inner products have magnitude at most
\[
\mu=\sqrt{\frac{2\log(2p(p-1))}{n}}.
\]
Such a matrix has RIP distortion at most $(s-1)\mu$ at order $s$ whenever this expression is below one.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For a fixed pair of columns their inner product is $n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^n X_j$, where the $X_j$ are independent uniform signs. The series comparison $(2k)!\ge2^k k!$ gives $\cosh t\le\exp(t^2/2)$. Thus Markov's inequality, independence and $t=\mu$ yield
\[
\Pr\left\{n^{-1}\sum X_j>\mu\right\}
\le\exp(-nt\mu)(\cosh t)^n\le\exp(-n\mu^2/2).
\]
The lower tail has the same bound. Summing over $p(p-1)/2$ pairs gives a failure probability at most $p(p-1)\exp(-n\mu^2/2)=1/2$. For a vector supported on at most $s$ columns, expansion of its squared output norm bounds the error by $\mu\sum_{i\ne j}|x_ix_j|\le\mu(s-1)\|x\|_2^2$, again by Cauchy--Schwarz.
\end{proof}
For fixed desired $0<\delta<1$, this proves only
\[
s-1\le\delta\sqrt{\frac{n}{2\log(2p(p-1))}}.
\]
The proved guarantee is smaller than a fixed multiple of $\sqrt n$ by a factor growing as $\sqrt{\log p}$. This is a limitation of this elementary argument; it is not an upper bound on the achievable RIP order of random matrices.
\subsection{A finite search is not the requested explicit construction}
The probabilistic proof implies at least one acceptable sign matrix, so one may enumerate all $2^{np}$ sign patterns and compute every correlation. The verification can avoid irrational entries by using the integer unnormalized column dot products. This gives a finite deterministic search with an exponential worst-case bound, not a polynomial construction. The search space and the logarithmic loss remain even when $n=(\log p)^{O(1)}$. Thus substituting exhaustive search for randomness does not finish the target.
