\paragraph{Definitions and notation.}A mathematical law for a biological model is a relation between specified state variables, parameters and their evolution, accompanied by a domain of validity. Universality would mean applicability across a declared class of biological systems. The challenge asks whether principles with explanatory and predictive roles comparable to physical laws can be identified; the system class and standard of validation remain to be formulated.

\paragraph{Testing a candidate universal monotonicity law.}Let \(x_i(t)>0\), \(1\le i\le m\), be frequencies with \(\sum_i x_i=1\), and assign fixed reproductive rates \(a_i\in\mathbb R\). The replicator equations are \(\dot x_i=x_i(a_i-\bar a)\), where \(\bar a=\sum_i x_i a_i\). Summing proves preservation of total frequency; the explicit solution is \(x_i(t)=x_i(0)e^{a_it}/\sum_jx_j(0)e^{a_jt}\), so positivity is preserved. Direct differentiation gives
\[\dot{\bar a}=\sum_i x_i a_i^2-\bar a^2=\sum_i x_i(a_i-\bar a)^2\ge0.\]
Thus average reproductive rate increases in this sharply specified constant-environment model, and equality occurs precisely when all currently supported types have equal rates.
\paragraph{An explicit failure beyond that class.}Permit a common environmental decline \(a_i(t)=b_i-t\). The common \(-t\) cancels from all frequency equations, so frequency trajectories are exactly those generated by the constants \(b_i\). Nevertheless
\[\bar a(t)=\sum_i x_i(t)b_i-t,\qquad \dot{\bar a}=\operatorname{Var}_{x(t)}(b)-1.\]
Choosing all \(b_i=0\) gives constant frequencies and \(\bar a(t)=-t\), strictly decreasing. Even choosing unequal rates in an interval of length less than one makes the variance at most one quarter, by centering at the interval midpoint, so the average rate still decreases. The failure is not caused by extinction of types or a boundary singularity.
\paragraph{Programme-level obstruction.}A frequency law alone is invariant under adding the same time-dependent rate to every type; absolute mean growth is not. Consequently one cannot infer a universal mean-fitness increase principle from frequency selection dynamics without fixing an environmental convention and admissible coupling. This countercheck narrows a natural candidate law but does not identify the requested general biological principles. The system class and validation standard, left open by the supplied source, remain essential missing inputs; the unavailable historical mirror has not been independently recovered.
