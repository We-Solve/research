\section{Sensitivity Growth Under Boolean Conjunctions}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.


The sensitivity of $f$ at $x$ is the number of coordinate flips changing $f(x)$. A conjunction outputs one exactly when every selected Boolean function outputs one.

A Boolean function maps $\{0,1\}^n$ to $\{0,1\}$. A Boolean circuit is an acyclic network of logical gates; a formula is a circuit with no sharing of intermediate gate outputs. Size and depth measure its number of gates and longest gate path.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
If Boolean functions $f_1,\ldots,f_n$ have average maximal sensitivity $O((\log n)^d)$, and each $g_i$ is the conjunction of a subset of the $f_j$, must the average maximal sensitivity of the $g_i$ be $O((\log n)^{d+1})$? \href{https://doi.org/10.4171/owr/2009/52}{[S2]}

The averaging measure and the precise average maximal sensitivity functional must be taken from the report; ordinary average sensitivity is not substituted.

\section{Literature and context check}
The primary Rossman report on printed page 2825 defines average maximal sensitivity as $\mathbb E_x\max_j\operatorname{sens}(f_j,x)$ under uniform Boolean inputs. This differs from both maximum sensitivity and the average of the individual average sensitivities. The attempt uses this exact functional. It proves the conjectured logarithmic growth for coordinate functions and shows that a logarithmic loss is necessary in that subclass; the general function family remains untreated.
\section{Research attempt: A sharp logarithmic coordinate-function case}

\paragraph{A general pointwise bound and its limitation}
Write $A(x)=\max_j\operatorname{sens}(f_j,x)$. If $g=\bigwedge_{j\in S}f_j$ and $g(x)=0$, choose any $j\in S$ with $f_j(x)=0$. Every flip changing $g$ to one changes that particular $f_j$, so $\operatorname{sens}(g,x)\leq A(x)$. If $g(x)=1$, a flip changing $g$ must change at least one selected function, giving
\[
 \operatorname{sens}(g,x)\leq\sum_{j\in S}\operatorname{sens}(f_j,x)\leq |S|A(x).
\]
Empty conjunctions are constant and have sensitivity zero. Thus conjunction width at most $w\geq1$ gives $\operatorname{ams}(g_1,\ldots,g_n)\leq w\operatorname{ams}(f_1,\ldots,f_n)$. The source allows width as large as $n$; this bound does not prove the requested logarithmic loss.

\paragraph{An upper bound for all conjunctions of input coordinates}
Take $f_j(x)=x_j$ on $\{0,1\}^n$, so $A(x)=1$. Each output is a conjunction on some coordinate subset of size $w_i$. If its value is zero, its sensitivity is at most one. If its value is one, its sensitivity is exactly $w_i$, and that event has probability $2^{-w_i}$. Let $Z(x)=\max_i\operatorname{sens}(g_i,x)$. For every integer $k\geq2$,
\[
 \Pr(Z\geq k)\leq\sum_{i:w_i\geq k}2^{-w_i}\leq n2^{-k}.
\]
Using the identity $\mathbb EZ=\sum_{k=1}^n\Pr(Z\geq k)$ and splitting at $K=\lceil\log_2n\rceil+1$ gives
\[
 \operatorname{ams}(g_1,\ldots,g_n)
 \leq K+\sum_{k=K+1}^{\infty}n2^{-k}
 =K+n2^{-K}\leq\lceil\log_2n\rceil+\tfrac32.
\]
The same inequality holds at $n=1$ directly. The essential extra fact is the exact probability of an all-one conjunction of independent coordinate bits, not a bound supplied by average sensitivity alone.

\paragraph{A matching order lower bound}
Let $w\geq1$, $m=2^w$, and $n=mw$. Partition the $n$ input coordinates into $m$ disjoint blocks of size $w$. Use the $m$ block conjunctions among the $n$ outputs, repeating any of them if necessary to reach exactly $n$ outputs. The input functions remain all coordinate functions and have average maximal sensitivity one. If at least one block is all ones, some output has sensitivity $w$. Independence of the blocks gives
\[
 \operatorname{ams}(g_1,\ldots,g_n)
 \geq w\bigl(1-(1-2^{-w})^{2^w}\bigr)
 \geq(1-e^{-1})w.
\]
The last inequality follows from $1-u\leq e^{-u}$. Since $\log_2n=w+\log_2w$, this is order $\log n$ along the displayed infinite family. Thus one cannot improve the source's proposed extra logarithm to a uniform constant even in the coordinate subclass.

\paragraph{The unresolved inference}
For arbitrary $f_j$, an output of conjunction width $w$ need not have probability $2^{-w}$ of being one, and its selected functions may be strongly dependent. The proof of the logarithmic upper bound cannot replace that probability by a product of marginal probabilities. The general pointwise bound above leaves a factor $n$, and the source's hypothesis only controls the expectation of $A(x)$. Obtaining an appropriate joint tail or charging inequality from that hypothesis is the first missing step of this route.

\section{Verification and adversarial audit}
The maximum is inside the expectation throughout. The lower-bound family uses exactly $n$ input functions and at most $n$ distinct conjunctions, with repetition making the output count exact. Its rare-event probabilities are justified by disjoint coordinate blocks; that independence is not transferred to arbitrary Boolean functions. Both values of a conjunction and the empty conjunction are covered in the pointwise argument.
