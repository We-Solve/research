\section{Monochromatic vertex colorings inherited from Perfect Matchings}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


Let $V=\{v_1,\ldots,v_n\}$ be ordered. A bi-colored weighted graph is a finite loopless undirected multigraph on $V$ with a complex weight $w_e$ and an ordered color pair $(c_1(e),c_2(e))\in\{1,\ldots,d\}^2$ on each edge $e=v_iv_j$, $i<j$; its colors at $v_i,v_j$ are respectively $c_1(e),c_2(e)$. A perfect matching $M$ selects one incident edge at each vertex, giving an inherited coloring $c_M(v)$ from that endpoint's edge color. For any coloring $c:V\to\{1,\ldots,d\}$, put \[W(c)=\sum_{M:c_M=c}\prod_{e\in M}w_e,\] with an empty sum equal to zero.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Determine all positive integers $n,d$ for which such a graph exists with \[W(c)=\begin{cases}1,&c\text{ is constant},\\0,&c\text{ is nonconstant}.\end{cases}\] Thus each of the $d$ monochromatic inherited vertex colorings has unit weight and all other colorings cancel. \sref{statement}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
For which numbers of vertices and colors can weighted perfect matchings produce exactly the monochromatic colorings, each with weight one?
