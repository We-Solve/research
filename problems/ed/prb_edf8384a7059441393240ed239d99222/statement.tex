\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For positive integers $m_1,\ldots,m_n$, let
\[
 P=[m_1]\times\cdots\times[m_n],\qquad
 [m_i]=\{1,\ldots,m_i\},
\]
ordered coordinatewise, and put $N=\prod_i m_i$. A linear extension is an ordering $L=(x_1,\ldots,x_N)$ respecting this partial order. Its jump count is
\[
 J(L)=\#\{i\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\}:x_i
                         \text{ and }x_{i+1}\text{ are incomparable in }P\}.
\]
Let $J_{\max}(P)=\max_LJ(L)$ and choose $L$ uniformly from all linear extensions. This distinguishes the jump count of an extension from the usual optimization parameter sometimes called the jump number of a poset, which uses a minimum.

The source asks to show that $J(L)$ is close to $J_{\max}(P)$ with high probability for grid posets. A precise theorem needs an asymptotic family of chain lengths/dimensions and a specified closeness scale, such as an additive error or a relative error. ``With high probability'' means probability tending to one in that family. No family, error bound, or rate is supplied, so one is not silently selected. Relative normalization also needs care when the maximum is zero, as for a single chain.

The source explicitly reports this behavior known for the symmetric grid $[m]^n$ in the regime

\]
