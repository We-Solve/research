\section{Mathematical Nuclear Shell Model}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


\paragraph{Definitions and notation.}The primary source concerns the shell model of an atom with fermionic electrons, not a nuclear shell model. Electron states are antisymmetric in position and spin. The source's Coulomb Hamiltonian is $H(N,Z)=\sum_{i=1}^N(-\Delta_i-Z/|x_i|)+\sum_{i<j}|x_i-x_j|^{-1}$ on the fermionic space. Shell descriptions group effective one-electron orbitals into energy shells; the question asks how that approximation relates to exact quantum theory.

\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}
Make mathematical sense of the shell model of an atom and its relationship to the exact quantum theory, as in Simon’s original Problem 11.
\paragraph{Scope and source qualification.}The registered title and short catalogue target say nuclear shell model, but the primary source explicitly says shell model of an atom and discusses atomic/chemical physics. The visible source target is corrected to atomic, while the canonical ID and inherited title/status are preserved. The source itself calls the programme vague.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Justify the atomic electron shell model from exact quantum theory.
