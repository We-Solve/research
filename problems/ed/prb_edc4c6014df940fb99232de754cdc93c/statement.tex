\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(k\) be the intended ground field. A Hopf \(k\)-algebra \(H\) is a unital associative \(k\)-algebra with a coassociative coproduct \(\Delta:H\to H\otimes_k H\), counit \(\epsilon:H\to k\) and antipode \(S:H\to H\), with the bialgebra compatibilities and antipode identities
\[
 m(S\otimes\operatorname{id})\Delta
 =m(\operatorname{id}\otimes S)\Delta=\eta\epsilon.
\]
It is affine when finitely generated as a \(k\)-algebra, and prime when the product of two nonzero two-sided ideals is nonzero. Primeness is not replaced by the stronger domain condition.

For a finite-dimensional generating subspace \(V\subseteq H\) containing \(1\), the Gelfand--Kirillov dimension is
\[
 \operatorname{GKdim}H=
 \limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\dim_k(V^n)}{\log n},
\]
independent of the choice of such \(V\). The question is to describe all Hopf algebras satisfying
\[
 H\text{ prime and affine},\qquad\operatorname{GKdim}H=1.
\]
In the usual classification interpretation, give explicit models up to Hopf-algebra isomorphism, prove that every object in the class occurs, and determine when model parameters give isomorphic Hopf algebras. A Hopf isomorphism must respect the coproduct, counit and antipode, not merely the underlying algebra. The source does not explicitly specify that equivalence relation, so an algebra-only classification cannot automatically substitute for it.

The record omits the ground-field restrictions, including characteristic and algebraic closedness. These affect the class to be described and are not filled in by assuming a familiar characteristic-zero theorem. In particular GK dimension one is not the same requirement as global dimension one.
