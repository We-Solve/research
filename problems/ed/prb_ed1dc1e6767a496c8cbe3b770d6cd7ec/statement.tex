\section{Chromatic Reflection along the Squared Second Uncountable Ordinal}\label{statement}


\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Unless a section says otherwise, $\N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$,
$\Z$ is the ring of integers, $\Q,\R,\C$ are the rational, real, and complex
fields, $[N]=\{1,\ldots,\lfloor N\rfloor\}$, and $\log$ is the natural
logarithm. For real functions of a parameter tending to infinity,
$f\ll g$ and $f=O(g)$ mean $|f|\leq Cg$ eventually for some fixed $C$;
$f\gg g$ reverses this comparison; $f=o(g)$ means $f/g\to0$;
$f\sim g$ means $f/g\to1$; and $f\asymp g$ means both order bounds.
A subscript on $O$ or $\ll$ specifies allowed constant dependence.
The expression $n^{o(1)}$ in an upper bound means growth smaller than
$n^\epsilon$ for each fixed $\epsilon>0$. Local definitions govern any
exception, finite-field convention, or use of the same symbol for another
object. An asymptotic claim is not automatically an effective algorithm.



The vertex set is the ordinal product $\omega_2\cdot\omega_2$, where $\omega_2$ is the least ordinal of cardinality $\aleph_2$. Lesser type'' refers to the order type inherited by a subset of this well-ordered vertex set.

A graph has a vertex set $V$ and edges that are unordered pairs of distinct vertices. A subgraph need not be induced unless that word is stated. A proper vertex colouring gives adjacent vertices different colours; $\chi(G)$ is the least number of colours. \sref{statement}{1} \sref{statement}{2}

\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Is there a graph $G$ with vertex set $\omega_2^2$ and chromatic number $\aleph_2$ such that every subgraph whose vertices have a lesser type has chromatic number $\leq \aleph_0$?
What if instead we ask for $G$ to have chromatic number $\aleph_1$? \sref{statement}{1}

\paragraph{Residual task reported by the archive.}

The embedded review (2026-08-17) records: Lower the local chromatic bound at omega\_2\textasciicircum{}2 from aleph\_1 to aleph\_0 while retaining global chromatic number aleph\_2 or aleph\_1, or prove this impossible. \sref{statement}{1}

\subsection{Short English statement}

Can a graph on the specified ordinal require uncountably many colours while every smaller-order-type restriction remains countably colourable?
