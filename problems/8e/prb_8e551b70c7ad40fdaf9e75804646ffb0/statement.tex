\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(M\) be a closed hyperbolic \(3\)-manifold, \(\widetilde M\cong\mathbb H^3\) its universal cover, and \(\Gamma=\pi_1(M)\) its deck group. A surface map \(f:S\to M\) is \(\pi_1\)-injective if its induced homomorphism on fundamental groups is injective. The source asks for a closed such surface ``contained'' in \(M\), without explicitly specifying embedded versus immersed or the surface's connectedness and orientability.

For lifted surface images \(P\subset\widetilde M\), define \(\operatorname{Stab}_\Gamma(P)=\{g\in\Gamma:gP=P\}\). Agol's Problem 3.4 asks whether every \(M\) admits a surface with the source's \(1\)-line property. Literally, for each pair of distinct preimages \(P,Q\) of the surface, the record requires
\[
 P\cap Q\text{ is a single line},\qquad
 \operatorname{Stab}_\Gamma(P)\cap\operatorname{Stab}_\Gamma(Q)\cong\Z.
\]
The record does not define a line as a topological, properly embedded, or geodesic line. It also does not say ``whenever the preimages intersect.'' The alternative condition that every nonempty pairwise intersection is one line permits disjoint lifts and is weaker than the literal universal-intersection wording. These formulations must not be silently identified. Distinct lifts of an embedded surface are disjoint, so the embedded/immersed and nonempty-intersection conventions are essential to recovering the intended question.

The supplied source says Kahn--Markovic surfaces likely do not have the property because they tend to overlap on large subsurfaces. This is a qualified source observation, not a proven universal failure statement. The summary reports known quasi-Fuchsian surface-subgroup existence but says the particular cyclic-intersection/\(1\)-line property is not established in the located literature.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every closed hyperbolic three-manifold have a closed injective surface with the stated single-line lift intersections and infinite-cyclic common stabilizers? The extract leaves the immersion convention and whether disjoint lifts are permitted unresolved. The supplied source cautions that known surface constructions may not provide the desired property.
