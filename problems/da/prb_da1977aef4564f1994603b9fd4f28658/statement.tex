\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A convex body $K\subset\R^n$ is compact and convex with nonempty interior. It is origin-symmetric if $K=-K$. For $\xi\in S^{n-1}=\{x:\|x\|=1\}$ put $\xi^\perp=\{x:x\cdot\xi=0\}$. Let $\operatorname{vol}_n$ be Euclidean $n$-volume and $\operatorname{vol}_{n-1}$ the induced volume in that central hyperplane.

The symmetric slicing question is
\[
 \exists C\in(0,\infty)\ \forall n\geq2\ 
 \forall K\subset\R^n\text{ an origin-symmetric convex body}:\qquad
 \operatorname{vol}_n(K)^{(n-1)/n}
 \leq C\max_{\xi\in S^{n-1}}
              \operatorname{vol}_{n-1}(K\cap\xi^\perp).
\]
The same $C$ must work in every dimension and for every body; a dimension-dependent bound is weaker. Sections are central and have codimension one. No isotropic-position, smoothness or extra symmetry hypothesis is added.

By uniform scaling, the statement is equivalently that every such body of volume one has a central hyperplane section of volume at least an absolute $c>0$. This does not change the shape class or permit an affine section away from the origin. If $n=1$ is included in the literal all-dimensional wording, the usual zero-dimensional volume of the singleton section is one and that case imposes only the harmless requirement $C\geq1$.

\subsection{Short English statement}
Is there a dimension-independent constant ensuring that every volume-one origin-symmetric convex body has a central hyperplane section of volume bounded below? The bound must be uniform over all dimensions and bodies. The supplied open label is a dated assessment, not independent verification.
