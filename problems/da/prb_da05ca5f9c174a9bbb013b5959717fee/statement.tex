\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 701559}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a finite group \(G\), let \(|G|\) denote its order and let
\[
k(G)=\bigl|\{\{gxg^{-1}:g\in G\}:x\in G\}\bigr|
\]
be its number of conjugacy classes. The intended question is whether
\[
\exists c>0\quad\forall\text{ finite groups }G,\qquad k(G)\ge c\log_2|G|.
\]
Here \(c\) must be independent of \(G\). The source asks for a ``constant \(c\)'' without explicitly requiring positivity; the formulation above records the intended nontrivial interpretation. Without positivity, \(c=0\) satisfies the literal question. The trivial group is included: its right-hand side is zero.

The supplied editors' comment, dated 2021, reports two partial bounds. It attributes to L. Pyber (1992) a bound of the form
\[
k(G)\ge \varepsilon\frac{\log|G|}{(\log\log|G|)^8}
\]
for some fixed \(\varepsilon>0\). The comment says ``every group'', although the surrounding problem concerns finite groups; it does not specify the base of these logarithms or the order restrictions needed for the expression to be defined with nonzero denominator. Thus this reported bound is retained as background with unresolved domain qualifications, rather than asserted here for all finite orders.

The same comment attributes to B. Baumeister, A. Mar\'oti and H. P. Tong-Viet (2017) the statement
\[
\forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists\delta>0\ \forall\text{ finite groups }G\text{ with }|G|\ge3,\qquad
k(G)\ge\delta\frac{\log_2|G|}{(\log_2\log_2|G|)^{3+\varepsilon}}.
\]
Here \(\delta\) may depend on \(\varepsilon\), but not on \(G\). These are supplied editorial reports, not independently verified results in this draft. The registry labels the main question open; that label is supplied source data.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is there a universal positive constant such that every finite group has at least that constant times the base-two logarithm of its order many conjugacy classes? Positivity is implicit in the intended question but absent from the literal source wording. The supplied 2021 editorial comment reports weaker bounds.
