\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a singular reduced plane curve $C$ of degree $d$, let $x_1,\ldots,x_s$ be its distinct proper singular points and $m_i$ their multiplicities, with $s>0$. The supplied source convention is
\[
 h(C)=\frac{d^2-\sum_{i=1}^s m_i^2}{s}.
\]
Only proper points in $\mathbb P^2$ occur in this sum and denominator; infinitely near points are not additional counted singular points.

\textbf{AIM problem 5.3.} For irreducible curves, the source reports the best known examples approaching $h=-2$ and asks whether one can do better. Seek irreducible examples with smaller values or an improved asymptotic family, specifying which improvement is meant. A single value below $-2$ and a limiting family below $-2$ are different targets; the excerpt does not formalize that distinction or explicitly state the base field.

Remark 5.5 reports $h\to-3$ and $h\ge-3$ over $\R$, no analogous result known over $\Q$ at the time, and asks about bounded negativity for tropical surfaces. The exact curve/configuration class for the real bound and the tropical intersection convention are not restated, so these are retained as qualified historical remarks rather than unrestricted theorems.
