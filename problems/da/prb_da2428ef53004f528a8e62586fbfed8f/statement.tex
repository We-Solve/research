\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A group \(G\) is periodic if every element has finite order, that is,
\[
\forall g\in G\;\exists n\in\Z_{\geq 1}\quad g^n=1_G.
\]
It is simple if it is nontrivial and its only normal subgroups are \(\{1_G\}\) and \(G\). A finite Frobenius group is a finite group \(F\) having a subgroup \(H\) such that \(\{1_F\}<H<F\) and
\[
H\cap xHx^{-1}=\{1_F\}\qquad\text{for every }x\in F\setminus H.
\]
