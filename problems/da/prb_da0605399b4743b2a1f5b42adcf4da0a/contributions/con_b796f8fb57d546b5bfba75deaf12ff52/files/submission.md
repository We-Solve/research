# Finite torsion support of idempotent traces for every discrete abelian group

Claim type: Partial result

Exact scope: For every discrete abelian group G, every q≥1 and every matrix idempotent over ℓ¹(G), its diagonal trace is supported on a finite torsion subgroup of G.

Let a=sum_i e_{ii}∈ℓ¹(G), and let G-hat be the compact character group Hom(G,S¹), with its topology as a closed subset of (S¹)^G. For each character χ, evaluation sum_g a_gχ(g) is defined by absolute convergence. It is continuous, uniformly approximated by finite sums. Applying evaluation to e gives a complex matrix idempotent, so
\[
 r(\chi)=\widehat a(\chi)\in\{0,1,\ldots,q\}.
\]
No self-adjointness is required: an idempotent matrix has minimal polynomial dividing X(X−1), so its trace equals its rank.

Choose a finite set S⊂G so sum_{g∉S}|a_g|<1/3, and let H be the finitely generated subgroup it generates. If two characters agree on H, their trace values differ by at most twice this tail, hence by less than one. Since the values are integers they agree exactly. Every character of H extends to G: adjoining one group element at a time requires choosing an n-th root in S¹ if a multiple is already in the subgroup, and Zorn’s lemma completes the extension. Thus the restriction map G-hat→H-hat is surjective. A continuous surjection from compact to Hausdorff is a quotient map, so r descends to a continuous integer-valued function on H-hat.

Write H=T×Z^d, with T finite. Its character group is T-hat×(S¹)^d. Each torus component is connected, so the descended integer-valued function is constant on each component and depends only on T-hat. Finite Fourier inversion on T expresses it as
\[
 r(\chi)=\sum_{t\in T}c_t\chi(t).
\]
Therefore a and the finite group-ring element sum_{t∈T}c_t t have the same Fourier transform on all of G-hat.

For clarity, injectivity of this transform on ℓ¹(G) can be proved without assuming G countable. Suppose b has zero transform and fix g_0. Given ε>0, choose a finite set carrying all but ε of its absolute coefficient sum, and let J be the finitely generated subgroup generated by that set and g_0. Every character of J extends to G, so the finite or absolutely summable Fourier series on J has absolute value at most ε on all J-hat. Multiply by the inverse g_0-character and integrate over the finite group times torus J-hat. Orthogonality of the ordinary circle monomials (and finite characters) recovers b_{g_0}, whose absolute value is at most ε. Let ε decrease to zero. Hence every coefficient is zero. The same proof works when b has infinite support, since uniform absolute convergence permits termwise integration.

Applying injectivity proves a_g=0 outside T. Abelian conjugacy classes are singletons, and every member of T has finite order, so this is exactly the prompt’s finite torsion-class support conclusion. For torsion-free abelian G, T is trivial and the trace is supported only at the identity. For arbitrary abelian G there is no claim that e itself has finite support; only its diagonal trace is forced to do so.

Nonabelian groups are not covered: their scalar characters do not detect all conjugacy-class sums, so the separating Fourier argument fails. Absolute summability alone also would not force finite support; the integer rank and connected-torus argument are the substantive additional constraints.

Relation to earlier contributions. The only listed prior is an unverified all-discrete-group OpenAI announcement. Here a complete abelian proof supplies actual finite torsion support, not merely vanishing on infinite-order elements or a summability statement. Its compactness step explicitly accommodates arbitrary, possibly uncountable discrete abelian groups.
