\section{Odd incongruent covering systems}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For integers $a$ and $q\ge1$, let $a+q\mathbb Z=\{a+qt:t\in\mathbb Z\}$. A covering system is a finite family of these residue classes whose union is $\mathbb Z$. It is distinct if its moduli are pairwise unequal.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Can a finite covering system have pairwise distinct odd moduli $q_1,\ldots,q_k>1$? The conjecture says that no integers $a_1,\ldots,a_k$ and such moduli satisfy
\[
 \mathbb Z=\bigcup_{i=1}^k(a_i+q_i\mathbb Z).
\] \sref{statement}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Must every distinct covering system with moduli greater than one contain an even modulus?
