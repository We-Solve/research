\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
The supplied question is: ``Does there exist an expander that does not coarsely embed into any CAT(0) space?'' Here is a precise formulation using the conventional interpretation of an expander as a family of finite graphs with their unscaled graph metrics. The supplied record does not explicitly specify this convention.

An \emph{expander family} is a sequence \((G_n)_{n\geq 1}\) of finite connected simple undirected graphs \(G_n=(V_n,E_n)\), with \(|V_n|\to\infty\), for which there exist an integer \(D\geq 1\) and a real number \(h>0\) such that every vertex has degree at most \(D\), and
\[
 |\partial_{E_n}A|\geq h|A|
 \quad\text{whenever }A\subseteq V_n,\quad 0<|A|\leq |V_n|/2.
\]
Here \(\partial_{E_n}A\) consists of the edges with exactly one endpoint in \(A\). Let \(d_n\) be the shortest-path metric with every edge of length \(1\).

Represent the family by the metric space \(X=\bigsqcup_{n\geq1}V_n\). For definiteness, write \(\Delta_n=\operatorname{diam}(V_n,d_n)\) and set
\[
 d_X(x,y)=
 \begin{cases}
 d_n(x,y),&x,y\in V_n,\\
 \Delta_n+\Delta_m+n+m,&x\in V_n,\ y\in V_m,\ n\neq m.
 \end{cases}
\]
This is a coarse disjoint union metric: it retains each graph metric and separates distinct components at distances tending to infinity. The displayed choice fixes the otherwise unspecified distances between components.

A metric space \(Y\) is \(\operatorname{CAT}(0)\) if it is geodesic and every geodesic triangle satisfies Euclidean comparison: distances between points on its sides are no greater than the distances between the corresponding points on the sides of a Euclidean triangle with the same side lengths. Completeness is not imposed here. Requiring complete \(\operatorname{CAT}(0)\) targets gives the same existence question, since the metric completion of a \(\operatorname{CAT}(0)\) space is again \(\operatorname{CAT}(0)\).

A map \(f:X\to Y\) is a \emph{coarse embedding} if there exist nondecreasing functions \(\rho_-,\rho_+:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)\), with \(\lim_{t\to\infty}\rho_-(t)=\infty\), such that
\[
 \rho_-(d_X(x,x'))\leq d_Y(f(x),f(x'))\leq\rho_+(d_X(x,x'))
 \qquad(x,x'\in X).
\]

\textbf{Question.} Does there exist an expander family as above such that, for every \(\operatorname{CAT}(0)\) metric space \(Y\), there are no map \(f:X\to Y\) and control functions \(\rho_-,\rho_+\) satisfying these conditions? The same family must obstruct every such target; the family cannot depend on \(Y\).

\subsection{Short English statement}
Is there one family of finite connected graphs, with uniformly bounded degrees and uniformly positive edge expansion, whose coarse disjoint union cannot coarsely embed into any \(\operatorname{CAT}(0)\) metric space? Graph distances are unscaled, and the same family must work for every target. These conventions make explicit the otherwise unspecified meaning of ``an expander'' in the supplied question.
