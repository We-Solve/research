\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A quaternary quadratic form is a homogeneous polynomial of degree two in four variables. It is positive definite if \(Q(x)>0\) for every \(x\in\R^4\setminus\{0\}\). The supplied question concerns such forms and denotes their discriminant by \(D(Q)\), but does not specify the coefficient domain, integrality convention, discriminant normalization, or any primitivity hypothesis.

Under the usual integral-lattice interpretation, let \(Q\) be integer-valued on \(\Z^4\). A positive integer \(n\) is represented by \(Q\) if there exists \(x\in\Z^4\) with \(Q(x)=n\). It is locally represented if \(Q(x_\infty)=n\) for some \(x_\infty\in\R^4\), and, for every prime \(p\), there exists \(x_p\in\Z_p^4\) with \(Q(x_p)=n\), where \(\Z_p\) is the ring of \(p\)-adic integers. The real condition is automatic for positive \(n\) and positive-definite \(Q\). Define
\[
E(Q)=\left\{n\in\Z_{>0}: n\text{ is locally represented by }Q\text{ and }Q(x)\ne n\text{ for every }x\in\Z^4\right\}.
\]
These definitions describe a conventional interpretation; the supplied record does not explicitly confirm it or impose primitive representation.

The proposed bound, under this interpretation and the uniform reading of \(O_\varepsilon\), asks whether
\[
\forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists C_\varepsilon>0\ \forall Q\ \forall n\in E(Q),\qquad n\le C_\varepsilon D(Q)^{1+\varepsilon},
\]
where \(Q\) ranges over the intended class of positive-definite quaternary forms and \(C_\varepsilon\) is independent of \(Q\) and \(n\). The class of forms and the positive discriminant convention must be fixed before this becomes an unqualified statement.

The source phrases the target as a bound for the largest element of \(E(Q)\). If \(E(Q)\) is nonempty and finite, this is a bound on \(\max E(Q)\). The quantified formulation above is vacuous when \(E(Q)=\varnothing\), and would imply finiteness for every fixed \(Q\); it does not assume that a largest exception already exists. The supplied record gives no convention for the empty case and no separate finiteness hypothesis.
\subsection{Short English statement}
The question asks whether every positive integer represented locally everywhere but not by integer vectors of a positive-definite quadratic form in four variables is bounded by a constant, depending only on \(\varepsilon>0\), times \(D(Q)^{1+\varepsilon}\). The integral interpretation, exact class of forms, and discriminant convention require confirmation. The supplied record labels the problem open; this draft does not independently verify that status.
