\section{Optimal Neural-Network Depth for Sparse Recovery}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.


Let $\Sigma_s=\{x\in\mathbb R^n:|\operatorname{supp}(x)|\leq s\}$ and let $A\in\mathbb R^{m\times n}$ satisfy $\ker A\cap\Sigma_{2s}=\{0\}$, so the measurements distinguish all $s$-sparse vectors. A ReLU network uses the activation $t\mapsto\max(0,t)$; exact recovery means $N(Ax)=x$ for every $x\in\Sigma_s$. The source distinguishes hidden layers from the final linear output layer and allows arbitrary width.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For exact recovery of $s$-sparse vectors by ReLU networks, is the known depth bound $\lceil\log_2s+2\rceil$ optimal? \sref{statement}{2}
\paragraph{Scope qualification.}
The sparse-recovery source uses underdetermined measurements and the injectivity condition above; its exact-recovery depth theorem assumes $s\geq2$. The stated bound concerns exact recovery, while its two-hidden-layer result concerns arbitrary-precision approximation. Layer-count conventions must remain explicit; the displayed bound is interpreted according to the source's hidden-layer convention.

Determine the smallest number of hidden layers required for exact recovery of every sparse vector from the given linear measurements.
