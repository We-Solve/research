\section{Research attempt}
\subsection{Literature and scope}
Problem 56 combines incomplete three-way tables, margins and likelihood without a missingness mechanism. I chose two fixed, ignorable observation patterns: one independent cohort observes $(X,Y)$ and another observes $(Y,Z)$, under the model $X\perp Z\mid Y$. This is an explicitly restricted likelihood model. I tested the route of gluing the two empirical pair margins without first fitting their common $Y$ distribution.
\begin{proposition}
For strictly positive pair counts $n_{xy}$ and $m_{yz}$ of respective totals $N_1,N_2$, the unique interior MLE in the model $p_{xyz}=\pi_y a_{x\mid y}b_{z\mid y}$ is
\[
\widehat\pi_y=\frac{n_{+y}+m_{y+}}{N_1+N_2},\quad
\widehat a_{x\mid y}=\frac{n_{xy}}{n_{+y}},\quad
\widehat b_{z\mid y}=\frac{m_{yz}}{m_{y+}}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The two observed margins are $\pi_ya_{x\mid y}$ and $\pi_yb_{z\mid y}$. Their independent multinomial likelihood therefore has log, up to constants,
\[
\sum_y(n_{+y}+m_{y+})\log\pi_y+
\sum_{x,y}n_{xy}\log a_{x\mid y}+
\sum_{y,z}m_{yz}\log b_{z\mid y}.
\]
The probability vectors in its three groups are constrained separately. For positive counts $c_i$ of total $C$, the vector $r_i=c_i/C$ uniquely maximizes $\sum c_i\log p_i$: the difference from its value at $r$ is $C\sum r_i\log(p_i/r_i)\le C\sum r_i(p_i/r_i-1)=0$, using $\log s\le s-1$, with equality only when $p_i=r_i$ for all $i$. Applying this to each group proves the formulas and uniqueness.
\end{proof}
\subsection{Failed route and verification}
Take $n=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}6&1\\2&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ with rows $X$ and columns $Y$, and $m=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\2&6\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ with rows $Y$ and columns $Z$. Both totals are ten, but the empirical $Y$ distributions are $(4/5,1/5)$ and $(1/5,4/5)$. No joint distribution can have both empirical margins, since marginalizing either would give contradictory $Y$ laws. The MLE instead uses pooled $\widehat\pi=(1/2,1/2)$. Exact rational substitution gives joint probabilities, in lexicographic $xyz$ order, $(3,3,1,3,1,1,1,3)/16$, summing to one.
