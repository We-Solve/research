\section{A constructive special case}
The primary question requires capacities $x$ themselves. We prove
an explicit signing and flow when $2x$ describes a connected
Eulerian multigraph, and examine why clearing arbitrary denominators
does not preserve the unit supplies. The source's broader half-integral
discussion is not imported without checking its Eulerian hypothesis.

\paragraph{Euler-tour construction with exact capacities.}
Assume $m_e=2x_e$ are nonnegative integers and the multigraph
with $m_e$ copies of edge $e$ is connected on the relevant
vertices and has even degree at every vertex. It has a closed
walk using each edge copy once. For completeness, follow unused
edges from any start until returning to it: at a different vertex,
the number of used incidences upon an arrival is odd, so an
unused edge remains unless all incidences there had odd total,
contrary to even degree. This forms a closed trail. If edges
remain, connectivity supplies a vertex of this trail incident to
an unused component; form another closed trail there and splice it
into the first. The number of unused edges decreases, so this
process terminates with an Euler tour.

Let $T$ have even size. If $T$ is empty use zero flow.
Otherwise mark one occurrence, for example the first occurrence,
of every terminal on the cyclic tour. There are $2h$ marks.
Give them alternating signs $+1,-1$ in tour order. On the
tour segment from mark $i$ to mark $i+1$, send signed
flow $+1/2$ in the tour direction if $i$ has positive sign,
and $-1/2$ in the tour direction otherwise; indices are cyclic.
At an unmarked occurrence the incoming and outgoing signed values
coincide, so the divergence is zero. At a marked occurrence the
outgoing minus incoming signed values equal its assigned sign.
Even though a vertex may occur elsewhere on the tour, those
unmarked occurrences contribute zero. Thus the resulting divergence
is the required unit sign at each terminal and zero elsewhere.
The number of positive and negative marks is equal, so the signing
is balanced.

Aggregate signed traversals on each original undirected edge, using
an arbitrary reference direction. The absolute aggregate is at most
the sum of traversal magnitudes, namely $m_e/2=x_e$.
Cancellation can only decrease it. Therefore this is a transshipment
within precisely the original capacities, with no appeal to a
separate cut-sufficiency theorem. It proves the desired conclusion
for this explicitly stated Eulerian special case.

\paragraph{What arbitrary denominator clearing actually gives.}
For rational $x$, choose an integer $D$ with $Dx$ integral.
The multigraph with multiplicities $2Dx_e$ has even degrees
and is connected when the positive-capacity support is connected.
The same construction gives unit supplies, but its capacity bound
is $Dx_e$. Dividing the flow by $D$ restores capacities $x_e$
and simultaneously divides every nonzero supply by $D$.
Those supplies are $\pm1/D$, not the required $\pm1$.
Thus denominator clearing alone does not extend the proof with a
capacity factor independent of $D$. The missing step is to
coordinate sufficiently many terminal markings without increasing
edge usage, rather than merely rescale the Euler tour.

\paragraph{Averaging feasible signings does not repair the problem.}
The balance and flow constraints are linear once the signs are
fixed, but the requirement $s(v)\in\{-1,1\}$ is not convex.
If $(s,f)$ is a valid nonempty-terminal construction, reversing
all signs and flows gives $(-s,-f)$ within the same capacities.
Their average is $(0,0)$, which has no allowed terminal signs.
Hence a convex decomposition of capacities into convenient cases,
together with an arbitrary signing for each case, does not produce
a valid signing by averaging. This does not prove that compatible
choices of signs cannot exist; their compatibility is precisely an
additional unresolved requirement of that route.
