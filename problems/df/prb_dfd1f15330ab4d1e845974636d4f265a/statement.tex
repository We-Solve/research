\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
The source asks to determine the size and structure statistics of connected components when the root's age is not conditioned on. The supplied assessment places this in a branching-process setting, but the excerpt does not give the stochastic process, offspring/reproduction law, component definition, observation time, or root-sampling rule. In particular, it does not justify substituting an ordinary Galton--Watson tree.

Let $C$ denote the rooted connected component in the intended model, let $A\geq0$ denote the age of the selected root, and let $\nu$ be the distribution of $A$ under that same selection rule. If $K(a,\cdot)$ is a regular conditional law of the rooted component given $A=a$, the requested unconditioned law is
\[
 \mathbb P(C\in B)=\int K(a,B)\,\nu(da)
\]
for each measurable component event $B$. Its size marginal, with $N=|V(C)|\in\Z_{\geq1}\cup\{\infty\}$ when that convention applies, is
\[
 \mathbb P(N=m)=\int\mathbb P(N=m\mid A=a)\,\nu(da).
\]
The structural statistics concern the rooted component law itself, not only its size; possible age marks, orientation, temporal information and equivalence up to rooted isomorphism must be specified by the model.

These identities describe unconditioning but do not determine the statistics: both the conditional kernel and the correct root-age distribution must come from the missing process. Sampling an initial ancestor, a uniformly chosen observed individual, or a component followed by a root can produce different age and size biases.
