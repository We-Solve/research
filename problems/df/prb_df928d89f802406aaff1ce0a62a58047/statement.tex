\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a finite group \(G\), let \(\pi(G)\) be the set of prime divisors of \(|G|\). Its Gruenberg--Kegel graph (prime graph) \(GK(G)\) is the simple undirected graph with vertex set \(\pi(G)\), in which distinct primes \(p,q\) are adjacent if and only if \(G\) contains an element of order \(pq\). A \(3\)-coclique is a set of three distinct vertices, no two of which are adjacent. A group \(H\) is solvable if its derived series, defined by \(H^{(0)}=H\) and \(H^{(i+1)}=[H^{(i)},H^{(i)}]\), reaches the trivial group after finitely many steps.

Does there exist a finite nonsolvable group \(G\) such that
\[
\begin{gathered}
\text{for every three distinct }p,q,r\in\pi(G),\text{ at least one of }pq,pr,qr\\
\text{is the order of an element of }G,
\end{gathered}
\]
and
\[
\text{for every finite solvable group }H,\qquad GK(G)\not\cong GK(H)?
\]
Here graph isomorphism means an isomorphism of abstract graphs; it need not preserve the prime labels of vertices.

The supplied source further states that no such example exists among almost simple groups. A finite group \(A\) is almost simple if \(S\leq A\leq\operatorname{Aut}(S)\) for some finite nonabelian simple group \(S\), identified with its inner automorphism group. This exclusion is retained as supplied background; the record gives no separate theorem reference or proof for it, and it has not been independently verified here.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does there exist a finite nonsolvable group whose prime graph has no three pairwise nonadjacent vertices and is not isomorphic to the prime graph of any finite solvable group? The supplied record labels the problem open and states that almost simple groups provide no examples; these are supplied source assessments, not independent verifications.
