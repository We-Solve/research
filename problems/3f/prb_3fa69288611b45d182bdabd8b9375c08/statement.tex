\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 701029}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a finite group \(X\), two elements \(x,y\in X\) are conjugate if \(y=txt^{-1}\) for some \(t\in X\). Let \(k(X)\) denote the number of conjugacy classes of \(X\). For subgroups \(A,B\leq G\), write \(AB=\{ab:a\in A,\ b\in B\}\).

Is it true that for every finite group \(G\) and every pair of subgroups \(A,B\leq G\) satisfying
\[
G=AB\qquad\text{and}\qquad \gcd(|A|,|B|)=1,
\]
one has
\[
k(G)=k(AB)\leq k(A)\,k(B)?
\]
No normality assumption on either subgroup is imposed.

The supplied source states that the coprimeness hypothesis cannot be omitted and that the inequality holds if either \(A\) or \(B\) is normal in \(G\), referring to Archive problem 11.43. These contextual assertions are retained as supplied source claims and have not been independently verified.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Must the number of conjugacy classes of a finite group that is a product of two subgroups of coprime orders be at most the product of their numbers of conjugacy classes? The supplied registry labels this problem open; that status has not been independently verified.
