\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
The exact extraction asks whether an unbounded operator $T$ can satisfy
\[
 \forall E_0\text{ infinite-dimensional closed}\ \forall(n_k)\ 
 \exists x_0\in E_0:\quad T^{n_k}x_0\longrightarrow0.
\]
\textbf{The literal linear-operator statement is vacuous:} it does not require $x_0\ne0$, and $x_0=0$ belongs to every subspace and to every iterate domain of a linear operator, with $T^n0=0$. Thus this text cannot be promoted as a nontrivial open property without checking omitted hypotheses.

To expose a possible intended formulation, let $E$ be an infinite-dimensional Banach space and $T:D(T)\subset E\to E$ a linear operator, and put $D_\infty(T)=\bigcap_{m\geq0}D(T^m)$. A nontrivial candidate would ask for an operator satisfying
\[
 \forall E_0\subset E\text{ closed, infinite-dimensional}\ 
 \forall(n_k)\text{ strictly increasing in }\Z_{\geq0}\ 
 \exists x_0\in(E_0\cap D_\infty(T))\setminus\{0\}:\quad
 \|T^{n_k}x_0\|\to0.
\]
This candidate adds requirements not present in the source and is therefore \emph{not} silently asserted as the recovered problem. The ambient space may instead be another topological-vector-space class; the source gives no domain, closedness, dense-definition or convergence topology. If arbitrary sequences include $n_k=0$ for all $k$, a nonzero-vector version is impossible, so the sequence convention is also material.

``Unbounded'' must be defined: an unbounded densely defined operator, a discontinuous everywhere-defined linear map, and a bounded operator with unbounded powers are distinct notions. The excerpt selects none, and for an unbounded-domain interpretation every displayed iterate must be defined.
