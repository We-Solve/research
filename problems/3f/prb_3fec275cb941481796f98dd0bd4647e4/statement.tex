\section*{Acyclic orientation with connectivity prescriptions}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $G=(V,E)$ be a finite loopless undirected graph and let $k\ge1$.
An orientation replaces each edge by one of its two possible directed
arcs. It is acyclic if it has no directed cycle. Write $\lambda_D(u,v)$
for the maximum number of pairwise arc-disjoint directed $u$--$v$ paths
in an orientation $D$.

For two distinct specified vertices $s,t$, determine the complexity of
deciding whether there is an acyclic orientation satisfying
\[
 \lambda_D(s,v)\ge k,\qquad \lambda_D(v,t)\ge k
                 \quad(v\in V\setminus\{s,t\}).
\]
The source also asks the following separate variant. Given distinct
$s,t_1,t_2$, decide whether an acyclic orientation has
$\lambda_D(s,t_1)\ge k$ and $\lambda_D(s,t_2)\ge k$.

In each case, seek a polynomial-time recognition and construction method,
or a precise complexity classification. The integer $k$ is part of the
input. Paths belonging to a single connectivity requirement must be
arc-disjoint; path collections for different destinations may overlap.
There is no demand for one simultaneous collection disjoint across all
requirements. The orientation must satisfy all the requirements at once.
Deleting the acyclicity condition, or replacing path connectivity by
local degree inequalities without proving equivalence, gives a different
decision problem.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can one efficiently orient a graph without directed cycles while
meeting the specified source-to-vertex and vertex-to-terminal connectivity demands?
