\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 2306009}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(\mathbb D=\{z\in\C:|z|<1\}\), and let \(S\) be the class of holomorphic injective functions \(f:\mathbb D\to\C\) normalized by \(f(0)=0\) and \(f'(0)=1\). A function in \(S\) is convex if its image is a convex domain. For
\[
f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_nz^n,\qquad
g(z)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty b_nz^n,
\]
define the Hadamard product (coefficientwise convolution) by
\[
(f*g)(z)=\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_nb_nz^n\qquad(z\in\mathbb D).
\]
The conjecture asks whether, for every pair of convex \(f,g\in S\), the function \(f*g\) also belongs to \(S\) and has convex image. The supplied research summary records an affirmative resolution of this P\'olya--Schoenberg conjecture by Ruscheweyh and Sheil-Small (1973). This is a supplied literature assessment, not a new proof.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Is the coefficientwise convolution of two normalized convex univalent disk functions again convex and univalent? The supplied summary reports that the conjecture was proved in 1973.
