\section{Circulant mass compatibility and a six-body temporal Fourier obstruction}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


\paragraph{Definitions and notation.}In the Newtonian $n$-body problem, positions $q_i\in\mathbb R^d$ and masses $m_i>0$ obey $m_i\ddot q_i=\nabla_{q_i}U$, where $U(q)=\sum_{i<j}m_im_j/|q_i-q_j|$ and the gravitational constant is normalized to one. Here $T=\tfrac12\sum_i m_i|\dot q_i|^2$, $E=T-U$, and a collision has $q_i=q_j$. The center of mass can be fixed at zero; moment of inertia is $I=\sum_i m_i|q_i|^2$. An equal-time-shift choreography has $q_i(t)=q(t+(i-1)P/n)$ for one $P$-periodic curve, with distinct bodies avoiding collision. The masses still appear separately in the Newtonian equations. Equal phase shifts are an assumption of this source question, not of every generalized single-trace motion.
