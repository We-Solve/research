\section*{Kourovka Notebook Problem 21.48}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A quasimorphism on a group $G$ is a function $f:G\to\mathbb R$ with
finite defect
\[
 D(f)=\sup_{g,h\in G}|f(gh)-f(g)-f(h)|<\infty.
\]
It is homogeneous if $f(g^n)=nf(g)$ for every $g\in G$ and every
integer $n$. Equivalently, its restriction to each cyclic subgroup is
a homomorphism into the additive group of real numbers. Homogeneity
in particular gives $f(1)=0$ and $f(g^{-1})=-f(g)$.

Assume that $G$ admits an unbounded homogeneous quasimorphism which is
not a homomorphism. Unbounded means $\sup_{g\in G}|f(g)|=\infty$;
not being a homomorphism means that $f(gh)\ne f(g)+f(h)$ for at least
one pair. Must $G$ contain a nonabelian free subgroup?

The desired conclusion is the existence of elements $a,b\in G$ such
that every nonempty freely reduced word in $a^{\pm1},b^{\pm1}$ is
nonidentity. Equivalently, the map from the free group on two generators
sending its generators to $a,b$ is injective.

The group is arbitrary: finite generation, a topology, and a geometric
action are not assumed. The defect has one finite bound over all pairs,
not a bound depending on $g,h$. A real homomorphism alone, even an
unbounded one, does not satisfy the hypothesis. Likewise a free quotient
of $G$ is not a substitute for the explicitly required free subgroup
without an accompanying argument producing its embedding.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does a non-homomorphic homogeneous real quasimorphism force a group to contain a free subgroup of rank two?
