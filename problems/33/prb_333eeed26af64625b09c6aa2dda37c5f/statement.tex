\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 30000114}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(n\geq2\), and let a convex body \(K\subset\R^n\) mean a compact convex set with nonempty interior. Write
\[
G(K)=|K\cap\Z^n|
\]
for its lattice-point enumerator. Use Euclidean intrinsic volumes \(V_0(K),\ldots,V_n(K)\), normalized by the Steiner formula
\[
\operatorname{vol}_n(K+tB^n)=\sum_{i=0}^n\kappa_{n-i}V_i(K)t^{n-i}\quad(t\geq0),
\]
where \(B^n\) is the Euclidean unit ball and \(\kappa_j\) its \(j\)-dimensional volume, with \(\kappa_0=1\). Thus \(V_n\) is volume, \(V_{n-1}\) is half the boundary area in this convention, and \(V_0(K)=1\).

The source question is whether
\[
\forall n\geq2\quad\exists c(n)\in[0,\infty)\quad
\forall K\subset\R^n\text{ convex body},\qquad
G(K)\leq V_n(K)+V_{n-1}(K)+c(n)\sum_{i=0}^{n-2}V_i(K).
\]
The same finite constant must work for all bodies in a fixed dimension, including all translations; it may not depend on the body's size, shape, or position. The coefficients of the two highest intrinsic volumes are exactly one. No lattice-polytope, symmetry, or origin-containment assumption is present. For \(n=1\), if the empty lower sum is interpreted as zero, the analogous displayed formula reads \(G(K)\leq V_1(K)+V_0(K)\); the source does not discuss its dimension convention explicitly. Whether lower-dimensional convex sets are also included under ``convex body'' must be confirmed.

The supplied literature assessment checked 2026-08-21 says that the full Wills conjecture has high-dimensional counterexamples, but that this weaker inequality with a dimension-dependent coefficient on the lower intrinsic volumes remains open in the located literature. Failure of a bound with coefficient one on every intrinsic volume does not itself decide the weaker bound above.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can lattice-point counts be bounded by volume plus the next intrinsic volume with coefficient one, allowing a dimension-dependent multiplier only on the remaining intrinsic volumes? The multiplier must work uniformly over all convex bodies in that dimension.
