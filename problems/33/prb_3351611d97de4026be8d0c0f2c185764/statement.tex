\section{Density of Current–Current Correlation Measures}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


\paragraph{Definitions and conventions.} $H_\omega$ is the report's covariant random Schrödinger operator, $E_{H_\omega}(B)$ its spectral projection on a Borel set $B\subset\mathbb R$, and $\mathbb E$ expectation over the disorder. The lattice vector $|0\rangle$ is localized at the origin. The current operator $\nabla_iH_\omega$ is the derivation in position direction $i$ (with the convention fixed by the source). The summed diagonal current--current correlation defines a positive measure on the energy plane. Having a density means absolute continuity: $dm(E,E')=m(E,E')\,dE\,dE'$.

\paragraph{Source scope and qualifications.} The trace-per-unit-volume and spectral-product convention of the report is essential to the measure definition. The source proves analytic densities off a disorder-dependent neighborhood of the energy diagonal for a particular analytic single-site distribution; the question includes the diagonal and broader source setting.

\paragraph{Catalogue mathematical target.}
\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}
For the random Schrödinger operator $H_\omega$, let the current–current correlation measures be $$dm_{ij}(E,E')=\mathbb E\!\left[\langle0|\nabla_iH_\omega\,E_{H_\omega}(dE)\,\nabla_jH_\omega\,E_{H_\omega}(dE')|0\rangle\right],$$ and put $dm=\sum_i dm_{ii}$. Does the positive measure $dm$ have a density $m(E,E')$ with respect to Lebesgue measure?

\paragraph{Saved literature context.} The supplied catalogue's assessment dated 2026-08-21 records: The general density question is not settled, but Combes, Germinet, and Hislop prove density at coincident energies in a localization regime, with vanishing-rate bounds. The assessment is retained as dated provenance; it is not a new comprehensive literature or open-status certification.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Determine whether the current--current energy correlation measure of the random Schrödinger model is absolutely continuous.
