\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


\paragraph{Definitions and conventions.} $H_\omega$ is the report's covariant random Schrödinger operator, $E_{H_\omega}(B)$ its spectral projection on a Borel set $B\subset\mathbb R$, and $\mathbb E$ expectation over the disorder. The lattice vector $|0\rangle$ is localized at the origin. The current operator $\nabla_iH_\omega$ is the derivation in position direction $i$ (with the convention fixed by the source). The summed diagonal current--current correlation defines a positive measure on the energy plane. Having a density means absolute continuity: $dm(E,E')=m(E,E')\,dE\,dE'$.

\paragraph{Source scope and qualifications.} The trace-per-unit-volume and spectral-product convention of the report is essential to the measure definition. The source proves analytic densities off a disorder-dependent neighborhood of the energy diagonal for a particular analytic single-site distribution; the question includes the diagonal and broader source setting.

\paragraph{Why regular marginals are insufficient.} Let $\mu$ on $[0,1]^2$ be the pushforward of Lebesgue measure under $t\mapsto(t,t)$. Both marginals have density one, but $\mu$ gives mass one to the diagonal, whose two-dimensional Lebesgue measure is zero. Thus even sharp one-energy spectral regularity cannot alone imply a two-energy density.

\paragraph{The operator version of the same trap.} On $L^2([0,1])$ let $H$ multiply by $t$, let $J=I$, and use the vector state $\tau(B)=\langle1,B1\rangle$ on the commutative multiplication algebra. For Borel sets $B,C$,
\[
 \tau(J1_B(H)J1_C(H))=|B\cap C|.
\]
This reproduces $\mu(B\times C)$. However $J$ is not the current $i[H,X]$ of a random Schrödinger operator, so it is not a counterexample to the source question.

\paragraph{A current-specific check.} In finite dimension, if $Hv_a=E_av_a$ and $J=i[H,X]$, then $\langle v_a,Jv_b\rangle=i(E_a-E_b)\langle v_a,Xv_b\rangle$. Current matrix elements vanish within exactly degenerate energy spaces, eliminating the preceding simple diagonal mechanism. This does not prove absolute continuity: finite systems still yield point masses off the diagonal, and infinite-volume position operators are unbounded. The first missing estimate is joint two-energy regularity, uniform in volume and strong enough near $E=E'$ to survive the trace-per-volume limit. The source's off-diagonal results cannot be extended by marginal regularity alone.
