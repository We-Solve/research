\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Fix \(g\geq3\). In the usual closed oriented surface interpretation, let \(H=H_1(\Sigma_g;\Z)\), let \(\omega=\sum_{i=1}^g a_i\wedge b_i\) be its symplectic class, and set
\[
 A_g=(\bigwedge^3 H)/\{h\wedge\omega:h\in H\}.
\]
Let \(I_g\) be the Torelli group, the kernel of the homology action of \(\operatorname{Mod}_g\). Under the standard Johnson-quotient interpretation of the source's undefined \(\pi\), it is the Johnson homomorphism \(\pi:I_g\to A_g\), whose kernel is \(K_g\).

For each subgroup \(L< A_g\), determine whether
\[
 \pi^{-1}(L)=\{f\in I_g:\pi(f)\in L\}
\]
is finitely generated as a group. The task is to characterize precisely those \(L\) with this property, not to assert finite generation for every inverse image or a uniform bound on the number of generators.

The source identifies \(K_g=I_g(2)\) in the Johnson filtration \(\{I_g(k)\}\), and begins a transition to subgroups deeper in that filtration, but gives no further statement in this extract. Its preceding definitions of \(H\), the embedded copy of \(H\) in \(\bigwedge^3H\), and \(\pi\) are absent; the standard interpretation above must be checked against them.
\subsection{Short English statement}
For every subgroup of the Johnson quotient \(\bigwedge^3H/H\), decide whether its full inverse image in the Torelli group is finitely generated, for \(g\geq3\).
