\section{Reconstruction of Many-Body Dynamics from One-Particle Density}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


\paragraph{Definitions and conventions.}
For an $N$-particle state $\Psi(t)$, the one-particle reduced density matrix is obtained by tracing out $N-1$ particles. Its diagonal is the one-particle density $n(x,t)$; for a normalized wave function one convention multiplies this diagonal by $N$ so that $\int n=N$. Bosonic and fermionic wave functions are respectively symmetric and antisymmetric. Reconstruction is relative to fixed interactions and the supplied initial state, with external potentials equivalent up to a purely time-dependent additive scalar when that only changes the global phase.

\paragraph{Target retained from the canonical source catalogue.}
\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}
For time-dependent $N$-boson or $N$-fermion systems with known interactions and initial state, can the full quantum dynamics, and in particular the external potential, be reconstructed from the one-particle density?

\paragraph{Source scope and qualification.}
The scalar density is not the entire one-particle density matrix. The appropriate potential class, time regularity and gauge equivalence must be kept in a precise reconstruction theorem; these are not supplied in the extraction.
\subsection{Short English statement}
With the interaction and initial state known, does a particle density determine the driving potential and the resulting many-body evolution?
