\subsection{What local elimination proves, and what it does not price}
The report already compares high-order methods through interpolation estimates, local solves, sparse direct-solver work and adaptation under stated smoothness and mesh assumptions. We do not treat its empirical conclusions as our measurements. We test the algebraic premise behind hybridization, using a finite block system with interior unknowns $u$ and interface unknowns $\lambda$:
\[
\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\C&D\end{pmatrix}\binom u\lambda=\binom f g.
\]
\begin{proposition}
If $A$ is invertible, the full system is equivalent to
\[
(D-CA^{-1}B)\lambda=g-CA^{-1}f,\qquad u=A^{-1}(f-B\lambda).
\]
If the full matrix is real symmetric positive definite and $C=B^T$, its Schur complement $S=D-B^TA^{-1}B$ is positive definite, and
\[
\lambda^TS\lambda=\min_u (u,\lambda)^T\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\B^T&D\end{pmatrix}(u,\lambda).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The first block equation uniquely gives the displayed expression for $u$; substituting it into the second gives the interface equation, and substitution in reverse recovers the original system. In the positive-definite case, $A$ is positive definite by restricting the quadratic form to $(u,0)$. Completing the square yields
\[
u^TAu+2u^TB\lambda+\lambda^TD\lambda
=(u+A^{-1}B\lambda)^TA(u+A^{-1}B\lambda)+\lambda^TS\lambda.
\]
The first term has minimum zero at $u=-A^{-1}B\lambda$. For $\lambda\ne0$ the corresponding full vector is nonzero, so the minimum is positive. This proves both assertions.
\end{proof}
The equivalence is not an unconditional wave-solver guarantee. The globally invertible matrix $\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$ has singular interior block $A=0$, so this elimination route is undefined. This auxiliary indefinite example is not claimed to be a particular stabilized HDG discretization; it identifies the local-invertibility hypothesis that a wave formulation must establish.
For tensor-product degree $p$ on a three-dimensional element, an illustrative volume polynomial basis has $(p+1)^3$ coefficients, while six separate face bases have $6(p+1)^2$ coefficients. Their ratio is $6/(p+1)$. Fewer interface coefficients at large $p$ alone do not count the cost of eliminating the volume block, filling the Schur complement, solving the global system, or meeting a fixed error tolerance.
