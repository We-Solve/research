\section{Finite Markov templates with several varying level sets}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.


For an integer matrix $A$, the fiber at $b$ is $\mathcal F_b=\{x\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^d:Ax=b\}$. A Markov basis is a finite set of integer moves $u$ with $Au=0$ such that steps $x\mapsto x\pm u$ staying nonnegative connect every nonempty fiber. A hierarchical categorical model is specified by a collection of allowed interactions. Its level sets give the possible values of each variable, and varying their sizes changes the model matrix and its Markov bases. Finiteness can concern move degrees, symmetry classes, or a finite collection of templates rather than a bound on the total number of moves.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Question 27. What types of "finiteness" statements can be made about Markov bases for hierarchical models as more than one set of levels is allowed to vary? \sref{statement}{2}
