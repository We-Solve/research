\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 701059}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a finite group \(H\), define
\[
k(H)=\bigl|\{\{xhx^{-1}:x\in H\}:h\in H\}\bigr|,
\]
the number of conjugacy classes of \(H\). Let \(G\) be any finite group, and write
\[
|G|=\prod_{i=1}^{s}p_i^{a_i},
\]
where the \(p_i\) are the distinct prime divisors of \(|G|\) and each \(a_i\geq 1\). For each \(i\), choose a Sylow \(p_i\)-subgroup \(P_i\leq G\), so that \(|P_i|=p_i^{a_i}\) and \(|G|=\prod_{i=1}^{s}|P_i|\).

Is it true that, for every such \(G\) and every such choice of Sylow subgroups,
\[
k(G)\leq\prod_{i=1}^{s}k(P_i)?
\]
For the trivial group, take \(s=0\) and the empty product to be \(1\); the inequality then reads \(1\leq 1\). The supplied registry labels the problem open; that label is not an independently verified assessment.
\subsection{Short English statement}
L. Pyber asks whether the number of conjugacy classes of every finite group is at most the product of the numbers of conjugacy classes of one Sylow subgroup for each prime dividing its order.
