\section{Local Density Approximation for the Canonical Functional}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


For a prescribed particle density $\rho$, the canonical functional is a fixed-particle-number constrained search, schematically $F(\rho)=\inf_{\Gamma\mapsto\rho}\operatorname{Tr}[(T+W)\Gamma]$, over admissible fermionic states. The local-density approximation replaces a slowly varying inhomogeneous energy by the integral of a uniform-gas energy evaluated at the local density.

\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}
Can the local-density approximation be rigorously justified for the canonical density functional $F(\rho)$?

\paragraph{Scope and source qualification.}The canonical constraint is essential: a grand-canonical result does not automatically settle this question. Scaling, particle number and interaction are those of the report.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Justify the local-density approximation for the fixed-particle-number density functional.
