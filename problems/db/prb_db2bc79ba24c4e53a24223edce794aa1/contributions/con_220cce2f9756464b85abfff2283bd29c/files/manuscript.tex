\section{Local Density Approximation for the Canonical Functional}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


For a prescribed particle density $\rho$, the canonical functional is a fixed-particle-number constrained search, schematically $F(\rho)=\inf_{\Gamma\mapsto\rho}\operatorname{Tr}[(T+W)\Gamma]$, over admissible fermionic states. The local-density approximation replaces a slowly varying inhomogeneous energy by the integral of a uniform-gas energy evaluated at the local density.

\paragraph{Catalogue wording retained for traceability.}
Can the local-density approximation be rigorously justified for the canonical density functional $F(\rho)$?

\paragraph{Scope and source qualification.}The canonical constraint is essential: a grand-canonical result does not automatically settle this question. Scaling, particle number and interaction are those of the report.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Justify the local-density approximation for the fixed-particle-number density functional.

\paragraph{Testing projection from fluctuating particle number.} A proposed route to the canonical functional is to build a grand-canonical local-density trial state and project to the desired total number. Even for two disjoint one-mode cells this changes the local density. Let independent occupations \(X_1,X_2\in\{0,1\}\) have probabilities \(p_1=1/4\), \(p_2=3/4\). Their expected total is one, but
\[
 \mathbb P(X_1=1,X_2=0)=1/16,\quad
 \mathbb P(X_1=0,X_2=1)=9/16.
\]
Conditioning on \(X_1+X_2=1\) gives local means \(1/10\) and \(9/10\), instead of \(1/4\) and \(3/4\). These probabilities define legitimate diagonal fermionic density matrices, so the obstruction is present without coherences or interactions.
\paragraph{A repair in the finite example.} Correlating the original cells instead, use the canonical mixture
\[
 \Gamma=\tfrac14|10\rangle\langle10|+\tfrac34|01\rangle\langle01|.
\]
It has exactly one particle and the prescribed local means. For a diagonal one-body energy \(h_1X_1+h_2X_2\), its expectation equals the independent state's energy. For an interaction \(JX_1X_2\), however, the expectations are respectively zero and \(3J/16\). Thus exact density repair is possible here but changes correlations and interaction energy in a controlled, explicitly nonzero way.
\paragraph{Remaining canonical estimate.} The continuum constrained search must preserve the entire density, not just its integral. The first missing step is a number-correlation or density-repair construction with kinetic and interaction costs negligible in the source local-density scaling. This two-cell calculation supplies neither that uniform estimate nor an asymptotic local-density theorem, and no grand-canonical result is substituted for the canonical target.
