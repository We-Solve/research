\section{Existence of a four-dimensional Euler brick}\label{9300001}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 9300001 · Number Theory}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


A four-dimensional rectangular box has positive edge lengths $a_1,a_2,a_3,a_4$. The diagonal in the coordinate face determined by edges $i,j$ has length $\sqrt{a_i^2+a_j^2}$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Do there exist $a_1,a_2,a_3,a_4\in\mathbb N$ and $d_{ij}\in\mathbb N$ for $1\le i<j\le4$ such that
\[
a_i^2+a_j^2=d_{ij}^2\qquad(1\le i<j\le4)?
\]
Construct such a box or prove its nonexistence. \sref{9300001}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Find four positive integer edge lengths such that each of the six face diagonals also has integer length, or prove that no such choice exists.
