\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 10000030}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let a circle packing of a planar graph \(G=(V,E)\) in \(\R^2\) have disks with centers \(z_v\), pairwise disjoint interiors, and tangency graph \(G\). Let \(f:V\to\Z\) satisfy
\[
 |f(v)-f(w)|\leq1\qquad(\{v,w\}\in E).
\]
For each vertex \(v\), the proposed construction chooses a Euclidean-nearest vertex of strictly lower height:
\[
 u_v\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{u:\ f(u)<f(v)}|z_u-z_v|,
 \qquad E'=E\cup\{\{v,u_v\}:v\in V\}.
\]
The new edges join to lower vertices anywhere in \(V\), not only to original neighbors. Their orientation is ignored for the resulting undirected graph.

The question is whether the resulting graph \((V,E')\) is the exact tangency graph of a sphere packing in \(\R^3\): there should exist closed balls of positive radii with disjoint interiors, indexed by \(V\), such that two balls are tangent if and only if their indices form an edge of \(E'\). No prescribed three-dimensional centers, unit radii, or vertical lifting of the given packing is required by the excerpt.

The construction is undefined when a vertex has no lower vertex; every nonempty finite height-labeled graph has a global minimum. One possible convention is to add no edge at such a vertex, but that convention is not supplied and requires confirmation. On an infinite packing a nearest lower vertex need not exist without additional assumptions, and ties also need a rule. It is not stated whether all choices or some choices must yield a sphere-packable graph. The Euclidean distance is interpreted between the packing centers, the usual embedded-vertex convention, rather than between disk boundaries.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Add to each vertex of a circle-packed graph an edge to its nearest lower-height vertex, for an integer height function changing by at most one along original edges. Must the augmented graph be sphere-packable in three dimensions? Vertices with no lower target, ties, and infinite-packing minima need conventions.
