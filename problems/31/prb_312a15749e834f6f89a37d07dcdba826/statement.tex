\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(D=\{(x,y)\in\R^2:x^2+y^2\leq 1\}\) be the closed disk, and let \(\mu\) be normalized Euclidean area on \(D\), so that \(\mu(D)=1\). A \(C^\infty\)-diffeomorphism \(f:D\to D\) is understood as a diffeomorphism of smooth manifolds with boundary: both \(f\) and \(f^{-1}\) are smooth up to the boundary. It is area-preserving if \(\mu(f^{-1}(A))=\mu(A)\) for every measurable \(A\subseteq D\). Such a diffeomorphism necessarily maps \(\partial D\) onto itself; the supplied statement imposes no pointwise boundary condition.

Under the measure-theoretic interpretation, \(f\) is mixing if, for every pair of measurable sets \(A,B\subseteq D\),
\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu\bigl(f^{-n}(A)\cap B\bigr)=\mu(A)\mu(B).
\]
To specify topological entropy, use the Euclidean metric \(d\) on \(D\). For each integer \(n\geq1\), set
\[
d_n(x,y)=\max_{0\leq k<n}d\bigl(f^k(x),f^k(y)\bigr).
\]
For \(\varepsilon>0\), let \(s_n(\varepsilon)\) be the largest cardinality of a subset of \(D\) whose distinct points have \(d_n\)-distance greater than \(\varepsilon\). Define
\[
h_{\mathrm{top}}(f)=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log s_n(\varepsilon).
\]

With the measure-theoretic interpretation of mixing, the question is whether there exists an area-preserving \(C^\infty\)-diffeomorphism \(f:D\to D\) such that
\[
\left(\forall A,B\subseteq D\text{ measurable},\quad
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu\bigl(f^{-n}(A)\cap B\bigr)=\mu(A)\mu(B)\right)
\quad\text{and}\quad h_{\mathrm{top}}(f)=0.
\]

The supplied record does not explicitly define ``mixing.'' The displayed question is therefore conditional on its measure-theoretic meaning. If topological mixing was intended instead, its condition would be that for every pair of nonempty relatively open subsets \(U,V\subseteq D\), there exists an integer \(N\geq0\) such that \(f^n(U)\cap V\neq\varnothing\) for every integer \(n\geq N\). The intended convention requires source review.
