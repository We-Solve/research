\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 700988}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(G\) be a finite group, and let \(\Z[G]\) be its integral group ring, whose elements are sums \(a=\sum_{g\in G}a_g g\) with \(a_g\in\Z\), with multiplication induced by the group multiplication and distributivity. Write \(\Z[G]^\times\) for its group of units and \(Z(G)\) for the center of \(G\). A unit \(u\in\Z[G]^\times\) is central if \(ua=au\) for every \(a\in\Z[G]\). Define the canonical antiinvolution by
\[
\left(\sum_{g\in G}a_g g\right)^*=\sum_{g\in G}a_g g^{-1}.
\]
Thus \((ab)^*=b^*a^*\). A unit \(v\) is symmetric if \(v^*=v\).

The question is whether, for every finite group \(G\) and every central unit \(u\in\Z[G]^\times\), there exist \(z\in Z(G)\) and a central unit \(v\in\Z[G]^\times\) such that
\[
u=zv\qquad\text{and}\qquad v^*=v,
\]
where \(z\) is identified with its basis element in \(\Z[G]\). No augmentation restriction on the units is stated in the supplied record.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every central unit of the integral group ring of every finite group factor as a central group element times a symmetric central unit, where symmetry means invariance under inversion of the group basis elements?
