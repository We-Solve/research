\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A group identity is an equation \(w(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=1\), where \(w\) belongs to the free group \(F_n\) on \(n\) generators; it holds in a group if every substitution of group elements satisfies the equation. It is nontrivial if \(w\ne 1\) in \(F_n\). For an integer \(p\ge 2\), define
\[
N_{n,p}=\langle\!\langle u^p : u\in F_n\rangle\!\rangle_{F_n},
\]
the normal subgroup generated by all \(p\)-th powers. Thus \(w\in N_{n,p}\) means that \(w=1\) is a consequence of the identity \(x^p=1\).

For a fixed integer \(p\ge 2\), a precise candidate formulation of the intended existence question is: does there exist an infinite group \(G\) such that
\[
\begin{gathered}
\text{every nontrivial proper subgroup }H<G\text{ is cyclic of order }p,\\
 g^p=1\quad\text{for every }g\in G,\\
\text{for every }n\ge 1\text{ and }w\in F_n,\qquad
\bigl(\forall(g_1,\ldots,g_n)\in G^n,\ w(g_1,\ldots,g_n)=1\bigr)
\ \Longleftrightarrow\ w\in N_{n,p}?
\end{gathered}
\]
The last condition states that the identities of \(G\) are exactly the consequences of \(x^p=1\).
