\paragraph{Recovering the proposed spin-wave coefficient.} For independent bosons with continuum quadratic dispersion \(\varepsilon(k)=S|k|^2\) in two dimensions, the formal thermodynamic free energy density is the convergent integral
\[
 f_{\rm sw}=\frac1\beta\int_{\mathbb R^2}\frac{d^2k}{(2\pi)^2}\log(1-e^{-\beta S|k|^2}).
\]
Expand \(-\log(1-z)=\sum_{n\ge1}z^n/n\); its nonnegative terms justify integrating the negative of this expression term by term. The Gaussian integral is \(\int d^2k\,e^{-n\beta S|k|^2}=\pi/(n\beta S)\). Hence
\[
 f_{\rm sw}=-\frac1{4\pi S\beta^2}\sum_{n\ge1}\frac1{n^2}=-\frac\pi{24S\beta^2},
\]
using the elementary Euler sum \(\sum n^{-2}=\pi^2/6\).
\paragraph{The infrared obstruction.} The same approximation has divergent particle density: for sufficiently small \(|k|\), \(e^{\beta S|k|^2}-1\le2\beta S|k|^2\), so the density integral dominates a positive constant times \(\int_0^\delta dr/r\). Free energy is integrable at the origin, while density is not. Therefore controlling spin-wave interactions by a uniform small boson density cannot follow from this free-energy computation in infinite volume.
