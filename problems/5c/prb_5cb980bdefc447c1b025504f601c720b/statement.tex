\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
The source asks for the joint $R$-transform of $(c,c^*)$ when $c$ is unbounded and $R$-diagonal. Its appended ``Focus Group on Dirichlet Forms, from Classical to Quantum (day 5)'' is the following section heading. The source specifies neither moment assumptions nor whether a formal cumulant series or an analytic transform is intended.

In a finite tracial von Neumann algebra, an affiliated $R$-diagonal distribution can be represented by $uh$, with $h\ge0$ affiliated and a Haar unitary $u$ free from $h$. Its moment-free distribution within this class is determined by the spectral probability law $\mu_h$ of $h=|c|$. Let $\nu$ be its symmetrization on $\R$. For $z$ in the upper half-plane define
\[
 G_\nu(z)=\int\frac{\dd\nu(t)}{z-t},\qquad F_\nu(z)=1/G_\nu(z).
\]
The analytic Voiculescu transform uses the appropriate inverse branch $F_\nu^{-1}$ near infinity on a nontangential domain; equivalently an analytic $R$-transform is $G_\nu^{-1}(w)-1/w$ on the corresponding near-zero domain. No finite moments or analytic extension through zero is automatic. The aid reports that this transform is information-complete for the $R$-diagonal class and that symmetrized modulus laws linearize free sums:
\[
 \widetilde\mu_{|c+d|}=\widetilde\mu_{|c|}\boxplus\widetilde\mu_{|d|}
\]
for the appropriate free affiliated $R$-diagonal operators, with sum interpreted as an affiliated closure.

When moments through degree $2N$ exist, the aid identifies the available alternating determining coefficients with scalar even cumulants of $\nu$:
\[
 \alpha_k=\kappa_{2k}(\nu)\quad(1\le k\le N).
\]
If all moments are available, the tracial formal joint series is
\[
 R_{c,c^*}(z_1,z_2)=\sum_{k\ge1}\alpha_k
 \bigl[(z_1z_2)^k+(z_2z_1)^k\bigr],
\]
with nonalternating cumulants zero. Without the requisite moments this series is undefined, and even all finite moments do not by themselves give a convergent series at zero.

For a moment-free bounded regularization, the aid sets
\[
 b_s=c(1+s c^*c)^{-1/2},\qquad s>0,\qquad \|b_s\|\le s^{-1/2}.
\]
It is $R$-diagonal and has an ordinary joint $R$-transform. Its first alternating coefficient is
\[
 \alpha_1(s)=\tau(b_s^*b_s)=\int_0^\infty\frac{t^2}{1+st^2}\,\dd\mu_h(t).
\]
The function of all $s>0$ determines $\mu_h$, since $1-s\alpha_1(s)=\int(1+st^2)^{-1}\,\dd\mu_h(t)$ is a Stieltjes encoding of the squared-modulus law. The aid also states that every alternating cumulant justified by a finite $2k$-th modulus moment is recovered as $\lim_{s\downarrow0}\alpha_k(b_s)$. This supplies analytic and regularized alternatives to the possibly nonexistent literal joint cumulant transform; it does not silently impose moments on the original unbounded input.
\subsection{Short English statement}
An unbounded $R$-diagonal pair need not have the moments required for a formal joint $R$-series. The aid instead uses the analytic transform of the symmetrized modulus and bounded regularizations. The entire unbounded distribution is encoded by the first regularized coefficient as a function of its damping parameter.
