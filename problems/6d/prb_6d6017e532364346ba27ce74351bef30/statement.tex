\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A Kleinian group is a discrete subgroup $\Gamma\le\mathrm{PSL}_2(\C)$. In the usual finitely generated setting, degeneracy concerns geometrically infinite ends of the associated hyperbolic $3$-orbifold or manifold $\mathbb H^3/\Gamma$; the excerpt does not say whether ``degenerate'' means singly degenerate, doubly degenerate, or another specified end condition.

Let $\widetilde\Gamma$ be the full inverse image of $\Gamma$ in $\mathrm{SL}_2(\C)$. Its ordinary trace field can be written
\[
 k_{\mathrm{tr}}(\Gamma)=\Q\bigl(\operatorname{tr}(A):A\in\widetilde\Gamma\bigr).
\]
The sign choice of a lift does not change this field. An algebraic trace field means $k_{\mathrm{tr}}(\Gamma)\subset\overline\Q$, so its elements are algebraic over $\Q$; if a finite-degree number-field convention is intended, that must be stated with the relevant finite-generation assumptions. The ordinary trace field is not silently replaced by the invariant trace field.

A fiber group, in the customary hyperbolic-$3$-manifold interpretation, is the image of the fundamental group of a surface fiber in a fibration of a hyperbolic $3$-manifold over $S^1$. Whether ``fiber'' includes virtual fiber groups, and what finiteness or boundary assumptions are imposed on the ambient manifold, are not specified in the excerpt.

\textbf{Question (Agol).} Does there exist a degenerate Kleinian group $\Gamma$ that is not the fiber of a fibration and has algebraic trace field? Equivalently, seek existence subject simultaneously to the three source conditions: degeneracy, exclusion of the intended fiber-group class, and algebraicity of $k_{\mathrm{tr}}(\Gamma)$. The exact degeneracy, finite-generation, torsion, and fiber conventions require source confirmation. This is not a claim that every degenerate group must be a fiber, and the algebraic-trace-field condition must not be dropped.

The registry summary places the question near tameness and ending-lamination results, while expressly stating that those results do not settle the existence question. Tameness of the quotient and realization as a fiber group are distinct requirements.
