\subsection{Randomization is necessary even before computational questions}
The source record is broad workshop framing and is retained as such. We test a concrete proposed simplification: use a deterministic release to avoid the complexity of randomization. Assume finite databases of a fixed size over a finite nonempty universe, with one-row replacement as adjacency and pure differential privacy at some finite $\varepsilon\ge0$.
\begin{proposition}
Every deterministic pure-private mechanism on this database space is constant. For one binary record, a randomized binary estimate has worst-case probability of being correct at most $e^\varepsilon/(1+e^\varepsilon)$, and randomized response attains this value.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If neighboring databases $D,D'$ had different deterministic outputs, the event consisting of the output on $D$ would have probability one on $D$ and zero on $D'$, contradicting the privacy inequality. Any two fixed-size databases are joined by changing their rows one at a time, so the output is constant throughout the space.
For a binary estimate let $p$ be its worst-case correct probability. Then $\Pr(M(1)=1)\ge p$ and $\Pr(M(0)=1)\le1-p$. Privacy for the event $\{1\}$ gives $p\le e^\varepsilon(1-p)$, hence the upper bound. Randomized response outputs the true input with probability $a=e^\varepsilon/(1+e^\varepsilon)$ and its complement with probability $1-a$. For either singleton output, the likelihood ratio between the two inputs is either $a/(1-a)=e^\varepsilon$ or its reciprocal. Summing over outputs verifies privacy for every event. Both inputs have success probability $a$, proving attainment.
\end{proof}
