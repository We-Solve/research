\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Use the usual finite simple underlying-graph convention for a topological graph: vertices are distinct points in the plane and edges are Jordan arcs joining their endpoints with no vertex in an edge interior. Crossings are proper intersections of edge interiors, so a shared endpoint alone is not a crossing. The precise drawing general-position convention is not supplied. The graph is $k$-quasiplanar when there are no distinct edges $e_1,\ldots,e_k$ such that every pair $e_i,e_j$ crosses. The pairwise crossings need not occur at one common point. The source asks whether \[\forall k\in\Z,\ k>4\ \exists C_k>0\ \forall n\geq1\ \forall\text{ $k$-quasiplanar topological graphs }G\text{ on }n\text{ vertices}:\qquad |E(G)|\leq C_k n.\] Thus the constant may depend on the fixed $k$ but must be independent of $n$ and the drawing. No straight-line condition or restriction that two edges meet at most once is inserted; that latter condition would restrict the drawing to a smaller class than general topological graphs. The underlying simple-graph convention is needed to exclude arbitrary parallel noncrossing edges, and should be confirmed from the report. The supplied 2026-08-21 triage labels the conjecture open for $k>4$ and reports improving upper bounds and special cases without the general linear bound.
\subsection{Short English statement}
For each fixed $k>4$, must a topological graph with no $k$ pairwise crossing edges have at most $C_k n$ edges? The drawing is not silently restricted to straight edges or at most one crossing per edge pair.
