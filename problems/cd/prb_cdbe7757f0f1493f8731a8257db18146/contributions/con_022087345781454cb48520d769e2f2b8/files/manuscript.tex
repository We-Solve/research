\section*{Ecosystem resilience and tipping points}
\label{prob:198}
\noindent\textbf{Status: restricted result or obstruction; full target not established.}
\subsection{Target and scope}
\paragraph{Catalogue definitions and assumptions.}
Let ecosystem state $x$ obey $\,\mathrm{d} x=f_\theta(x,u_t)\,\mathrm{d} t+\sigma_\theta(x)\,\mathrm{d} W_t$, with standard Brownian motion $W_t$, bounded smooth rates and a specified slowly varying driver $u_t$. Measurements are $y_t=H_{\mathrm{obs}}x_t+\epsilon_t$ with calibrated error. Under frozen driver $u$, deterministic attraction basin is $B_\theta(u)$. Define normalized distance $d_\theta(x,\partial B)=\inf_{z\in\partial B}\|D^{-1}(x-z)\|$, with declared scaling matrix $D$. Stochastic regime-exit time $\tau$ is defined by an operational ecological state criterion. Here $H$ is the warning horizon, while $H_{\mathrm{obs}}$ is the known observation matrix. Distances use positive diagonal $D$ with biological scale units; unobserved basin features produce intervals, not point certainty. Warning performance is checked on independent perturbation sequences under the declared observation law. Let $\mathcal D_{\le t}$ contain observed states and driver histories through time $t$.
\paragraph{Original target.}
Estimate basin-distance bounds and a warning rule $A(\mathcal D_{\le t})$ for exit within horizon $H$ satisfying
\[
 \Pr(A=1\mid\tau>t+H)\le\alpha,
 \qquad \Pr(A=1\mid\tau\le t+H)\ge1-\beta,
\]
for a specified separated model class. Determine which observations or controlled perturbations make these bounds attainable.
\paragraph{Catalogue alternative formulations.}
(i) Near a slowly traversed saddle-node test critical-slowing-down indicators and recovery experiments. (ii) For noise-induced switching, abrupt driver shifts or hidden state variables use exit-probability prediction instead; no universal slowing-down precursor is assumed.
\subsection{Attempted answer}
\begin{proposition}
For community label $J$ sampled with weights $w_j$ and species identity $S$ sampled within that community, Shannon diversity satisfies $H(S)=\sum_jw_jH(S\mid J=j)+I(S;J)$. Exponentiation gives multiplicative effective-number diversity with between-community factor between one and $e^{H(J)}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Apply the mutual-information identity and its bounds $0\leq I(S;J)\leq H(J)$. Define within-community effective diversity by exponentiating the weighted mean entropy, rather than averaging the effective numbers themselves.
\end{proof}
\subsection{What remains unresolved}
The decomposition depends on the diversity convention and sampling weights. It does not generate abundance distributions or attribute beta diversity to dispersal, environment or history.

\section{Shared mathematical bounds}\label{app:bounds}
\begin{lemma}[Independent bounded sums]\label{lem:bounded}
Let independent real variables $X_i$ satisfy $a_i\leq X_i\leq b_i$ almost surely,
and let $S=\sum_{i=1}^n(X_i-\mathbb{E} X_i)$. If
$V=\sum_i(b_i-a_i)^2>0$, then for every $t>0$,
\[
 \mathbb{P}(|S|\geq t)\leq 2\exp(-2t^2/V).
\]
In particular, independent losses in $[0,1]$ have mean-error bound
$\mathbb{P}(|\overline X-\mathbb{E}\overline X|\geq\epsilon)
\leq2e^{-2n\epsilon^2}$. The same expression for variables in $[-1,1]$
is $2e^{-n\epsilon^2/2}$. If $V=0$ then $S=0$ almost surely.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $X\in[a,b]$ with mean $\mu$, the nonnegative product
$(X-a)(b-X)$ gives
$\operatorname{Var} X\leq(\mu-a)(b-\mu)\leq(b-a)^2/4$.
Let $K(s)=\log\mathbb{E}\exp[s(X-\mathbb{E} X)]$.
Boundedness permits two differentiations. Its second derivative is the variance
under the exponentially tilted law, which has the same support, so
$K''(s)\leq(b-a)^2/4$ for every real $s$.
Because $K(0)=K'(0)=0$, integration yields
$K(s)\leq s^2(b-a)^2/8$.
Independence adds these log moment-generating bounds for $S$.
For $s>0$, Markov's inequality gives
$\mathbb{P}(S\geq t)\leq\exp(-st+s^2V/8)$.
Choosing $s=4t/V$ yields the upper-tail bound $e^{-2t^2/V}$.
Apply it also to $-S$ and add. The stated mean versions follow by substituting
$t=n\epsilon$ and the respective support widths.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Finite-family extension.}
For $M$ prespecified bounded-loss comparisons, apply the relevant inequality to
each and use a union bound. Independence between comparisons is not required;
independence of the biological units within each comparison is. Repeated cells,
reads or time points from one dependent biological unit cannot automatically be
counted as independent units.

\paragraph{No data-dependent shortcut.}
The bound does not justify choosing a model or validation loss using the same
outcomes and then reporting single-model coverage. A prespecified finite family,
a separate validation sample, or an appropriate complexity or sequential argument
is needed. Marginal predictive coverage likewise should not be read as coverage
for every subgroup or under arbitrary distribution shift.
