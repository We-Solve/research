\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $K\subset\R^d$ be a smooth convex body. For $N\geq d+1$, let $\mathcal P_N(K)$ be the class of full-dimensional polytopes $P\subseteq K$ with at most $N$ vertices, all lying on $\partial K$, using the usual inscribed-polytope convention.

One affine-invariant comparison is Banach--Mazur distance. For full-dimensional convex bodies $K,L$, an explicit general-body convention is
\[
d_{\mathrm{BM}}(K,L)=\inf\{\lambda\geq1:\ K-a\subseteq T(L-b)\subseteq\lambda(K-a),\
 a\in\operatorname{int}K,\ b\in\operatorname{int}L,\ T\in\operatorname{GL}_d(\R)\}.
\]
Corresponding approximation errors include
\[
\begin{aligned}
E_N^{\mathrm{BM}}(K)&=\inf_{P\in\mathcal P_N(K)}(d_{\mathrm{BM}}(K,P)-1),\\
E_N^{\mathrm{vol}}(K)&=\inf_{P\in\mathcal P_N(K)}(\operatorname{vol}_d(K)-\operatorname{vol}_d(P)).
\end{aligned}
\]
The source asks how well approximation is possible under the complexity restriction, especially prescribed vertex number: determine optimal quantitative bounds or asymptotics for such errors in the intended smoothness and dimension classes. Other intended complexity measures must be specified separately.

Raw volume difference, although explicitly listed in the source, is not invariant under every invertible affine transformation: it scales by the absolute determinant of the linear part. Relative volume deficit $E_N^{\mathrm{vol}}(K)/\operatorname{vol}_d(K)$ is affine invariant. Both are distinguished rather than silently normalizing the source's volume difference. The extract does not specify differentiability order, positive curvature, fixed-body versus uniform estimates, exact versus at-most vertex number, or a requested rate, constant or asymptotic regime.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Determine optimal approximation of smooth convex bodies by inscribed polytopes with controlled complexity, especially vertex count. Banach--Mazur error and volume deficit are distinct targets, and only a normalized volume deficit is affine invariant under arbitrary affine changes.
