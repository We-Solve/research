\section{Capacity and Reliability of Mismatched Decoding}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.


For a memoryless channel $W(y\mid x)$, a mismatched decoder ranks codewords by a prescribed metric $q$ instead of the channel likelihood; in the product-metric formulation it maximizes $\prod_{t=1}^n q(x_t,y_t)$. Mismatched capacity is the supremum of rates achievable with vanishing decoding error for this fixed metric. At rate $R$, the reliability function measures the best attainable exponent $\liminf_{n\to\infty}-n^{-1}\log P_e^{(n)}$. A single-letter characterization uses distributions on one-use variables rather than a limit over block lengths.

\section{Literature and context check}
The primary report, p.~699, asks for general single-letter capacity and reliability formulas while keeping the additive decoding metric fixed. The preserved definition uses a product metric. Below the additive score $s(x,y)=\mathbf1\{x\ne y\}$ is equivalent to the positive product metric $q(x,y)=\exp(s(x,y))$. This distinction matters: a product of zero-one mismatch indicators would define a different decoder.
\section{Research attempt: A reverse-Hamming metric with a uniform error floor}
Consider a binary symmetric channel with crossover $p\in[0,1/2]$, and a decoder maximizing total Hamming distance from the received word. Let two distinct codewords $x,x'$ have distance $d\geq1$. When $x$ is transmitted, let $K$ be the number of channel flips among the $d$ coordinates on which the codewords differ. Coordinates on which they agree contribute equally to their scores. On the differing coordinates, the scores of $x$ and $x'$ are $K$ and $d-K$. Hence $K<d/2$ forces the wrong word to score strictly above the true word, regardless of all other codewords and all tie conventions.

Using independent uniform random variables $U_j$, the representation $K_p=\sum_{j=1}^d\mathbf1\{U_j\leq p\}$ couples $K_p\leq K_{1/2}$. Therefore
\[
 \Pr\{K_p<d/2\}\geq\Pr\{K_{1/2}<d/2\}\geq\tfrac14. \tag{3}
\]
For odd $d$, symmetry makes the middle probability $1/2$. For $d=2l\geq2$, it is
\[
 {1\over2}\left(1-\binom{2l}{l}4^{-l}\right)\geq\tfrac14.
\]
Indeed the central probability equals $1/2$ at $l=1$, and the ratio of its value at $l+1$ to that at $l$ is $(2l+1)/(2l+2)<1$. This proves (3), including the worst small-distance boundary.

In any code with at least two distinct codewords, fix one competitor for each transmitted word and apply (3). Every message then has conditional error at least $1/4$, and so does the average. Consequently the mismatched capacity of this channel-metric pair is zero. At every positive rate for which code sequences are considered, $P_e\geq1/4$ implies
$0\leq-n^{-1}\log P_e\leq n^{-1}\log4\to0$; its achievable reliability exponent is therefore zero. Rate zero with a one-message code is excluded from this last assertion.

At the sharper boundary $p=0$, every different codeword has strictly greater score than the transmitted word, so the error is one whenever there is a competitor. Yet the same noiseless physical channel with maximum-likelihood decoding can transmit all $2^n$ binary words with zero error. This exhibits the exact obstruction to replacing a fixed mismatched metric by an optimized likelihood metric.
\section{Verification and adversarial audit}
The event used in (3) gives a strict score inequality, so even favorable ties do not invalidate the lower bound. The cases $d=1$, $d=2$, $p=0$, and $p=1/2$ are explicit. Codewords are distinct as in ordinary block codes; repeating them cannot supply a positive reliable rate. The product/additive conversion uses exponentiation, not a zero-one product.
