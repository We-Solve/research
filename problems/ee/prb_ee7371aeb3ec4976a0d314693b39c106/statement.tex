\section{Strong Cosmic Censorship for Spherical Einstein–Vlasov Collapse}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$; the standard number fields have their usual meanings. All additional conventions are stated locally.


\paragraph{Definitions and conventions.} The Einstein--Vlasov system couples $G_{\mu\nu}=8\pi T_{\mu\nu}[f]$ to collisionless transport of a nonnegative phase-space density $f$ along the spacetime geodesic flow on the particle mass shell. The stress-energy tensor is obtained by integrating particle momenta against $f$. Asymptotically flat initial data describe an isolated system; spherical symmetry reduces the metric and matter fields to radial variables. Weak cosmic censorship asks for complete future null infinity for generic admissible data. Strong cosmic censorship concerns inextendibility of the maximal globally hyperbolic development in a specified regularity class. In the source's advanced Eddington--Finkelstein coordinates $(v,r)$, $ds^2=-a(v,r)b(v,r)^2\,dv^2+2b(v,r)\,dv\,dr+r^2d\Omega^2$, with $b>0$. A sphere of constant $v,r$ is trapped when $a<0$. The cited class of initial data begins with $a>0$ and is arranged so that a trapped surface subsequently forms. A curvature singularity means blow-up of curvature in the sense to be specified by the collapse statement.

\paragraph{Source scope and qualifications.} The claim concerns the particular shell-like initial-data class in the report's cited collapse theorem, not arbitrary data merely satisfying a>0 somewhere. The report proves future completeness of the event-horizon generator but only conjectures the central singularity and strong censorship. A regularity class for inextendibility remains necessary.
