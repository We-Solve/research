\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(G=(V,E)\) be either the infinite hexagonal lattice (the vertices and edges of the regular hexagonal tiling) or the square lattice (\(V=\Z^2\), with nearest-neighbor edges). For an integer \(q\ge 2\), a spin configuration is an element of \(\Omega_q=\{1,\ldots,q\}^{V}\). A conventional dimensionless parameter for the antiferromagnetic Potts model is \(K\ge 0\), with equal neighboring spins penalized by the factor \(e^{-K}\). For finite \(\Lambda\subset V\) and exterior configuration \(\tau\), its finite-volume conditional distribution is
\[
 \gamma_{\Lambda,K}^{\tau}(\sigma_\Lambda)
 =\frac{1}{Z_{\Lambda,K}^{\tau}}
 \exp\!\left[-K\!\sum_{\substack{\{x,y\}\in E\\\{x,y\}\cap\Lambda\ne\varnothing}}
 \mathbf{1}_{\{\eta_x=\eta_y\}}\right],
 \qquad
 \eta|_\Lambda=\sigma_\Lambda,\quad
 \eta|_{V\setminus\Lambda}=\tau.
\]
Here \(Z_{\Lambda,K}^{\tau}\) normalizes the distribution. An infinite-volume Gibbs measure is a probability measure on \(\Omega_q\) whose conditional distributions in every finite \(\Lambda\) agree almost surely with these kernels. If zero temperature is included, the corresponding hard-constraint model is the uniform proper-coloring specification: conditionally on the exterior, all proper extensions to \(\Lambda\) have equal probability, for admissible exterior configurations.
