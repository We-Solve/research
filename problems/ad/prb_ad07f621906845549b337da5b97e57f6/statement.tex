\section{Decidability of Linear Information Inequalities}\label{30005059}
{\small\sffamily UnsolvedMath ID 30005059 · Computer Science}
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.


For finite-valued jointly distributed variables $X_1,\ldots,X_m$, let $X_S=(X_i)_{i\in S}$ and let $H(X_S)=-\sum_x p_S(x)\log p_S(x)$, with $0\log0=0$ and $H(X_\varnothing)=0$. The input to the decision problem is a finite rational coefficient list $(c_S)_{S\subseteq[m]}$. Validity means $\sum_S c_SH(X_S)\geq0$ for every joint distribution and every choice of finite alphabet sizes. Decidability asks for an algorithm that halts with the correct yes/no answer for every such input.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Is the validity of linear information inequalities decidable? \sref{30005059}{2}
\paragraph{Scope qualification.}
Alphabet sizes are unbounded. This concerns unconditional linear inequalities; implications from additional equalities or inequalities are separate decision problems.

\subsection{Short English statement}
Can an algorithm always decide whether a proposed linear inequality between entropies holds for every finite joint distribution?
