\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(G=(V,E)\) be a finite labelled graph with prescribed bar lengths, realized by placements \(p:V\to\R^d\) satisfying \(|p_i-p_j|=\ell_{ij}\) for \(ij\in E\). A finite symmetry group consists of graph automorphisms \(\Gamma\leq\operatorname{Aut}(G)\), preserving these lengths, together with ambient Euclidean isometries realizing the relabellings at an initial placement \(p_0\). Let \(\mathcal C\) be the configuration space modulo all ambient Euclidean congruences. For a path \(q(t)=[p(t)]\), call the motion expansive if every labelled pairwise squared distance
\[
D_{ij}(q(t))=|p_i(t)-p_j(t)|^2
\]
is nondecreasing; bar distances remain constant. Schulze asks whether a symmetric framework with a one-degree-of-freedom expansive mechanism must preserve its symmetry.

The supplied research aid gives a regular-case statement. Suppose the connected component \(C\subset\mathcal C\) containing \(q_0=[p_0]\) is a smooth regular one-manifold, all its placements affinely span \(\R^d\), and the finite relabelling action is smooth. Assume there is a nonzero tangent \(0\ne\tau\in T_{q_0}C\) with
\[
\dd D_{ij}|_{q_0}(\tau)\geq0\qquad(i,j\in V).
\]
The aid reports that every \(\gamma\in\Gamma\) acts as the identity on all of \(C\). Thus every congruence class on the component is fixed by the original relabellings. The reported obstruction is that a reversing action on \(T_{q_0}C\) would send the nonnegative vector \((\dd D_{ij}(\tau))_{i<j}\), up to permutation, to its negative. This would force all its entries to vanish. Affine spanning and infinitesimal rigidity of the complete framework then contradict \(\tau\ne0\) in the congruence quotient. \subsection{Short English statement}
Must a one-degree-of-freedom expansive motion of a symmetric linkage preserve symmetry? The supplied aid answers under a regular, affinely spanning, one-dimensional moduli hypothesis with a nonzero expansive tangent. Singular branches and motions whose first distance changes occur only at higher order remain outside that conclusion.
