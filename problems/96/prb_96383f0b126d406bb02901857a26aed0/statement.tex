\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For $m\geq1$ let $\mathbb B^m=\{z\in\C^m:\|z\|<1\}$. A map $R:\mathbb B^n\to\mathbb B^N$ is proper if inverse images of compact sets are compact. A rational proper map means a holomorphic vector-valued map whose components are rational functions. For a holomorphic map define its interior jet at zero by $j^r_0R=(\partial^\alpha R(0))_{|\alpha|\leq r}$, including its value. Define $r_{\mathrm{rat}}(n,N)$ as the least nonnegative integer $r$ such that every pair of rational proper maps $R,\widetilde R:\mathbb B^n\to\mathbb B^N$ with $j^r_0R=j^r_0\widetilde R$ satisfies $R=\widetilde R$; set it to $+\infty$ if no finite order works. Define $r_{\mathrm{hol}}(n,N)$ analogously for all proper holomorphic maps. Lamel's question is to find the smallest uniform order, first for the rational class and then without rationality. This is equality of maps in fixed coordinates, not equality modulo automorphisms. The supplied aid interprets the extraction-damaged jet as an interior jet at $0$.

For integers $2\leq n\leq N$ put
\[
D=\left\lfloor\frac{N(N-1)}{2(2n-3)}\right\rfloor,\qquad
h=\max\left\{d\in\Z_{\geq1}:\binom{n+d-1}{d}\leq N\right\}.
\]
The supplied partial answer is
\[
h\leq r_{\mathrm{rat}}(n,N)\leq2D.
\]
It invokes a D'Angelo--Lebl degree bound $\deg R\leq D$, with degree measured by a reduced common-denominator representation $R=P/q$. The auxiliary rational-germ statement is: two holomorphic rational germs with numerators and denominators of degree at most $d$ and nonzero denominators at the center coincide if their $2d$-jets coincide. Indeed the cross-multiplied numerator has degree at most $2d$; this explains the stated uniform bound without asserting sharpness.

For the lower bound the aid uses the symmetric tensor map
\[
V_d(z)=\bigl(\sqrt{d!/\alpha!}\,z^\alpha\bigr)_{|\alpha|=d}\in\C^{\binom{n+d-1}{d}},
\qquad \|V_d(z)\|^2=\|z\|^{2d}.
\]
After zero padding into $\C^N$, $V_d$ and $-V_d$ are distinct proper maps with identical jets through order $d-1$. In the nonempty subclass of homogeneous degree-$d$ proper polynomial maps the exact uniform order is $d$; this subclass requires enough target components, in particular $N\geq\binom{n+d-1}{d}$. The aid also reports the exact value $r_{\mathrm{rat}}(n,n)=1$ for $n\geq2$.

For source dimension one, no finite uniform order exists even in the rational class: for each proposed $r$ choose $d>r$ and the distinct maps $z\mapsto(z^d,0,\ldots,0)$ and $z\mapsto(-z^d,0,\ldots,0)$. Hence $r_{\mathrm{rat}}(1,N)=r_{\mathrm{hol}}(1,N)=+\infty$ for every fixed $N\geq1$. The supplied finite bracket concerns the rational class with $n\geq2$; it neither finds the exact value for arbitrary $(n,N)$ nor resolves the unrestricted proper-holomorphic question. Empty map classes, for example possible dimension ranges excluded from the displayed assumptions, need a separate convention for a vacuous least order.
\subsection{Short English statement}
The problem asks how many derivatives at an interior point determine a proper map between fixed balls. The supplied rational-map result gives an explicit dimension-dependent lower and upper bound, with exact answers for equal dimensions and homogeneous polynomial subclasses. Disk source maps have unbounded degree and no finite uniform order. The general question without rationality is retained separately.
