\subsection{Attempt: false certification under repeated attempts}
The source gives no certification domain or validity standard. I tested whether controlling the false-pass chance of one attempt automatically controls false issuance when a persistently unqualified candidate may retry. Here ``unqualified'' is a declared fixed state solely for the model; learning between attempts is not assumed away in any real assessment.
\begin{proposition}
If each attempt independently passes an unqualified candidate with probability $\alpha\in(0,1)$, issuance within $T$ attempts has probability $1-(1-\alpha)^T$, tending to one. More generally, if the conditional probability of a false pass on attempt $t$, given the complete preceding history, is at most $\alpha_t$, then the chance of any false pass is at most $\sum_{t\ge1}\alpha_t$, without independence.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
In the independent model, absence of a pass in all $T$ attempts has probability $(1-\alpha)^T$; complementing proves the first formula and limit. For the general statement, let $I_t$ indicate a false pass, defining it to be zero if the process has stopped before attempt $t$. The conditional assumption gives $E(I_t)\le\alpha_t$ by taking expectations. For any finite $T$, the indicator of at least one false pass is at most $\sum_{t=1}^TI_t$, so its probability is at most $\sum_{t=1}^T\alpha_t$. Letting $T$ increase and using continuity of probability for increasing events gives the infinite bound. Choosing $\alpha_t=\delta/2^t$ makes the sum equal to $\delta$.
\end{proof}
\subsection{Verification and remaining gaps}
At per-attempt error $1/20$, 20 independent tries yield false issuance probability $1-(19/20)^{20}\approx0.6415$. This is the familiar multiple-attempt effect, not a new certification theorem.
