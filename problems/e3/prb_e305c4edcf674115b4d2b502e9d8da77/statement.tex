\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
The source asks for necessary or sufficient conditions on Borel-fixed monomial ideals to occur as generic initial ideals of locally Cohen--Macaulay curves. It does not state the field, term order or ambient projective space. The supplied aid treats the following precise space-curve setting: $k$ is infinite of characteristic zero, $R=k[x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3]$, and initial ideals use degree reverse lexicographic order with $x_0>x_1>x_2>x_3$.

An ideal is strongly stable if replacing a factor $x_j$ of any monomial by $x_i$, $i<j$, keeps the monomial in the ideal; in this characteristic this is the relevant Borel-fixed condition. For a homogeneous ideal $I$, $\operatorname{gin}(I)$ is the initial ideal after a general linear coordinate change. Suppose the candidate monomial ideal $J$ is saturated and strongly stable and has a curve Hilbert polynomial $dt+1-g$, $d>0$.

The aid reports the exact ACM criterion:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 J\text{ is the gin of an arithmetically Cohen--Macaulay curve in }\PP^3_k
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 &\text{no minimal generator of }J\text{ involves }x_2\text{ or }x_3\\
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 &J=B R\text{ for a finite-colength strongly stable }B\subset k[x_0,x_1].
 \end{aligned}
\]
Here ACM means that the homogeneous coordinate ring is Cohen--Macaulay, which is stronger than the local Cohen--Macaulay condition on the projective curve. Under the baseline saturation and strong-stability assumptions, no minimal generator involves $x_3$ already, so the remaining finite check is absence of $x_2$.

When the condition holds, $J$ itself supplies a realization: its projective scheme is an ACM, hence locally Cohen--Macaulay, multiple line supported on $x_0=x_1=0$, and $\operatorname{gin}(J)=J$. For the finite Hilbert function
\[
 h_i=\dim_k(k[x_0,x_1]/B)_i,
\]
the degree and arithmetic genus are
\[
 d=\sum_i h_i,\qquad g=1-d+\sum_i i h_i.
\]
Conversely, if a minimal generator involves $x_2$, every locally Cohen--Macaulay curve realizing $J$ as gin must be non-ACM and have nonzero Rao module
\[
 M_C=\bigoplus_{t\in\Z}H^1(\PP^3_k,\mathcal I_C(t)).
\]
This is a conditional obstruction on any realization, not a proof that such a non-ACM realization exists or cannot exist. Thus the reported criterion settles the entire ACM region of the stated reverse-lexicographic problem but leaves the broader locally Cohen--Macaulay realizability problem open in the supplied assessment. No characteristic-$p$ replacement of Borel fixation by strong stability is asserted.
\subsection{Short English statement}
In the aid's characteristic-zero reverse-lexicographic setting, a candidate gin has an ACM curve realization exactly when its minimal generators use only the first two variables. The ideal itself then gives a multiple-line witness and explicit degree and genus. A generator in the third variable forces any locally Cohen--Macaulay witness to be non-ACM, without resolving its existence.
