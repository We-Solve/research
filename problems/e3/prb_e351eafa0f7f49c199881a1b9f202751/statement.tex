\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 2301042}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $k\geq3$ be an integer, let $a_0,\ldots,a_{k-2}\in\C[z]$, and let $f$ be meromorphic on $\C$. Define
\[
F(z)=f^{(k)}(z)+\sum_{j=0}^{k-2}a_j(z)f^{(j)}(z),
\qquad f^{(0)}=f,
\]
and assume $F$ is nonconstant. The coefficient of $f^{(k-1)}$ is absent. Frank and Langley ask to characterize all such $f$ for which neither $f$ nor $F$ has any zero in $\C$; poles are permitted, and zeros of any multiplicity are excluded.

The source reports that Frank--Hellerstein settled the case of entire $f$, and Steinmetz settled the case of constant coefficients $a_j$ using their results. The remaining general meromorphic, nonconstant-coefficient setting is the stated target, including functions with either finitely or infinitely many poles.

The source separately suggests, without asserting a theorem, the following possible necessary form for the infinite-pole case: if the $a_j$ are not all constant and $f$ is a solution with infinitely many poles, then
\[
f=(H')^{(k-1)/2}H^{-l},\qquad
l\in\Z_{>0},\qquad H''/H'\in\C[z].
\]
This is a proposed restriction on solutions, not a claim that every function of the displayed form satisfies the zero-free conditions for arbitrary coefficients. The record does not specify the function class and normalization of $H$ or the branch convention for $(H')^{(k-1)/2}$ when $k$ is even; these must make the displayed expression globally meromorphic. The speculative infinite-pole form also does not by itself classify the finite-pole cases. The supplied summary retains partial-progress status and says no recent complete resolution was located.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Classify meromorphic functions for which both $f$ and a specified polynomial-coefficient differential expression are zero-free, with the latter nonconstant. Entire functions and constant-coefficient cases are reported solved. A proposed form for infinite-pole solutions in the remaining case is only a conjectural necessity, with auxiliary-function conventions missing.
