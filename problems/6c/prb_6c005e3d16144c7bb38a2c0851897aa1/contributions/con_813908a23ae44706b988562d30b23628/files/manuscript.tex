\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Are ensemble-based data assimilation methods really necessary for accurate filtering? We investigate the ability to discover data assimilation (DA) schemes meant for chaotic dynamics with deep learning. The focus is on learning the analysis step of sequential DA, from state trajectories and their observations, using a simple residual convolutional neural network, while assuming the dynamics to be known. Experiments are performed with low-order dynamics which display spatiotemporal chaos and for which solid benchmarks for DA performance exist. The accuracy of the states obtained from the learned analysis approaches that of the best possibly tuned ensemble Kalman filter, and is far better than that of variational DA alter- natives. Critically, this can be achieved while propagating even just a single state in the forecast step. We investigate the reason for achieving ensemble filtering accuracy without an ensemble. We diagnose that the analysis scheme actually identifies key dynamical perturbations, mildly aligned with the unstable subspace, from the forecast state alone, without any ensemble-based covariances representa- tion. \subsection{Exact filtering without particles, but with covariance information}
Take $X_{t+1}=aX_t+W_t$ and $Y_t=X_t+V_t$, where $W_t\sim N(0,Q)$, $V_t\sim N(0,R)$, $Q\ge0,R>0$, all noises independent, and the initial state is Gaussian and independent of all process and observation noises. If a forecast has mean $m$ and variance $P>0$, then conditioning on $Y=y$ gives
\[
 m^+=m+\frac{P}{P+R}(y-m),\qquad P^+=\frac{PR}{P+R}.
\]
Indeed multiplying the forecast and likelihood densities makes the exponent
$-(x-m)^2/(2P)-(y-x)^2/(2R)$; completing the square gives precisely those mean and variance parameters. The next forecast is Gaussian with mean $am^+$ and variance $a^2P^++Q$, because independent Gaussian sums have those parameters (their characteristic functions multiply). Induction proves exact recursive filtering with two scalars and no ensemble. For $P=0$, the state is deterministic and the same formulas hold by direct conditioning with gain zero.
\subsection{A checked failure of retaining only the forecast mean}
Set $R=1$. The priors $N(0,1)$ and $N(0,4)$ have the same forecast mean, but their posterior means are $y/2$ and $4y/5$. An update rule $F(m,y)$ receiving only the mean and current observation therefore cannot be exact for both priors. At $y=1$ the required outputs are $1/2$ and $4/5$. This is not just a zero-probability observation issue: both observation laws have strictly positive densities everywhere, and the two required continuous formulas differ at every $y\ne0$, so no single measurable function can agree with both almost surely.

The failed inference is that one propagated point necessarily determines uncertainty for every forecast distribution. The exact recursion above retains covariance, and a trained network or a fixed steady-state gain can encode other model-specific information. Thus the obstruction does not contradict the reported experiment or show that ensembles are necessary.
