\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(M\) denote the underlying manifold in the intended setting of Donaldson's almost-complex Calabi--Yau equation, let \(g\) denote the background metric, and let \(\widetilde g\) denote a metric associated with a solution. Write
\[
B_g(p,r)=\{x\in M:d_g(p,x)<r\},\qquad f=\operatorname{tr}_g(\widetilde g),
\]
where \(d_g\) is the distance induced by \(g\), and let \(dV_g\) be its volume measure. For real Riemannian metrics the relative trace is \(g^{ij}\widetilde g_{ij}\); the supplied record does not specify whether this convention or a Hermitian trace convention is intended.

The proposed \(\varepsilon\)-regularity assertion asks whether there exist uniform constants \(\varepsilon>0\) and \(C>0\) such that, for every solution metric \(\widetilde g\), every center \(p\), and every radius \(r>0\) admitted by the intended setting,
\[
r^{-2}\int_{B_g(p,r)} f\,dV_g\leq\varepsilon
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\sup_{B_g(p,r/2)} f
\leq C r^{-4}\int_{B_g(p,r)} f\,dV_g.
\]
The same constants must apply throughout the intended range of solutions, centers, and radii. However, the supplied record does not state the equation, the manifold dimension, the geometric hypotheses defining that range, restrictions on radii or boundary behavior, or the background data on which the constants may depend.
