\section{Polynomial Tractability of Permutation-Invariant Integration}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Standard number systems are denoted by $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$; index ranges, computational models and specialized notation follow the cited problem source and the definitions below.


$n(\varepsilon,d)$ is the minimum number of permitted function observations needed to approximate the integration functional in dimension $d$ with relative or normalized error $\varepsilon$. Polynomial tractability asks for $n(\varepsilon,d)\leq C d^a\varepsilon^{-b}$ with constants independent of $d,\varepsilon$.

Fixed-parameter tractability for parameter $k$ means running time $f(k)N^{O(1)}$, where $N$ is the input length and $f$ is computable. The exponent is independent of $k$.

For a discrete random variable $X$, entropy is $H(X)=-\sum_xp(x)\log p(x)$, and mutual information is $I(X;Y)=H(X)+H(Y)-H(X,Y)$. Coding rates, capacities, and exponents depend on the stated channel or source model and error criterion.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For integration over the permutation-invariant spaces $S_{I_d}(E_{d,\alpha})$, under what conditions on the coordinate sets $I_d$ is the information complexity $n(\varepsilon,d)$ polynomially bounded in $d$ and $\varepsilon^{-1}$? \sref{statement}{2}

The Korobov-space norm, weights, observation class, error criterion, and precise symmetry sets $I_d$ are those in the report.
