\subsection{A dimension argument survives every choice of cubature weights}
Use precisely the source's periodic Fourier class, with $\alpha>1$ and
\[
\|f\|_{E_{d,\alpha}}=\sup_{k\in\mathbb Z^d}|\widehat f(k)|\prod_{j=1}^d\max(1,|k_j|)^\alpha.
\]
Let $s$ of the $d$ coordinates be required to be permutation invariant, and put $r=d-s$. A cubature rule uses $N$ fixed point values with arbitrary complex linear weights. We rederive the source's known sample threshold, then examine why it does not by itself settle tractability.
\begin{proposition}
For $N<(s+1)2^r$, every such rule has worst-case error at least 1 on the invariant unit ball. Consequently strong polynomial tractability fails; ordinary polynomial tractability would require $r=O(\log d)$ along the family of invariant coordinate sets.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $V$ be the space spanned by the orbit sums of $e^{2\pi i a\cdot x}$ for $a\in\{0,1\}^d$ under permutations of the $s$ symmetric coordinates. An orbit is determined by the number of ones in those coordinates and the exact bits in the remaining $r$ coordinates. There are $D=(s+1)2^r$ orbits, whose disjoint Fourier supports make their orbit sums linearly independent. Thus $\dim V=D$.
Vanishing at $N<D$ sample points imposes at most $N$ homogeneous linear conditions, so some nonzero $g\in V$ vanishes at every node. Set
\[
f=\frac{|g|^2}{\int_{[0,1]^d}|g|^2}.
\]
It is invariant, nonnegative, has integral 1 and vanishes at the nodes. Its Fourier support is contained in $\{-1,0,1\}^d$. Every Fourier coefficient has absolute value at most $\int f=1$, while all frequency weights on this support equal 1. Hence $\|f\|_{E_{d,\alpha}}=1$, with equality supplied by the zeroth coefficient. The cubature gives zero on $f$, proving error at least 1. The zero-output rule has error at most 1, so this threshold statement is exact below $D$.
For any fixed requested error below 1, at least $D$ nodes are necessary. Since $(s+1)2^r\ge(s+1)(r+1)\ge s+r+1=d+1$, a dimension-independent bound is impossible. If a polynomial bound $N(\varepsilon,d)\le C\varepsilon^{-b}d^a$ existed, setting $\varepsilon=1/2$ would give $2^r\le C2^bd^a$, hence $r\le a\log_2 d+\log_2(C2^b)$, the claimed necessary condition.
\end{proof}
\subsection{Verification and remaining gaps}
Orbit counts were enumerated for all $0\le s\le d\le8$. For $d=3,s=2$ and a node at zero, $g=e^{2\pi ix_3}-1$ yields $f=1-\cos(2\pi x_3)$, with Fourier coefficients $1,-1/2,-1/2$, integral 1 and node value 0. This checks the normalization and the particular Fourier norm.
The source already proves this lower threshold. The attempted next step would infer sufficiency from it or from the source's fixed-dimension upper estimate. Neither follows: a lower bound alone constructs no rule, and the displayed source factor $(1+\zeta(\alpha)/2^{\alpha-1})^d-1$ grows with $d$ when $\alpha$ is fixed. Thus the condition $r=O(\log d)$ is only necessary here; no complete polynomial-tractability characterization is obtained. No new result beyond the known lower bound is claimed.
