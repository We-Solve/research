\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $G=(V,E)$ be an $n$-vertex sparse graph with $2|E|/n\leq C$ for a constant independent of $n$. Each vertex has a distinct label $\ell(v)$ over a fixed finite alphabet, of length at most $L\log n$ for a fixed $L$. Construct universal lossless graph codes analogous to Lempel--Ziv, with asymptotically optimal compression.

A lossless code consists of an encoder and decoder recovering the labeled graph exactly. Universality should mean that the same coding rule works without knowing the generating source within a specified source class. In a probabilistic formulation, for a graph law $P_n$ the benchmark might be its entropy
\[
H(P_n)=-\sum_G P_n(G)\log_2 P_n(G),
\]
and one would require encoded expected length to approach this benchmark at a stated redundancy scale. Alternatively a worst-case combinatorial objective would require a different benchmark. The source does not select either criterion or an underlying graph-process/model class.

The source also does not say whether label strings must be encoded or are available as an external dictionary, whether vertex renaming is allowed, what computation time is required, or whether optimality concerns the leading $n\log n$ term or finer terms. These choices matter for sparse graphs and cannot be supplied by treating an arbitrary labeled graph as a generic string. The requested Lempel--Ziv analogy is retained as a universal compression program rather than replaced by a model-specific code.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Construct universal Lempel--Ziv-type lossless codes for bounded-average-degree graphs with distinct logarithmic-length vertex labels. Specify the source class, label costs, efficiency, and asymptotic optimality benchmark.
