\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(n\in\Z\) with \(n\geq 2\), and let \(\A^n_{\Q}\) denote affine \(n\)-space over \(\Q\), with coordinate functions \(x_1,\ldots,x_n\). Let \(V\subseteq\A^n_{\Q}\) be a hypersurface defined over \(\Q\), and write \(V(\Q)\) for its rational points. A subset of \(V(\Q)\) is Zariski dense in \(V\) if its Zariski closure in \(V\) equals \(V\). Define
\[
S_V=\{P\in V(\Q):x_1(P)\notin\Z\}
=V(\Q)\cap\bigl((\Q\setminus\Z)\times\Q^{n-1}\bigr).
\]
\textbf{Non-Integrality Conjecture, as recorded.} Is it true that, for every such \(n\) and every such \(V\),
\[
\overline{V(\Q)}^{\,\mathrm{Zar}}=V
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\overline{S_V}^{\,\mathrm{Zar}}=V?
\]

\subsection{Short English statement}
The recorded question asks whether rational points whose first coordinate is not an integer are Zariski dense on every rationally densely populated hypersurface in affine space of dimension at least two. The intended hypotheses need clarification, especially when the first coordinate is constant and integral. The supplied open status and attribution of the plane case to Siegel are unverified source claims.
