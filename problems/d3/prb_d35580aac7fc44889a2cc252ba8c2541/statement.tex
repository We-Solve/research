For distinct indices define
\[
e_{ij}(p)=\lVert p_i-p_j\rVert^2-\bar d_{ij}^{\,2},\qquad
F_i(p)=\sum_{j\in I\setminus\{i\}}e_{ij}(p)(p_j-p_i).
\]
Consider the system \(\dot p_i=F_i(p)\) for every \(i\in I\). An equilibrium is a configuration \(p^*\) satisfying \(F_i(p^*)=0\) for all \(i\); it is incorrect if \(e_{ij}(p^*)\neq0\) for at least one pair \(i<j\). No restriction excluding collisions or collinear configurations is supplied for the actual equilibrium \(p^*\).

\textbf{Source question.} For every desired configuration of either stated type, and every incorrect equilibrium \(p^*\) of the associated system, is \(p^*\) a saddle point?

The term ``saddle point'' needs clarification. The system has the gradient representation
\[
V(p)=\frac14\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq4}e_{ij}(p)^2,
\qquad F(p)=-\nabla V(p).
\]
One possible precise interpretation of the requested conclusion is
\[
\forall p^*\in(\R^2)^4,\quad
\left[F(p^*)=0\ \text{and}\ \exists i<j:\ e_{ij}(p^*)\neq0\right]
\Longrightarrow
\exists v\in\R^8\setminus\{0\}:\
v^{\mathsf T}\nabla^2V(p^*)v<0.
\]
This is the negative-curvature interpretation, equivalently a positive eigenvalue of \(DF(p^*)\). It is presented conditionally, not as a resolved interpretation of the source: requiring both positive and negative Hessian directions, or a nonlinear saddle property, can give different statements. Translations and rotations preserve the distance errors, so the treatment of symmetry directions also needs to be specified; hyperbolicity in the full configuration space must not be silently assumed.
