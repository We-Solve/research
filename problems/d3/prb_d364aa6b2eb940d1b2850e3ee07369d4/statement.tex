\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 30001049}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $\mathcal A_2$ be the mod-$2$ Steenrod algebra, acting by operations $\operatorname{Sq}^i$ of degree $i$. An unstable module is a nonnegatively graded $\F_2$-vector space $M$ with an $\mathcal A_2$-action satisfying
\[
\operatorname{Sq}^i x=0\quad\text{for every homogeneous }x\in M^d\text{ and }i>d.
\]
Let $\mathcal U$ be the category of these modules and degree-preserving $\mathcal A_2$-linear maps.

Classify those $M\in\mathcal U$ for which there is a topological space $X$ and an isomorphism
\[
H^*(X;\F_2)\cong M\qquad\text{in }\mathcal U.
\]
Equivalently, determine the essential image, up to isomorphism, of the ordinary mod-$2$ cohomology functor from spaces to unstable modules. The isomorphism must preserve degrees and every Steenrod operation. Cohomology is ordinary, not silently replaced by reduced cohomology. No connectedness, finite-type, finite-dimensional or finite-complex assumption on $X$ or $M$ is supplied.

The target category remembers the unstable module structure, not a specified cup-product algebra structure on $M$. A space realizing $M$ has such a product on its cohomology, but an algebra structure is not part of the prescribed isomorphism data in this question. The problem is a characterization of all realizable modules, not merely whether one particular displayed module is realizable or an assertion that every unstable module is realizable.

The supplied literature assessment checked 2026-08-21 labels this broad classification question open, says the report presents it as substantially harder, and says no complete classification is known. These are dated attributed claims; the comparison underlying ``harder'' is not elaborated in the record.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Characterize the unstable Steenrod modules that occur as ordinary mod-$2$ cohomology of spaces, with degrees and Steenrod operations preserved. The source imposes no finiteness or connectedness restriction and asks for module realization rather than a prescribed cohomology algebra.
