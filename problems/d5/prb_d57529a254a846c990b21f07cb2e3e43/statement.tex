\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Under the standard orientation-preserving convention, let \(\operatorname{IET}([0,1))\) be the group, under composition, of bijections \(T:[0,1)\to[0,1)\) for which there exist a finite partition \(0=a_0<a_1<\cdots<a_n=1\) and real numbers \(c_1,\ldots,c_n\) such that
\[
T(x)=x+c_i\qquad\text{for }x\in[a_{i-1},a_i),\quad 1\leq i\leq n.
\]
Thus each transformation exchanges finitely many half-open intervals by translations.

A group \(G\), regarded as a discrete group, is \emph{amenable} if there exists a linear functional \(m:\ell^\infty(G;\R)\to\R\) on all bounded real-valued functions on \(G\) satisfying
\[
m(\mathbf{1})=1,\qquad m(f)\geq0\ \text{whenever }f\geq0,\qquad m(L_gf)=m(f)\ \text{for every }g\in G,
\]
where \((L_gf)(h)=f(g^{-1}h)\).

\textbf{Question.} Is every subgroup \(G\leq\operatorname{IET}([0,1))\) amenable? No countability or finite-generation hypothesis is imposed. Equivalently, is \(\operatorname{IET}([0,1))\) itself amenable, since amenability passes to subgroups?
