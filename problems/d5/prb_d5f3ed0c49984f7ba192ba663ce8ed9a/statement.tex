\section*{UnsolvedMath ID: 30000702}
\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $\Omega\subseteq\C$ be a domain and let $H(\Omega)$ have the compact-open Fr\'echet topology, defined by locally uniform convergence. Let $(\varphi_n)_{n\geq1}$ be arbitrary holomorphic self-maps of $\Omega$. For the chosen function class $X$, a universal $f$ (in the usual $f\in X$ convention) satisfies
\[
 \forall g\in X\ \forall K\subset\Omega\text{ compact}\ 
 \forall\varepsilon>0\ \exists n\geq1:\quad
 \sup_{z\in K}|f(\varphi_n(z))-g(z)|<\varepsilon.
\]
This is density of the composition sequence, not of all iterates of one map unless the sequence is specified to consist of such iterates.

The source gives two choices for $X$: $H(\Omega)$ or ``its unit ball''. A general Fr\'echet space $H(\Omega)$ has no distinguished single norm and hence no canonical unit ball. A common intended bounded-holomorphic choice is $\{f:\sup_\Omega|f|\leq1\}$ with the \emph{locally uniform} topology; another is holomorphic maps into the open unit disk. These must be distinguished from supremum-norm density and from a ball for one compact seminorm. The excerpt does not choose the precise ball or explicitly say whether universal $f$ must belong to it, so those conventions are not silently imposed.

The main question asks for necessary and sufficient conditions on the given sequence for such an $f$ to exist on an arbitrary domain. Neither automorphisms, eventual injectivity, simple connectivity nor a single-generator semigroup assumption is added. The annulus question is a special existence/classification case; for a usual bounded annulus $A(r,R)=\{r<|z|<R\}$, $0<r<R<\infty$, the intended quantifier over moduli and permitted sequences also needs confirmation. It is not an assertion that every self-map sequence is universal.

The literature triage checked 21 August 2026 reports criteria for automorphism sequences and several simply connected or eventually injective settings, but says the arbitrary-domain/self-map formulation is broader. Its recent classification reference must be applied with its hypotheses, not promoted to a result for all the source's sequences. The dated partial-status claim is retained without independent verification.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Classify self-map sequences for which one holomorphic function has compositions locally uniformly dense in the chosen function class, and resolve the intended annulus case. The unit-ball convention is unspecified, and criteria for automorphisms or injective sequences do not automatically answer the arbitrary-self-map problem.
