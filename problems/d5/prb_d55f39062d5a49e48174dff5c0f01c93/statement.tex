\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A number field is a finite extension \(k/\Q\), here with an embedding \(k\subset\C\). A Kleinian group is a discrete subgroup of \(\mathrm{PSL}_2(\C)\). In the usual surface-group setting, singly degenerate means that its hyperbolic quotient has one geometrically finite end and one geometrically infinite/degenerate end, with suitable cusp conventions. The precise surface and parabolic assumptions are not supplied in this record.

Schleimer's Problem 7.5 asks whether there exists a singly degenerate Kleinian group whose elements all have matrix entries in one fixed number field. In a determinant-one projective-matrix interpretation, the target is
\[

 \exists\Gamma\subset\mathrm{PSL}_2(\C):\quad
 \Gamma\text{ singly degenerate},\quad
 \Gamma\subset\operatorname{image}\bigl(\mathrm{SL}_2(k)
                          \to\mathrm{PSL}_2(\C)\bigr),

\subsection{Short English statement}
Does some singly degenerate Kleinian group admit a single matrix realization over a number field? The common field is fixed across all elements. The source leaves cusp, conjugacy and matrix-lift conventions implicit, and its summary's one-ended terminology needs qualification.
