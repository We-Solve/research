\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A space is zero-dimensional if it has a base consisting of sets that are both open and closed. It is first-countable if each point has a countable neighborhood base, and homogeneous if for every pair of points \(x,y\) there is a homeomorphism of the space onto itself carrying \(x\) to \(y\).

A left topological group structure on a nonempty topological space \(X\) is a group operation \(\cdot\colon X\times X\to X\) such that, with the given topology on \(X\), every left translation
\[
L_a\colon X\longrightarrow X,\qquad L_a(x)=a\cdot x,
\]
is continuous for every \(a\in X\). Each such translation is then a homeomorphism, since its inverse is \(L_{a^{-1}}\). Joint continuity of multiplication and continuity of inversion are not required by this definition.

\textbf{General question, reported negatively answered in the supplied source.} Does every nonempty zero-dimensional compact homogeneous space admit a left topological group structure? The supplied record reports that Banakh answered this question negatively in the 2008 paper cited below; its title specifies a homogeneous first-countable zero-dimensional compactum that fails to be a left topological group. This historical resolution is reported from the supplied record, not independently verified here.
