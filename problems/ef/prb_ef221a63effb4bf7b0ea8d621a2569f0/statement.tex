\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Interpret a real as an element of Cantor space \(2^{\N}\), identified with a subset of \(\N\). Write \(a\leq_T b\) for Turing reducibility, \(a<_T b\) for \(a\leq_T b\) and \(b\not\leq_T a\), and \(a\equiv_T b\) for mutual Turing reducibility. The Turing jump \(a'\) is the halting problem relative to \(a\), and \(0'\) is the ordinary halting problem.

Use the standard algorithmic-randomness convention: \(\mathcal R_a\) denotes the reals that are Martin--L\"of random relative to \(a\). Thus \(r\in\mathcal R_a\) if, for every uniformly \(a\)-effectively open sequence \((U_n)_{n\in\N}\) with fair-coin measure \(\mu(U_n)\leq 2^{-n}\), one has \(r\notin\bigcap_{n\in\N}U_n\). A real is \(2\)-random if it belongs to \(\mathcal R_{0'}\). Interpret an ``\(a\)-random real'' as an element of \(\mathcal R_a\). These conventional meanings are not explicitly defined in the supplied record.

Fix an optimal prefix-free machine and write \(K(\sigma)\) for the prefix-free Kolmogorov complexity of a finite binary string \(\sigma\). Write \(x\upharpoonright n\) for the initial segment of \(x\) of length \(n\). The use of prefix-free complexity is a conventional interpretation of the source's undefined notation \(K\).

A Turing ideal is a nonempty set \(B\subseteq 2^{\N}\) closed downward under \(\leq_T\) and closed under Turing join \(a\oplus b\), where \((a\oplus b)(2n)=a(n)\) and \((a\oplus b)(2n+1)=b(n)\). A chain \(C\) is linearly ordered by \(\leq_T\). Its generated ideal is
\[
\langle C\rangle_T=\left\{a\in 2^{\N}:\exists F\subseteq C\text{ finite},\quad a\leq_T\bigoplus_{c\in F}c\right\},
\]
where the empty join is a computable real.

\subsection{Short English statement}
