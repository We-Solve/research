Let \(n\geq1\), let \(R>0\) be a required centre separation, and let \(f\) be a real even Schwartz function on \(\R^n\), with
\[
 \widehat f(y)=\int_{\R^n}f(x)e^{-2\pi i x\cdot y}\,\dd x,
 \quad \widehat f(0)>0,\quad \widehat f(y)\geq0,
 \quad f(x)\leq0\text{ if }|x|\geq R.
\]
Such an auxiliary function gives the centre-density upper bound
\[
 \rho\leq f(0)/\widehat f(0)
\]
for all sphere packings with centre separation at least \(R\), not only lattice packings. Multiplication by \(\operatorname{Vol}(B_n(R/2))\) converts this to the occupied-volume density bound.

For the exact periodic identity, let the centres be
\[
 P=\bigcup_{i=1}^N(t_i+\Lambda),\qquad
 V=\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda),\qquad \rho=N/V,
\]
with \(t_i\) distinct modulo the full-rank lattice \(\Lambda\) and all distinct centre distances at least \(R\). Write \(\Lambda^*\) for the dual lattice and
\(S(y)=\sum_{i=1}^Ne^{2\pi i y\cdot t_i}\). Define nonnegative slacks
\[
 D_f=-\sum_{\substack{1\leq i,j\leq N,\ \lambda\in\Lambda\\
                   (i,j,\lambda)\ne(i,i,0)}}
                  f(t_i-t_j+\lambda),
\]
where the exclusion means omit precisely the \(N\) diagonal self-terms \(i=j,\lambda=0\), and
\[
 D_{\widehat f}=\frac1V\sum_{\substack{y\in\Lambda^*\\y\ne0}}
                     \widehat f(y)|S(y)|^2.
\]
Schwartz decay ensures convergence. Poisson summation gives
\[
 Nf(0)-D_f=\frac{N^2}{V}\widehat f(0)+D_{\widehat f},
\]
and hence the exact normalized centre-density deficit
\[
 \frac{f(0)}{\widehat f(0)}-\rho
 =\frac{D_f+D_{\widehat f}}{N\widehat f(0)}.
\]
Equality is equivalent to both slacks vanishing; it requires primal zeros at the nonzero centre differences and \(\widehat f(y)|S(y)|^2=0\) at every nonzero dual lattice point. This is complementary slackness for this certificate, not a general quantitative stability or uniqueness theorem.

The aid reports that Viazovska's dimension-eight and the CKMRV dimension-twenty-four magic functions provide sharp certificates. With the usual unimodular normalizations, \(E_8\) has minimum separation \(\sqrt2\) and centre density one, and the Leech lattice has minimum separation two and centre density one; the respective auxiliary ratios are one. Their attained packing densities are
\[
 \Delta_8=\frac{\pi^4}{384},\qquad
 \Delta_{24}=\frac{\pi^{12}}{12!}.
\]
The all-packing upper bound matches these constructions, explaining the source's optimal-density implication. The phrase ``complete solution'' is not expanded here to a classification or uniqueness statement absent from the supplied record.
