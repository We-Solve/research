\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(S\) be a closed oriented surface with \(\chi(S)<0\), and let \(\mathcal T_S\) be its marked Teichm\"uller space. The canonical extraction writes \(TS\); the supplied source verification identifies this as lost Teichm\"uller notation, not the tangent bundle. Bill Goldman's question asks for the most general representation hypotheses making the energy proper on \(\mathcal T_S\).

For the supplied sufficient criterion, let \(\rho:\pi_1(S)\to G\) be reductive, with \(G\) a semisimple symmetric-space isometry group and \(X=G/K\) equipped with a fixed metric. In the energy convention
\[
 E_\rho(J)=\inf_u\frac12\int_S\|\dd u\|^2\,\dd A_J,
\]
the infimum is over \(\rho\)-equivariant maps \(\widetilde S\to X\), with descent of the energy density; the reductive harmonic-map formulation is the intended realization. The normalization must be kept fixed. For an essential simple closed curve \(\gamma\), put
\[
 \lambda_\rho(\gamma)=\inf_{x\in X}d_X(x,\rho(\gamma)x),
\]
and let \(\operatorname{Ext}_J(\gamma)\) be extremal length. The aid's basic estimate is
\[
 E_\rho(J)\geq\frac{\lambda_\rho(\gamma)^2}{2\operatorname{Ext}_J(\gamma)}.
\]

The two simultaneous hypotheses in its properness theorem are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A positive simple translation systole,
\[
 \inf_{\gamma\text{ essential simple}}\lambda_\rho(\gamma)>0.
\]
\item There is one fixed finite filling set \(\mathcal F\) of essential simple curves such that
\[
 A_\rho(\varphi)=\max_{\gamma\in\mathcal F}\lambda_\rho(\varphi\gamma),
 \qquad\varphi\in\operatorname{Mod}(S),
\]
is proper on the discrete mapping class group: for every finite \(R\), the set \(\{\varphi:A_\rho(\varphi)\leq R\}\) is finite. Filling means the curves jointly meet every essential curve, with the usual equivalent closed-surface filling convention.
\end{enumerate}
Under these hypotheses, the aid asserts that \(E_\rho\) is proper; equivalently its bounded sublevel sets are compact, in the usual continuous energy setting. The first condition controls thin degeneration, while the second controls changes of marking within the thick part. It is a sufficient criterion, not a characterization or a claim that either condition alone suffices. The aid says it recovers usual well-displacing and Anosov sufficient conditions, but does not supply their exact definitions or full group hypotheses.

A separate necessary obstruction is an infinite mapping-class stabilizer of the representation class. With
\[
 \operatorname{Stab}([\rho])=\{\varphi\in\operatorname{Mod}(S):[\rho\circ\varphi_*]=[\rho]\},
\]
the aid states
\[
 |\operatorname{Stab}([\rho])|=\infty\quad\Longrightarrow\quad E_\rho\text{ is not proper on marked }\mathcal T_S.
\]
The stabilizer is in the mapping class group, not the centralizer in \(G\). This obstruction uses energy invariance and the proper discontinuity of the marking action; it is not automatically a statement about an energy descended to a quotient.

The original Comment 5.6 reports properness for Hitchin representations in split groups and maximal symplectic representations [Lab05], and for convex-cocompact representations [GW05]. These historical supplied cases are retained separately from the new two-condition formulation. The aid leaves the request for the most general conditions open and supplies no necessary-and-sufficient classification for arbitrary representations, especially outside its reductive setup.
\subsection{Short English statement}
The supplied partial theorem separates proper energy into control of pinching curves and control of marking drift, using a fixed filling set and an explicit extremal-length estimate. An infinite mapping-class stabilizer obstructs properness on marked Teichm\"uller space. The domain correction and all reductive hypotheses are explicit.
