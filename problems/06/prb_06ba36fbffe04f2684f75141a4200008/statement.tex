\section{Automatic Recognition of Numerically Unstable Quadratic Programs}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Standard number systems are $\mathbb N,\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$. Specialized notation and index ranges follow the cited source and the definitions below.


A quadratically constrained quadratic program has a quadratic objective and quadratic equality or inequality constraints. Numerical conditioning measures how perturbations in the encoded problem affect its solution or residuals; repeated nonlinear operations can amplify errors.

\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Consider a quadratically constrained quadratic program with $$ (x_1-1)^2+x_2^2\geq3+s^2,\qquad a_i-a_{i-1}^2=0,\qquad a_0=s/2,$$ where a finite chain of the $a_i$ amplifies numerical error through repeated square roots. How can solvers automatically recognize such severely ill-conditioned instances? \sref{statement}{2}
