\subsection{A precise absolute-sensitivity diagnostic for the displayed chain}
The preserved extract specifies neither an objective nor a terminal-value constraint nor a universal conditioning criterion. We therefore do not invent one. Let the displayed chain have length $L\ge1$ and restrict for this calculation to $s\ge0$. Repeated substitution gives
\[
a_L=(s/2)^{2^L},\qquad s=2a_L^{1/2^L}.
\]
The sign restriction matters: without it, the terminal value cannot even determine the sign of $s$.
\begin{proposition}
On the positive branch, the inverse map from terminal value $y=a_L$ to $s$ has derivative $2^{1-L}y^{1/2^L-1}$ and relative condition number $2^{-L}$. It has no finite absolute Lipschitz constant on any interval $[0,\eta]$ with $\eta>0$. If a terminal measurement only specifies $0\le a_L\le\tau$, then the compatible $s$ values are exactly $0\le s\le2\tau^{1/2^L}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The chain formula follows by induction. Differentiating its positive inverse gives the derivative. Multiplying this derivative by $y/s$ gives $2^{-L}$, proving the relative-condition assertion. For absolute Lipschitz continuity at zero, the difference quotient is $2y^{1/2^L}/y=2y^{1/2^L-1}$, which tends to infinity as $y\downarrow0$ because $L\ge1$. Finally the inverse is continuous and increasing on nonnegative values, so it maps $[0,\tau]$ onto exactly the interval stated.
\end{proof}
Thus an absolute terminal tolerance $\tau$ can conceal a much larger variation in the recovered variable. For example $s=1$ gives $a_L=2^{-2^L}$ exactly, even though every chain equality has zero residual. A diagnostic for an explicitly encoded chain can report the exponent $2^{-L}$ and propagate the interval bound without numerically taking repeated roots. Detecting this exact syntactic pattern is a limited task; it is not a complete detector of general QCQP ill-conditioning under algebraic reformulations.
\subsection{Verification and remaining gaps}
Exact rational forward chains for several $L$ were computed and the inverse-tolerance bounds evaluated. The attempt exposes a distinction that would invalidate a vague diagnosis: large absolute sensitivity near zero coexists with a small relative condition number away from zero. Also, the source's first inequality alone does not fix a terminal value: for every $s\ge0$, taking $x_1=1$ and $x_2=\sqrt{3+s^2}$ satisfies it, with the chain completed exactly. Our terminal-measurement interval is explicitly an auxiliary diagnostic assumption, not an added original constraint. A small residual cannot be declared a wrong optimizer without an objective, additional restrictions and a numerical success criterion. Those missing specifications prevent a full automatic-recognition theorem here.
