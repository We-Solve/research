\textbf{Source problem code:} catalogue entry
\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let $S\subset\R^3$ be the source's closed surface, with Euclidean diameter
\[
 d=\sup\{|x-y|:x,y\in S\}.
\]
For a unit vector $u$ and $t\in\R$, put
\[
 H(u,t)=\{x:\langle u,x\rangle=t\},\qquad
 Z(u,t,h)=\{x:t\le\langle u,x\rangle\le t+h\}.
\]
A precise global-constancy reading of the hypothesis is that there exist a separation $h<d$ and a number $A$ such that, for every $u\in S^2$ and $t\in\R$,
\[
 S\cap H(u,t)\ne\varnothing,\quad
 S\cap H(u,t+h)\ne\varnothing
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \mathcal H^2\bigl(S\cap Z(u,t,h)\bigr)=A.
\]
Thus it is the area of the part of the surface in a slab, not area of a planar section of an enclosed body. Both planes are taken to meet $S$, and $A$ is independent of direction and position.

The question is whether this forces
\[
 S=\{x\in\R^3:|x-c|=r\}
\]
for some $c\in\R^3$ and $r>0$.

The source states only $h<d$, although a nondegenerate distance condition needs $h>0$; allowing $h=0$ would change the problem substantially. This missing strict-positivity condition is flagged rather than silently added. The regularity, connectedness, embeddedness and compactness meaning of ``closed surface,'' area convention, and whether ``constant'' is global or only directionwise also require confirmation. No convexity assumption is stated or imposed.
\subsection{Short English statement}
If every width-$h$ slab whose two boundary planes meet a closed surface contains the same amount of surface area, must the surface be a round sphere? The fixed width should be checked for strict positivity, and the area is surface area in the strip, not cross-sectional area.

The supplied summary calls the specific strip-area problem unresolved and mentions Funk/Radon and section-rigidity themes.
