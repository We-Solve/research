\section*{Kourovka Notebook Problem 21.41}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For $m\ge2$, let $T_m$ be the rooted tree of finite words over
$\{1,\ldots,m\}$, rooted at the empty word, with edges from $v$ to
$vi$. A rooted tree automorphism $a$ has a section $a|_v$ at every
vertex, characterized by $a(vw)=a(v)\,a|_v(w)$.
A group $G$ is self-similar if it has an injective homomorphism
$\rho:G\to\operatorname{Aut}(T_m)$ for some $m$ such that every section
of every $\rho(g)$ again belongs to $\rho(G)$. No transitivity of the
action, and no finite bound on the number of different sections, is
required.

A group is torsion-free if $g^k=1$ with $k\ge1$ implies $g=1$.
It is finitely presented if it is given by finitely many generators
and finitely many relators. It is metabelian if its commutator subgroup
is abelian.

Define the restricted wreath product
\[
 W=\mathbb Z\wr\mathbb Z
     =\left(\bigoplus_{j\in\mathbb Z}\mathbb Z\right)\rtimes\mathbb Z,
\]
where the direct sum consists of finitely supported integer sequences
and a generator of the last factor shifts their indices by one.
The problem asks whether there exists a torsion-free, finitely presented,
metabelian, self-similar group $G$ with an injective homomorphism $W\to G$.

The subgroup embedding need not respect a preassigned tree action or
wreath-product decomposition. The self-similar action must be faithful
on the full ambient group, not just on the embedded copy of $W$.
All four conditions on the ambient group must hold simultaneously.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Can a torsion-free finitely presented metabelian self-similar group contain the restricted wreath product $\mathbb Z\wr\mathbb Z$?
