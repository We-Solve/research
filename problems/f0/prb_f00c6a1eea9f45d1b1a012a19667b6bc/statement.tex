\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(S,S'\) be closed surfaces of genera at least \(2\), in the usual orientable convention, and let \(G\leq\operatorname{MCG}(S)\) be isomorphic to \(\pi_1(S')\). The genera need not agree. Retain \(\Gamma_G\) as in the source's omitted setup. In the standard surface-group-extension interpretation it is the pullback over \(G\) of the marked-point mapping-class extension, giving
\[
 1\longrightarrow\pi_1(S)\longrightarrow\Gamma_G
 \longrightarrow G\longrightarrow1.
\]
This construction must be checked against the source; no arbitrary extension with the same kernel and quotient is substituted.

\emph{Problem 4.2.} Do there exist such \(S,S',G\) for which \(\Gamma_G\) is word hyperbolic? Separately, do there exist such \(S,S',G\) for which \(G\) is convex cocompact in \(\operatorname{MCG}(S)\)? Word hyperbolicity means hyperbolicity of a Cayley graph for a finite generating set. In the standard mapping-class convention, convex cocompactness means quasiconvexity of a \(G\)-orbit in Teichm\"uller space with its Teichm\"uller metric: there is a uniform neighborhood of the orbit containing all geodesic segments between orbit points. The excerpt does not define its chosen convention.

These are existential questions over both closed surfaces and the embedded surface group; neither finite index nor equal genus is required. Does a mapping class group contain a closed surface group whose associated surface-group extension is word hyperbolic? Does one contain a convex-cocompact closed surface group? The source treats these as separate existence questions and records their equivalence as unresolved in its context.
