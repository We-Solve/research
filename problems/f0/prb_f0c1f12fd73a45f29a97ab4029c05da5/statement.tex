\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Walter Craig asks how the regularity of a Mather set depends on the arithmetic of its minimizing rotation vectors. The extraction calls \(L:TM\times\mathbb T\to\R\) positive definite, where \(\mathbb T=\R/\Z\), but does not state all existence hypotheses. A standard precise setting is a closed smooth manifold \(M\) and a time-periodic Tonelli Lagrangian: \(L\) is sufficiently smooth, strictly convex in each velocity fiber with positive definite velocity Hessian, and superlinear in velocity, with the required complete Euler--Lagrange flow. These are explicit supplementary hypotheses, not a complete transcription of the phrase positive definite.

For \(c\in H^1(M;\R)\), choose a closed 1-form \(\eta_c\) representing \(c\), and define
\[
 \alpha(c)=-\min_{\mu}\int_{TM\times\mathbb T}
       \bigl(L(x,v,t)-\eta_c(x)[v]\bigr)\,\dd\mu.
\]
Here \(\mu\) ranges over invariant Borel probability measures with finite action. A minimizing measure has rotation vector \(\rho(\mu)\in H_1(M;\R)\), defined by
\[
 \langle[\eta],\rho(\mu)\rangle=\int\eta(x)[v]\,\dd\mu.
\]
The lifted Mather set \(\widetilde{\mathcal M}_c\) is the union of supports of the \(c\)-minimizing measures; its projection to \(M\times\mathbb T\) is the projected Mather set. The frequency set is naturally \(\partial\alpha(c)\subset H_1(M;\R)\), the set of minimizing rotation vectors. At a differentiability point it is the singleton \(\{D\alpha(c)\}\). This corrects the lost cohomology/homology typography and makes explicit that a derivative need not exist everywhere.

\textbf{Problem.} Relate the regularity or geometric structure of \(\mathcal M_c\) to the arithmetic properties of \(\partial\alpha(c)\), or of \(D\alpha(c)\) when it exists. The source does not specify lifted versus projected set, the regularity class to be measured, a rationality or Diophantine condition, or the intended direction and quantifiers of such a relation. Thus it does not supply a universal equivalence to be asserted.

The supplied research aid gives the following limitation on a frequency-only characterization. For every nonempty closed \(F\subset\mathbb T\), it reports a smooth function \(b_F\ge0\), zero exactly on \(F\), such that the smooth Tonelli Lagrangian
\[
 L_F(x,v,t)=\tfrac12\bigl(v-b_F(x)\bigr)^2
\]
has
\[
 \partial\alpha_F(0)=\{0\},\qquad D\alpha_F(0)=0,\qquad
 \widetilde{\mathcal M}_{F,0}=F\times\{0\}\times\mathbb T.
\]
The same rational frequency and differentiability datum therefore allow a full circle, a finite set, a Cantor set, or any other nonempty closed spatial footprint. This statement concerns the support's geometry, not a failure of the Mather graph theorem.

For comparison, in the integrable torus model \(M=\mathbb T^n\), \(L(x,v,t)=\ell(v)\), with smooth strictly convex superlinear \(\ell\) and positive definite Hessian, let \(v_c\) satisfy \(D\ell(v_c)=c\). The aid describes
\[
 \alpha(c)=\ell^*(c),\qquad
 \widetilde{\mathcal M}_c=\mathbb T^n\times\{v_c\}\times\mathbb T,
\]
a smooth full graph for every frequency. Arithmetic instead affects invariant minimizing measures. For the spatial translation flow, unique ergodicity requires \(k\cdot v_c\ne0\) for every \(0\ne k\in\Z^n\). For the extended time-periodic flow on \(\mathbb T^n\times\mathbb T\), the corresponding condition is
\[
 k\cdot v_c+m\ne0
 \quad\text{for all }(k,m)\in\Z^n\times\Z\setminus\{(0,0)\}.
\]
A nonzero integer relation produces multiple invariant orbit-closure measures. These two arithmetic conventions must be distinguished; neither changes the full-graph Mather set in this example.
\subsection{Short English statement}
Craig asks for relations between Mather-set regularity and the arithmetic of minimizing frequencies. The supplied aid realizes arbitrary nonempty closed circle footprints at the same zero frequency, while integrable examples have smooth full graphs at every frequency. These examples limit a characterization based on frequency alone; they do not replace the broader research question with a complete classification.
