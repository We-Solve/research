\[
 B_s=\left\{(\alpha,\beta)\in\R^2:
 \inf_{n\geq1}n\min(\|n\alpha\|,\|n\beta\|)^s
                  \max(\|n\alpha\|,\|n\beta\|)^{2-s}>0\right\}.
\]
The infimum is over all positive integers, not just a subsequence or an asymptotic rate. The source identifies $B_0$ with the simultaneously badly approximable vectors, of dimension $2$, and $B_1$ with the possible Littlewood-exception set, of dimension $0$; the latter does not assert nonemptiness.

The aid gives a pointwise reduction, not the requested intermediate dimensions. For a fixed pair put $m_n=\min(\|n\alpha\|,\|n\beta\|)$, $M_n=\max(\|n\alpha\|,\|n\beta\|)$, and
\[
 A_n=nM_n^2,\qquad P_n=n\|n\alpha\|\|n\beta\|,\qquad
 F_s(n)=A_n^{1-s}P_n^s.
\]
This is exactly the defining expression. For $s<t$ it decreases, so $B_t\subset B_s$. Its infima $C_s=\inf_nF_s(n)$ obey
\[
 C_{\theta s+(1-\theta)t}\geq C_s^\theta C_t^{1-\theta}
       \quad(0<\theta<1),
\]
with the usual zero-value convention. Thus the pointwise infimum is log-concave in the parameter, while $\dim_HB_s$ is nonincreasing; neither statement evaluates that dimension.

For a pair in $B_0$, both coordinates are irrational: a rational coordinate combined with one-dimensional approximation of the other would force $\inf_n nM_n^2=0$. Hence $m_n>0$. Define
\[
 r_n=\log(M_n/m_n)\geq0,\qquad a_n=\log A_n,
\]
so $\log F_s(n)=a_n-sr_n$ and $a_n$ is bounded below. The aid's critical imbalance parameter is
\[
 \tau=\lim_{K\to\infty}\inf\{a_n/r_n:r_n\geq K\},
\]
using $\inf\varnothing=+\infty$. For the tested parameters, $s<\tau$ implies membership in $B_s$, while $s>\tau$ implies nonmembership. At a finite critical value the exact extra condition is
\[
 (\alpha,\beta)\in B_\tau
       \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \inf_n(a_n-\tau r_n)>-\infty.
\]
A ratio threshold alone does not decide this additive endpoint. Bounded-imbalance terms are controlled by the $B_0$ lower bound; the tail detects failure along arbitrarily imbalanced terms.

Therefore any subsequence with $P_n\to0$ must have $A_n\to\infty$ and imbalance tending to infinity. This is a restriction on possible Littlewood-small subsequences, not exclusion of them.
