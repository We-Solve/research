\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
A standard interpretation of minimal dispersion is as follows; the supplied record does not explicitly specify the class of boxes or the boundary convention, so this interpretation requires source verification. Let \(n,d\in\Z_{\geq 1}\). For a set \(P\subseteq[0,1]^d\) with \(|P|=n\), define
\[
\operatorname{disp}(P)=\sup\left\{\prod_{j=1}^{d}(b_j-a_j):\ 0\leq a_j<b_j\leq1\text{ for every }j,\quad P\cap\prod_{j=1}^{d}(a_j,b_j)=\varnothing\right\}.
\]
Thus the boxes in this provisional definition are open and axis-parallel, and their size is their \(d\)-dimensional volume. Define
\[
\operatorname{disp}^{*}(n,d)=\inf_{\substack{P\subseteq[0,1]^d\\ |P|=n}}\operatorname{disp}(P).
\]
Under this interpretation, the explicit proposed upper-bound target is
\[
\exists C>0\quad\forall d\in\Z_{\geq1}\quad\forall n\in\Z_{\geq1},\qquad n\geq d\ \Longrightarrow\ \operatorname{disp}^{*}(n,d)\leq C\frac{d}{n},
\]
