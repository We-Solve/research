\section{Finiteness of D(n)-quadruples for nonsquare n}\label{statement}

\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}$\mathbb N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$, and $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Q,\mathbb R,\mathbb C$ denote the integers, rationals, reals and complex numbers. Any other conventions are specified locally.


For $n\in\mathbb Z$, a $D(n)$-$m$-tuple is a set $A=\{a_1,\ldots,a_m\}\subset\mathbb N$ of distinct positive integers such that $a_i a_j+n$ is the square of an integer for every $i<j$.
\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
For each fixed nonzero integer $n$ which is not a perfect square, are there only finitely many $D(n)$-quadruples? \sref{statement}{2}
\subsection{Short English statement}
Does every fixed nonzero nonsquare parameter admit only finitely many positive integer quadruples?
