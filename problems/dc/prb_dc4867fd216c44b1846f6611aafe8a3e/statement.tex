\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
For a rational function $f$, its spherical derivative is
\[
 f^{\#}(z)=\frac{|f'(z)|}{1+|f(z)|^2},
\]
with the continuous spherical interpretation at a pole. The degree of $p/q$ in reduced form is $\max(\deg p,\deg q)$; polynomials are included among rational functions. The question concerns degree $N$ and varying positive radii.

\textbf{First requested bound.} Find the least upper bound $\phi(N)$ of the source expression
\[
 I(r,f)=\frac1r\int_0^r dt\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}
        \frac{|f'(re^{i\theta})|}{1+|f|^2}\,d\theta,
\]
for varying $r$ and $f$ of degree $N$. The argument of $f$ in the denominator is omitted; its natural literal reading is also $re^{i\theta}$. With that reading the integrand is independent of $t$, so the expression reduces to $\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f^{\#}(re^{i\theta})\,d\theta$. It scales by a positive factor $a$ under $f(z)\mapsto f(az)$ and $r\mapsto r/a$, which is incompatible with a finite uniform bound over both $r$ and $f$. Thus a missing argument, factor, normalization, or restriction must be checked before the intended finite extremal problem is determined. Replacing $r$ by $t$ alone would not remove this inverse-length scaling.

The record nevertheless reports
\[

 \phi(N)\ne O\bigl((\log N)^{1/2-\varepsilon}\bigr)
 \quad(N\to\infty),
\]
without specifying the quantifier or range for $\varepsilon$. These bounds are retained as claims about the intended source quantity, not asserted for the inconsistent literal reading above.
