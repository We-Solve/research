\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
Let \(X\) be a smooth projective surface over \(\C\). For a reduced irreducible curve \(C\subset X\), let \(C^2=C\cdot C\in\Z\) denote its self-intersection number in the intersection pairing of divisors on \(X\). Say that \(X\) has bounded negativity if there exists a finite constant \(b(X)\in\R_{\geq 0}\) such that every reduced irreducible curve \(C\subset X\) satisfies \(C^2\geq-b(X)\). Taking the bound to be nonnegative loses no generality.

\textbf{Bounded Negativity Conjecture for Complex Projective Surfaces.} Does every smooth projective surface over \(\C\) have bounded negativity? Explicitly, is it true that
\[
\forall\,X\text{ smooth projective surface over }\C,\quad
\exists\,b(X)\in\R_{\geq0},\quad
\forall\,C\subset X\text{ reduced irreducible curve},\quad
C^2\geq-b(X)?
\]
The bound may depend on \(X\), but must be independent of \(C\). No smoothness assumption is imposed on \(C\).

The supplied record labels this conjecture open. Its dated curation states that bounded negativity is false in positive characteristic and remains open in general in characteristic zero, including difficult blow-ups of the projective plane. These are supplied literature assessments, not independently verified conclusions. The question above retains the stated base field \(\C\). The report's supplied 2020 designation and its 2019 DOI and proposal-year metadata have not been reconciled.
\subsection{Short English statement}
For each smooth projective complex surface, is there a finite lower bound for the self-intersection numbers of all its reduced irreducible curves? The bound may vary with the surface. The supplied open status requires independent verification.
