\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}
In the conventional Euclidean formulation, fix a dimension $n\geq2$ and a bounded nonempty domain $\Omega\subset\mathbb R^n$, where domain means open and connected. A congruent copy is $R\Omega+a$, with $R\in O(n)$ and $a\in\mathbb R^n$. Consider continuous complex-valued functions on $\mathbb R^n$ and Lebesgue integration.

\textbf{Pompeiu problem, in the source's failure-of-uniqueness form.} Characterize the domains $\Omega$ for which there exists $f\in C(\mathbb R^n;\mathbb C)$, not identically zero, such that
\[
\int_{R\Omega+a}f(x)\,dx=0
\qquad\text{for every }R\in O(n)\text{ and }a\in\mathbb R^n.
\]
A domain is conventionally said to have the Pompeiu property when this vanishing condition instead forces $f=0$. Thus the displayed target asks for domains failing that property, not for those having it. \subsection{Short English statement}
Classify domains admitting a nonzero function whose integral vanishes over every rigidly moved copy. This is the failure of the usual Pompeiu uniqueness property; the source leaves the function and domain classes unspecified.
