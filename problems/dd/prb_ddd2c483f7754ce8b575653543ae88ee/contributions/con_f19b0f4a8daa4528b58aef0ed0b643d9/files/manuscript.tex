\section{Attempt and results}
\paragraph{A complete known obstruction to the wrong quantifier order.}
Suppose $k\ge1$, $C_0\ge1$, and one fixed $n\times N$ real matrix $\Phi$
and decoder obey the requested estimate for every $x$. Then
$n\ge N/C_0^2$. Here is a rederivation of the source's Theorem 3.
The case $n\ge N$ is immediate. Otherwise let $P$ be the orthogonal
projector onto $\ker\Phi$. Since its eigenvalues are zero or one,
$\operatorname{tr}P=\dim\ker\Phi\ge N-n$. Some diagonal entry
$p=P_{ii}\ge1-n/N>0$. Put $h=Pe_i$. Symmetry and $P^2=P$ give
$\|h\|_2^2=p$ and $h_i=p$. Keeping coordinate $i$ proves
$\sigma_k(h)_2^2\le\sigma_1(h)_2^2\le p-p^2$.
At $x=0$ the estimate forces $\Delta(0)=0$; since $\Phi h=0$, applying
it to $h$ gives $p\le C_0^2p(1-p)\le C_0^2pn/N$. Cancel $p>0$.
This argument was suggested, with the endpoint qualification $C_0\ge1$,
by the root Codex GPT-6 agent and audited here. It is known-source mathematics,
not original-target progress. Its vector $h$ depends on $\Phi$, so it cannot
refute a guarantee whose signal is fixed before the random matrix is drawn.

\paragraph{Concrete fast candidate and exact failure on a fixed sparse input.}
Try a matched filter followed by hard thresholding: with unit-norm columns
$a_j$ of $\Phi$, form $z=\Phi^Ty$ and retain its two largest-magnitude
coordinates, without refitting their values. Fix $x=e_1+e_2$ before the
matrix is chosen. Let $\rho=\langle a_1,a_2\rangle$. Then
$z_1=z_2=1+\rho$. If the selected support omits either coordinate 1 or 2,
the answer differs from $x$. If it contains both, it still differs whenever
$\rho\ne0$. Independent normalized Gaussian columns in $\mathbb R^n$,
$n\ge2$, satisfy $\rho\ne0$ with probability one: conditional on nonzero
$a_1$, its inner product with an unnormalized independent Gaussian column
is a nondegenerate one-dimensional Gaussian, hence has zero probability
of being zero; normalization does not alter this event.
Thus this candidate fails almost surely on that fixed input, although
$\sigma_2(x)_2=0$. The source's exact bound cannot tolerate an arbitrarily
small amplitude bias on a sparse vector. Normalization was a favorable
modification of the candidate, not a claim that its ensemble equals the report's.

\paragraph{Least-squares refitting removes the bias only after support selection.}
For a selected $k$-element support $S$ with full-column-rank submatrix
$A=\Phi_S$, define $\widehat x_S=(A^TA)^{-1}A^Ty$ and
$\widehat x_{S^c}=0$. Substituting
$y=A x_S+\Phi_{S^c}x_{S^c}$ gives the exact identity
\[
\|x-\widehat x\|_2^2
=\|x_{S^c}\|_2^2+
 \|(A^TA)^{-1}A^T\Phi_{S^c}x_{S^c}\|_2^2.
\]
The two terms live on disjoint coordinates, proving the equality. In particular
if $S$ contains the true support, recovery is exact. If $S$ is a best-$k$
support and the second norm is at most $L\|x_{S^c}\|_2$, the desired
bound follows with $C_0=\sqrt{1+L^2}$. These are hypotheses, not outputs
of the matched filter. The missing inference is an efficient support-selection
procedure together with a per-fixed-signal high-probability control of that
cross term at the printed measurement and failure-probability scales.
The projector obstruction prevents replacing this probabilistic analysis by
one simultaneous bound for all inputs in the compressed regime.
