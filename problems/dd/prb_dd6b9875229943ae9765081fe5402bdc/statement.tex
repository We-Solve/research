\section{Maximum Sets Avoiding One Prescribed Subset Sum}\label{statement}


\subsection{Definitions and mathematical statement}

\paragraph{Shared notation.}Unless a section says otherwise, $\N=\{1,2,\ldots\}$,
$\Z$ is the ring of integers, $\Q,\R,\C$ are the rational, real, and complex
fields, $[N]=\{1,\ldots,\lfloor N\rfloor\}$, and $\log$ is the natural
logarithm. For real functions of a parameter tending to infinity,
$f\ll g$ and $f=O(g)$ mean $|f|\leq Cg$ eventually for some fixed $C$;
$f\gg g$ reverses this comparison; $f=o(g)$ means $f/g\to0$;
$f\sim g$ means $f/g\to1$; and $f\asymp g$ means both order bounds.
A subscript on $O$ or $\ll$ specifies allowed constant dependence.
The expression $n^{o(1)}$ in an upper bound means growth smaller than
$n^\epsilon$ for each fixed $\epsilon>0$. Local definitions govern any
exception, finite-field convention, or use of the same symbol for another
object. An asymptotic claim is not automatically an effective algorithm.



For fixed $c>0$, write $H_c(n)=\max\{|A|:A\subseteq[1,\lfloor cn\rfloor]\cap\N,\ n\notin\Sigma(A)\}$, where $\Sigma(A)=\{\sum_{a\in B}a:B\subseteq A\}$. Each summand is used at most once. The source asks for the asymptotic extremum and its dependence on the arithmetic of $n$; it specifies no precise meaning of irregular''. \sref{statement}{1} \sref{statement}{2}

\paragraph{Statement recorded in the supplied source.}
Let $c>0$ and $n$ be some large integer. What is the size of the largest $A\subseteq \{1,\ldots,\lfloor cn\rfloor\}$ such that $n$ is not a sum of a subset of $A$? Does this depend on $n$ in an irregular way? \sref{statement}{1}

\subsection{Short English statement}

How many integers can be chosen from a proportionally sized interval while ensuring that no subset sums to one prescribed target?
